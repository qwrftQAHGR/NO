# R05 · Nobility

*Who counts as noble in the Double Crown, how you prove it, what it buys you, what it costs, and how blood, karat and cards have turned a ladder of titles into a ladder of bodies.*

## 1. The ladder

### Valdau
| Rank | Title (m / f) | Address (from the Titelbuch, → I01) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Royal | König / Königin | *Euer Majestät* | the Greifenstein |
| Royal blood | Erzherzog / Erzherzogin | *Euer Königliche Hoheit* | children and grandchildren of kings |
| Princely | Fürst / Fürstin | *Euer Durchlaucht* | heads of the Seven Chairs and a few others; their children are Prinz / Prinzessin |
| Comital | Graf / Gräfin | *Euer Hochgeboren* | unmarried daughters are *Komtesse* |
| Baronial | Freiherr / Freifrau / Freiin | *Euer Hochwohlgeboren* | the backbone of the Landtage |
| Knightly | Ritter, Edler / Edle | *Euer Wohlgeboren* | the lowest titled rank; *Edler von …* is the usual patent of the ink-nobility |
| Landed gentry | *Landmann* (no title) | *Euer Gnaden* | old families with Landtag seats and no title, and proud of it |

In Valdau, **wives take their husbands' ranks and offices**: *Frau Hofrätin*, *Frau Oberstleutnantin*, *Frau Bürgermeisterin*. Widows keep them as *Frau Hofratswitwe*. In the **Spindelhäuser** (see §6) it goes the other way: a husband who marries in takes his wife's name and is *Herr Gemahl*. His own title, if he had one, becomes a courtesy.

### The honorific nobility of the offices
Wiedenbrunn's peculiar mania. Beside the real titles there is a swarm of **honorary titles** given by the Crown to officials and to anyone who has paid for one: **Hofrat** (court councillor), **Regierungsrat**, **Kammerrat**, **Kanzleirat**, **Kommerzialrat** for merchants, **Medizinalrat** for physicians. They carry no land and no blood, only the right to be addressed by them. In practice the right is a duty, because failing to call a Hofrat "Herr Hofrat" is an insult that can end a friendship or start a lawsuit. Coffeehouse joke: *"In Wiedenbrunn every second man is a Rat, and the rest are rats."*

In formal Catalan, nobles and honored citizens are **En** (m) and **Na** (f): *En Galceran Mirabent*, *Na Elisenda Ferrer*. Valdauers can never remember which of them get it.

## 2. Kinds of nobility

- **Uradel**: the ancient houses, noble before the union of 1291. Their charters are in Latin and their manners in disrepair. Ilsenau (since 1229), Kalteneck and Starkenberg are Uradel.
- **Kronadel**: houses raised by the Crown between 1291 and the Distaff Sanction of 1555. Most of the counts.
- **Briefadel** ("letter-nobility"): families ennobled by letters patent since 1555. They are officials, army officers, bankers and contractors. Valdauers sort them by origin:
  - **Tintenadel**, the ink-nobility of the offices, whose coat of arms usually features a pen;
  - **Kanonenadel**, the cannon-nobility of officers ennobled for service;
  - **Geldadel**, money-nobility; bankers who lent the Crown enough.
  Old nobles call them all "*die von Gestern*," the ones from yesterday.
- **Krautjunker** ("cabbage squires"): poor country nobility with one decayed manor, three cows, a family tree going back to 1300 and nothing to eat. There are a great many in the Kornau and the Hallgau. They are fiercely proud, the backbone of every Landtag's resistance to everything, and the source of most of the realm's officers below the rank of major.
- **Pit-blood**: commoners whose mining ancestry gives them noble-grade karat (→ I50). They are not noble. They want to be. See §8.

## 3. Estates and diets

- **The four Stände of Valdau**: **Prälaten** (prelates: abbots, bishops, the great convents), **Herren** (lords: the titled), **Ritter** (knights: the untitled and lesser nobility) and **Städte** (the royal towns). Each Valdauer land has a **Landtag** of the four. They vote taxes, hear grievances, and spend three weeks arguing over seating.
- **The four braços of Rocalba**: **Oros**, **Copes**, **Espases** and **Bastos** (→ R01, I29). Rocalban nobles sit in Espases. Rocalban honored citizens sit in Oros and despise the Espases nobles for being poor.
- **The Seven Chairs**, the planetary college of the great houses (→ I38), sits above all of them in matters of succession.

## 4. Privileges and duties

**What a noble gets:**
- **Steuerfreiheit**: freedom from the Kontribution on their demesne lands. Their peasants pay instead.
- **Degenrecht**, the sword-right: to wear a sword in the city. Commoners may carry a knife no longer than a hand.
- **Gerichtsstand**: to be tried only by the Hofgericht or the Landmarschall, before peers. Commoners can't sue a noble in an ordinary court. The **Kavaliersdelikt** (a gentleman's offense) is an offense the courts treat as a matter of manners: a duel, a seduced chambermaid, an unpaid tailor (→ I20).
- **Robot**: labor service from their peasants (→ R01).
- **Jagdrecht**: hunting rights. Poaching on a noble's land is a crime; a noble's hunt trampling a peasant's crop is weather.
- **Patronat**: the right to appoint the Zeuge of the parish church on their land.
- **Pfandrecht**, the right to hold pledges for "Leib" (bodily) service. Since a Patent of 1519 only nobles and licensed houses may contract body-pledges (→ R07). Bürger may hold household pledges only, and they get round it.
- **Kammerzutritt**: access to the royal apartments at the grade one's rank and office allow (→ I69).
- **Precedence** at every door, table and procession (→ I69).

**What a noble owes:**
- **Military service**, in theory personal, in practice by raising, funding or commanding men. The Inhaber system means great houses own regiments (→ R01).
- **Attendance** at the Landtag, and at court when summoned.
- **Ehrenpflicht**, the duty of honor: to answer an insult, to keep one's word to one's equals, and to pay gambling debts before tradesmen's (→ I20).
- **The Kontribution** on non-demesne lands, voted by the Landtag and paid late.
- **The Assay** (see §5).

## 5. Proving you are noble: Hoffähigkeit and the Doppelprobe

To be presented at court, to receive Kammerzutritt, to serve as a Hofdame or Kämmerer, or to marry a member of a Seven Chairs house without a scandal, one must be **hoffähig**: court-fit. Hoffähigkeit requires the **Doppelprobe**, the double proof:

1. **The Ahnenprobe**, the proof of ancestors: **sixteen noble great-great-grandparents**, *die sechzehn Ahnen*, shown on a painted family tree certified by the **Heroldsamt** (heralds' office). One commoner among the sixteen spoils it. Families commission elaborate trees with all sixteen shields, and families with a gap commission elaborate lies. Rocalba's version needs eight.
2. **The Blutprobe**, the proof of blood: an assay by the **Hofprobieramt** showing at least **8 karat** in any metal, entered in the **Feinbuch** (→ I49). The Blutprobe was added in 1591 by the Flask King. Old families resented it, and new families bought their way around it. It is why ink-nobles marry pit-blood daughters, and why Krautjunker, who have sixteen ancestors and no karat because they married their cows' owners, are bitter.

Exceptions are granted by royal grace, the **Gnadenzutritt**: the King can declare anyone hoffähig. He does this for favorites, foreign ambassadors, mistresses and sometimes their husbands. A hoffähig person's name is entered in the **Hofbuch**, the court register. Being struck from it, *gestrichen*, is social death until reinstatement, which can be bought.

## 6. The distaff: Spindelhäuser and the Distaff Sanction

Valdau has a peculiarity Valdauers barely notice and foreigners never stop remarking on: **a sizeable part of its high nobility inherits through women.**

- **The Ilsenbrief of 1229** allowed the daughters of Ilse "the Assayer" to inherit the silver-house of Ilsenau ahead of her sons (→ R12). For three centuries this was a single oddity.
- **The Distaff Sanction of 1555** (*die Spindelsanktion*) was issued by **Queen Margarete "die Spinnerin"** (r. 1549–1571), who had inherited the crown herself only because she had no brothers. It did two things. It declared the Crown a **Kunkellehen**, a fief that passes to daughters if all male heirs of the body fail. And it allowed **any noble house** to petition to become a **Spindelhaus**, a house that inherits through the distaff side (*die Spindelseite*), where daughters inherit before sons.
- About **twenty-three houses** are Spindelhäuser today: Ilsenau first and proudest, and a scattering of Moon- and Venus-blooded counts and barons. They are disproportionately silver and copper houses, because those bloodlines throw more daughters, and because their gifts (fertility, kin-sense, allure) are thought to pass better through mothers.

**How a Spindelhaus works:**
- The **Hausmutter** (house-mother) rules. Formally she is the Fürstin, Gräfin or Freifrau in her own right.
- The **Erbtochter** (heir-daughter) is designated by the Hausmutter with the consent of the house's women in council, the **Spindelrat**.
- **Husbands marry in** (*Einheirat*). They take the house name and its livery and have no vote. They are chosen for blood, money, alliance or looks, and the Spindelhäuser have a famous taste for **femme husbands**: Zierherren, soft-voiced men who "don't compete" (→ R11). Ilsenau's in-married husbands are called **Mondbräutigame**, Moon-bridegrooms. Outsiders call them **Spindelmänner**.
- **Sons** are given a **Sohnesmitgift**, a son's dowry, and married *out* into other houses, sent to the Church, or kept at home as companionable uncles.
- **Futa daughters** are the hidden engine. A daughter of both natures can sire heirs inside the female line without involving an outside man. The 1558 **Patent on Distaff Paternity** recognizes a futa's fatherhood for inheritance in a Spindelhaus (→ R10). It also means the Spindelhäuser have a quiet, intense relationship with kin intimacy (→ R06).

**What others say:** *Pantoffelhäuser*, slipper-houses, where the men are henpecked. Starkenberg officers call Spindelhaus husbands *Kammerkätzchen mit Bart*, chambermaids with beards. Women of non-distaff houses tend to envy Spindelhaus women and say so only after the second glass.

## 7. The supernatural ties

Nobility in the Double Crown is not only a matter of law. It is in the body.

- **Karat** (→ R03, I49). A noble house is a metal. Its fineness is public and measured, and it decides whom one may marry, what one is fit for, and whether one's grandchildren will break.
- **Handwerk**. The gifts of fine blood make the nobility useful as well as decorative: Mars officers, Jupiter judges, Mercury couriers, Moon nurses, Venus diplomats, Saturn physicians and the Sun on the throne.
- **The Trumps** (→ R04). Seven are held in Valdauer great houses. A house with a Trump is a different order of power from one without, and it lives differently, as §5 of R04 describes.
- **Planetary precedence.** Since 1591 the Seven Chairs walk, sit and are announced in planetary order: **Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn**. At any door where two great houses meet, the one with the earlier planet goes first. Ilsenau's Moon puts its women second only to the Crown, ahead of the Starkenberg princes who command the army. Starkenberg have never accepted this (→ I38, I40).
- **The Assay as a sacrament of class.** Commoners believe, wrongly, that nobles have different blood. The Hofprobieramt's records show that some miners have finer blood than some counts. This is the most dangerous fact in the realm, and the Knappen petition is built on it (→ I50).

## 8. Who is changing it, and who resents it

- **House Ehrenreith's reformers** want: a capped Robot, a register of all pledges (the Codex), the abolition of the Gnadenzutritt, and taxes on noble demesne in wartime. They are the Hofkammer's clerks, and most of them are ink-nobles who would like the old nobility to pay (→ I43).
- **House Sulzbach and the Geldadel** want what the old houses have: a Chair (the **Salt Chair** campaign), karat (bought by marrying pit-blood and Krautjunker daughters), and the sixteen ancestors (bought from heralds) (→ I45).
- **The pit-blood families** want **Grubenadel**: a recognized lesser nobility for commoners with 12+ karat. They want the Degenrecht, freedom from Robot, and the right to marry into the Briefadel without scandal. The 1679 petition was tabled; the 1698 petition is waiting (→ I50).
- **The Krautjunker** want everyone else to stop changing things.
- **The Clear Church** (→ I74) condemns noble incest and the purchase of dispensations, and preaches that a miner's soul is as fine as a prince's. It is very popular among miners.
- **The Rocalban nobles** want Wiedenbrunn's nobility to stop pretending that Rocalban counts are country gentry.

## 9. Noble life (a sketch)

- **Childhood**: wet-nurses (→ I57), governesses, tutors (*Hofmeister*). The children of great houses grow up surrounded by servants and slaves and very little by their parents.
- **Youth**: for sons the **Kavalierstour**, one to three years abroad with a Hofmeister (→ I19). For daughters the **Stiftsjahr**, a year as an *Elevin* in a great household, the Ilsenau most prestigious, or at a Damenstift (→ I19, I31).
- **Majority**: **Volljahr** in Valdau comes at **eighteen** for nobles and twenty-one for commoners. The odd rule dates from 1555, when the Distaff Sanction needed noble heiresses to be able to sign contracts young. At eighteen a noble is presented at court, enters the Feinbuch with a fresh assay, and may be pledged, married, laid over (→ R04) or ruined.
- **Marriage**: contracts negotiated by families, certified by the Hofprobieramt, timed by the Stundenamt, blessed or bought from the Church (→ I17, I55).
- **Service**: court offices, the army, the Church, diplomacy, the Landtag.
- **Pleasure**: everything else in this bible.
- **Money**: estates, offices, the Banco, gambling, and marrying it. Most great houses are rich in land and poor in coin, and owe Vellora (→ I35).
- **Death**: a beautiful funeral, with the heart and body buried apart (→ I18).

## 10. Views

- **Nobles on commoners:** *"die Leut',"* "the people," in the tone one uses for weather. To the Uradel, Bürger are useful; to the Briefadel they are dangerous, because the Briefadel were Bürger last generation.
- **Commoners on nobles:** deference to the face; satire in the Gstanzl; gossip without end. Wiedenbrunners adore the royal family and the great ladies as one adores actors, and mock them the same way: *"Herr Graf, Sie haben was verloren!" — "Was denn?" — "Ihr Kinn."* ("Count, you've lost something!" "What?" "Your chin.")
- **Old nobles on new:** *Katzengold*, fool's gold (→ R03).
- **New nobles on old:** *Krautjunker*, *Spröde*, the brittle ones. They point out that the old blood is cracking and their own is healthy.
- **Foreigners:**
  - **Almagrans** consider Valdauer nobility lax: too many women inheriting, too many ink-nobles, too little closed blood (→ I32).
  - **Ormándi gentry**, one in twelve of whom is noble, find Valdauer counts effete and Valdauer titles absurd (→ I33).
  - **Velloran** patricians are noble by ledger. They consider the Valdauer obsession with karat a superstition and the Valdauer obsession with titles a market (→ I35).
  - **The Sworn Valleys** abolished their nobility in 1386 and remind everyone of it (→ I36).

## 11. Slang and insults

- **Blaublut / Blaues Blut**: noble blood. Odd in a realm that assays it as metal.
- **Feinling**: a fine-blooded snob.
- **Spröder / Spröde**: brittle-blooded; also prudish.
- **Katzengold / Katzengoldadel**: fake nobility, bought.
- **Tintenadel, Kanonenadel, Geldadel**: see §2.
- **"von und zu Habenichts"**: "of and at Have-Nothing," a nobleman with nothing but a name.
- **Krautjunker**: country squire.
- **Pantoffelhaus**, **Spindelmann**: a distaff house; a husband who married into one.
- **Hofratswitwe**: the widow of an honorary councillor. Used for any pretentious woman.
- **"Sechzehn Ahnen und kein Hemd"**: sixteen ancestors and no shirt.
- **"Gestrichen"**: struck from the Hofbuch; dropped from society.

## HOOKS

1. **The sixteenth ancestor.** A family that wants to marry a daughter to an Ilsenau cousin presents its Ahnenprobe. One of the sixteen shields belongs to a great-great-grandmother who, it can be shown, was a bath-maid bought in 1611. The heralds haven't noticed. Someone in the household has.
2. **The Gnadenzutritt.** The King grants court access by grace to a woman of no family, and the Obersthofmeisterin must present her at the next Cercle. Which Ilsenau woman does it, and how warmly, will decide whether the court accepts her or eats her.
3. **Volljahr.** On his first day of legal majority a noble is assayed, presented and entered in the Hofbuch. A noble who has never had one (a son of nineteen, asleep at eighteen) has missed all three, and the Heroldsamt sends a polite letter asking when.
4. **The ink-noble's offer.** An Edler von Tannberg, the Hofkammer's most useful clerk, offers to make a property problem of the household disappear. In return he wants an invitation to the Ilsenau Mondempfang (→ I14), where nobody of his rank has ever been seen.
5. **The Spindelmann's complaint.** An Ilsenau Mondbräutigam, a husband who married in twelve years ago, comes to the protagonist as the only other man of the house. He wants to talk, or to complain, or something less clear, and he doesn't know which.
6. **The Krautjunker's daughter.** A penniless Krautjunker with sixteen spotless ancestors and two karat offers his daughter's hand to any Geldadel family that will pay his debts. The daughter wants nothing to do with it and is looking for a way out, by way of a Stiftsjahr in the Ilsenau household.
