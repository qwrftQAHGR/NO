# I32 · Almagra

*The Castilian-flavored kingdom of the red plateau west of Rocalba: the cousin-dynasty whose king believes he is made of glass and holds the World with no heir; its closed blood and its Holy Crucible; its honor, its veiled ladies and its licensed gallants; and the succession everyone in Wiedenbrunn is waiting for.*

**See also:** R01 (the Glass King's empty cradle) · I39 House Greifenstein (the cadet branch) · I69 The Ceremonial (which came from here) · I05 (chocolate, the Schokoladenpartei) · I46 The Register of Trumps (XXI) · I45 (Casa de Ossorno) · I75 The Glass Prince · R06 Kinship

## 1. The land

**Los Reinos de Almagra** (the Kingdoms of Almagra; Valdauers say **Almagra**) lie **west of Rocalba**, over the dry **Serra del Vent**, on a vast high **red plateau**: **la Meseta Roja**. *Almagra* is the red ochre of its earth. It is a hard, beautiful, empty country of wheat-fields and sheep-walks, stone villages, castles on every hill, dry riverbeds, black bulls, and a sky that never ends. Winters are freezing and summers burning, and the wind never stops.

- **Peñalcázar**: the capital, a fortress-city on a sheer rock above a gorge, with walls, towers, a hundred churches, a palace of grim stone (the **Alcázar Real**), and a great arcaded square, the **Plaza Mayor**, for markets, bullfights and the Santo Crisol's public trials. About 60,000 people, most of them noble, clerical or serving those who are.
- **Toralba**: the university city and seat of the **Santo Crisol** (§5), famous for its theology, its swords and its executions.
- **Medinaval**: the wool-city, seat of **el Honrado Concejo de la Cañada**, the sheep-owners' guild that drives a million merino sheep twice a year across the whole plateau on the royal drove-roads.
- **Almenara del Mar**: the western seaport on the outer Ocean, from which galleons sail to Almagra's **Islas de Poniente** (the Western Isles), a distant colonial empire of islands and coasts that sends back **silver**, **cacao** and **sugar**, and swallows Almagra's younger sons.
- **The Rocalban border**: the Serra del Vent and its passes. The Almagran frontier fortresses face Rocalba, and they have not always been friendly.

## 2. Names and ranks

- **Names**: given name(s), then **two surnames**, the father's and the mother's joined by *y*: *Doña Inés Carvajal de Ossorno y Peñaroja*. Nobles add their lands: *Don Rodrigo Pacheco de Alcorza, Conde de Valdesotos*. Valdauers can never remember all of it, and Almagrans never let them shorten it.
- **Don / Doña** for gentlefolk. *Vuestra Merced* ("your grace") to equals and above, *Vuestra Excelencia* to grandees, *Vuestra Majestad* to the King.
- **Los Grandes** (the grandees): the twenty highest houses, who may **keep their hats on** in the King's presence. They live in Peñalcázar on their rents and their debts.
- **Títulos**: dukes, marquises and counts.
- **Hidalgos**: the lesser nobility, numberless and often penniless. They own a sword, a family tree, a rusty cuirass and a coat-of-arms carved over the door of a ruined house. *"Hidalgo de gotera"* (a hidalgo of the leaking roof). Valdauers call them **Hidalgos von Habenichts**, of Have-Nothing.

## 3. The Crown and the Glass King

**Government**: absolute monarchy, ruled in practice through **Consejos** (councils of State, War, the Isles and the Crisol) and through the King's **Valido**, the royal favorite who acts as first minister.

**The King**: **Fernán VI**, called **el Rey de Vidrio**, the Glass King.
- **Born 1662**, king since **1666** at the age of four, now thirty-seven.
- He is of the **Casa de Grifo**, the Almagran branch of the **Greifenstein** (→ I39), descended from a younger son of the Valdauer royal house who married the Almagran heiress in 1519.
- The Almagran branch never accepted the Valdauer **Annealing Edict** of 1633 (→ I75). It kept **closing its blood** (§4) for a further three generations. Fernán's parents were **uncle and niece**, and their parents were first cousins, twice.
- He assays at **23 karat of gold**, the highest fineness ever entered in either kingdom's books, with a streak that **splinters** across the whole needle (→ R03).
- He is **brittle in every way**:
  - **Glanzwahn**: he believes **he is made of glass**. He will not be touched without gloves. He sleeps on straw and furs, and won't sit on a hard chair for fear of shattering. He walks very slowly.
  - Fragile bones, the **Greifenkinn** so heavy he can barely chew, fits of melancholy and falling-sickness.
  - **Two marriages and no child.**
- He is also, by every account, **gentle, devout, frightened, kind to animals and to servants, and very lonely.**
- **The Glass King bears the World.** Trump **XXI, Die Welt** (Almagran *el Mundo*) has stayed in the Almagran royal line for a century and a half (→ I46).
  - **Gift**: he understands and is understood in every tongue. No border, wall or law of passage can stop him. Those who swear to him in his lands feel him as their own.
  - **Burden**: **Weltschmerz**, world-ache. He feels the distant sufferings of his lands (droughts, famines, wars on the Isles) as pain in his own body. A strong king could bear it. Fernán can't. Courtiers say the World is killing him, and physicians say his blood is, and both are probably right.
- **His Valido**: **Don Gaspar de Ossorno, Count-Duke of Valdesotos**. He is a grandee of the **Casa de Ossorno**, the closest-blooded house in Almagra after the royal one (22 karat of gold, → I45), and rules for him, devoted, ambitious and ruthless.

### The queens
- **The first queen**, **María Luisa**, a Valdauer princess of House Holdegg, married 1679 and died 1689, childless. Almagra whispered poison. Valdau whispered the Glass King. Holdegg whispered nothing, and stopped sending daughters (→ I42).
- **The second queen**, **Mariana**, an Ormándi princess of the **Kerecsényi** family (→ I33, I45), married 1690. She is thirty, clever, bored, ambitious, and has announced **three pregnancies** that came to nothing. She is said to keep a Valdauer Cortejo, a Rocalban one, and an Almagran guardsman.

### The succession
When the Glass King dies (and every physician in two kingdoms says it will be soon), **two things fall at once**:
1. **His crown**, to be claimed by:
   - **Archduke Ferdinand Karl of Greifenstein**, younger brother of Albrecht VII of Valdau, grandson of an Almagran infanta. He is backed by the Valdauer **Schokoladenpartei** (→ I05), and opposed by everyone in Almagra who remembers Valdau marrying out.
   - **The Count-Duke of Ossorno** himself, through an illegitimate royal ancestor of 1580, legitimized in 1601. He is backed by the grandees of closed blood.
   - **Don Juan Manuel de Grifo**, the Glass King's **illegitimate half-brother**, son of the late king by a mistress. He is healthy, outbred, popular with the army and the people, and loathed by the grandees.
   - Almagran law requires the **Cortes of Almagra** to confirm a king when the line fails. They haven't met in forty years.
2. **His card**, the **World**, to the **nearest heart** (→ R04). The Fall-Reckoners' books in Wiedenbrunn carry long odds on:
   - **Queen Mariana** (bed 3, if he goes to her bed, which is disputed);
   - **his half-sister, the Infanta Teresa** (blood 2, beloved, the only person he lets touch him without gloves);
   - **his confessor**, **Fray Anselmo** (no tie, but holds his hand every night);
   - **his dwarf companion, Pedrito** (no tie, but sleeps in his room);
   - **Don Juan Manuel** (blood 2);
   - and at long odds **House Ilsenau**, by **milk**: an Ilsenau daughter gave him **First Milk** in 1662 (→ R12, I57).

   A World that falls to a confessor, a dwarf, a bastard or a Valdauer Moon-house would be a disaster or a miracle, depending on who you are.

## 4. Sangre cerrada (closed blood)

Almagra's nobility practices **sangre cerrada**, closed blood: marriage within the house and among the closest-blooded houses, to keep the fineness high.
- **Uncle-niece** and **first-cousin** marriages are the norm. Royal sibling marriages have happened three times since 1500.
- The **Santo Crisol** keeps **el Libro de la Sangre** (the Book of Blood), Almagra's Feinbuch. It records every noble's fineness and every marriage's degree, and grants **dispensaciones de sangre** (blood-dispensations) for a price that makes Valdau's Tariff look cheap (→ I55).
- **Pruebas de sangre** (blood-proofs): to hold office, enter an order or marry into the grandees, one must prove one's fineness and one's ancestry. Forged proofs are a capital crime and a flourishing trade.
- Almagra regards Valdau's **Annealing Edict** and its Drittel rule as **cowardice**: Valdau has **lost its nerve**. Valdau regards Almagra's Glass King as the proof.
- **Commoners** are forbidden all nearness, and the Crisol prosecutes **commoner incest** with whippings and the galleys. The nobility's closed blood and the peasants' scourged backs are the same law read from two ends, and the peasants have noticed.

## 5. El Santo Crisol (the Holy Crucible)

Almagra's great institution: a **tribunal of faith and of the Art**, founded 1478 to test alchemists and assay blood, which has spread to cover everything.
- **It licenses alchemists** and burns frauds. Transmutation-swindlers are burned in the Plaza Mayor with their furnaces.
- **It keeps the Libro de la Sangre** and judges blood-proofs.
- **It hunts heresy**: the Clear Church, Kesrâni converts who relapse, the **Nestelknüpferin** (knot-curser, → I13), and anyone who says the Bride is not a virgin. (The Almagran church holds the Bride's virginity *before* the Wedding as dogma. Montalba thinks this is absurd.)
- **It prosecutes the pecado nefando**, the unspeakable sin of men with men, with burning, in theory; with galleys, in practice, unless you are a grandee (→ R09).
- **Its trials** end in the **auto de crisol**: a great public ceremony in the Plaza Mayor of Peñalcázar or Toralba. The accused walk in yellow robes painted with flames, the court watches from balconies, sentences are read for hours, and the condemned are handed to the secular arm. The last **burning** was in 1694, of a Velloran alchemist who claimed to have made gold. Most sentences are penances, fines, galleys and exile.
- **Its head**: **el Crisolero Mayor**, the Grand Crucible-Master, a Dominican-like friar of the **Order of the Assay** (*Orden de la Prueba*).
- **Its reach**: it has agents in Llucmar watching Rocalban alchemists. Valdau won't allow it in Valdau, and it does not need to be allowed.

## 6. Religion

The **Wedding Church**, in its strictest form, under the **Patriarch of Toralba**, who acknowledges the Archbishop of the Bridegroom's See in Wiedenbrunn and **not the Abbess of Montalba**, a woman and a Rocalban (→ I54).
- **The Bridegroom** is emphasized, and the **Bride** is shown **veiled in black** and holding her hands over her heart.
- **The Läuterwochen** processions are famous: **los encapuchados** (the hooded), thousands of penitents in tall pointed hoods with eyeholes, barefoot, carrying candles and crosses and great carved scenes of the Wedding on platforms, through the night streets of Peñalcázar to drums.
- The Almagran church **opposes the Tariff of Nearness** as too cheap. Almagra's own dispensaciones cost more and require more proofs.

## 7. Honor, desire and the veil

- **La honra** (honor): Almagra's obsession. A man's honor lies in his blood, his courage and **his women's chastity**. A wife's affair is a husband's death unless he kills someone, and the stage is full of husbands who do.
- **And yet**: private life is famously supple, and honor is about **appearances**:
  - **The dueña**: an older woman, widowed or poor, who chaperones a lady everywhere. Dueñas are bribed constantly.
  - **The reja**: the iron window-grille of every ground-floor window. Lovers court through it at night, the **galán** in the street with a guitar, the lady inside. *"Pelar la pava"* ("plucking the turkey") is courting at the reja. What can be passed through a reja is a famous joke.
  - **The cortejo**: a married lady's **acknowledged gallant**. He carries her fan, accompanies her to Mass and the theatre, sits at her estrado, visits her at her toilette, and is understood to do more. Her husband tolerates him, because the husband is someone else's cortejo. Valdau imported the cortejo with the Ceremonial (→ R08).
  - **Las embozadas** (the veiled): ladies walk out **veiled** with a black mantle drawn across the face so that **only one eye shows**. They are entirely anonymous, so they go where they like and meet whom they like, and a husband can pass his wife in the street and not know her. The Crisol has tried to ban the one-eyed veil four times.
  - **The estrado**: the ladies' raised platform in every great house, where women sit on cushions in the Kesrâni way and receive. Men may not step onto it unless invited, and an invitation onto the estrado means a great deal (→ I02).
  - **The convents** are full of **amistades particulares** (particular friendships), which the abbesses ignore (→ R09).
  - **The Sarabanda**, banned since 1583 and danced since 1583 (→ I11).
  - **La Mancebía**: the licensed brothel-quarter of Peñalcázar, under the Crown's licence and the Crisol's inspection.
- **The lindo**: the stage-type of the vain, perfumed, effeminate man. He is laughed at in every comedy, and the court is full of lindos (→ R11).

## 8. Slavery

Harsh and bureaucratic (→ R07):
- **galley-slaves** (convicts and captives) on the Almagran galleys;
- **slaves in the silver mines** of the Western Isles, and in Almagra's own mercury mine, which is poorer than Cinabre and which they envy (→ I30);
- **household slaves** in every great house;
- and **esclavos de ganancia**: slaves who work for wages in the city (water-carriers, porters, laundresses, prostitutes) and **pay their owners a fixed daily fee**, keeping the rest. Some buy their freedom. Most don't.

## 9. Trade and stereotypes

- **Exports**:
  - **merino wool** (*lana fina*), the finest in the world, from the Cañada's flocks;
  - **saffron**;
  - **warhorses** of the Meseta, whose white strain founded the Kornau stud (→ R01);
  - **Toralba blades**;
  - **silver**, **cacao** and **sugar** from the Isles;
  - leather, olive oil, and **perfumed gloves** (*guantes de ámbar*, → I07).
- **Imports**: **quicksilver** from Cinabre (needed for its silver mines), cannon from the Eisenmark, Velloran glass and credit, Rocalban cloth.
- **What others say of Almagrans**:
  - proud, pious and poor; *"garlic and genealogy"*;
  - dressed in black, and sitting on their honor;
  - *"an Almagran would rather starve than work, and rather die than be laughed at."*
- **What Almagrans say of others**:
  - **Valdauers**: *los primos blandos*, the soft cousins, who marry out, let women inherit, drink Kesrâni coffee and can't hold their blood;
  - **Rocalbans**: shopkeepers in lace;
  - **Velloran**: *cambistas del mar*, money-changers of the sea, who would sell the Bride's veil by the yard;
  - **Kesrâni**: the enemy of the faith, though the Almagran court buys their silk.

---

## 10. Relations with the Double Crown

- **Cousins**: the same dynasty in two branches, intermarried for two centuries.
- **The Ceremonial** came from Almagra, and so did chocolate and the black court dress (→ I69, I05, I06).
- **The Milk Treaty of 1631**: Ilsenau supplies the First Milk and trained dides for Almagran royal infants (→ R12, I57).
- **The Succession**: the great question of the next years (§3). Valdau's war party would fight for Archduke Ferdinand Karl. Rocalba fears an Almagran war on its border, or a Valdauer one marching through it.
- **Rocalba**: Almagra eyes **Cinabre** and courts the **Rocalbistes** and **Casa Mirabent** (→ I29, I30, I41).
- **The ambassador in Wiedenbrunn**: **Don Baltasar de Ossorno, Marqués de Peñaroja**, the Valido's nephew. He is of closed blood (21 karat), exquisitely courteous, black-clad, chocolate-pouring, and the Schokoladenpartei's host.

## HOOKS

1. **The gloved hand.** The Glass King's half-sister, the **Infanta Teresa**, the only person he lets touch him bare-handed, arrives in Wiedenbrunn incognito with one dueña. She asks to meet the Ilsenau, his milk-kin, to ask what the Fall-Reckoners are saying about who his heart is.
2. **The embozada.** On the Graben, a woman in the Almagran one-eyed veil falls into step beside the protagonist and asks, in perfect court Valdauer, whether he'd like to know what the Almagran ambassador says about the Ilsenau after his third cup of chocolate.
3. **The estrado.** At the Almagran embassy's tertulia (salon), the ambassador's wife invites the protagonist up onto her estrado, the ladies' platform, in front of the whole room. An Almagran gentleman may not refuse. A Valdauer may, and the ambassador is watching to see which he is.
4. **The World's odds.** The Café Sküs betting-book posts new odds on the World's fall: *"Haus Ilsenau, by milk, 40 to 1."* Within the hour the Almagran ambassador's secretary has put money on it, and the Valido's agents in Wiedenbrunn want to know what he knows.
5. **The auto de crisol.** A Rocalban alchemist of the Mondküche's acquaintance has been arrested by Santo Crisol agents in Llucmar, illegally, and smuggled toward the Almagran border. He knows the Mondküche's recipes. A rescue must happen before the Serra del Vent.
6. **The fourth pregnancy.** Queen Mariana announces a fourth pregnancy. Her Valdauer cortejo is in Wiedenbrunn and very frightened. A Valdauer physician who examined her says privately that she is not pregnant, and then that she will be, *"if someone of the right blood can be got to Peñalcázar quickly."*
