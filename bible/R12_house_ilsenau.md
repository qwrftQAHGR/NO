# R12 · House Ilsenau

*The protagonist's house. A matriarchal Moon-house of silver blood that has nursed every royal infant since 1352, runs the Queen's household, presides over the court's marriage-beds, holds the Moon among its women, and has one son: the one who slept.*

**See also:** I57 The Ammenhaus & Milk-Kinship · I26 The Weißach & the Spiegelsee · I53 The Bleischlaf & the Sleepers · I69 The Ceremonial · I19 The Stiftsjahr · I72 The Bath-Slave Empress · I46 The Register of Trumps · R05 Nobility (§6) · R10 Futas · R13 The Quickening

*This file describes the house as an institution: its history, places, customs, offices, gifts and machinery. Its living members are left to the cast.*

Wiedenbrunn calls the women of the house **die Mondfrauen**, the Moon-women, sometimes fondly and sometimes not. Behind their backs they are also **die Ammen**, the wet-nurses. Spoken in a certain tone that is an insult, and the Ilsenau take it as a compliment.

## 2. History

**Ilse the Assayer (c. 1180–1251).** The house begins with a woman, as the house never tires of saying. **Ilse** was the daughter of a silver-assayer in the Weißach valley. She married **Ritter Konrad von der Au**, lord of a damp castle on a lake promontory, a small knightly house living off the silver tithes. When he died young she took over the assaying of the mines herself. The miners swore she could read a streak of ore by touch, and they were probably right, since she had Moon blood and a gift for it. She lent King Ottokar II of Valdau silver for a war, and he could not repay it. He paid instead with the **Ilsenbrief of 1229**, a charter letting her daughters inherit the castle, the tithes and the assay ahead of her sons. The castle was renamed **Ilsenau**, Ilse's meadow. Her portrait in the Mondsaal shows a narrow, unsmiling woman with a touchstone in one hand and a key in the other.

**1291: the union.** Ilsenau silver helped pay for the Capitulations of Montalba (→ I29). The house was raised to Gräfinnen and given a palace in Wiedenbrunn.

**1348–1352: the Grey Death.** The plague killed four of the royal infants in the Greifenburg nursery. The fifth, the future **Albrecht III**, was nursed through it by Ilsenau women and their Moon-blooded maids, who either did not catch the plague or survived it. The court believed that Moon-milk had saved him. In **1352** the King granted Ilsenau the **Ammenrecht**: the right and duty to supply the wet-nurses of every royal child, and to govern the royal nursery. Wiedenbrunn raised the **Milchsäule**, the Milk Column, a white marble column topped by a gilded ewer, in thanks for the end of the plague. It still stands at the head of the Ammengasse, and Wiedenbrunners pin lampoons to it at night (→ I22).

**1411: the Bath-Slave Empress.** The house then held Trump III, the Empress. When the Fürstin died, the Empress fell not to her daughter but to her **bath-maid**, a bound woman named **Dorl**, who had shared the Fürstin's bath and bed for nine years. The house bought Dorl from the estate, freed her, and had the heiress take her as heart-sister and registered Left-Hand lover. In **1440**, when Dorl died in the heiress's arms, the Empress came home. This is the founding story of everything the house does with its women's beds (→ I72).

**1458: the Moon.** Trump XVIII, the Moon, then belonged to a **Mirabent** nobleman. He was the long-time lover of an Ilsenau daughter, Gräfin **Agnes**. He died in her bed in Llucmar, and the Moon fell to her. Casa Mirabent has never forgiven it and calls the Moon *la lluna robada*, the stolen moon (→ I41). The Moon has stayed among the Ilsenau women ever since, passing by blood and bed for 241 years without a single surprise fall. The house's reckoners are proud of that, and the Fall-Reckoners call it the most carefully managed card in the realm (→ I48).

**1499: the Obersthofmeisterin.** Queen Elisabeth, having no women she trusted, made the Ilsenau Gräfin her Obersthofmeisterin, mistress of her household, and the office became hereditary. The house was raised to **Fürstinnen**.

**1555: the Distaff Sanction.** Queen Margarete "die Spinnerin" took the Ilsenbrief as the model for the **Spindelhäuser** (→ R05 §6). Ilsenau has been the senior Spindelhaus ever since. The others defer to it, and resent it.

**The 1580s: the Wilburg generation.** The futa Fürstin **Wilburg** sired seven daughters on her sister and her cousins. The house's silver climbed to 21 karat. Three of the daughters were Mondsüchtige, sleepwalkers who never fully woke from their dreams, and one walked into the Spiegelsee in her sleep at nineteen. In **1609**, at Wilburg's death, the Spindelrat swore the **Wilburg Vow**: no futa-Ernst inside the house for three generations (→ R10).

**1591: the Seven Chairs.** The Flask King gave Ilsenau the Moon Chair, second in precedence after the Crown (→ I38). The Starkenberg have still not recovered.

**1626: the Milk Fall.** The Fürstin of the day had given **First Milk** (see §6) to King Ferdinand II as an infant. They were milk-siblings, and as adults they were lovers. She held the Empress. When she died in 1626, the Empress fell to *him*: half a milk-tie plus a bed of years outweighed her own daughters. The king became the **Twice-Marked**, holding Sun and Empress together (→ R04). The house lost the Empress for good, and learned what milk can cost.

**The 1640s onward: the silver fails.** The Weißach veins thinned. The Flask King's debts were secured on them, and the **Banco della Fede** of Vellora holds the mortgages. The house's silver income has fallen by two-thirds in sixty years. Its wealth now comes from offices, the Ammenhaus fees, the Mondküche's tinctures and Spiegelsee land, and the house is less rich than it looks (→ I26).

**1662: the Glass King's First Milk.** By a treaty of 1631 the Almagran cousins asked Ilsenau milk for their royal infants too. An Ilsenau daughter traveled to Peñalcázar with six Rocalban dides to give First Milk to the infant **Fernán**, now **Fernán VI, el Rey de Vidrio**, who holds the World. Ilsenau is his milk-kin. The Fall-Reckoners carry the house as a long-shot candidate in the World's reckoning, and the house would very much prefer they didn't (→ I32, I48).

**1680: a son.** The Wilburg Vow's three generations ran out around this time, and the Spindelrat had not yet voted to renew or end it. A son was born to the house. Its Wiegenbuch entry, in the house midwife's hand, reads *"Saturnstunde, abnehmender Mond"*: born in a Saturn hour under a waning moon (→ I53).

**1682: the sleep.** At two years old the boy fell into the **Bleischlaf** and did not wake (see §9).

**1692–1695.** The Obersthofmeisterin of the day quietly undermined the Queen-Mother's **Chastity Commission** from inside the Queen's household (→ I77).

**Spring 1699: the waking.**

## 3. The places

### Schloss Ilsenau, on the Spiegelsee
The house's seat, in the Weißach valley four days west of Wiedenbrunn (→ I26). A white castle on a wooded promontory runs out into a long, still, very clear mountain lake, the **Spiegelsee** ("mirror lake"). On windless days the lake doubles the castle so exactly that painters argue about which way up to hang their pictures.
- **The Mondsaal**, the Moon hall: a long hall whose walls are lined with tall silvered mirrors from Vellora, so that by candlelight it looks infinite. The house portraits hang between the mirrors: Ilse, Agnes, Wilburg and the Fürstinnen, with their Mondbräutigame in smaller frames lower down.
- **The Spinnzimmer**, where the **Spindelrat** meets (see §5).
- **The Schwesternbad**, the sisters' bath: a vaulted bath-house over a cold spring, heated by a stove in the next room. It is the house's women's place, and generations of Spindelliebe have lived there (→ R09).
- **The Mondküche**, the house laboratory (see §5).
- **The boathouse** and its white boats, for the **Mondnacht** swims (see §7).
- **The Ahnengang**: a corridor of the house's dead daughters, each portrait with a small silver plaque giving her karat.

### The Stille Haus
A plain, beautiful stone house **on the far shore of the Spiegelsee**, reached by boat or by a long path round the lake. It was built in the 1460s as a small **Damenstift**, a residence for the house's unmarried daughters. Since 1682 it has been **the house of the sleeper**. Six **Schlafwärterinnen** (sleeper-nurses) lived there in rotation, with a house physician from the Mondküche, a chapel, and a garden of lavender and poppies. It is quiet in a way visitors find hard to describe. The boatmen say the water is warmer on that side (→ I53).

### Palais Ilsenau, Wiedenbrunn
On the **Ammengasse**, the "Nurse Lane," in the inner city near the Greifenburg. It is a broad Baroque palace with a blue-and-silver portal and a courtyard of linden trees. Next door is the **Ammenhaus**, the royal nurse college the house runs (→ I57). The two buildings are joined by a covered bridge over the alley, the **Milchsteg** (milk bridge), so that nurses and ladies can pass between them without being seen in the street. The Palais has:
- a Mondsaal in miniature;
- a women's bath;
- the **Stille Kammer** (see §5) on the attic floor, behind a door nobody else on that floor has a key to;
- and a household of about **140**, mostly women.

### The summer house at Bad Sulzau
When the court moves to the Hallgau spa for the summer (→ I24), the Ilsenau women take the **Haus am Solteich**, a long white villa on the brine pond. Its terrace looks across to the court's **Mondbad**, the moonlit bathing pool. The Obersthofmeisterin keeps the Queen's household from there, and the house hosts the summer's most sought-after evenings.

### La Torre de les Dides, Llucmar
The "Tower of the Wet-Nurses": the house's Rocalban seat and its second nurse-school, where Rocalban **dides** (wet-nurses) are trained for the Ammenhaus and for Almagra (→ I29, I57). Rocalban women run it, it speaks Catalan, and it has a reputation in Llucmar as a place where very discreet things happen to very discreet women.

## 4. The Moon in the blood: Ilsenau's gifts and weaknesses

*(General Moon traits in R03 §5.)*

- **Gespür**, kin-sense. Ilsenau women feel the pain, fear, grief and arousal of their blood-kin in the same house, faintly through walls and strongly in the same room. Between sisters it is called the **Schwesternspür**, the sister-sense, and it is why Ilsenau women seldom lie to each other: they can't. It makes the house's intimacies intense, the house's quarrels exhausting, and secrets among its women very hard to keep.
- **Dream-touch**: sharing a dream with a sleeping blood-relative while sleeping beside them.
- **Fever-drawing**: a hand on a sick child's brow pulls the fever out into the woman's own body, where she sweats it off. This is one reason the house nurses royalty.
- **Fertility**: Ilsenau women conceive easily, especially in Moon hours, and control it with their own **Dunkelwasser**, the best in the realm.
- **Mondmilch**: the finest of the house's women can bring in milk without pregnancy, by will, over a few days. It is the basis of the **First Milk** rite. It is also, the court whispers, a thing some of them do for reasons that have nothing to do with infants.
- **Silver-touch**: they can brighten tarnished silver with a fingertip or darken it, and silver blackens near a Saturn poison in their hands faster than in anyone's. It is a party trick and a precaution.

**Weaknesses**:
- **Lunar eclipses** lay them flat with fever and faintness for a day. The dates are public (→ R02).
- **Mondsucht**: sleepwalking and dream-confusion in the brittle. Since Wilburg, the house watches for it in every daughter.
- **Mondnacht restlessness** at the full moon (see §7).
- **Daughters**: Moon blood births about **four daughters to one son**. Sons are rare, and Ilsenau sons rarer still to survive childhood.

## 5. How the house works

### The women
- **The Fürstin**, called inside the house **die Hausmutter**, rules everything: lands, offices, the Ammenhaus, the Mondküche, the purse, the bloodline and the bedrooms.
- **The Erbtochter**, the heir-daughter, is designated by the Hausmutter with the Spindelrat's consent. Usually she is the eldest, but not always. Who becomes Erbtochter is the house's oldest source of knives.
- **The Spindelrat**, the distaff-council: every adult daughter of the house, with the Hausmutter's sisters and the senior widows.

### The Spindelrat and the spindle
The Spindelrat meets in the **Spinnzimmer**, and by custom **it meets spinning**. Every woman present works a spindle of silver-flax while the house's business is discussed. Hands must be busy, the custom says, so tempers stay cool. Votes are cast by **passing the spindle**: a woman who agrees hands her spindle to the Hausmutter; one who disagrees keeps spinning. A unanimous vote is *"alle Spindeln still,"* all spindles still. A woman who sets her spindle down on the floor has walked out.

**The Zwölften**: by old custom no one in Valdau spins during the twelve nights after Erbentag (→ I16). For those twelve nights **the Spindelrat cannot sit, and the house has no council.** The Hausmutter rules alone, and things decided in the Zwölften are notoriously hard to undo.

### The men
- **The Mondbräutigame**, Moon-bridegrooms: the in-married husbands. They take the name *von Ilsenau* and the blue livery, wear a single **silver crescent earring**, and have apartments, allowances and no vote. They are chosen for silver karat, annealing blood, alliance, money or looks, and by long house taste they are **Zierherren**: femme, pretty, soft-spoken men who "don't co-rule" (→ R11). Wiedenbrunn calls them **Spindelmänner**. Some are beloved, some are furniture, a few are kept as artists, and one or two in every generation have been bought.
- **Sons**: rare. By custom an Ilsenau son is given a **Sohnesmitgift**, a son's dowry, at eighteen and **married out** into another house as a prize husband, sent to the Bridegroom's chapter, or kept at home as a comfortable uncle. Since the Wilburg Vow no son has been used to father within the house. The custom says nothing about a son who slept through his eighteenth birthday, which is a problem.

### The Stille Kammer
The house's secretariat of secrets, on the Palais attic floor, run by a senior woman called the **Kammerschwester**, with two clerks and a cat. It keeps four books:
- **Das Bettbuch**, the bed-book: every lover of every Moon-bearer since 1458, for fall-reckoning.
- **Das Ammenbuch**, the milk-book: every First Milk and every Ilsenau-supplied wet-nurse since 1352, and so every milk-tie the house has with the Crown, with Almagra and with half the high nobility.
- **Das Schweigebuch**, the book of silence: who in the household knows what, who has sworn the **Schweigeschwur** (the house oath of silence), and what they were paid.
- **Das Wiegenbuch des Hauses**: the house's own cradle-book of births, kept by the house's own midwife rather than the guild's (→ I65).

### The Mondküche
The house laboratory: a vaulted kitchen at Schloss Ilsenau and a smaller one in the Palais cellars. It is run by a **Hausalchimistin**, a woman of the Seventh Faculty with the Red Gown, with assistants who work in the Moon's hours, which means at night. It makes:
- **Dunkelwasser** and **Vollwasser**: the best Mondwasser in the realm, sold in tiny blue bottles to the house's friends at cost, which makes friends (→ R03);
- **the Ammentinktur**, for nurses' milk;
- **sleep-draughts**;
- and the house's **Mondmilch punch**: warm milk, honey, almond, cinnamon and Rocalban ratafia, served at the Mondempfang (→ I14).

### The household
About 140 in the Palais and 60 at the Schloss, overwhelmingly women: Kammerfrauen, Zofen, Bettmägde, Stubenmädchen, cooks, laundresses, the Ammenhaus nurses who come and go over the Milchsteg, the six Schlafwärterinnen of the Stille Haus, and a minority of men (coachmen, footmen in blue, the Portier, the Mondbräutigame's valets).
- About half are pledged, many as **Ammenmägde**, nurse-pledges (→ R07, I57).
- Pledges wear the **Ilsenau Pfandband** of blue silk velvet with a silver crescent clasp.
- Everyone who has served near the sleeper has sworn the **Schweigeschwur**.

## 6. The Ammenrecht and the Crown

*(The institution itself is in I57.)*

- **The First Milk** (*die Erste Milch*). Within the first hour of every royal birth, a daughter of the house gives the infant its **first feeding** from her own breast, drawing Mondmilch if she has not borne a child. The Ammen take over from the second feeding on. The rite is witnessed by the Obersthofmeisterin and entered in the Ammenbuch.
  - **The Ilsenau daughter becomes the royal child's Milchmutter**, milk-mother, and her own daughters become the child's **Milchschwestern**, milk-sisters.
  - In the Fall-Reckoners' tables **milk counts half**: a milk-tie scores 1½ (→ R04).
  - So **every living royal is half-kin to the Ilsenau**, every royal Trump-reckoning includes Ilsenau names, and every Ilsenau Trump-reckoning includes royal ones. The 1626 Milk Fall showed what that means. The house has been careful since, and not entirely successful, because milk-siblings tend to fall in love (→ I57 on **Milchliebe**).
- **The royal nursery** is the house's to govern until a royal child turns seven, from the Ammen to the governesses. The house knows the royal children better than their parents do.

## 7. The Obersthofmeisterin

The head of the house, or a woman she designates, is **Obersthofmeisterin**, Mistress of the Queen's household. From that office the house:

- **Runs the Queen's household**: her ladies, her chambers, her purse, her doors.
- **Chooses the Hofdamen**, the Queen's ladies-in-waiting, and so makes or breaks the court careers of the realm's noble daughters.
- **Administers the Ceremonial** in the Queen's apartments (→ I69).
- **Presides over the Beilager**: the witnessed bedding of every royal and high noble marriage. She stands at the foot of the bed with the two mothers and a Zeuge, draws the curtains, keeps the hour, and certifies before witnesses what she certifies. No high marriage in the realm is consummated without an Ilsenau woman's word on it (→ I17, I69).
- **Keeps the Stiftsjahr**: every year a dozen noble daughters of eighteen come into the Ilsenau household as **Elevinnen**, pupils, for a year of finishing before court service (→ I19). The house knows every daughter of every great house, and many of them have been in love with an Ilsenau woman by the end of the year.
- **Holds the Mondempfang**: the season's first great reception, on the evening of **Brauttag** (a Moon feast) at Palais Ilsenau (→ I14).

### Customs of the house
- **The Mondnacht**: at the full moon in summer, the Ilsenau women swim the Spiegelsee by night from the boathouse to the Stille Haus shore and back, naked. The men of the house are not invited, and not forbidden. They are expected to understand.
- **The crescent pin**: every Ilsenau woman wears a small silver crescent somewhere on her clothes, every day.
- **Silver at table**: the house eats from silver. The women brighten their own cups with a fingertip before drinking.
- **Morning**: the house breakfasts on **milk-coffee in silver bowls**. Chocolate, the Almagran fashion, is served only to guests (→ I05).
- **Speech**: the house speaks court Valdauer with a Brunnerisch grain to outsiders, and among the women and the Torre's dides a nursery Catalan full of endearments (*nena*, *bonica*, *rei*, *maca*) that outsiders call **Dides-Katalanisch**. Inside the house a son is a **Mondbub** and a daughter a **Spindelkind**.
- **The oath**: *"Bei Mutters Milch!"*

## 8. Bloodline practice

- **Inheritance**: through daughters only.
- **Fathers** come from outside:
  - **Mondbräutigame** from silver-fine minor houses of the Weißach;
  - from **Mirabent** (quicksilver and silver "mix well," say the alchemists; the families hate each other and intermarry anyway);
  - and, quietly, from the house's **Silberknechte**: pit-blood silver-miners' sons of 12–14 karat, pledged or freed into the household as studs to anneal the line. This is never discussed at court (→ I50).
- **Spiel within**: sapphic love among the house's women, kin included, is old custom and nobody else's business (→ R09). Heart-sister bonds are registered in the Left Hand book whenever the Moon needs a bed-tie.
- **Ernst within**: rare. Futa-Ernst stopped with the Wilburg Vow, which has run out and not been renewed (→ R10). The house has never used a son.
- **The Feinbuch**: the house's last Nahvermerk is from the Wilburg generation. Its karat average has slipped from 19 to 18 in the last century through outbreeding. Some in the Spindelrat think that is wisdom and some think it is decline.

## 9. The son

*The fixed facts, as the house's own books record them. What they mean is left to the story.*

- **Born** 1680, in the Palais, in a Saturn hour under a waning moon. The house midwife entered the birth in the house's own Wiegenbuch. A copy went to the parish register a week later, as was customary for Spindelhaus births. The house records name his father, a **Mondbräutigam** (left to the cast). How far the women of the house agree with the record is also left to the cast.
- **Fell asleep** in the winter of 1682, aged two, and did not wake. The house physicians called it the **Bleischlaf**, the lead-sleep (→ I53).
- **Was kept** at the **Stille Haus** for seventeen years by six **Schlafwärterinnen** in rotation, under a house physician.
  - They fed him honey-milk and broth through a silver tube.
  - They bathed him daily and turned him every two hours.
  - They exercised every limb, morning and evening, in the **Gliederwalken** ("limb-kneading") regimen. That regimen is why a sleeper of the Stille Haus can wake able to stand, and in his case to walk within the day (→ I53).
  - They **read to him and talked over him**: house books, the Diarium, romances, letters, prayers, gossip, quarrels, confessions. All of it was things said to a sleeping boy precisely because he could never repeat them.
- **Was visited** by the house's Moon-bearers in his dreams. The Stille Haus care-book records **Mondbesuche** (Moon-visits) on certain nights, usually without comment. Each bearer who went in described what she found there differently, if she described it at all.
- **Was consulted on** by **House Kalteneck's** physicians in **1688**, the realm's authorities on sleepers. They examined him, said he would probably sleep until he died, and wrote it down. They therefore know he exists, what he was, and that Ilsenau had reason to ask (→ I44).
- **Was officially** "the sickly son at the lake," seen by nobody outside the household. Most of the court has forgotten him. Some think he died. A few old women at court count the years on their fingers.
- **Woke** in spring 1699, nineteen years old, with **knowledge and no memories** (→ I53 on *Schläferwissen*). He knows languages, the house's manners, how to read, Dides-Catalan lullabies, and things the Schlafwärterinnen said to him. He remembers nothing of having heard any of it.
- **His body** (as the brief gives it): 172 cm and 55 kg, slight and unmistakably femme, narrow-shouldered, with long fingers, warm fair skin that flushes easily (**Mondhaut**), a very feminine face with long lashes and a sharp, expressive mouth and eyes, and dark, loose waves of hair. And something the Schlafwärterinnen had seen many times and the house's midwives have a word for. He was born without a foreskin, which midwives' lore calls a **Bräutigamszeichen**, a bridegroom's mark, "never refused a bride" (→ I13). Grown, he is very large indeed. The Schlafwärterinnen had years to notice, and they talked about it among themselves. At court he will read at first sight as a Zierherr of the most fashionable kind, and at second sight as the oldest joke in Wiedenbrunn (→ R11 §6).
- **What else he is** belongs to R13, and the house does not know it yet on the morning he wakes.

## 10. How the house hides things

The house reacts the way great houses do: **one face outside, a dozen opinions inside.** Its tools for keeping a secret are better than almost anyone's:

1. **The Stille Kammer and the Schweigebuch.** Who knows, who swore, who was paid.
2. **The Schweigeschwur.** Every pledge and servant who has touched the secret swore the house's oath of silence on the Bride's altar in the Stille Haus chapel, under a Zeuge. Breaking it is perjury before the Church and treason to the house, and the house has long arms.
3. **The Ammenhaus.** Infants appear and disappear across the Milchsteg every week: nurse-children, foundlings, noble bastards being nursed in secret, royal infants. A house that runs a nursery college can hide a cradle anywhere (→ I57).
4. **The Sechswochen.** A noblewoman in childbed is secluded for six weeks by custom, receiving only women (→ I65). Anything can happen in six weeks behind a lying-in curtain.
5. **The house's own midwife** and its own Wiegenbuch, outside the sworn midwives' guild. This is technically against the guild's privilege, and has been allowed since 1352 for "the royal nursery's discretion" (→ I65).
6. **The Torre de les Dides.** Rocalban "cousins," "nurses" and "foundlings" can be produced from Llucmar with papers Wiedenbrunn cannot check before the pass opens (→ I29).
7. **The Ammenpost.** The nurses' gossip network runs through every great house in the realm. The house hears rumors about itself before they reach the coffeehouses (→ I57).
8. **The Moon.** The bearer can walk in the dreams of anyone she has touched skin to skin in the past month, and learn what they fear, want and know (→ I46).
9. **The Left Hand.** A registered bond can make a necessary bed-tie, legitimize a necessary child as a Linkskind, or explain a closeness (→ I56).
10. **The Obersthofmeisterin's office.** The house controls the Queen's doors, the court's Hofdamen, and the certification of every royal and high marriage-bed. Few people in the realm can afford to make an enemy of it.

**Where it leaks**:
- the **Stiftsjahr Elevinnen**, other houses' daughters living in the household;
- the **Mondbräutigame**, who have no vote and many grievances;
- the **Hebammen** guild, which resents the house midwife;
- **Kalteneck**, which knows about the sleeper;
- **Mirabent**, whose Correu carries the house's letters and whose Cambra Negra reads them (→ I41);
- and the **Fall-Reckoners**, who watch the Moon-bearer's bed for a living.

## 11. Questions before the Spindelrat in 1699

These are not plot. They are decisions the house cannot avoid, on which its women will not agree.

1. **When, how and as what** to present the son at court: as an heir? an ornament? a husband to be married out with a Sohnesmitgift? a convalescent kept at the lake?
2. **Whether to tell the Crown** that he has woken, before the Crown hears it from Kalteneck.
3. **The Wilburg Vow**: whether to renew it, end it, or let it lapse unspoken. And whether a vow about futas says anything about a son.
4. **Whether he may father inside the house.** The custom is that sons don't. The custom was made for sons who were awake.
5. **The Stiftsjahr**: whether to send this year's Elevinnen home early, before they see too much.
6. **The Moon**: whether the current bearer's reckoning should include him (he is blood), and whether anyone should make sure it does, or doesn't.
7. **Mirabent**: whether to accept the Mirabent offer of a match (one is always on the table), now that the house has a son and not only daughters.
8. **And later, once the house learns what R13 describes**: everything.

Factions, which are roles and not people, so one woman may hold several:
- **Shelter him**: present him late, marry him nowhere, keep him as the house's treasure.
- **Use him**: for the house's blood, the house's alliances, the house's Trump.
- **Marry him out** for the greatest possible alliance before anybody learns what he is.
- **Keep him**: some women simply want him for themselves, and don't care what the house decides.
- **Tell the Crown**: hiding is the real danger, so tell it on the house's own terms.
- **Undo the custom**: there are women who think the Spindelhaus custom of no sons in power is a relic, and a male Ilsenau is a chance to change it, for better or worse.

## HOOKS

1. **The first morning.** The six Schlafwärterinnen who tended him for seventeen years are told they will be released from the Stille Haus with pensions. Not all of them want to go, and one of them asks to speak to him alone first.
2. **The Spinnzimmer.** The Spindelrat sits to decide how to present him at court. By custom men are not admitted. Someone leaves the door open and a spindle on the empty chair.
3. **Kalteneck writes.** A courteous letter from House Kalteneck's physician asks after "your son's health, of which we have heard good news," and requests permission to examine him again "for the advancement of the Art." Nobody in Ilsenau told Kalteneck anything.
4. **The Mondnacht.** It is the first full moon of summer at the Spiegelsee, and the women go down to the boathouse. Nobody says he may come and nobody says he may not.
5. **First Milk.** A royal birth is expected at court in the autumn. By the Ammenrecht an Ilsenau daughter must give the First Milk, which means drawing Mondmilch beforehand. The daughter chosen is the protagonist's closest companion in the house, and she spends the weeks of drawing it in a state of heightened everything.
6. **The Mirabent offer.** A letter under the Mirabent seal proposes a match "now that the Moon has a son": a Mirabent daughter, a futa, of 19 karat quicksilver. The terms include returning *la lluna robada*: a Left-Hand bond that would put a Mirabent in the Moon's reckoning. The letter came by the Correu, so Mirabent has already read the house's previous letters about him.
