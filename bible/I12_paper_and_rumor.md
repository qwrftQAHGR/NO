# I12 · Paper & Rumor

*How news, gossip and secrets travel in the Double Crown: the court gazette and its list of arrivals, the cheap slips that announce card-falls, lampoons pasted on the Milk Column at night, the censors and their banned list, letters and their seals and folds, the books people read in bed, and the spoken networks that are faster than all of it.*

## 1. The Wiedenbrunner Diarium

**Das Wiedenbrunner Diarium**, the court gazette, has appeared every **Merkurtag and Saturntag** since **1679**: four quarto pages printed by the court printer under the eye of the Hofkanzlei.

It contains:
- **Hofnachrichten**, court news: audiences, ceremonies, the King's movements, appointments to offices, promotions, honors, Hofrat-titles granted.
- **Die Angekommenen**, the arrivals: a list of every person of standing arriving in Wiedenbrunn, with their inn or palace. It is the most-read column in the realm. Mothers read it for sons-in-law, creditors for debtors, wives for husbands' mistresses, the Naderer for everybody (→ I20). Being in the Angekommenen is how Wiedenbrunn learns you exist.
- **Die Abgereisten**, the departures.
- **Births, marriages and deaths** of the nobility, from the parish Wiegenbücher and the Heroldsamt. A death notice of a card-bearer is followed by:
- **Blattnachrichten**, card-news: the Diarium's **official notice of a Trump fall** once the Fall-Reckoners' guild has confirmed where it went (→ I48). It is terse, accurate and a week late.
- **Prices** at the markets, the exchange rate of the Greif against the Velloran ducat, and the Banco's Lotto numbers (→ I09).
- **Royal Patents and decrees.**
- **Foreign news**, copied from Velloran newsletters.
- **Notices**: lost dogs, runaway pledges (with descriptions and rewards: *"a Leibmagd, twenty-two years, dark-haired, scar on the left hand, answers to Resi, wearing the blue ribbon of the house of —"*), houses to let, tutors wanted, and **matrimonial notices** placed by Bürger widows, which the young court reads aloud for laughs.

Subscriptions cost **2 Greif a year**. Every coffeehouse keeps copies on wooden rods.

## 2. Fallzettel, Pasquille and the street

- **Fallzettel** (fall-slips): cheap single sheets, printed overnight in the Vorstädte, announcing, speculating about or inventing **card-falls**. When a bearer dies they are on the streets by morning, sold by the **Zettelweiber** (slip-women), who cry them in the squares: *"Neuer Fall! Der Turm ist gefallen! Wer hat ihn? Ein Kupferling!"* They are wrong about half the time. Being named in a wrong Fallzettel is embarrassing. Being named in a right one, before your family knew, is a catastrophe. The Fall-Reckoners hate them and buy them all, because sometimes the Zettel printers know something (→ I48).
- **Pasquille** (lampoons): anonymous satirical verses, handwritten or crudely printed, **pasted at night on the Milchsäule**, the Milk Column at the head of the Ammengasse (→ I22, R12). They mock whoever deserves it, or whoever someone wants mocked. Every morning a Rumorwache man scrapes the column, and every morning there's a crowd reading before he arrives. A good Pasquill is copied out and passed round the coffeehouses for a week. **Milchsäulenzettel** ("milk-column slips") is the general term. Ilsenau, whose column it is, finds this either amusing or a desecration, depending on who is being lampooned.
- **The Bänkelsänger** sing the news with painted boards (→ I11).
- **Flugblätter** (broadsides): single sheets with a woodcut and a story about a monster, a comet, a miracle or a murder, sold at fairs and church doors.

## 3. Censorship: the Bücherkommission

The **Bücherkommission** (Book Commission) was founded in **1572**. It is a joint body of the Hofkanzlei and the Bridegroom's See, with two Zeugen, two clerks and a secretary, and it licenses every press in Valdau and reads everything printed.

- It keeps the **Verbotsverzeichnis**, the banned list: Clear Church tracts, Velloran political pamphlets, astrology that prophesies, books that mock the royal family by name, and erotica. On it are the **Thirty-Two Hours** (→ I08) and most of the **Kammerbüchl**, along with a Rocalban history of the Brides' War, three treatises on transmutation, and one Kesrâni love-poet.
- **Smuggling**: banned books come from **Vellora** (by sea through Llucmar), from the **Sworn Valleys** (Clear tracts over the passes), and from the Rosengrund's own back-room presses. They are sold under the counter by booksellers on the **Bücherbogen**, the arcade by the university. A banned book costs three times its price. Being on the banned list is the best advertisement there is.
- **Rocalba** has its own censor, the **Censor de la Llotja**, whose list is shorter and differently political. A book banned in Wiedenbrunn is often printed in Llucmar, and the reverse.
- The **Bridegroom's See** wants the Commission stricter, and the **Bride's See** wants no part of it. Montalba's press prints the Pronòstic, a few devotional works, and things the Archbishop would rather not see.

## 4. Letters

### Paper, ink and seals
- **Paper**: rag paper from the Selde mills, cream for ordinary letters, gilt-edged for love and court. **Asagiri paper**, thin, strong and faintly patterned, is the luxury of 1699, and a letter on it is a statement.
- **Ink**: iron-gall, black-brown. Ladies' ink is scented. The Mondküche's **Silbertinte** (silver ink) shows only when the paper is warmed by a candle, and is used for the Ilsenau's private notes (→ R12).
- **Sealing wax**, whose **color is a message**:
  - **red** for business and formal letters;
  - **black** for mourning;
  - **green** for love and Venus (sending it to a married woman is a provocation);
  - **blue** for confidential, Moon. Ilsenau uses midnight blue, and its color is copied by anyone who wants a letter to look as though it came from Ilsenau;
  - **gold** for royal letters only;
  - **grey** for the Lautere.
  - **Jupiterlack**, tin-wax that warms under the thumb if the seal is genuine, is used by the Hofkammer and by anyone afraid of forgery (→ R03).
- **The seal** is a signet ring with the house arms, or a private device for private letters: a crescent, a rose, a Pagat, a pair of hands.
- **Wafers** (*Oblaten*) of colored paste are a cheaper seal, used by Bürger and by lovers who don't want their arms seen.

### Folding and locking
Before envelopes, letters are folded into a packet and sealed. Lovers and secretaries use **Briefschlösser** ("letter-locks"): folding the paper through slits cut in itself and threading a paper strip through, so that **the letter cannot be opened without tearing**, and a reader can't hide that it was read. There are dozens of patterns. A letter that arrives *locked*, with its strip intact, is proof against the **Cambra Negra** (see §5). Lovers learn letter-locking from their Kammerfrauen, and the Kammerfrauen have their own patterns.

### Briefsteller
**Briefsteller** (letter-manuals) are books of model letters for every occasion: to a superior, a creditor, a widow, a son abroad. They include **love-letters** in graded intensity, from *"Hochgeehrtes Fräulein"* (most honored miss) to the final pages, which are in the Bücherkommission's file. Bürger lovers copy them word for word, and recipients who have read the same Briefsteller recognize them and are disappointed.

## 5. Who reads your letters

- **The Correu**: Casa Mirabent holds the **postal monopoly** of the Double Crown: post-stations, riders and the express courier service (→ I41, I21). Every letter sent by post passes through Mirabent hands.
- **The Cambra Negra** (the Black Chamber): in the Mirabent post-house in Wiedenbrunn, a room where letters are opened overnight with hot wires and steam, read, copied by fast clerks, resealed with forged seals and sent on by morning. Everyone knows it exists, and nobody can prove it. The Crown uses its copies, and Mirabent keeps a set. Hence letter-locks, Jupiterlack, private couriers, ciphers, and silver ink.
- **Ciphers**: great houses have family ciphers, and lovers have private ones, usually simple. The **Ilsenau cipher** is said to be based on the spindle-count of the Spindelrat's votes, and nobody outside the house has broken it, or nobody has said so.
- **Private couriers**: footmen, Läufer, pledges, the **Postillon d'amour** (the go-between who carries love letters, usually a Zofe or page, for tips). For secret letters the great houses don't use the Correu at all.
- **The Ammenpost** (→ I57): the nurses' network. Wet-nurses and nursemaids move between great houses constantly with babies, and they carry letters, messages and gossip faster than the Correu and safer from the Cambra Negra. It is Ilsenau's private post, and the best in the realm.

## 6. Spoken networks

Faster than print:
- **The Tratsch** (gossip) of the coffeehouses, where the Diarium is read aloud and rewritten (→ I67).
- **The Kaffeekränzchen** of the ladies (→ R09).
- **The servants**: every great house's servants know every other's, and meet at the Bader, the market, the Heuriger and the Ammenhaus courtyard (→ I04). A thing done in a great house at midnight is known in the servants' halls of three other houses by morning Mahl.
- **The Fiaker** (hackney-coach drivers), who see who goes where at night (→ I21, I22).
- **The Ofenheizer** in the Heizgang (→ I03).
- **The Bader and the Bademägde** (→ I64).
- **The Ammenpost**.
- **The Zeugen**, through what they hear in confession, which they never repeat, and which somehow everybody knows.

The Wiedenbrunn saying: *"Was der Portier um Mitternacht sieht, weiß die Fiakerin zum Frühstück, und das Diarium in der nächsten Woche."* What the Portier sees at midnight the Fiaker's wife knows by breakfast, and the Diarium next week.

Words:
- **"Tratsch"**: gossip.
- **"Klatschbase"**: gossip-aunt.
- **"Das weiß schon der Fiaker"**: "even the Fiaker knows," i.e. old news.
- **"Das steht in den Angekommenen"**: "it's in the arrivals," i.e. public now.

## 7. What people read

- **Devotional books**: Stundenbücher (books of hours of prayer), lives of the saints, Läuterwochen sermons. Some have the Thirty-Two Hours bound inside them (→ I08).
- **Almanacs**: the Praktik and the Pronòstic (→ R02).
- **Romances**:
  - **Schäferromane** (pastoral romances): shepherds and shepherdesses loving chastely for six hundred pages.
  - **Fallromane** (fall-romances): the most popular genre of the 1690s, novels of Trump-falls. A beautiful servant girl beloved of a dying Trump-bearer, the scheming heirs, the deathbed, the Blatt appearing on the wrong skin, and justice. Every Stiftsdame and every lady's maid reads them. The best-known is ***Die schöne Dorl***, a sentimental retelling of the Bath-Slave Empress, much reprinted to Ilsenau's annoyance (→ I72). Close behind is ***Der verlorene Mond*** ("The Lost Moon"), about a card that falls to a sleeper, whom nobody can wake.
  - **Almagran picaresque**: rogues' adventures in translation.
  - **Velloran courtesans' memoirs**: largely invented and wholly popular.
- **The Kammerbüchl** and the **Thirty-Two Hours** (→ I08).
- **Learned books**: alchemy, law, medicine, astronomy, the Dean's Tables on kinship and brittleness (→ R06), Fall-Reckoners' handbooks.
- **The Titelbuch** (→ I01).

**Who reads**: nobles and Bürger, men and women; servants in great houses, often well; Zeugen. Peasants rarely, and the Zeuge reads the Praktik aloud to them. Literacy is a mark of rank and of upper service. A pledge who reads is worth more (→ R07).

**Libraries**: the great houses have them. The **Mondbibliothek** at Schloss Ilsenau is the best private library in the Weißach. The Stille Haus's books, the ones read aloud to the sleeper for seventeen years, are a strange mixed collection (→ R12, I53). The **Lesekabinett** of the Café Sküs keeps newspapers and banned books for its regulars (→ I67).

## HOOKS

1. **Die Angekommenen.** The Diarium's arrivals column lists "*S. D. der Prinz von Ilsenau, aus dem Weißachtal, im Palais Ilsenau*" (His Serene Highness the Prince of Ilsenau, from the Weißach, at the Palais Ilsenau). Nobody in the household authorized the notice, and by Jause there are thirty cards on the Portier's tray.
2. **The Milchsäulenzettel.** A lampoon is pasted on the Milk Column overnight about "the Moon's sleeping son," with a coarse verse about what nineteen years of nurses must have taught him. By the time the Rumorwache scrapes it off, the Café Sküs has three copies.
3. **The locked letter.** A letter arrives for the protagonist, letter-locked in a pattern the household's Kammerfrauen identify at once as one used only by the Stille Haus's Schlafwärterinnen. It is in a hand nobody knows.
4. **The Cambra Negra copy.** A Mirabent clerk offers to sell the household a copy of a letter the household sent last month, as proof the Cambra reads Ilsenau's post. He is offering it, he says, because he has a conscience, and a debt.
5. **Der verlorene Mond.** The Fallroman *Der verlorene Mond*, about a sleeper and a Moon-card, is suddenly being read by every Stiftsdame at court. Its author is anonymous, and it was published in 1698, before the waking.
6. **The runaway notice.** The Diarium prints a notice for a runaway Leibmagd, *"wearing the blue ribbon of the house of Ilsenau."* The house placed no such notice and has no runaway. Somebody wants a woman in a blue ribbon to be stopped by the Rumorwache, or wants it believed that one exists.
