# I08 · The Chamber Lexicon & the Thirty-Two Hours

*The realm's erotic vocabulary from the court to the gutter; the banned book everyone has read, which gives a name to thirty-two ways of making love; the toys, prints and chapbooks; and the manners of a bed in a house where the servants never quite leave the room.*

## 1. Die Zweiunddreißig Stunden (the Thirty-Two Hours)

### The book
- **Le Trentadue Ore**, "The Thirty-Two Hours," was printed in **Vellora in 1641**. The text is by the courtesan-poet **Cassandra Moro**, in thirty-two sonnets, one for each "hour." The engravings, thirty-two full-page plates of a frankness Vellora had never seen bound, are by **Zuan Fenzo**.
- **1658**: a German translation, **Die Zweiunddreißig Stunden**, printed secretly in Wiedenbrunn by **Matthäus Kolb**. In **1659** he was pilloried on the Schandplatz for it, sold three hundred more copies from the pillory to the crowd, and retired rich.
- **1662**: a Catalan edition, **Les Trenta-dues Hores**, from a Llucmar press nobody will name.
- **Banned** by the Bücherkommission in 1659 and again in 1693 by the Chastity Commission (→ I12, I77). It is on every banned list in the realm and in every great library, usually bound inside the covers of a devotional **Stundenbuch** (a book of hours of prayer) so that it stands on the shelf beside the real ones.
- **Everyone has read it, nobody owns it.** Its names for things have entered the language.

### The thirty-two
The book's conceit is that each of the twelve houses of the sky and each of the seven planets has an act proper to it, and that thirteen "side-hours" (*Nebenstunden*) belong to nothing in heaven and everything on earth.

**The twelve houses**
1. **Der Widder** (the Ram): face to face, he above, her knees drawn up high. Vigorous and direct; "the soldier's hour."
2. **Der Stier** (the Bull): standing. He lifts and carries her, her legs locked round his waist. Strength.
3. **Die Zwillinge** (the Twins): three in a bed. Also two lovers mirroring each other's motions.
4. **Der Krebs** (the Crab): lying on their sides, spooned from behind, slow. The sleepy hour; the hour for the pregnant.
5. **Der Löwe** (the Lion): she above, riding, commanding. "The Lioness." Said to be the favorite hour of the Spindelhäuser.
6. **Die Jungfrau** (the Virgin): hands only, mutual and patient, no entry. Pure **Spiel** (→ R06).
7. **Die Waage** (the Scales): seated face to face in a chair, balanced, neither above. "The lovers' equal."
8. **Der Skorpion** (the Scorpion): from behind, with teeth at the nape of the neck. The bite.
9. **Der Schütze** (the Archer): she kneels on the bed, he stands behind at its edge.
10. **Der Steinbock** (the Goat): standing against a wall, half-dressed, hurried. "The stairwell hour."
11. **Der Wassermann** (the Water-bearer): in the bath. The Badstube, the Mondbad, the tub by the stove.
12. **Die Fische** (the Fishes): each with mouth to the other's sex, head to foot.

**The seven planets**
13. **Die Sonnenstunde**: in full daylight, before mirrors or before witnesses. Display; the Beilager's hour.
14. **Die Mondstunde**: by moonlight, slow and tender. The hour for conceiving (→ R02).
15. **Die Merkurstunde**: quick, clothed, somewhere one shouldn't be, with discovery near. The hour of affairs.
16. **Die Venusstunde**: long and slow, with oil and mirrors, holding back again and again. Pleasure for its own sake.
17. **Die Marsstunde**: rough. Wrestling, holding down, marks left on skin.
18. **Die Jupiterstunde**: marital, dutiful, for heirs, to the Stundenglöckner's bell.
19. **Die Saturnstunde**: binding with silk ribbons, denial, slowness, waiting at the edge for a long time. Patience made into pleasure. The book calls it "the hour that most rewards those who can bear it."

**The thirteen side-hours**
20. **Die Finsternis** (the Eclipse): blindfolded.
21. **Die Konjunktion** (the Conjunction): two couples in one bed or one room.
22. **Die Rückläufige** (the Retrograde): roles reversed. The woman takes the man, with a Tröster (see §4); the servant commands the master; the bridegroom is the bride.
23. **Der Pagat**: the lowest serves the highest. Master and servant, mistress and maid, in play or in earnest.
24. **Der Sküs**: masked, no names, no rules, the hour that never happened.
25. **Die Hintertür** (the Back Door): the act that cannot make a child.
26. **Die Flöte** (the Flute): her mouth on him.
27. **Die Rose**: his mouth on her ("kissing the rose").
28. **Die Glocke** (the Bell): racing the Stundenglöckner's second bell (→ R02).
29. **Der Spiegel** (the Mirror): before a mirror, each watching the other watch.
30. **Das Band** (the Ribbon): the pledge's ribbon pulled; obedience as play (→ R07).
31. **Die Brautnacht** (the Bride-Night): a first time, taught slowly by the one who knows.
32. **Die Hochzeit** (the Wedding): all the hours in one night, for those who can.

People speak of the hours by number and name: *"a Waage,"* *"they kept a Saturnstunde,"* *"the Sküs at the Redoute."* The Rosengrund's houses price by hour (→ I23). A **Pagat** in a lady's patch-box is a joke at the Toilette and an invitation after it (→ I07).

## 3. Erotic print and literature

- **The Thirty-Two Hours** (§1).
- **Kammerbüchl** ("chamber booklets"): cheap chapbooks of erotic tales sold under the counter in the Rosengrund and at the Ägidi Fair (→ I63), with crude woodcuts. Popular titles:
  - ***Die lustige Badmagd*** (The Merry Bath-Maid): a filthy parody of the Bath-Slave Empress story, which the Ilsenau have tried to suppress for two centuries (→ I72).
  - The ***Wolfsgeschichten***: tales of Zierherren who turn out to be wolves in dressing-gowns (→ R11).
  - ***Die Nonnen von Brautzell*** (The Nuns of Brautzell).
  - ***Der Glöckner und die Gräfin*** (The Bell-Ringer and the Countess).
  - ***Läuterwochengeschichten*** (Lent stories, → I05).
  - ***Der Fall der Kaiserin*** (The Fall of the Empress), about what really happened in 1693, entirely invented, three editions.
- **Kissenbilder** (pillow-pictures): **Asagiri** erotic prints, painted with a delicacy and anatomical frankness that astonished Wiedenbrunn. The embassy's people gave a few as gifts in 1696. Copies now change hands for a Greif apiece, and the Velloran printers are trying to copy the style (→ I37).
- **The Book of Graces** (Vellora's catalogue of courtesans, with prices and specialties) is read in Wiedenbrunn as both a guide and a romance (→ I35).
- **Erotic Gstanzl**: improvised filthy verse at the Heuriger (→ I11).
- **Les Cobles de la Hereva**: a Rocalban song-cycle about a futa and the women of a village, sung at weddings after midnight (→ R10).
- **Deckelbilder**: the second-lid miniatures of snuffboxes (→ I05).
- **Kehrseite fans** (→ I07).
- **Briefsteller** (letter-manuals) with model love-letters, some very explicit (→ I12).

## 4. Objects of pleasure

- **Tröster** ("comforters"; Rocalban *consoladors*): dildos.
  - **Velloran glass**, the *vetro di grazia*: hollow blown glass that can be filled with warm water or cooled milk. Fragile, beautiful, and the most expensive.
  - **Rocalban leather** stuffed with wool. Some come with straps, the **consolador de la hereva** (→ R10): a strap-on for women without a futa's second nature. Fashionable among Stiftsdamen.
  - **Kesrâni ivory**, carved, sometimes with silver bands.
  - **The Asagiri** kind, of lacquered horn or tortoiseshell in a lacquered box. Only the envoy's people have them. The court talks about them anyway.
- **Liebeskugeln** (love-balls): two small hollow balls, one with a smaller ball or a drop of quicksilver inside, worn within a woman so they move and chime faintly as she walks. From **Asagiri**, where they are called something prettier. Brought by the embassy, copied by Velloran glassblowers, and now the most whispered-about item of the season (→ I37).
- **The Venushemd**: a sheath of fine sheep's gut tied on with a silk ribbon, against conception and the pox. Velloran ones are best, and they are washed and reused (→ R08).
- **Ribbons, blindfolds and silk cords**: for the Saturnstunde and the Finsternis. The **Finsternisbinde** (eclipse-band) is a black silk blindfold sold in pretty boxes in the Rosengrund.
- **Feathers, fans and gloves**, used in ways the fan-makers didn't intend.
- **Oils**: almond oil, rose oil, Venusbitter rubbed on the skin (which stings), and the Mondküche's **Silbersalbe** for skin made sore by a long night.
- **The Spiegelkabinett**: a small room lined with mirrors (→ I03).
- **The Konjunktionssessel**: a Velloran chair with stirrups and a curved seat, designed for the Waage and other hours. Three great houses in Wiedenbrunn have one, and everyone knows which three.

## 5. The manners of a bed

- **Servants are present, and absent.** In a great house the Bettmagd or Kammerdiener sleeps in the Garderobe, the night-servant across the antechamber door, the Stundenglöckner outside (→ I03, R02). The **drawn bed-curtain** is the convention of privacy: once the curtains of the Alkoven are closed, the servants hear nothing, by custom. This is the **Vorhangrecht**, curtain-right. Everyone knows they hear everything, and nobody speaks of it.
- **Ask the hour.** In great houses, lovers who mean to conceive, or mean not to, consult the Stundenuhr. Lovers who don't care say *"jede Stunde ist eine Venusstunde"* (every hour is Venus's), which is a compliment.
- **Wash.** A basin and ewer stand by every bed. The Velloran **bidetto**, a small low basin on a stand, is the newest luxury.
- **The Bride must rejoice.** A man who leaves a woman unsatisfied has failed at a duty the Church preaches (→ R08). Courtesans teach young men; mothers tell daughters to expect no less.
- **Leave by the night-stair.** A lover in a respectable house leaves before dawn by the private stair, with the Nachtschlüssel, and tips the Portier the Schlüsselgeld (→ I03, I04).
- **The Morgengabe.** After a first night (a wedding, but also a first night between lovers of rank) a gift is given in the morning: a jewel, a fan, a ribbon. Among the rich, sometimes a pledge (→ I17, R07).
- **Saturn nights.** By old custom a wife may refuse her husband on Saturn nights without fault, because nothing good is conceived on them. Wives who want a night off have been known to consult the Stundenamt very carefully.
- **In the Rosengrund: pay first.** The **Rosengeld** is put on the table before the door is shut, and anything after is a gift (→ I23).
- **Thank.** A well-mannered lover says *"Dank"* at parting, to the lover and to the servants. Ill-mannered lovers don't, and the servants remember (→ I04).

## HOOKS

1. **The Stundenbuch.** In the Ilsenau library, among the devotional books, the protagonist takes down a Stundenbuch whose covers are newer than its pages, with a pressed violet at the Saturnstunde.
2. **"Ein Wolf."** A Kammerbüchl seller at the Ägidi Fair is hawking a brand-new *Wolfsgeschichte* whose hero is "a sleeping prince of the Moon who woke to find…" Somebody in the household has been talking to a printer.
3. **The Liebeskugeln.** At a Cercle a lady of the household rises, crosses the room, and there is a very faint chime. Three people hear it. Nobody says anything, and the Asagiri envoy, who knows exactly what it is, looks at the ceiling.
4. **The Vorhangrecht.** A pledge who has slept in the Garderobe beside a great lady's bed for six years is offered money by a Fall-Reckoner to break the curtain-right: to say who comes through the curtains on which nights. She brings the offer to the protagonist, because the great lady in question is someone he has just begun visiting.
5. **The Saturnstunde.** A lady who lent the protagonist her copy of the Thirty-Two Hours "to complete his education" sends a note asking which hour he found most instructive. She is expecting an answer at her Toilette tomorrow.
6. **The Rückläufige.** A Stiftsdame with a Rocalban *consolador de la hereva* in her trunk, and a theological interest in the Confraria's idea of the Heir, would like to discuss with the protagonist which of them ought to play the bride.
