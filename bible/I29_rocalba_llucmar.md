# I29 · Rocalba & Llucmar

*The Catalan half of the Double Crown: a white mountain where the faith began, a port city of merchants and galleys, a parliament whose four chambers are named for the suits of the card-deck, a market where captives are sold under a notary's eye, a night of licensed madness, a day of roses and keys, and four hundred years of grievance against the ones up there.*

## 1. The land

**Rocalba** ("white rock"), the **Principat**, is a coastal country **southwest of Valdau**, across the southern ranges by the Brautsteig pass, which closes in winter (→ I21). It runs along the **Middle Sea** for some hundred and fifty miles, between the mountains and the water. Its parts:
- **La Costa**: the coast. Fishing towns with white houses, coves, headlands with watch-towers against corsairs, the **almadraves** (tuna-traps), the coral-fishers' harbors and the port-capital **Llucmar**.
- **La Plana**: the plain behind the coast, with olives, vines, almonds, wheat and the stone **masies** (farmhouses) of the free farmers (→ I59).
- **Montalba**: the great **white limestone massif** that gives the country its name. Its jagged pale peaks are visible forty miles out to sea. The abbey of the **Bride's See** sits on a ledge halfway up (→ I54).
- **La Serra del Cinabre**: the red mountains inland, with the **quicksilver mines** of **Cinabre** (→ I30) and the red river **Vermell**, which runs pink after rain and reaches the sea at Llucmar.
- **The northern valleys** below the Brautsteig: pastures, chestnut woods, Romanesque churches and shepherds.

**The climate** is Mediterranean: mild wet winters, blazing dry summers, the scent of rosemary and pine, and the **tramuntana**, the cold north wind that blows down from the mountains for three days at a time. It makes everyone quarrelsome, and Rocalbans blame it for murders, adulteries and votes in the Corts.

**The people**: about **1.3 million**. They speak **Catalan** and are proud of it, and of their law, their saints, their bread, their sea and their sense.

---

## 2. The Rocalban temperament: seny and rauxa

Rocalbans describe themselves by two words:
- **seny**: good sense, prudence, thrift, patience, the cool head, the careful bargain, the household kept in order. A Rocalban merchant counts his change after a funeral. *"La pela és la pela"*: money is money.
- **rauxa**: sudden madness, the outburst, the night of excess, the riot, the passion that burns a customs-house.

The Rocalban is all **seny** for a year, Valdauers say, and then all **rauxa** for a night. Rocalbans say that is exactly right, and that Valdauers have neither.

## 3. Government: the Corts and the Four Suits

*(How Rocalba sits within the Double Crown is in R01.)*

- **The Comte-Príncep**: the King of Valdau, as Count-Prince of Rocalba, who must swear the **Jurament** at Montalba to keep Rocalba's laws (→ R01).
- **The Lloctinent**: the King's lieutenant-general in the **Palau del Lloctinent** on the harbor. At present a count of House Ehrenreith, called **el Rellotge** (the Clock), punctual and loathed (→ I43).
- **Les Corts**: Rocalba's parliament, sitting in the **Palau de les Corts** in Llucmar's old quarter. It has **four braços** ("arms," chambers), named since **1340** for the **four suits** of the Rocalban baralla. The suits in turn were drawn from the living cards on people's skins (→ R04). The braços:

| Braç | Suit | Who sits there | Its temper |
|---|---|---|---|
| **braç d'Oros** | coins | merchants, shipowners, bankers, the *ciutadans honrats* (honored citizens) | rich, calculating, the real power in Llucmar |
| **braç de Copes** | cups | the clergy, led by the **Abbess of Montalba** | holds the Nahbrief money; lends it to the others (→ I55) |
| **braç d'Espases** | swords | the nobility and the *cavallers* (knights) | proud, poor, touchy, always quarreling about precedence |
| **braç de Bastos** | batons | the towns, the guilds and the free farmers (*pagesos*) | loud, numerous, essential, ignored until they riot |

- **Els Quatre Pals** ("the Four Suits"): the Corts' standing deputation, one deputy per braç, sitting between sessions in the **Casa dels Pals**. They collect the **dret del General**, Rocalba's own tax, which the Crown cannot touch. They guard the **Constitucions**, and write to Wiedenbrunn in a Catalan so correct it reads as an insult. Without their consent no Rocalban money leaves Rocalba.
- **El Consell dels Quaranta** (the Council of Forty): Llucmar's city council, elected by its guilds and honored citizens.
- **The Llotja**: the merchants' exchange and court (§5).
- **Law**: the **Usos de Montalba** (old custom), the **Constitucions** (laws made by the Corts with the prince), and the **Sentència de Montalba of 1486**, which abolished labor dues on Rocalban farmers (→ R01). Rocalban *pagesos* are freer than Valdauer peasants, and Rocalba buys slaves instead.

## 4. Llucmar

The capital and port: **about 45,000 people** on a crescent bay at the mouth of the **Vermell**, under the hill of **el Puig de la Mar**, crowned by a fortress. It is a city of narrow stone lanes, Gothic churches and arcaded squares, with the smell of the sea, tar, oranges, fish and incense. It has:

- **El Port**: the harbor, with the **Drassanes** (the great galley-sheds, long stone vaults where the **fourteen galleys of Llucmar** are built and kept); merchant ships of every flag (Rocalban *sagetes* and *polacres*, Velloran galleys, Almagran carracks); the fish-market; and the **Barri de la Mar**, the sailors' quarter.
- **La Llotja**: the merchants' exchange, a soaring Gothic hall with spiral columns, where cargoes, insurance and bills of exchange are traded and the **Consolat de Mar** (sea court) sits. Off its cloister is **la Llotja dels Captius**, the captives' exchange, where **slaves and captives are sold**, every sale before a notary, recorded, taxed and guaranteed (→ R07). War-captives from the Wehrgrenze and the sea, Rocalban **Erbleute** (born slaves), and corsair crews all pass through it. Velloran brokers buy for the eastern markets.
- **El Passeig de les Veles** (the Walk of Sails): the waterfront promenade under palm trees, where Llucmar walks at dusk.
- **The Carrer de les Naus** ("street of ships"): the palaces of the great merchant houses, among them **Casa Ferrer de la Llotja**, whose patrona **Na Elisenda Ferrer** holds the **Star** (→ I45, I46).
- **Palau Mirabent** on the **Carrer dels Correus** (post street): the city palace of **Casa Mirabent**, Mercury house and postmasters of the realm (→ I41).
- **The Torre de les Dides**: the Ilsenau's Rocalban seat and nurse-school, where the **dides** (wet-nurses) are trained for the Ammenhaus and for Almagra (→ R12, I57).
- **The Seu de Llucmar**: the cathedral, with its cloister of orange trees and its **white peacocks**, kept there since 1300 for reasons nobody agrees on. A peacock's cry during a wedding is considered the best possible luck.
- **The house of the Confraria de l'Hereu** in the **Carrer de Sant Feliu** (→ I58).
- **The Barri dels Esclaus** (the slaves' quarter), where freed Erbleute and captives settle. It has its own church and its own Lamp-house for Kesrâni freedmen.
- **El Carrer dels Coralers**: the old lane of the coral-carvers and silversmiths.
- **The masia**-lined hills behind the city, and their summer houses.

## 5. Trade

- **Quicksilver** from Cinabre, shipped in iron **flascons**: the realm's most strategic export, carried to Vellora for mirrors and amalgam, and to Almagra for its silver mines (→ I30).
- **Coral** from the **Costa del Corall**, fished by divers with weighted nets and carved in Llucmar into beads, charms and rosaries.
- **Tuna** from the **almadraves**, the great net-labyrinths set off the headlands each spring, into which the tuna-shoals are driven and killed in a bloody, festive slaughter called **la llevada**, then salted and sold.
- **Cloth**: the **drap de Llucmar**, fine woollens.
- **Wine**, **olive oil**, **almonds**, **ratafia** (a cherry and herb liqueur) and **vi escumós** (sparkling wine).
- **Captives** (§4).
- **Shipping and insurance**: the Llotja insures half the cargoes on the Middle Sea, and Llucmar's notaries are trusted everywhere.

## 6. Customs

- **La Nit de Rauxa** (the Night of Madness): once a year, on the eve of the **Corts' opening** in early autumn, Llucmar indulges its rauxa. There are bonfires in the squares, effigies of the year's villains (always including a Valdauer official) burned, the **gralla** shawms screaming, wine in the fountains, masks, the old law that no debt may be called and no wrong prosecuted until dawn, and the great **ball de bastons** stick-dance of the guilds. In the morning everyone returns to seny, sweeps up, counts the damage and opens the Corts.
- **La Diada de les Roses i les Claus** (the Day of Roses and Keys): **23 April**, Saint George's day. Lovers exchange gifts in the streets: **a rose for her and a key for him**, or for whoever. The rose means love. The **key** is the key to one's door, one's room or one's chamber, a real key, and giving it means exactly what it says. Every square has rose-sellers and locksmiths cutting copies. A Rocalban who receives three keys on Roses-and-Keys has had a good year. The Valdauer court has adopted the custom in its own way, and an Ilsenau woman's key is the most coveted gift at court on 23 April.
- **La Revetlla de les Noces**: the Hochzeitsnacht, with fireworks, the midnight sea-bath and the **Correfoc de les Escòries** (→ I15).
- **Els castells**: on great feast days, the guilds of the Bastos braç build **human towers**, men standing on men's shoulders, seven, eight, nine tiers high, topped by a small agile woman who raises her hand at the summit. Each guild competes for the highest. A tower that falls is a guild's shame. The crowd holds its breath at the top and roars when the tower comes down safely. Valdauers find it terrifying and Rocalbans find it a metaphor.
- **Sant Feliu Doble**: the futa saint's procession in Llucmar, with the reliquary carried by a futa (→ R10).
- **El Tió**, **el Llit de les Noces** and **el Pixaner** at Erbentag (→ I16).
- **The casa pairal**: the stem-household, the hereu and the pubilla, the unmarried fadrins at home (→ I59).
- **The greeting**: *"Bon dia tingui!"*, *"La Núvia us guard!"*, the embrace and two kisses (→ I01).
- **Dress**: the mantellina, the barretina, the espardenyes (→ I06).
- **Food**: **pa amb tomàquet** (bread rubbed with tomato and oil; the right bread), salt cod, **calçots** (grilled spring onions eaten by the hundred in a spring feast, black-fingered, with romesco sauce), **coca**, **crema cremada**, ratafia (→ X03).

## 7. Rocalba's grievance

Rocalbans have a list, and every Rocalban can recite it:
- **The Jurament**: kings who are slow to swear, or swear on the wrong altar (→ R01).
- **The Lloctinent**: always a Valdauer, never a Rocalban.
- **Taxes**: the Crown's constant demands for subsidies the Constitucions forbid. In **1698** the Corts refused the **Galley Subsidy** for new galleys until the King confirms three privileges he'd rather not.
- **The Brides' War**: Valdau dragged Rocalba into a religious war in the 1620s that Rocalba wanted no part of (→ I74).
- **The name**: Valdauers say "Valdau" for the whole realm.
- **The quicksilver**: the Crown, the Corts and Mirabent all claim the Cinabre lease, and the Crown keeps winning (→ I30).
- **The faith**: the faith came down from Montalba, Rocalbans say, and Wiedenbrunn's Archbishop acts as if it came from the Dom.

**Winter makes us free**: when the Brautsteig closes, Rocalba governs itself for five months (→ R01, I21).

The **independence party**, the *Rocalbistes*, would like the Corona to be a fiction. They are a minority in the Espases braç, a larger one in Bastos, and secretly funded by somebody, possibly Almagra, possibly Montalba's money, possibly both. **Casa Mirabent** flirts with them and denies it (→ I41).

---

## 8. Local custom, tension and a place worth visiting

- **Custom**: the Nit de Rauxa; Roses and Keys; the castells; the Llotja's notarized captives.
- **Tension**: the Galley Subsidy; the Jurament; the Lloctinent; the Rocalbistes; the Cinabre lease.
- **Worth a visit**:
  - **Montalba at the Contrapàs**, when the abbey's community dances its slow circle on the terrace above the sea (→ I54);
  - **the Llotja** on a busy morning;
  - **a castells day** in the Plaça de la Seu;
  - **the Passeig de les Veles at dusk** on Roses-and-Keys day, with a key in your pocket.

## HOOKS

1. **Three keys.** On the Day of Roses and Keys in Llucmar, the protagonist is given three keys by three different women: one Rocalban, one of his own household and one he has never seen before. By custom a key unused by the next morning must be returned in person.
2. **La Nit de Rauxa.** On the Night of Madness, when no debt may be called and no wrong prosecuted until dawn, someone in the crowd presses a mask into the protagonist's hand and says *"tonight you're nobody."* Someone else has been waiting all year for this night to settle an account with House Ilsenau.
3. **The Llotja dels Captius.** At a sale in the captives' exchange, the notary reads out the lot: a Grenzer Schwurjungfer captured by corsairs, sold as a woman, protesting in a man's voice that she is a man by oath. The Grenzer consul in Llucmar is outraged, and the buyer is a Mirabent.
4. **The castell.** A guild of the Bastos braç invites the protagonist to stand in the base of their castell at the feast of Sant Feliu, a great honor for a foreigner. The small agile woman who climbs to the top over his shoulders is the guild-master's daughter, and she has opinions about Valdauers.
5. **The Galley Subsidy.** The Quatre Pals propose a deal through the protagonist, as a man of a Seven Chairs house with milk-ties to the Crown: the Galley Subsidy in exchange for a Rocalban Lloctinent. The deal is honest. Mirabent's part in it is not.
6. **El Rellotge.** The Lloctinent, the punctual, loathed Ehrenreith count called the Clock, asks the protagonist to dinner in the Palau del Lloctinent. Over dessert he explains, with perfect courtesy, that he knows what the Torre de les Dides has been asked to prepare for, and asks what it's worth to him not to know it.
