# R13 · The Quickening

*The protagonist's gift, as the world would name, chart, fear and covet it. A woman he gets with child carries for ten to fourteen days. The child is grown, in body and mind, by the end of its mother's six weeks of childbed, and then stops growing. Nobody has ever done this before. This file covers what it looks like, what it costs, who would want it and why, how a house would hide it, and the theories that will be offered for it, none of them confirmed.*

**See also:** R12 House Ilsenau · I53 The Bleischlaf & the Sleepers · I65 The Hebammen & the Sechswochen · I57 The Ammenhaus · R04 The Cards (§3 the unripe) · R14 The Opened Womb · I58 The Confraria de l'Hereu · I16 The Zwölften (Erbentag and the Heir's haste)

---

## 1. What it is

The plain facts, as the house would eventually record them:

- **Any woman he gets with child carries for ten to fourteen days** instead of nine months. This includes futas carrying. Whether it applies to anyone else is a question for R14.
- **The child is born whole and healthy**, an ordinary-sized newborn.
- **The child grows to full adulthood within about six weeks of birth**, the length of the mother's **Sechswochen**, the customary six weeks of childbed (→ I65). Body and mind grow together. Then the growth **stops**, and from then on, as far as anyone can tell, the child ages like anyone else.
- **It happens only through him.** His children don't seem to pass it on, though nobody yet knows. No other man in the realm's records has done it, and neither has any futa.

### Names
Nobody has a name for it yet. When people find out, these are the words they will reach for:

| Who | Word | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Alchemists | **Merkurfrucht**, *fructus velox* | Mercury-fruit, quick fruit |
| Midwives | **Eilgeburt** | hasty birth |
| Church people | **die Eile des Erben** | the Heir's haste (see §8) |
| Rocalbans | **fruit de pressa** | fruit in a hurry |
| The Ilsenau household, privately | **die Mondfrucht** for the pregnancy; **die Schnellen**, "the quick ones," for the children | |

A child still growing is an **Eilkind**. A child who has finished is a **Gereifter**, a "ripened one."

## 2. Precedent (there isn't any)

Folklore offers only near misses, so when it is discovered nobody will have a frame for it:
- **The Seven-Day Prince**, a Kesrâni tale of a royal child born a week after its conception. It is told to flatter the dynasty, and nobody believes it.
- **The Figlio della Settimana**, a 1611 Velloran fraud in which a merchant swore his son had grown from infancy to youth in a few weeks. Exposed: the "son" was a nephew brought in from the terraferma.
- **The Heir**, born of the divine Wedding at **Erbentag**, half a year after the Wedding at midsummer. The Church calls this *die Eile des Erben*, the Heir's haste, and sees in it the urgency of grace (→ I16, I54). Every Zeuge in the realm has preached on it, and the Confraria de l'Hereu will make a great deal of it (§8).
- **Saint Severa the Awakened**, a sleeper-saint whose legend is disputed (→ R14, I53).

## 3. The pregnancy (as a midwife would chart it)

The timeline varies by about four days. High-karat mothers run faster, and so do Moon-blooded ones, and so does a conception in a Moon hour. Commoners and Saturn-blooded women run slower.

| Day | What happens |
|---|---|
| **0** | Conception. Within an hour the woman feels a spreading warmth low in the belly, **die Glut** (the glow). Moon-blooded women feel it strongly and unmistakably; others mistake it for wine or love. Desire flares rather than fades. |
| **1–2** | Breasts swell and ache. A day of nausea comes and goes. Hunger arrives, the **Eilhunger**: she eats three or four times what she normally would and craves salt, honey, milk and meat. She sleeps deeply and often. |
| **3** | She shows. Anyone who sees her undressed would think her three months gone. |
| **4–7** | The belly grows by weeks each day. The skin itches and burns, eased by salt baths and silver salves. Restlessness and **Mondhitze** (moon-heat): a strong, persistent arousal that the house's women will later describe, carefully, as "*not unpleasant*." Fetal movement begins on the fifth day and never stops. Milk comes in by the sixth. |
| **8–11** | Full size. She is heavy, hungry and sleepy, sometimes tearful, sometimes giddy. A midwife listening at the belly hears a heartbeat as strong as a nine-month child's. |
| **10–14** | Birth. Labor is short, two to four hours, the **Eilwehen**, and easier than an ordinary birth. The skin has stretched over days, not months, and has "quickened" with the child; there is little tearing. |
| **after** | The mother's body comes back fast. Within three days her belly is nearly flat, though her milk stays as long as the child feeds. She is ravenous for another week. Many mothers report a long, deep sleep on the second night after birth, and wake in a mood that varies from tender to strange. |

**Risks**: exhaustion if she isn't fed enough, and the ordinary risks of birth, though fewer. Twins are possible; nobody yet knows how often. The pregnancy can be hidden behind two weeks of "migraine," "a retreat," "the Kur at Bad Sulzau" or "a visit to the lake."

**Contraception against him** (→ R03, R08):
- **Mondwasser (Dunkelwasser) is unreliable.** It holds perhaps half the time. *Quick seed outruns the Moon*, the Mondküche will eventually write. Nobody realizes this until it fails.
- The **Venushemd** (gut sheath) works as it works for anyone.
- **Withdrawal** works as badly as it does for anyone.
- **The back way, mouths and hands** (Spiel) are safe.

Women who know the risk must choose. Women who don't know find out on day three.

## 4. The Ripening (the growing weeks)

The child is born an ordinary newborn and grows at a pace nobody who watches it ever gets used to. The household calls this time **die Reifung**, the Ripening.

| Days after birth | Stage | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **1–7** | **Wiegenwoche** (cradle week) | Feeds nearly every hour, so the house needs wet-nurses in shifts as well as the mother (→ I57). Sleeps most of the day. At night the growth is **audible**: a faint creaking of bones in the cradle, which nurses call the **Wachsnächte** (growing-nights). By day 7 the child is crawling, then standing. |
| **8–21** | **The child-weeks** | A child in body and mind, eating like a harvest crew. Speech comes in days, not years. They **learn by absorption, the way sleepers do** (→ I53). Everything said in their hearing settles into them: languages, songs, prayers, lessons, gossip, quarrels. What surrounds them shapes them very fast. |
| **22–35** | **The fever-weeks** | Youth compressed into a fortnight. Growth fevers at night, sudden moods, sudden heights, voices breaking, bodies changing week by week. They sleep a great deal. |
| **36–42** | **Finishing** | The body completes. Then comes the **Reifeschlaf**, the ripening-sleep: one long day and night of deep sleep, from which they wake **finished**. The **Stillstand** (standstill) has come. They are grown men and women, by appearance somewhere in their early twenties, at the full bloom of their bodies. |

### What a Gereifter is like
- **In body**: a grown adult at the peak of health. In looks they take after both parents, often strikingly. Nobody yet knows what traits run true, but the household will notice soon enough how often they have *that mouth*.
- **In mind**: an adult with **a library and no history**. A Gereifter knows what was taught, read, sung and said around them for six weeks, which can be a great deal: tutors' Latin, nurses' gossip, a mother's lullabies, a house's quarrels. They have lived only six weeks. They are **old in words and young in days** (*alt an Worten, jung an Tagen*). They believe what they heard most often, from whoever spoke most often in the nursery.
- **In blood**: they take the **mother's metal** at roughly her fineness. Their assay streak is odd: it **flimmert**, shimmers, like a heat-haze over the needle-marks. Hofprobieramt assayers have never seen this. The first assayer to see it will remember it forever (→ I49).
- **In the Cards**: no card settles on an Eilkind. The unripe are passed over (→ R04 §3). From the Stillstand onward a Gereifter is ripe like any adult, and can receive a card in a fall or an Umlegen. A Trump-bearer could therefore have a grown heir in eight weeks from conception. That is the single most dangerous fact in this file.
- **Do they quicken too?** Unknown. Nobody has yet had a Gereifter's child. The house will find out the first time one of them does.

### The house rule
The growing weeks are a **closed nursery**. Eilkinder are **children** until the Stillstand and are treated as children: fed, sung to, taught, watched over, kept apart from the household's adult life. Nothing about desire, bodies or beds touches them until the Reifeschlaf is over. The Ilsenau would set this rule as a matter of course, the Church's parable of the Feast backs it ("*the guests are the grown*"), and anyone in the world who learned of the Quickening would take it for granted. **After** the Stillstand they are adults, with everything that implies in this world, including every kind of nearness a house may permit (→ R06).

### What it costs
- **Food**: an Eilkind eats a year's food in six weeks.
- **Wet-nurses**: three or four in shifts for the first fortnight, which the Ammenhaus makes easy to hide (→ I57).
- **Clothes**: a new set every few days.
- **Space**: a nursery nobody else enters.
- **Teaching**: whoever spends six weeks talking at the child is choosing what kind of adult it will be. The house will fight over this.
- **Papers**: a person six weeks old has no history. Everything below in §6 follows from that.

## 5. Who would want it, and why

Nobody outside the house knows on the morning he wakes. Whoever learns of it will have reasons to want it. They are listed by how cold those reasons are.

**The warm:**
- **The women who simply want him.** Most of the people who will take him to bed will do it because he is beautiful, new, a rare Ilsenau son, a Wolf in a Schlafrock, or theirs to have. Many of them won't know. Some who find out will want him more: as something no one else has had, as a thrill, or to bear something nobody else could.
- **Women who long for a child and have been unable to have one**: the barren wife of Brautzell's incubation hall, the widow who waited too long (→ I31).

**The cold:**
- **The Crown**: an heir in two weeks, grown in six more.
- **The Almagran claim and the Schokoladenpartei**. The Glass King has no heir and holds the World. A child conceived *now* would be a grown man before the Glass King died. Whose child it could be *presented* as is the question nobody would dare ask aloud.
- **Trump-houses** (→ R04, I48). A bearer who conceives by him has a grown heir in eight weeks: blood 3 at once, and whatever else the house permits after the Stillstand. Every falls strategy in the realm becomes faster. Some will think of this coldly and be disgusted with themselves. Some won't be.
- **Spindelhäuser**: daughters in weeks, and karat inside the house.
- **The pit-blood families** (→ I50): a miner's daughter of 14 karat who bears his child produces a grown 14-karat heir in two months. That is a strong argument to put in front of the Hofprobieramt about Grubenadel.
- **Kesrân**: the Andarun's politics, if its agents ever hear.
- **Vellora**: they would rent him.

**The curious:**
- **The alchemists**: the Seventh Faculty, the Mondküche, the Mirabent laboratory at Cinabre, the Kalteneck Bleiküche. His seed would be the most valuable material in the Work: **Merkursame**. The Winkelalchimisten would sell fake "quick seed" tinctures within a week of any rumor.
- **The Stundenamt** would want his nativity: what hour, what sky (§8).
- **The Fall-Reckoners**: a new variable in every table.

**The pious and the appalled:**
- **The Confraria de l'Hereu**: proof of the Heir's haste in living flesh. They would want to venerate him, or at least have him at the Mercurial Supper (→ I58).
- **The Church of the Wedding**: a miracle or a monstrosity, depending on which see is asked first.
- **The Clear Church**: an abomination.

## 6. How a house would hide it

*(The house's general machinery is in R12 §10.)*

**The pregnancy (two weeks)**:
- a "migraine" in a darkened room;
- a retreat to the Stille Haus;
- "taking the brine" at Bad Sulzau (→ I24);
- "a visit to the Torre de les Dides" (→ I29);
- any noblewoman's lying-in can be pre-announced weeks early and nobody will count.

**The birth**:
- the **house midwife**, not the sworn guild;
- the **house Wiegenbuch**, not the parish's. Every birth should be entered in the parish book within a week, so this is illegal, and the house has done it since 1352 for the royal nursery's sake (→ I65).

**The Ripening (six weeks)**:
- the **Stille Haus**, across the lake from everything;
- the **Ammenhaus**, where infants appear and vanish every week;
- the **Sechswochen**: a mother in childbed is secluded for six weeks by custom, receiving only women. That is exactly long enough.

**The Gereifte (a grown stranger with no past)**:
- a **"cousin from Rocalba,"** papers from the Torre de les Dides, uncheckable until the pass opens;
- a **"returning Kavalierstour,"** a young lord or lady back from three years abroad (→ I19);
- a **"foundling of the Ammenhaus,"** raised elsewhere and now come home;
- a **"Linkskind,"** a left-hand child of some member of the house, registered in the Linkes Buch (→ I56);
- a **Salzung** (the newborn's salting-rite, → I54) by a cooperative Zeuge with an obliging memory for dates;
- forged **Stundenbriefe** and **Wiegenbuch** entries backdated twenty years, which costs money and puts someone at the Stundenamt in the house's debt.

**Where it breaks**:
- **Too many beautiful young cousins** with the same mouth and the same silver-pale eyes, all about twenty, all arriving from Rocalba in the same year.
- **The assay**: the shimmering streak (§4) will mark every Gereifter at their first Hofprobieramt test, and anyone hoffähig must be assayed.
- **The Hebammen** guild, already suspicious of the house midwife.
- **The Mirabent Correu**, which carries the house's letters ordering cradles, nurses and Rocalban baptismal papers in quantities a clerk would notice (→ I41).
- **The Moon-bearer's own dreams**, which leak into whoever shares her bed (→ R12, I46).
- **The children themselves.** Grown in six weeks by whoever talked most in the nursery, they will have opinions about their father, the house, their mothers and the secret. Not all of those opinions will agree with the house.

## 7. The legal and social questions nobody has answered

- **Legitimacy.** A child of an unmarried union is a bastard. A child of a Left-Hand bond is a **Linkskind**, supported but not an heir (→ I56). A child of a married woman is her husband's in law, which will create interesting husbands.
- **Kin.** A child by a near relative carries the **Nahvermerk** (→ R06). The house will have to decide whether to register a birth that, as far as any outsider knows, never happened.
- **Majority.** In Valdau a commoner comes of age at twenty-one and a noble at eighteen (→ R05). A Gereifter is legally a newborn. Every Gereifter who needs to sign anything, marry, inherit or be pledged needs papers that make them eighteen.
- **Their own heirs.** If the Quickening passes on, the question doubles every generation. If it doesn't, each Gereifter is a single surprising branch.
- **The Church.** Every newborn must be salted within eight days by a Zeuge, who enters it in the parish book. A Zeuge who salts a child in the Stille Haus chapel and sees it again in six weeks as a grown woman will have his own theology about it.

## 8. Four theories (none confirmed)

When people learn of it, they will explain it according to who they already are.

1. **The Stored Years** (Saturn): the theory of **Kalteneck's** physicians and anyone who studies sleepers. Saturn rules time. A sleeper of the Bleischlaf does not *spend* his years; Saturn *holds* them. Seventeen years of held time are now running out through his seed, quickening the women he touches with the years he never lived.
   *It predicts* that the gift may slacken as he spends the stored years, or that it is bottomless, or that it stops the day he would have turned thirty-six. The physicians will disagree. It also suggests other sleepers might wake with gifts, and Kalteneck has a hall of sleepers (→ I44, I53).
2. **The Moon's Overflow** (silver): the **Mondküche's** theory. Ilsenau Moon-fertility, concentrated over centuries in women, has here been concentrated in a male body that cannot carry it. So it overflows into whoever he lies with.
   *It predicts* that the quickening runs strongest with Moon-blooded mothers, and that past Ilsenau sons might have had it if any had lived to be tested.
3. **The Heir's Haste** (theology): the **Confraria de l'Hereu's** reading. The Heir was born in half the time, out of eagerness to fill the Feast, and the Heir is of both natures. A man so touched by the Heir that his children hasten is half-Heir already.
   *It predicts* that he will in time become both, and gain a womb (→ R14). The Confraria will want to be present when he does.
4. **The Hour** (the Sky): the **Stundenamt's** theory. He was born in a Saturn hour, under a waning Moon, with Mercury stationary in the sky, a configuration so rare the Hofastronom has a name for it, the **Stillstandsstunde** (the standstill hour).
   *It predicts* that his **Stundengeschwister**, anyone else born in that same hour in 1680, might share something. They would be grown and nineteen now, scattered across the realm, and have no idea (→ R02).

The house will hold all four at once, depending on the evening.

## 9. What it is like for him

- He feels no Glut himself. He may notice it in her: a change in breath, a hand going to her belly, a look.
- It doesn't tire him more than ordinary lovemaking would.
- Conception isn't certain each time. The odds are about what any young man's would be, higher in Moon hours and with Moon-blooded women.
- **He is not told.** On the morning he wakes, nobody knows, him included. The house will learn when the first belly rounds on the third day. Who that is, how it happened, whether she knew what she was risking, and what she wants afterwards are the story's business, not the bible's.

## HOOKS

1. **Day three.** A woman of the household who lay with him a few nights ago comes to the house midwife complaining that her stays don't fit, and is three months round by the look of her. The midwife has seen a great many bellies and has never seen this one.
2. **The Mondwasser failed.** A guest of the house who was taking the Mondküche's own Dunkelwasser, and trusted it, finds she is with child anyway, and that she will be delivered before the end of the month. She is married, and her husband is coming to Bad Sulzau on the twentieth.
3. **The Wachsnächte.** In the Stille Haus nursery, a nurse who has worked the Ammenhaus for twenty years hears the bones creak in the cradle on the second night and runs. She must be found before she reaches the Hebammen, or the Kalteneck.
4. **What the child heard.** A Gereifter wakes from the Reifeschlaf knowing, word for word, a quarrel two women of the house had over the cradle about who should have been his mother and what should be done with his father. She has questions.
5. **The shimmer.** A Gereifter presented as a Rocalban cousin must be assayed to be hoffähig. The Hofprobieramt's senior assayer looks at the shimmering streak for a long time, and asks to see the protagonist's.
6. **The Stillstandsstunde.** The Stundenamt's Hofastronom, casting the protagonist's nativity for the Feinbuch, finds the rare hour. He quietly begins searching the 1680 Wiegenbücher for anyone else born in it, and finds a name.
7. **The Schokoladenpartei's question.** A great lady of the chocolate party, who has learned more than she should, asks the protagonist one evening how long, exactly, it would take, and does not say for what.
