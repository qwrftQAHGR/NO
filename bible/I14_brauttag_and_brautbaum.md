# I14 · Brauttag & the Brautbaum

*The spring feast of the Bride: the end of the fast, the tree raised in every village and stolen by the next, the unmarried women's race and the Spring Bride who chooses first all day, the first night of the courting season, the court's procession, the ladies' tournament, and the evening the Moon-house opens its doors.*

**See also:** I05 (the Läuterwochen, which Brauttag ends) · I54 The Church of the Wedding · I10 Sport & the Hunt (the Damenkarussell) · R08 Desire & Custom (Fensterln) · R12 House Ilsenau (the Mondempfang) · I15 Hochzeitsnacht · X02 Calendar

---

## 1. The feast

**Brauttag** (Bride's Day; Rocalban **la Pasqua de la Núvia**) falls on the **first full moon after the spring equinox**. It is a moveable feast, ruled by the Moon, and anywhere from late March to late April.

In the faith it celebrates the day the **Bride came down from the white mountain** to meet the Bridegroom: the beginning of the Wedding, and the end of winter (→ I54). It ends the forty **Läuterwochen** of fasting (→ I05) and opens the **Brautwochen**, the season of weddings, courting and spring.

It is the **Moon's feast**. In Valdau that makes it the Ilsenau's day, and nobody is allowed to forget it.

---

## 2. The village: the Brautbaum

### Raising the tree
In every village of Valdau, the **Brautbaum** (bride-tree) goes up on the eve of Brauttag.
- It is a tall straight spruce or fir, chosen by the village's young men, the **Zeche** (→ I60), felled in secret, stripped of branches and bark except a green crown at the top, and painted in spiral stripes of blue and white.
- It is hung with **wreaths** and **ribbons**, and on its arms the **painted signs of the village's trades and households** (the smith's hammer, the miller's wheel, the innkeeper's tankard), with a **crescent moon** at the very top.
- It is raised by hand, with ropes and the **Schwaiberl** (pairs of long poles lashed into scissors). It takes forty men and an afternoon, and a great deal of beer and shouting. The village girls watch and comment.
- Once up it stands until Hochzeitsnacht at midsummer, when it is cut down and burned in the Brautfeuer (→ I15).

### Stealing the tree
For the **three nights** after it goes up, the Brautbaum must be **guarded**, because the young men of neighboring villages will try to **steal it**: saw it down, carry it off and set it up in their own square.
- A stolen Brautbaum is a village's humiliation, ransomed back with beer, smoked meat and a public apology in Gstanzl (→ I11).
- The rules are old and strict:
  - the tree may not be stolen if a guard's hand is on it;
  - it may not be stolen once it is up and the first dance has been danced round it;
  - violence is limited to wrestling and dousing.
- The guards drink, sing, and court the girls who bring them food. The guard on the third night is famously the hardest to keep awake.
- A raid on another village's tree is the season's great adventure, and the young court in the Wiedenwald has taken to raiding vine-villages' trees in disguise.

## 3. The Brautlauf and the Frühlingsbraut

On Brauttag afternoon, the village's **unmarried women** run the **Brautlauf** (bride-race): three times round the Brautbaum and once round the church, barefoot, skirts hitched, to the shouts of the whole village. Futas may run (→ R10). Widows may not.

The winner is **die Frühlingsbraut**, the Spring Bride, for the year:
- she is crowned with a wreath from the Brautbaum;
- she **leads the first dance**, and every dance that day;
- she has **Damenwahl** all day: she chooses her partner for every dance, and no man may refuse her;
- she sits at the head of the village table;
- her choice of first partner is watched like a battle, and is assumed (often correctly) to be her choice of husband, or at least of the first ladder at her window.

In towns the **guilds** choose their Frühlingsbraut. In Wiedenbrunn the **Wäschermädel** run their own Brautlauf on the river-meadows, and their Frühlingsbraut is a celebrity whose favor the young court competes for (→ R08).

## 4. The customs of the day

- **Brautwasser** (bride-water): at dawn the unmarried girls go out and **wash their faces in the dew**, or in a spring, before speaking a word to anyone, for beauty and for a husband within the year. Ladies of the court have their maids bring dew in silver basins.
- **Kranzlbinden** (wreath-binding): on Brauttag eve the village's unmarried young women gather to bind wreaths for the tree and for each other. It is a women's evening of flowers, songs and confidences, and of a certain kind of kissing the young women practice on each other "so as to be ready" (→ R09).
- **Brauteier** (bride-eggs): eggs dyed **blue** and **silver**, the Moon's colors, with Moon-silver salts or ash and woad, exchanged as gifts. Lovers trade eggs, and a girl who gives a boy a blue egg has said yes to something. Children hunt them in gardens. The court's eggs are gilded, jeweled and made by the Zuckerbäcker.
- **The Fleischweihe** (blessing of the meat): the first meat after forty days, blessed at the church door, a ham in every basket. Then everyone eats too much.
- **Brauttag bread**: a braided white loaf in the shape of a crescent.
- **Weddings resume**: the Church marries again from the day after Brauttag, and the Brautwochen are crowded with weddings postponed through the fast (→ I17).
- **Rocalba**: **la mona de la Núvia**, a ring-cake with whole eggs baked into it, given by godparents to godchildren. The **caramelles**, choirs of young men who go door to door singing the Bride's songs and collecting eggs and kisses. **El ball de la Núvia** in the squares.

---

## 5. The first night of the Fensterl season

The night after Brauttag, **the Fensterl season opens** (→ R08). The young men take their ladders down from the barn rafters and go out. The girls decide where their candles go. Fathers stay awake later than they admit.

In the vine-villages the court's young men go **fensterln** in disguise, to vintners' daughters, Wäschermädel and each other's sisters. Some court ladies leave a candle in a window of their country house to see who climbs.

## 6. The court's Brauttag

### The procession
In the morning, the **Brauttag procession** leaves the **Dom zum Bräutigam** carrying the statue of the **Bride**, silver-crowned and veiled. It goes across the inner city to the Bride's chapel at the Milk Column and back.
- The whole court walks in the **Almagran black** (→ I06), in planetary order (→ I69).
- **The Moon walks second, behind the Crown**: the Ilsenau women in black with silver crescents, immediately after the royal family, ahead of every other house.
- The Starkenberg walk fifth and look at the Ilsenau's backs the whole way.
- The Bürger line the streets, the bells of the whole city ring, and the Bänkelsänger sell songs about who is walking with whom.

### The Damenkarussell
At noon, the ladies' tournament in the **Winterreitschule** (→ I10). The court's women ride against the rings and the painted targets in costume, before the galleries. The prize is a **silver crescent** given by the **Obersthofmeisterin**. It is fiercely fought, wagered on and remembered.

### The Brauttag Corso
In the afternoon, the **Corso** in the **Hirschau** (→ I22). Every carriage in Wiedenbrunn drives slowly up the chestnut avenue and back, open to the spring air, to see and be seen.
- It is the day the court **shows its new arrangements**: whose wife is in whose carriage, whose Cortejo rides alongside, which Titelgeliebte has a new barouche, which young lord is driving which lady.
- *"Who's in whose carriage at the Corso"* is the season's first great gossip, and the Fiaker drivers sell it by the ride for a week.

### The Mondempfang
In the evening: **the Mondempfang**, the Moon Reception, at **Palais Ilsenau** on the Ammengasse (→ R12). Since Brauttag is the Moon's feast, the Moon-house opens its doors, and it is the first great reception of the spring season and the most sought-after invitation of the year.
- **The guests**: the royal family (who come for an hour), the Seven Chairs, the court, the foreign ambassadors, the Stiftsjahr Elevinnen in white, and a carefully chosen handful of others. The list is drawn up by the Obersthofmeisterin and the Spindelrat over weeks, and being left off it is a social death sentence for a season.
- **The house**: the whole Enfilade is open, the Mondsaal lit with a thousand white candles in its mirrors, the courtyard hung with silver lanterns, and the Milchsteg lit for the occasion.
- **The Mondmilch punch**: warm milk, honey, almond, cinnamon and Rocalban ratafia, from silver bowls (→ R12).
- **The music**: the Hofmusikkapelle, lent by Holdegg. A Menuett, the Kontratänze, and late at night, in the Spiegelsaal, the Spiegeltanz (→ I70). Whether this year there is a **Dreher** is a question the whole court is asking (→ I11).
- **The moment** everyone comes for: at midnight the house's women stand together under the Mondsaal's great mirror and the Hausmutter raises a silver cup to *"die Braut, die vom Berg kam"* (the Bride who came from the mountain). The **Moon-bearer** of the house is among them, unnamed and known to all.
- **The gift**: each departing guest receives a tiny silver crescent (→ I02).

## HOOKS

1. **The guest list.** The Spindelrat is drafting the Mondempfang list, the first since the waking. Three great families have each made it known, separately and delicately, that they would like their daughters presented to the house's son that evening. Each has something the house needs.
2. **The stolen tree.** The young court plans to raid the Brautbaum of a vine-village in the Wiedenwald and has asked the protagonist to come, disguised, as one of the thieves. The village's guards on the third night are vintners' sons and a rather formidable Frühlingsbraut.
3. **The Frühlingsbraut's choice.** The Wäschermädel's Spring Bride, crowned on the river-meadows, has Damenwahl all day and every right to choose any man present, of any rank, for every dance. She has heard about the Wolf im Schlafrock.
4. **The blue egg.** On Brauttag morning a blue egg is left on the protagonist's pillow. Everybody in the house knows what a blue egg means, and nobody will say who left it.
5. **The Damenkarussell.** The Obersthofmeisterin must hand the silver crescent to this year's winner, who has let it be known she wants the protagonist to fix it to her collar in front of the galleries.
6. **The midnight toast.** At the Mondempfang's midnight toast, the house's women stand together under the mirror as they have for two centuries. For the first time there is a man of the house in the room, and nobody has decided where he should stand.
