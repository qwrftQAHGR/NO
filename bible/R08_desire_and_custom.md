# R08 · Desire & Custom (sex culture)

*How the Double Crown makes love: what its faith says about pleasure, how villages court, how towns sin, how the court arranges marriage for blood and desire for everything else, what the sky and the Work add, and what the realm takes for granted that a modern reader wouldn't.*

## 1. What the faith says about pleasure

The Church of the Wedding has no quarrel with pleasure. Its god is a marriage, its liturgy a feast, its afterlife a banquet. **Joy is a guest's duty**: *"Freude ist Gastpflicht."* A sour face at a wedding is a small sin, and a husband who leaves his wife unsatisfied is confessing a fault, not a virtue.

The Church's physicians, following the old authorities, teach that **a woman must reach her pleasure to conceive**: *"die Braut muss sich freuen,"* the bride must rejoice. Every midwife repeats it, every Zeuge preaches it at marriages, and every bridegroom is told by his father, his confessor and his barber that his duty in bed is first to his wife's delight. It is a medical error, and it has done wonders for Valdauer marriages.

What the Church calls sin in this area:
- **Schlacke** (dross): dishonor, waste and harm. Shaming a house in public, abandoning a child, spreading the pox knowingly, breaking a sworn vow in a way that wounds.
- **Hoffart** (pride): above all, presuming to imitate the divine wedding (→ R06).
- **Unordnung** (disorder): conceiving in the wrong place, the wrong hour, the wrong bed. A bastard in the wrong house is a sin against order, not against chastity.

What it doesn't much care about: pleasure for its own sake, between whoever, so long as it is discreet, doesn't wreck a house and doesn't make a child in the wrong place. Adultery is a matter of **house honor**, to be settled by families, duels, fines and gossip, not by priests. A Zeuge hearing a confession of adultery asks two questions: *"Was there a child?"* and *"Does anyone know?"*

**The Clear Church** (→ I74) disagrees with all of this, and the Lautere Bürger of Wiedenbrunn are the realm's prudes.

## 2. The village

- **Fensterln** (window-going). In the Hallgau, the Eisenmark and the Sonnleiten, an unmarried young man climbs a ladder at night to an unmarried young woman's chamber window. Custom says he may talk at the window, and she decides whether the shutter opens wider.
  - She signals with a **candle in the window** (come), a **pot of carnations on the sill** (talk only), or **nothing** (go away). A **rosemary sprig** means "my parents are home."
  - The ladder is supposed to be the young man's own. Borrowing one is a joke, and stealing one is an insult to a whole family.
  - A young man who climbs to a girl who didn't signal risks a bucket of slops and a beating by her brothers. One who climbs to a girl who did and is caught by her father risks the same, plus marriage.
  - Fensterln season opens the night after **Brauttag** (→ I14) and closes at the first snow. Rocalba's equivalent is **la Festejada**, courting at the window-grille, which never involves a ladder and which everyone agrees is less fun.
- **Probenächte** (trial-nights). Once a couple are betrothed, custom in the mountains lets them sleep together under her parents' roof to see whether they suit, and above all whether she conceives. A betrothal that produces a pregnancy is sealed. One that doesn't, after a year of trying, may be broken without shame. The Church disapproves and blesses the results.
- **The Spinnstube** (spinning-room). On winter evenings the village's unmarried women gather in one farmhouse to spin, gossip and sing. The young men arrive later "to carry the lamps." The Spinnstube is where courting begins, where Gstanzl are sung (→ I11) and where a great deal of what happens later is arranged. The Clear Church has tried to ban it for eighty years.
- **Kirtag** (the parish feast-day), the harvest dances, **Hochzeitsnacht** at midsummer (→ I15) and the masked **Lesenacht** of the wine country (→ I27) are the village's licensed nights. On them more is allowed and less is remembered.
- **The Straw Court** (→ I60) polices the line: cuckolds, scolds, girls who opened too many shutters, men who climbed too many ladders, kin who were too close. It doesn't punish desire. It punishes clumsiness.

## 3. The town

- **The Bürger home** is the realm's most respectable, prudish and curious place. Daughters are watched, and wives receive their women friends at **Kaffeekränzchen**. Husbands keep a mistress in a rented room in another quarter, called the **Absteige** (the "stepping-off place"), and the wife keeps the **Hausfreund**: the husband's friend who dines with them on Pfinztag and stays when the husband travels. Everyone knows. Nobody says.
- **The Wäschermädel** (washer-girl). The Wiedenbrunn laundresses who carry linen from the river-meadows to the great houses are free, loud, independent, underpaid and in songs. They are famous for taking gentleman-lovers above their station and for dropping them. They have a ball of their own in Fasching that half the young nobility attends masked (→ I70).
- **The Heuriger at two in the morning** (→ I22, I27): long tables under the pine-bush sign, every class drinking new wine together, fiddles and Gstanzl. This is where mistakes get made.
- **The coffeehouse back room**, the **Extrazimmer** with its locking door, where a card game becomes a different game (→ I67).
- **The Rosengrund** (→ I23), the licensed pleasure-quarter, with its **Rosen** (licensed courtesans), its bath-houses, its pledge-schools and its **Doppelrose** cabaret.
- **The Badstube** (→ I64): the public baths, sorted by sex, rank and bond, where the Bademägde do more than scrub. They are less respectable than they were before the pox and more fun than the Bürger admit.

## 4. The court

At the top of society, **marriage is for blood and desire is for everything else.** Marriages are made by families, certified by the Hofprobieramt, timed by the Stundenamt and, if near, bought from the Church (→ I17). What happens outside them has a whole institutional vocabulary:

- **The Titelgeliebte** (titled mistress). A king's or prince's acknowledged mistress, with apartments, a pension, a seat at table and a household of her own. Her children are **Linkskinder** if registered (→ I56). She may be the most powerful woman at court, and the Queen may be her friend, her enemy or her lover.
- **The Cortejo**, imported from Almagra with the Ceremonial (→ I69). A married lady's acknowledged gallant, who carries her fan, holds her place in the Loge, escorts her to the Corso and is understood to hold more. Her husband tolerates him, because the husband is somebody else's Cortejo. Valdauers call him **der Kavalier** or **der Hausfreund von Stand**.
- **The Left Hand** (→ I56): morganatic bonds registered in the indelible **Linkes Buch**. They cover lovers of lower rank, sapphic partners, kin partners without a Nahbrief and favored pledges. They give a lover legal standing, support and, for Trump-houses, a recorded bed-tie.
- **The Toilettenkavalier**: the gentleman who attends a lady's late-morning toilette daily (→ I07), hands her the patches and sees her in her shift. His access is public and his intimacy assumed.
- **The Kurschatten** ("cure-shadow"). The lover one acquires at the spa of **Bad Sulzau** during the summer court and loses at the end of the season (→ I24). *"Was in Sulzau geschieht, bleibt in der Sole"*: what happens in Sulzau stays in the brine.
- **The Ball season** (→ I70): masked Redouten, **Damenwahl** (ladies' choice, when women ask men, and the choice is understood to run past the dance), Ballspenden favors, and the kin-dance **Spiegeltanz**.
- **The Opera Loge** (→ I68), with the curtain drawn.
- **The Beilager** (→ I69): the witnessed bedding of high marriages, presided over by the Obersthofmeisterin of Ilsenau. It is solemn, comic and sometimes deeply erotic, depending on the couple, the witnesses and the hour.
- **The Venus Vigil and the Stundenglöckner** (→ R02 §8): conception timed to the bell.

The Nähespiel, the court's public flirtation between kin, is in R06 §6.

## 5. The Sky and the Work in bed

- **Hours** (→ R02 §8): Venus for pleasure, the Moon for conception, Mars for roughness, Mercury for affairs, Jupiter for duty, the Sun for display, Saturn for denial (or impotence). The illustrated **Thirty-Two Hours** names an act for each sign and planet, and everyone has read it, though nobody owns it (→ I08).
- **Tinctures** (→ R03):
  - **Mondwasser**: Dunkelwasser to prevent conception, Vollwasser to invite it.
  - **Venusbitter**: to heighten what is already there.
  - **Schwefelbad**: sulphur baths for cold beds.
  - **Kupfertropfen**: for a blush.
  - **Eisenmut**: forbidden in bed by good manners, and used anyway by men who fear their age.
- **Contraception** besides Mondwasser:
  - the **Venushemd** (Venus-shirt), a sheath of fine sheep's gut tied with a silk ribbon, best from Vellora;
  - a **sponge** soaked in vinegar;
  - withdrawal, *"aussteigen vor der Kirche"*, getting off before the church;
  - the back way (*durch die Hintertür*);
  - keeping to Spiel.
- **Abortion**: the **Engelmacherinnen** ("angel-makers"), illegal and everywhere, with Saturn preparations and steady hands (→ I65).
- **Fertility**: Vollwasser; Moon hours; pilgrimage to **Brautzell**, where barren wives sleep a night in the incubation hall and often wake pregnant (→ I31); the touch of the Empress (lost since 1693); and, unknown to the world, one particular young man (→ R13).
- **Disease**: the **Lagoon-Pox**, which came from Vellora in 1495 and is cured, painfully, by quicksilver (→ I66). *"Eine Nacht mit Venus, ein Leben mit Merkur."* Courtesans are inspected, and slaves are sold with a **rein** (clean) certificate.

## 6. Bodies and beauty

*(Detail in I07.)*

- **Valdau's ideal woman** is *voll und weiß*: full and white. Plump arms, a round bosom, pale skin, small mouth, white hands, dimples, and a high color in the cheeks that is admired when natural and painted when not.
- **Valdau's ideal man** has good **Waden**, shapely calves in white silk stockings, which every gentleman shows and some pad. Small hands. A good leg for the minuet. A well-dressed wig, or since the 1690s, the daring **Eigenhaar**: one's own long hair, loose, a fashion of the young and the femme.
- **The femme man** (the **Zierherr**, → R11) is fashionable at court as companion, dance partner and Spindelhaus husband. **Futas** (the **Zweierlei**, → R10) are admired, desired and gossiped about in roughly equal measure.
- **Rocalbans** prize dark hair, dark eyes, olive skin and slenderness: *la bellesa morena*. They think Valdauer women look like bread rolls and say so in Catalan.
- **The protagonist**, read by Wiedenbrunn's eye: slight, smooth, unmistakably femme, with a face that flushes at a glance (Valdauers call that **Mondhaut**, moon-skin) and long dark waves worn loose, the Eigenhaar fashion. That makes him a Zierherr at first sight, and a very fashionable one. What he turns out to have below the waist is the occasion of a Viennese joke so old it has a name: the **Wolf im Schlafrock**, the wolf in the dressing-gown (→ R11).

## 7. Power, coercion and willingness

This is not a world that thinks in terms of consent. It thinks in terms of **rank, bonds, house honor, propriety, discretion and appetite**, and its erotic vocabulary takes pressure for granted:

- **"Sich zieren"**, to play coy. It is expected of a well-bred woman, and of a Zierherr, to make a show of reluctance. A suitor is expected to press through it. *"Sie ziert sich"* (she's playing coy) is said fondly. It is also said, in court and in the Straw Court, when she wasn't playing.
- **Willig / unwillig**: the words exist, and they matter, but they are matters of feeling, not of law.
- **The bound**: a body-pledge's contract makes bed service a duty (→ R07). Owners may be kind or not, and pledges may want them or not. The contract doesn't ask.
- **Fensterln and festivals**: the line between a window opened and a window pushed open is drawn by gossip, brothers and the Straw Court.
- **Marriage**: there is no concept of a wife refusing a husband, only of a wife making him regret it. Spindelhäuser invert that: in a Spindelhaus the husband is the one without a vote.
- **The law** has **Notzucht** (rape) on the books, and enforces it by rank. A commoner who forces a noblewoman hangs. A noble who forces a commoner pays a fine for **Mutwille** (mischief) and her father an indemnity. Forcing a bound person is a matter between the owner and the person who damaged the owner's property. Within marriage it is no crime at all. Within the great houses, the house handles it.
- **The cards**: some Trumps reach into desire itself. The **Devil** binds those who want her, and the **Lovers** turn desire up and down (→ I46). Their bearers are feared and courted accordingly.

Characters in the world experience all of this as everything from delight to dread. The narration doesn't need to pass judgment; the world has its own, which is mostly about who will find out.

## 8. Foreign customs (in brief)

- **Almagra** (→ I32): ferocious public honor and supple private life. **Dueñas** chaperone the daughters, ladies keep the **cortejo**, and lovers court through the **reja**, the iron window-grille. Ladies go out veiled, so their husbands don't know where they are.
- **Ormánd** (→ I33): women choose their lovers at the harvest dances and inform the men later, and hussars' friendships are fierce and physical.
- **Kesrân** (→ I34): the **Andarun** (the women's palace), concubines who rise to queens, the **zenne** dancers, poetry of the beloved, and the hammam.
- **Vellora** (→ I35): carnival half the year, masks, the **Book of Graces** with the courtesans' prices, and the Night Wardens who prosecute sodomy while the courtesans dress as boys.
- **The Sworn Valleys** (→ I36): prudes in the town, **Kiltgang** (night-courting, Fensterln's cousin) in the mountains, and the summer alps of the **Sennerinnen**.
- **Asagiri** (→ I37): **yobai**, the night-visit to a lady's screen, which the Hallgau recognized at once as Fensterln with better poetry; pillow-prints; and blackened teeth.

## 9. Slang and euphemism (a sampler; the full lexicon is in I08)

- **Pantscherl**: an affair. **Gspusi**: a fling, a sweetheart.
- **Absteige**: a rented room for affairs.
- **"Hochzeit spielen"**: to "play wedding," i.e. to make love.
- **"eine Stunde halten"**: to "keep an hour" with someone.
- **"Sie hat den Schlüssel stecken lassen"**: "she left the key in the door"; she's available.
- **"Er geht fensterln"**: he's courting; he's off to see a woman.
- **"Kurschatten"**, **"Hausfreund"**, **"Cortejo"**, **"Titelgeliebte"**: see §4.
- **"Die Braut muss sich freuen"**: a husband's duty, said with a wink.
- **"Vor dem zweiten Glöckerl"**: too soon (→ R02).
- **"Saturn im Bett"**: impotence.

## HOOKS

1. **The candle in the window.** On the first night of the Fensterl season at Bad Sulzau, a candle appears in a window of the Ilsenau summer house that nobody in the household will admit to lighting.
2. **The Hausfreund's dilemma.** A Bürger friend of the household, the long-time Hausfreund of a Hofrat's wife, needs somewhere to be seen on Pfinztag evenings, because the Hofrat is starting to count the hours. He asks to be invited to the household's card evenings as cover.
3. **Damenwahl.** At the first Redoute of Fasching, a masked woman chooses the protagonist at Damenwahl and leads him off the floor. Before the unmasking, three different women in the household are sure it was a fourth.
4. **The bride must rejoice.** A young bride of an allied house confides to an Ilsenau woman that her husband is following his confessor's instruction ("the bride must rejoice") with such dogged, desperate diligence that she is miserable. She wants someone to tell him, or teach him, or replace him for a night.
5. **The Kurschatten list.** At the end of the Sulzau season someone circulates a handwritten list of the summer's Kurschatten pairs, *"to settle bets."* Two of the pairs on it never happened, one did and shouldn't be there, and one name is missing that everybody saw.
6. **Sich zieren.** A Zierherr friend of the household has been pursued for a month by a powerful lady who thinks his reluctance is the usual game. It isn't, or it wasn't, and he no longer knows which. He asks the protagonist, the only other man in the house who is read the same way, what to do.
