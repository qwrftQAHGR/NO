# I25 · The Eisenmark & the Red Kogel

*The iron country: a mountain of red ore quarried in giant steps, the hammer-lords who own the forges, the foundry where Valdau's cannon are poured in the hour of Mars, the miners' night of fire, the veterans who can't stop drinking courage, and the quarrel over who the iron belongs to.*

**See also:** R03 The Work (Marsstahl, Eisenmut, Kogelrohre) · I40 House Starkenberg · I50 The Pit-Blood Trade (Kogelbräute) · R02 The Sky (the Mars hours) · I06 (miners' dress) · I28 The Wehrgrenze

---

## 1. The land

**The Eisenmark** (iron-march) lies **three days south of Wiedenbrunn** in the **Kogelgebirg**, the iron mountains on the road toward the Hallgau and the southern passes. It is a land of narrow valleys and black rivers, of forests cut for charcoal, and of hammer-mills along every stream whose water-wheels drive the great forge-hammers day and night, so the valleys **thud** like a heartbeat.

At its center stands **der Rote Kogel** (the Red Kogel; *Kogel* is a rounded mountain): a whole mountain of rich red iron ore. It has been quarried from the outside for six hundred years in **giant terraces**, sixty great steps cut around its flanks, so that the mountain looks like a red stepped pyramid. Lit by the sunset, or by the furnaces at night, it is unforgettable. From the summit, miners say, you can see the Selde on a clear day, and from the Selde, on a clear night, you can see the Kogel's fires.

---

## 2. The iron and who owns it

- **The ore** is cut by the **Knappen** (miners), carted down the terraces in wheelbarrows and on sledges, smelted in charcoal furnaces (*Radwerke*, wheel-works) at the mountain's foot, and carried as pig-iron down the valleys to the **hammer-mills**, where it is forged into bars, plates, blades, nails, pans and plowshares. The best of it goes to the **Stückgießerei** for cannon.
- **The Hammerherren** (hammer-lords): the families that own the **Radwerke** and the **hammer-mills**. They are rich, half-noble, intermarried and powerful. Some are ennobled (*Edle von*, the **Kanonenadel**, → R05) and some are Bürger who live like barons. Their great houses stand in **Kogelsdorf**, the market town at the mountain's foot, with iron balconies and iron weathervanes. They call themselves **die Eisenbank** (the Iron Bench), their cartel. It sets prices, wages and quotas, controls the charcoal-burners and fights the Crown over every Kupferling.
- **House Starkenberg** (Mars, iron, → I40) are the overlords: Fürsten of the Eisenmark, owners of the Red Kogel by a charter of 1302, whose castle **Burg Starkenberg** looks down on the mountain from the opposite ridge. They hold the Kogel and the Hammerherren hold the hammers, and they have been quarreling about the difference for three centuries.
- **The Crown** owns the **Stückgießerei** (cannon foundry) and the **Hofzeughaus** (royal arsenal) in Kogelsdorf, and needs the Kogel's iron more than anything in the realm (→ R01).

---

## 3. The Stückgießerei (the cannon foundry)

The Crown's great foundry stands at **Kogelsdorf**, beside the river. It is a cathedral of soot: casting-pits, reverberatory furnaces, the drilling-mill and the proving-ground on the river meadow, where new cannon are test-fired with a double charge.

- **Kogelrohre** (Kogel-barrels): the foundry's cannon, cast from Red Kogel iron **in the hour of Mars**, by the Work's rules (→ R03). The founders are **Mars-blooded**: Starkenberg's **Kogelsöhne** (→ I40) and pit-blood men of the mountain (→ I50), whose touch can feel a flaw in a cooling barrel. A Kogelrohr takes a third heavier charge than an ordinary iron gun without bursting. That means longer range, heavier shot and fewer dead gunners. It is **Valdau's single greatest military advantage**.
- **The pour**: casting happens **only in the hour of Mars**, fixed months ahead by the **Stundenamt** and kept as a **state secret**. The casting calendar (the **Gießkalender**) is guarded like the treasury. An enemy who knew it would know when Valdau's guns were made and how many. The Kesrâni and the Almagrans have both tried to buy it (→ R02).
- **A pour by night** is a spectacle: the furnace tapped, white-gold iron rushing down the clay channels into the mold-pit with a roar, the founders chanting the hour, sparks to the roof.
- **Saturnblei**: the arsenal also casts musket-balls of lead in Saturn hours. These are the illegal **Schlafkugeln** (sleep-shot) forbidden by Article Nine of the Willow Bridge truce. Officially it doesn't (→ R03, I28).

## 4. The Knappen and their customs

- **The Knappschaft** (the miners' brotherhood): every miner of the Red Kogel belongs to the **Kogelknappschaft**, a sworn brotherhood with its own chest (for widows, orphans and the injured), its own chapel, its own banner (a red mountain on black), its own band and its own court for disputes among miners. Its leader is the **Bergmeister**, elected every three years.
- **Dress**: the black **Bergkittel** with brass buttons, and the **Arschleder**, a leather seat-apron worn behind (→ I06). The Arschleder is the miners' badge of pride, and the young women of Kogelsdorf have a dance in which they pull it off the young men at Gießnacht (§5).
- **The greeting**: *"Glück auf!"* ("Luck, up!"), a wish that the miner will come up from the mountain alive.
- **The patron saint** is **Sankt Kunigund of the Bellows**: a smith's daughter of legend who pumped her father's forge-bellows all night to finish a sword for the Bridegroom's wedding, and was rewarded with a husband of iron.
- **The pit-blood**: six centuries of breathing and eating red dust and drinking from iron streams have put **iron in the Knappen's blood**: 6 to 14 karat in the old families, by the Hofprobieramt's touchstone (→ R03, I50). The Knappen are proud of it, the Hammerherren envious, the Starkenberg acquisitive, and Kogelsdorf's daughters are courted by noblemen for reasons they understand perfectly.

---

## 5. Gießnacht

**Gießnacht** (casting-night) is **Saint Kunigund's Day** in early December, the Eisenmark's great feast.
- At dusk the **Stückgießerei** makes a ceremonial **pour**, a bell or a small cannon, in the hour of Mars, before the whole town. The **first ladle** is poured by the **Kunigundenbraut**, a miner's daughter chosen as the year's "Kunigund's bride." This is the only time a woman may pour.
- The **Knappen parade** with torches up the first terraces of the Kogel in their Bergkittel and Arschleder, singing the **Steigerlied**, the miners' hymn.
- **Bonfires** on the terraces, so the Red Kogel is ringed with fire from bottom to top.
- **The Leder-Tanz** (leather-dance) in the market square: the young women of Kogelsdorf chase the young miners and try to **pull off their Arschleder**. A woman who gets one may claim a kiss and keep the leather until Brauttag. A man who loses his to a particular woman is expected to climb to her window by spring. Lads who don't want to lose theirs run very fast, and lads who do run very slowly.
- **Drinking**: Kogel beer, Kogelsdorfer schnapps, and **Eisenmut** (see §6), which the Bergmeister forbids and everybody shares.

---

## 6. The Eisensüchtige

**Eisenmut** (iron-courage), the Mars tincture, dulls fear and pain and clots wounds (→ R03). The army issues it before battle as the **Mars ration**. The Eisenmark makes most of it, in the foundry's alchemical works and a dozen back-room stills.

**The Eisensüchtige** (iron-addicts) are veterans and miners who can't stop. They have red-rimmed **Rostaugen** (rust-eyes), shaking hands, flashes of rage and an iron smell on the breath, and they drink a little every day to feel anything at all. After years of it comes impotence, the dreaded **rostiger Kolben** (rusty retort). Kogelsdorf's lower town, **die Rostgasse** (rust-lane), is a street of veterans' lodgings, cheap stills and a hospital run by the **Open Hand** (→ I62). Starkenberg officers drink Eisenmut too, and don't call themselves addicts.

---

## 7. Tensions

- **The Knappschaft against the arsenal.** The Crown's Hofzeughaus wants Kogel iron at a fixed price and more Mars-hour shifts. The Knappschaft wants wages, safer terraces (seven men died in a terrace collapse in 1697) and **an end to conscripting miners as Kogelsöhne** for Starkenberg's regiments. It struck in 1688 and 1697, both times broken by Starkenberg dragoons.
- **The Hammerherren against Starkenberg**: over the iron price, the forests, the charcoal and the Kogel's ownership.
- **The Pit-Blood Petition**: the Kogelknappschaft co-signed the **Knappen petition** of 1679, renewed in 1698, asking the Crown to recognize **Grubenadel**, lesser nobility for miners of high karat (→ I50). Starkenberg opposes it fiercely. Its **Kogelbräute** system depends on miners' daughters being commoners who can be taken as concubines without a noble marriage (→ I40, I50).
- **The war party**: Kogelsdorf's foundry and the Hammerherren profit from war. The Eisenmark is the heart of the party that wants to let the **Willow Bridge** truce lapse in 1704 (→ I28, I76).

---

## 8. Local custom, tension and a place worth visiting

- **Custom**: *"Glück auf!"*; Gießnacht and the Leder-Tanz; the Arschleder; Saint Kunigund.
- **Tension**: the Knappschaft and the arsenal; the Hammerherren and Starkenberg; Grubenadel; war.
- **Worth a visit**:
  - **the Red Kogel at Gießnacht**, ringed with fire;
  - **a night pour at the Stückgießerei**, if you can get a pass, which means knowing the Gießkalender, which means being someone the Crown trusts or someone who has bought a clerk;
  - **the Bergmeister's tavern**, *Zum Feuerstein*, where the Knappschaft drinks and decides things, and where a stranger who says "Glück auf" correctly will be bought a beer.

## HOOKS

1. **The Leder-Tanz.** At Gießnacht, the year's Kunigundenbraut (who poured the first ladle) has pulled the Arschleder off a young miner in the market square, a pit-blood lad of 13 karat, and then turned to the visiting party from Ilsenau. She asks, laughing, whether the young Prinz has a leather she can pull.
2. **The Gießkalender.** A clerk of the Stückgießerei with gambling debts offers to sell a copy of the year's casting calendar to anyone who'll pay. The Kesrâni ambassador's secretary has already made an offer. The clerk would rather sell it to a Valdauer house, because he is a patriot, if one can match the price.
3. **The terrace.** A miners' delegation from the Kogelknappschaft comes to Wiedenbrunn to petition a member of the Seven Chairs, any one but Starkenberg, about the deaths on the terraces. They are led by the widow of one of the seven dead, a woman of 14 karat iron. Two Starkenberg officers try to take her away in the courtyard of the Palais Ilsenau.
4. **The Rostgasse.** The Open Hand's hospital in the Rostgasse asks for a patron. Its patients are Eisensüchtige veterans, among them a former Starkenberg officer who was dismissed in 1694. He swears he knows what the Hofkriegsrat did with Saturnblei on the frontier, and will say it if someone keeps him in Eisenmut until he can.
5. **The founder's touch.** At a pour, a Kogelsohn founder lays his hand on a cooling barrel, says it is flawed, and is overruled by the Hofkriegsrat's officer, who needs the gun for the inventory. The founder wants a witness from a house that doesn't answer to Starkenberg for when the gun bursts.
6. **Glück auf.** In Kogelsdorf a miner's daughter is promised to the Starkenberg household as a Kogelbraut, a concubine to anneal the line, and has run away the night before she was to be fetched. She has come to the one place she thinks they won't look: the Ilsenau party's inn.
