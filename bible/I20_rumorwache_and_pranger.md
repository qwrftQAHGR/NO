# I20 · The Rumorwache, the Pranger & the Naderer

*Crime, shame and honor in the Double Crown: the city watch nobody fears and everybody bribes, the courts that treat a noble's crime as manners and a commoner's as crime, the punishments that are mostly public embarrassment, the gentlemen's code of the duel, and the informers who sell what the coachmen and stove-boys hear.*

**See also:** I23 The Rosengrund (the bribe calendar) · I49 The Hofprobieramt (forgery) · I71 The Damenduell · R09 (the Widernatur statute) · I44 House Kalteneck (the Bleidächer) · I12 Paper & Rumor · I60 The Straw Court · R07 Bondage (Menschenfang)

## 1. The Rumorwache (the city watch)

**Die Rumorwache** (the "uproar-watch") is Wiedenbrunn's city watch: about **150 men** in grey coats and red stockings, armed with halberds and lanterns, under the **Rumormeister**, a minor noble whose office is purchased. They patrol the inner city and the nearer Vorstädte by night, keep the gates, clear the streets for processions, pull drunks out of the Selde, and arrest the poor.

- **The night-call**: on the hour, the watchmen call through the dark streets: *"Hört, ihr Leut', und lasst euch sagen, / die Glock' hat zehn geschlagen…"* ("Hear, people, let yourselves be told, the bell has struck ten…"), and a verse warning about fire and the Schlacken. In the Rosengrund the verse has unofficial endings.
- **Their reputation**: slow, drunk, bribable, and very good at not seeing. A watchman's wage is so small that **Schmiergeld** (grease-money, bribes) is understood to be most of his pay. A thing the Rumorwache *wants* to see, they see very well.
- **Their tariff**: everyone knows what things cost. A Kupferling to look away from a drunk; a Bräutling to forget a carriage at a door after midnight; a Greif to lose a complaint; a monthly fee from every house in the Rosengrund (→ I23).
- **The Kleidervisitatoren**, the eight dress-inspectors, are Rumorwache officers (→ I06).
- **In Rocalba**: the **Guàrdia de la Llotja** in Llucmar, more honest and more Rocalban, and therefore less friendly to Valdauers.
- **Outside the cities**: manorial justice, the lords' bailiffs, and on the frontier the Grenzer's own courts (→ I28).

## 2. The courts

| Court | Who it judges | What it's like |
|---|---|---|
| **Stadtgericht** (city court) | commoners in the city | quick, harsh with the poor, careful with the rich |
| **Landgericht** (rural court) | peasants and villages | the lord's bailiff, the lord's interest |
| **Hofgericht** / **Landmarschall's court** | nobles | slow, courteous, rarely convicts anyone |
| **Pfandgericht** (pledge court) | disputes over pledges | under the Pfandamt; pledges may complain (→ R07, I61) |
| **Kirchengericht** (church courts) | marriage, kinship, Nahbrief fraud, blasphemy, the Nestel | two, one for each see, which contradict each other (→ I54) |
| **Ehrengericht** (court of honor) | quarrels between noblemen; duels | a panel of senior nobles that arbitrates insults and tries to prevent duels, or approves them (§5) |
| **The Hofkriegsrat's courts** | soldiers | military law, the gauntlet, the firing squad |
| **The Llotja** (Llucmar) | sea-law, trade, captives | Rocalban merchants' court (→ I29) |

**Privilege** (*Standesvorrecht*): a noble can be tried only by the Hofgericht and can't be sued by a commoner in an ordinary court. A noble's **servants** may be fined for the noble's offenses and the noble pays. A noble convicted of something serious is usually exiled to his estates "for his health" for a season.

**The Kavaliersdelikt** (gentleman's offense) is the realm's name for crimes the courts treat as matters of manners when gentlemen commit them:
- dueling;
- an unpaid tailor;
- a seduced chambermaid, or a forced one;
- a Rumorwache officer thrown in the Selde;
- a wrecked tavern;
- a gambling debt to a commoner left unpaid.

The phrase is spoken with a shrug.

## 3. Punishments: shame, mostly

Valdau's punishments are mostly **public shame**, not pain or death. The tone of justice in Wiedenbrunn is humiliation, an audience, and a story told for years.

- **Der Pranger** (the pillory): on the **Schandplatz** (shame-square) by the Hoher Markt. The offender stands with neck and wrists locked in a wooden frame for hours or days, with a placard naming the offense, while the market jeers and throws old vegetables. It is for cheats, slanderers, false weights, minor thieves, the Widernatur-convicted (→ R09), smugglers, and in 1659 the printer of the Thirty-Two Hours, who sold copies from it (→ I08).
- **Der Schandmantel** (the shame-cloak): a wooden barrel with holes for head and arms, worn through the streets by **drunkards** and **brawlers**. *"Er trägt den Mantel"* means he's in disgrace.
- **Der Lasterstein** (the vice-stone): a heavy stone, sometimes carved with an ugly face, hung round the neck of a woman convicted of **slander** or **quarreling**. She must carry it round the market three times while the women she slandered walk beside her.
- **Die Strafgeige** (the punishment-fiddle): a wooden board shaped like a violin with holes for neck and wrists, locked on a scold or a shrew. The **Doppelgeige** (double fiddle) is one board with **two sets of holes**, facing each other. **Two women who have quarreled are locked into it face to face**, inches apart, neck and wrists, and left together in the square, or in a room, until they have made peace. It is famous for the peace sometimes made, and the Kammerbüchl has three stories about it.
- **Das Narrenhäusl** (the fool's cage): a wooden cage in the square for drunks and fools, displayed for a day.
- **Fines** (*Wandel*), the commonest punishment of all, paid to the city, the lord or the Church.
- **Stadtverweis** (banishment from the city), for repeat offenders. They walk out of one gate and come in by another.
- **Der Staupenschlag** (public whipping), for thieves and runaways. Rare in the city since the 1670s, common in the provinces.
- **The Bleidächer** ("the Leads"): the state prison under the lead roofs of the Kalteneck palace (→ I44). It is for nobles, state prisoners, forgers of seals and assay certificates, and those the Crown wants to forget for a while. It is hot in summer, cold in winter, and very quiet.
- **Cinabre**, **the galleys**, **the forges**: penal bondage for serious crimes (→ R07, I30). This is the realm's real cruelty, kept out of sight.
- **Execution**: for murder, treason, arson and highway robbery. Hanging on the **Rabenstein** (raven-stone), the gallows hill outside the walls. The sword for nobles. Burning has not been used for anything since 1641, except in Almagra. Executions are rare in Wiedenbrunn, a few a year, and are attended by everyone, with food stalls.

## 4. Common crimes

- **Theft**: purses at the Ägidi Fair, linen from the river-meadows, silver from great houses (usually an inside job).
- **Brawling**: at the Heuriger, the Kirtag and the student taverns.
- **Slander** (*Ehrenbeleidigung*): a huge proportion of the Stadtgericht's cases. Wiedenbrunners sue each other for insults constantly, and a Titelfehler can be actionable (→ I01).
- **Adultery**: not a crime unless a spouse brings a complaint, which is rare and humiliating for the complainant. Commoners are fined, nobles ignored.
- **Unzucht** (fornication) among commoners: fined if the Church or the Straw Court pushes (→ I60).
- **Gambling** (Hasard): fined, in the street (→ I09).
- **Smuggling**: salt (the **Salzpascher**, → I24), quicksilver (→ I30), coffee, banned books.
- **Forgery**: of pledge papers, Gesindebücher (→ I04), Nahbriefe (→ I55), Stundenbriefe (→ R02), assay certificates (→ I49) and seals. Forging a **Jupiterlack seal** of the Hofkammer is treason.
- **Menschenfang** (man-catching): kidnapping free people into false pledges (→ R07). It is a serious crime, rarely punished, because the catchers pay the Rumorwache.
- **The Nestel**: knot-curses, prosecuted by church courts (→ I13).
- **Widernatur**: enforced against the poor (→ R09).

## 5. The Ehrenhandel (the affair of honor)

Gentlemen settle insults with swords. **Duels are illegal**, by the **Duellmandat of 1682** (dueling decree), which threatens death to the principals and seconds. No one has been executed under it. Duels are fought weekly.

### The code
1. **The insult**: an affront to a gentleman's honor. That includes:
   - a word, a blow, a Titelfehler;
   - a slight to a lady of his house;
   - a doubt cast on his karat;
   - a card left at his door: **the Pik King** or **Rei d'Espases**, unfolded (→ I02).
2. **The challenge** (*Forderung*): delivered by a **Sekundant** (second), a friend of equal rank, who calls on the offender's second.
3. **The seconds negotiate**: whether an apology will do (it usually should and rarely does), weapons, place, hour, and terms:
   - **"auf erstes Blut"**: to first blood, the usual;
   - **"bis zur Kampfunfähigkeit"**: until one can't fight;
   - **"à outrance"**: to the death, rare and scandalous.
4. **The Ehrengericht** may be asked to arbitrate. It judges whether the insult was real and the challenge fit. A man ruled against by the Ehrengericht has either apologized or been disgraced.
5. **The meeting**: at dawn, in the Hirschau woods, on the Selde islands, or behind the Ballhaus. Small-swords or sabres, or pistols for officers and Grenzer. A surgeon attends, a Bader with bandages.
6. **The rules**:
   - no **Eisenmut** (Mars-tincture) or any courage-drink. The duel must be **"trocken"** (dry), and a man found to have taken Mars is disgraced and may be killed with impunity (→ R03);
   - no armor under the shirt;
   - the seconds stop the fight at the agreed terms.
7. **Afterwards**: the honor is satisfied, the men shake hands and are friends, or pretend to be. The wound is shown at the coffeehouse. The Duellmandat is not enforced. The Rumorwache knew the hour and was paid not to come.

**Card-bearers and duels**: a man with a **Trump** may not be challenged by a man without one, by custom, since the fight would be unequal; nor may a Trump-bearer challenge downward. The **Tower**'s Starkenberg bearer has never been challenged (→ I40). The **Sküs**-bearer can't be bound by any code at all, which highwaymen find convenient (→ I46).

**Ladies duel too**: bare-breasted, to first blood, with seconds and no men present. See **I71 The Damenduell**.

## 6. The Naderer (the informers)

**Naderer** is the Wiedenbrunn word for a police informer. They are everywhere and nowhere:
- **Fiaker** drivers, who see who goes where at night;
- **Portiers**;
- **Ofenheizer**, the stove-boys in the Heizgang behind every great house's walls (→ I03);
- **Zettelweiber**, the slip-sellers (→ I12);
- **Bademägde** (→ I64);
- **coffeehouse waiters** (→ I67);
- **Stundenglöckner**, the bell-ringers outside noble bedchambers (→ R02);
- servants dismissed without a reference;
- and anyone with a debt.

They sell to:
- **the Rumorkommission**, the city's office for "public order," which pays for information on smugglers, Lautere preachers, gamblers and pamphleteers;
- **the Fall-Reckoners**, who pay best of all, for information on beds (→ I48);
- **the Cambra Negra** of the Mirabent (→ I41);
- **foreign embassies**;
- **the great houses**, about each other.

A Naderer known to be one is shunned, beaten and sometimes found in the Selde. *"Du Naderer!"* is a deadly insult in the servants' hall.

The great houses have their own networks, which are better: Ilsenau's **Ammenpost** (→ I57), the Mirabent Cambra Negra, Kalteneck's patient prison clerks, Ehrenreith's archives.

## 7. Protektion

**Protektion** (protection, patronage, connections) is how anything gets done. A Wiedenbrunner in trouble with the law doesn't ask whether he's guilty. He asks *who he knows*. A word from a Hofrat to the Rumormeister; a card left at the right door; a Jour attended; a cousin in the Hofkammer. That is Protektion. Not having it is the most dangerous thing in the city. *"Ohne Protektion kommst nicht einmal in den Himmel"*: without connections you won't even get into heaven, or past the Halde's assayer.

## HOOKS

1. **The Doppelgeige.** Two Wäschermädel who quarreled at the Brauttag race over a young man, the protagonist, are sentenced by the Stadtgericht to the Doppelgeige for a day on the Schandplatz. By evening they have made peace, very thoroughly, and both want to tell him so.
2. **The Rei d'Espases.** A Starkenberg officer leaves an unfolded Rei d'Espases at the Palais door for the protagonist over an imagined slight at the Cercle. Under the code he must answer, under the Duellmandat he mustn't, and he has never held a sword that he remembers. His body, oddly, seems to remember (→ I53).
3. **The Bleidächer visitor.** A state prisoner in the Bleidächer, a forger of assay certificates, sends word that he forged the karat of a Starkenberg Kogelsohn now high in the Hofkriegsrat, and will swear to it in exchange for a pardon. He will deal only with a member of the Seven Chairs whose house has no stake in the army, and only in the Leads, in person.
4. **A Kavaliersdelikt.** A young lord of an allied house has forced a Bürger girl and paid a fine, which is all the law asks. Her brother, a journeyman on the Walz, has come back to Wiedenbrunn to settle it his own way, and the young lord is hiding at the Palais Ilsenau.
5. **The Naderer in the Heizgang.** The household discovers that one of its own stove-boys has been selling what he hears through the Palais walls to the Fall-Reckoners. What he has sold, and to whom, is the question, and how long he's been listening.
6. **The Ehrengericht.** A gentleman insulted at the Café Sküs by a wager in the betting-book (*"that the Prinz von Ilsenau is a Wolf,"* → I09) demands satisfaction from the man who wrote it. The Ehrengericht must rule whether the wager insults the protagonist, the gentleman, or Ilsenau. All three must appear.
