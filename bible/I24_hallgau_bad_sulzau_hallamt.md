# I24 · Hallgau, Bad Sulzau & the Hallamt

*The salt country: deep green lakes under limestone walls, mines worked since before memory, a wooden brine-pipe forty miles long, the painted skulls of the dead, the miners' slides, the smugglers on the mountain paths, the salt office whose complaints window is underground, and the brine-spa where the court spends every summer in the water.*

## 1. The land

**The Hallgau** ("salt-country"; *Hall* is the old word for a salt-works) lies **three days southwest of Wiedenbrunn**, in the mountains between the Selde valley and the southern ranges. Long narrow lakes lie at the bottom of steep green valleys: the **Hallersee**, the **Grundsee** and the **Solteich** (the "brine-pond") at Bad Sulzau. Above them stand white limestone cliffs, forests of spruce, high pastures, and villages wedged between water and rock, with wooden houses, onion-domed churches, and flower-boxes on every balcony.

**Salt** is everything here. It has been mined in the mountain above **Hallreith** since before history. Miners have found **ancient tools and a man preserved whole in the salt**, two thousand years old, still in his leather cap. He is displayed in a glass case at the Hallreith mine-chapel and called **der Salzmann** (the salt man). Pilgrims touch the case, and courting couples have their silhouettes cut beside it by the fair's paper-cutters.

## 2. The salt: mines, brine and the Hallamt

- **The mines**: miners cut galleries into the salt mountain, flood chambers with water to dissolve the salt into **Sole** (brine), and draw the brine off.
- **The Soleleitung** (brine-pipe): a wooden pipeline of bored spruce trunks, **forty miles long**, laid in 1595, which carries the brine from Hallreith down the valleys to the **Sudhäuser** (boiling-houses) at **Ebenau** on the Hallersee, where there is wood to boil it. Pipe-walkers (*Rohrwärter*) walk its length daily, mending leaks. It is the longest wooden pipe in the world, and Hallgauers will tell you so.
- **The Sudhäuser**: great sheds where brine is boiled in iron pans the size of rooms until the salt crystallizes. It is packed into conical **Salzstöcke** (salt-loaves) and sent down the Sulzach river and the Selde on Plätten (→ I21).
- **The miners' slides** (*Rutschen*): to get down from one gallery level to the next, Hallreith miners **slide** down long polished wooden banisters, sitting astride, in their leather seat-aprons (→ I06). Visitors are allowed to ride the slides, with a miner in front and a lantern. Court ladies scream, and it is the summer court's favorite outing from Bad Sulzau. Couples ride together, the lady in front, the gentleman holding her waist. It is very dark and the slide is very long.

### The Hallamt (the salt office)
**Das Hallamt** runs the Crown's **salt monopoly**: the mines, the brine-pipe, the boiling-houses, the transport, the price and the **salt tax**, which pays about a sixth of Valdau's revenue. It sits at **Hallreith**, in an old castle above the mine. Its president is a royal appointee, usually from **House Sulzbach**, the salt-rich new nobility (→ I45).

The Hallamt is famous for one thing above all: **its complaints window is underground.** By an ordinance of 1612, petitions and complaints about salt must be lodged "at the Hallamt's seat." The Hallamt's seat, its foundational chamber, is in the mine, **three hundred Klafter** (about five hundred meters) underground, where the first salt was cut. So complainants must descend the mine, ride the slides and find the **Beschwerdekammer**, the complaints-chamber, where a clerk with a candle receives their petition and files it in a salt-cave. The ordinance has never been repealed. Valdau's saying for a complaint that will go nowhere is:

> **"Beschwer dich beim Hallamt."**
> *Go complain to the Hallamt.*

Wiedenbrunners say it about the Kronrat, their husbands, the weather and God.

## 3. The people and their customs

- **Miners and boilers** (*Knappen* and *Sieder*): a brotherhood, the **Hallreither Knappschaft**, with its own saint (**Sankt Rupert the Salter**), its own band, its own Tracht (black coats with salt-white buttons) and its own grievances. Generations in the salt give the Hallgau miners a pit-blood fineness of their own. **Salt** is not a metal or a planet, so the Hofprobieramt does not assay it, and the miners resent that (→ I50, I45).
- **The Beinhaus** (bone-house): at **Hallreith**, where the churchyard is a ledge between cliff and lake with no room for the dead, bones are **dug up after ten years**. The **skulls are cleaned, painted** with the dead person's name and dates and a wreath (roses for women, oak leaves for men, ivy for the unmarried, a cross for a child of under five), and **stacked** in the charnel-house in family rows. Some twelve hundred painted skulls look out at visitors, who find their own family name among them (→ I18). Lovers leave notes in the eye-sockets of the dead they wish would bless them.
- **Tracht**: the women's Leibl, Kittel and apron, the **Fürtuchmasche** code of the apron-bow, and the men's leather breeches and green jackets (→ I06).
- **Fensterln**: the Hallgau is the heartland of window-courting (→ R08). Every unmarried man owns a ladder.
- **The Zeche**: the village lads' association, running the Brautbaum and the Straw Court (→ I14, I60).
- **The Fischerstechen** (boat-jousting) on the lakes in summer (→ I10).
- **Lake-fires** on the Hochzeitsnacht (→ I15).
- **The Salzbraut** (salt-bride): a miner's bride receives a salt-loaf as part of her dowry, and at the wedding the couple share bread dipped in salt. *"Salz und Brot macht Wangen rot"* (salt and bread make cheeks red).

### Hallreith and its Zeuge
**Hallreith**, the mine-village, is home to a famous **village priest**, the **Zeuge of Hallreith**, an old man with a vegetable garden who **bears Trump V, the Pope**. Vows he witnesses **bind**: a breaker sickens (→ I46). He refuses to come to court, to the Bridegroom's See, or to anyone's wedding outside his parish. So couples who want an unbreakable marriage, a binding contract or an oath that can't be broken **come to him**, in Hallreith's little church above the lake, to be married or sworn. The village is full of eloping lovers, nervous merchants, feuding families making peace, and very occasionally a great house's heir marrying someone the family doesn't know about, in front of the only witness who makes it permanent.

## 4. Bad Sulzau (the brine-spa and the summer court)

**Bad Sulzau** sits at the head of the **Solteich**, a small warm lake fed by salt springs, a day's ride from Hallreith. Since **1640** it has been a **spa** (*Bad*). Since **1672**, when King Leopold II's physicians sent him for his gout, it has been **the summer seat of the court**. Every June the **Hofreise** brings the court here (→ I21), and until September Bad Sulzau is Valdau's real capital.

### The town
- **The Kurhaus**: a long colonnaded hall on the lakefront with ballroom, gaming-room, reading-room and terraces over the water.
- **The Trinkhalle**: where the brine is drunk, warm and disgusting, in the morning, for the health, to music.
- **The Kurpark**: lawns, lindens, a bandstand, paths into the spa woods.
- **The Esplanade**: the lakeside promenade, walked every evening by the whole court.
- **Villas** of the great houses along the shore. **The Ilsenau summer house, the Haus am Solteich**, is a long white villa with a terrace over the water (→ R12).
- **The royal Sommerschloss**, a smallish palace with a large garden.

### The baths
- **The Solebad** (brine-bath): warm brine in marble basins and private wooden cabins, for health. Cabins are rented by the hour, which is the joke.
- **The Mondbad** (moon-bath): the court's great open-air **brine pool**, warm, salt-heavy, so you float, under the stars. It is bathed **separately by sex by day**, and since 1680 **mixed by night**, in shifts and with lanterns, under a custom no ordinance has managed to end. The salt holds you up, the water is warm, the night is dark beyond the lanterns, and the rules are understood by everyone and written nowhere. It is the most famous water in the realm, and the scene of a hundred Kurschatten (see below), the Wolf im Schlafrock (→ R11), and the **Night of the Wet Wigs** (→ I77).
- **The Badbuch**: the spa's register of who bathed in which cabin at which hour, kept by the **Badmeister**. It is the most valuable document in the Hallgau after the Hallamt's ledgers, and is sold page by page every September.

### The season
- **Mornings**: the cure, brine at the Trinkhalle, a bath, a walk.
- **Afternoons**: excursions to Hallreith's mine-slides, boating, sketching, Kegeln, card-parties, visits.
- **Evenings**: the Esplanade, the Kurhaus balls, theatricals by the Hofoper's summer company, music on the water.
- **Nights**: the Mondbad.
- **The Kurschatten** ("cure-shadow"): the lover one acquires at the spa and drops when the season ends (→ R08). *"Was in Sulzau geschieht, bleibt in der Sole"*: what happens in Sulzau stays in the brine. Bad Sulzau's Kurschatten are counted, wagered on and listed every September (→ I09).
- **The Hochzeitsnacht** at Bad Sulzau, with burning rafts on the Solteich and the garden fête (→ I15).
- **The Damenduell of 1671**: on the Kurpark's lawn at dawn, the **Two Countesses of Bad Sulzau** fought the most famous ladies' duel in Valdau's history, and were heart-sisters within the year (→ I71).

## 5. Smugglers and preachers

- **The Salzpascher** (salt-smugglers): since the Hallamt taxes salt heavily, salt is **smuggled** from the mines by miners in false-bottomed packs, sold off the back of Plätten, and carried over the mountain paths into the Sworn Valleys and Ormánd by the Pascher bands. *Paschen* is Hallgau for smuggling. The Pascher know every path, every cave and every shepherd's hut. They are local heroes and the Hallamt's despair, and the Hallgau girls' favorite ladders (→ I20).
- **The Lautere**: since the 1690s **Clear Church** preachers have been working the Hallgau salt villages (→ I74). They preach that a miner's soul is as fine as a prince's, that the Hallamt is a thief, that the Mondbad is a sink of sin and that incest-dispensations are simony. The miners listen, and some convert. The Hallamt is nervous, the Hallreith Zeuge is unbothered, and the summer court finds the preachers picturesque until one of them names a court lady from a rock above the Mondbad.

---

## 6. Local custom, tension and a place worth visiting

- **Custom**: the painted skulls; the miners' slides; the apron-bow; window-courting; the Salzbraut's bread and salt.
- **Tensions**:
  - **the Hallamt against the Pascher**;
  - **the Knappschaft against the Hallamt**, over wages, and over the refusal to assay salt as a fineness (→ I50);
  - **the Lautere against the Mondbad**;
  - **House Sulzbach against everybody**, as it pushes for a **Salt Chair** among the Seven (→ I38, I45).
- **Worth a visit**:
  - **the Hallreith mine**: the slides, the Salzmann in his case, and the underground Beschwerdekammer, to lodge a complaint for the story;
  - **the Beinhaus** at dusk;
  - **the Mondbad** at midnight in August.

## HOOKS

1. **The slide.** On the court's excursion to the Hallreith mine, the protagonist is assigned (by a lady's maneuver) to ride the longest slide behind a lady he barely knows, holding her waist in total darkness for a very long time. At the bottom, her husband is waiting with a lantern.
2. **The Beschwerdekammer.** A Hallreith miner's widow asks the protagonist, as a man of the Seven Chairs, to come down into the mine with her to lodge a complaint at the underground window, because a complaint lodged in person by a prince must be read. It's three hundred Klafter down, and the clerk with the candle has been waiting a long time for someone important.
3. **The Pope's witness.** A couple of the court ask the protagonist to come to Hallreith as witness at their secret wedding before the Zeuge who bears the Pope, where vows bind forever. Halfway through the vows, one of them hesitates.
4. **The Mondbad by night.** At midnight in the warm brine, in the dark beyond the lanterns, the protagonist finds himself floating beside someone who knows exactly who he is, someone he can't see.
5. **The Badbuch pages.** In September the Badmeister offers the Ilsenau the Badbuch pages for July, which show, cabin by cabin and hour by hour, who bathed with whom. Some of the entries concern Ilsenau's own women. Some concern the protagonist.
6. **The Pascher's ladder.** A Salzpascher on the run from the Hallamt's men climbs, by mistake or not, to the protagonist's window in the Haus am Solteich, and begs to be hidden until dawn. She is a woman, and carrying a pack of salt worth her life.
