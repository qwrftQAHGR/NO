# I33 · Ormánd

*The Hungarian-flavored horse-kingdom of the eastern plains: a gentry so numerous that one man in twelve is noble, a parliament of ten thousand riders in a field, a copper-and-Venus temper that dances while it weeps, a land just freed from a century and a half of Kesrâni rule, a Palatine who holds the Chariot, and an alliance with Valdau that both sides resent.*

**See also:** R01 · I28 The Wehrgrenze · I34 Kesrân · I76 The Second Siege (the Ormándi hussars) · I45 (the Kerecsényi) · I42 House Holdegg (the pro-Ormánd peace party) · I11 (the verbunk, the karikázó) · I27 (the wine rivals) · I04 (the Heiducken)

---

## 1. The land

**Ormánd** lies **east of Valdau**, beyond the **Ostpforte**, where the Selde breaks out of the hills onto an immense plain.
- **The Nagymező** (the Great Meadow): flat grassland to every horizon, with herds of half-wild **horses** and long-horned **grey cattle**, sweep-wells standing up against the sky, **csárdák** (lonely roadside inns), mirages in summer and snow-wind in winter. Its herdsmen are the **csikós** (horse-herders) and **gulyás** (cattle-herders), who live in the saddle in wide linen trousers and felt cloaks.
- **The Selde** runs on through Ormánd to the far south, and on it stands the capital.
- **The Hársvölgy** (Linden Valley): the northern hill-country of vineyards, which makes the famous sweet **Hárs Ausbruch**, honeyed wine from grapes left to shrivel on the vine.
- **The Rézhegy** (Copper Mountains) in the north: **copper mines**, which make Ormánd a **Venus** country (→ R03).
- **The east**: forested hills, Clear Church towns, and rebels (§8).
- **The south**: lately reconquered from Kesrân (§2), full of ruins, Kesrâni baths, converted Lamp-houses and mixed villages.

**Székvár** (seat-castle), the capital, is a castle-hill above the Selde with a royal palace, cathedral and the magnates' houses, and a lower town on the flat bank with markets and the famous **hot baths** (§6). It has about 40,000 people. Kesrâni minarets stand among its church towers, being converted one at a time.

---

## 2. History in brief

- The Ormándi are horse-people who came from the east nine centuries ago, took the Wedding faith in 1000, and have argued with everyone since.
- **1222**: **the Copper Bull**, the charter of noble liberties, cast as a copper seal. It includes the **ius resistendi**, the gentry's right to resist a king who breaks the charter, which they have used.
- **1541–1686**: **Kesrâni occupation** of central and southern Ormánd after a catastrophic defeat. A century and a half of Kesrâni pashas in Székvár, Lamp-houses, baths, coffee, paprika and captive-raids. The Ormándi north held out as a kingdom in miniature, allied with Valdau.
- **1683–1688**: after the **Second Siege of Wiedenbrunn** broke the Kesrâni army (→ I76), Ormándi hussars and Valdauer regiments **reconquered** Ormánd. Székvár fell in **1686** after a siege that burned half the lower town.
- **1689**: **the Compact of the Bridge** (*Hídi Szerződés*), the alliance treaty with Valdau, signed on the Selde bridge at Székvár. It promises mutual defense against Kesrân, and lets Valdauer garrisons stay in eight Ormándi fortresses "until the frontier is secure." They're still there.

## 3. Names, ranks and the gentry

- **Names: family name first.** *Kerecsényi Ádám* (Adam Kerecsényi), *Bánffy Ilona*. In Wiedenbrunn they reverse them for Valdauer ears, and in Székvár they're insulted if you do.
- **Predicates**: noble families add a predicate from their ancestral land, *"szentgyörgyi Kerecsényi Ádám"* (Adam Kerecsényi of Szentgyörgy).
- **Titles of address**: *nagyságos* (magnificent) for magnates; *tekintetes* (respectable) for gentry; *vitéz* (valiant) for a man honored in war, a title prized above a county.
- **The gentry**: **one Ormándi in twelve is noble** (*nemes*). Most are **köznemes**, common gentry: small landowners with a house, a horse, a sword and a vote. The poorest are the **bocskoros nemesek**, the **sandaled nobles**, who own nothing but their nobility and wear peasant sandals (*bocskor*) with their swords. They are fiercely proud, quick to fight and impossible to tax.
- **The magnates** (*főurak*): a few dozen great houses with castles, vast estates, private armies and Venus-fine copper blood. The **Kerecsényi** are the greatest (→ I45).
- **Serfs**: the **jobbágy**, peasants bound to the land, who owe their lord labor, produce and obedience and cannot leave. Ormánd has **no slavery** and is very proud of it. Valdauers point out that the jobbágy can't leave either.

---

## 4. Government

- **The King**: Ormánd's crown is **elective**, chosen by the Diet from among the great houses. The present king is **Lajos V of the House of Várady**, elected 1688 after the reconquest. He is a vigorous, capable, popular soldier of fifty with many daughters and no son, so the next election is already being fought.
- **The Palatine** (*nádor*): the second man of the kingdom, elected by the Diet, commander of the army and judge between king and nation. The present Palatine is **Kerecsényi Ádám**, magnate, general, hero of the reconquest, **bearer of Trump VII, the Chariot** (→ I46). Any host he leads **marches without tiring, at double pace**. It is the most feared military gift in the known world after the Emperor's, and the reason the Kesrâni lost the reconquest. His burden is that **he cannot stay still**: three days in one place and he can't sleep. He rides constantly, from county to county and camp to camp, and holds his court in the saddle.
- **The Field Diet** (*Mezei Országgyűlés*): Ormánd's parliament, held in the open on the **Kölesmező** (Millet Field) outside Székvár. **Ten thousand mounted nobles** gather in their best, every sandaled noble with a vote and a sabre. They debate by shouting, vote by acclamation, threaten each other with swords, and elect kings. When the gentry disagree with a decision they rattle their sabres in their scabbards, *kardcsörtetés*, a sound that has unmade kings.
- **The counties** (*vármegye*): local self-government by the county gentry assemblies, which defy Székvár and Wiedenbrunn equally.

---

## 5. Faith

- **The Wedding Church**, with a particular devotion to **the Bride**, called in Ormánd **Boldog Menyasszony**, the Happy Bride, patroness of the kingdom. The Holy Crown is "the Bride's Crown." Ormándi churches are full of her, laughing.
- **The Clear Church** is strong in the east. The city of **Cívisvár** is the "Clear Rome," with its great plain white church and its college. A quarter of Ormánd is **Lautere**, which makes Ormánd the Clear Church's largest stronghold after the Sworn Valleys (→ I74).
- **Kesrâni converts and the Lamp**: in the reconquered south, families who took the Lamp during the occupation, some converted back and some not. Their old Lamp-houses are being turned into churches with the crescent-lamp still visible on the dome.

---

## 6. Customs

- **The hussars**: Ormánd's light cavalry, in braided *dolmány* jackets, fur *mente* slung over one shoulder, tall boots and fur caps, with sabres and pistols. They are the best light horse in the world, and they know it. **Hussar culture**: drinking, dueling, dancing, debts, horses, mustaches, songs, sworn brotherhood, and a famous elegance. Valdauer great houses dress their tall footmen as **Heiducken** in imitation (→ I04).
- **The verbunk**: the recruiting dance. Hussar recruiters dance in a village square, stamping, leaping and clicking spurs, until the young men can't bear not to join, and drink the king's wine and are enlisted (→ I11).
- **Fogadott testvér** (sworn sibling): Ormándi gentry do not marry close kin, and find Valdau's "Nähe" disgusting (→ R06). Their substitute for kin-intimacy is the **sworn sibling**. Two people swear before witnesses, with wine and a cut finger, to be brother and brother, sister and sister, or brother and sister by oath, closer than blood, sharing fortune, feud and secrets. Sworn siblings are sometimes lovers. The Ormándi insist this is entirely different from what Valdauers do, and in law it is.
- **The harvest-balls** (*szüreti bál*) of the Hársvölgy: after the grape-harvest, the villages dance all night. By custom **the women choose**: the women's ring-dance, the **karikázó**, circles in the square, and a woman pulls the man she wants into the ring. She informs him later what she has chosen him for (→ R08).
- **Weddings**: the bride-price; the night-long feast; at midnight the **kontyolás**, when the bride's hair is put up under the married woman's cap (*konty*), and she dances her first dance as a wife, the **menyasszonytánc**. Each man pays to dance with her (→ I17).
- **The baths**: Székvár's **hot springs** were built into great domed **Kesrâni baths** during the occupation: octagonal pools under domes pierced with star-shaped windows, steam, massage and coffee. The Ormándi kept them. The baths have men's days, women's days, and since 1690, by a scandalous custom of the Székvár gentry, a few **evening hours** when couples may bathe in the private cabins. A visitor to Székvár who doesn't take the waters hasn't been there (→ I64).
- **Csárdák**: lonely inns on the great plain where herdsmen, outlaws, hussars, travelers and girls dance till dawn to gypsy-style fiddle bands and nobody asks questions. *"A csárdában nincs úr"*: in the csárda there's no lord.
- **Music**: the **cimbalom** (hammered dulcimer), fiddles, the **tárogató** (reed horn); slow sad songs (*lassú*) and wild fast dances (*friss*), often in the same song. *"The Ormándi weeps while he dances."*
- **Food**: **paprika**, brought by the Kesrâni and now in everything, red and fiery; stews of beef and onion cooked in a kettle over a fire on the plain (the herdsman's **gulyás**); fish soup; sweet wine; plum and apricot **pálinka** (fruit brandy).
- **Venus in the blood**: the Ormándi gentry, many with Venus (copper) fineness, are famously **passionate, musical, susceptible and jealous**. A Venus line that marries close goes mad with **Liebeswahn**, love-madness (→ R03), which is the Ormándi's practical reason for marrying wide.

---

## 7. Desire, queerness and the bound

- **Women**: Ormándi gentlewomen manage estates while the men are at war or the Diet, ride as well as their husbands, and are known for choosing lovers at the harvest-balls. Widows are powerful.
- **Men**: hussar sworn-brotherhood is fierce and physical, officially nothing and really everything (→ R09). The Ormándi publicly despise Zierherren and dress more lavishly than any of them (→ R11).
- **Kin**: horror, except for the sworn-sibling exception (§6).
- **Futas** (*kétnemű*): considered lucky at weddings, as in the Hallgau.
- **The bound**: no slavery, serfdom instead. Kesrâni captives taken in the reconquest have mostly been ransomed, freed or married, and the Ormándi are proud of it. The Valdauer Seelenmakler's agents who come to buy them are thrown in the Selde.

---

## 8. Tensions

- **The Valdauer garrisons**: eight fortresses held by Valdauer troops under the Compact "until the frontier is secure." The gentry want them gone and Valdau wants them kept. A Valdauer officer is called a **"Knödelgraf"** (dumpling-count) to his face.
- **The rebels**: in the eastern hills, the **Szabadok** ("the free ones"), Lautere gentry and outlaws, fight a small war against the Valdauer garrisons and the Wedding Church magnates. Valdau suspects Kesrân of funding them. Kesrân suspects Valdau of inventing them.
- **The next election**: King Lajos has daughters. The Diet will elect, and the Kerecsényi, the Várady and Valdau's Greifenstein all have candidates.
- **The Clear Church** in the east against the Wedding Church in the west.
- **Ormándi wine** against Sonnleiten's (→ I27).

---

## 9. Trade, stereotypes and relations

- **Exports**:
  - **horses**: Ormándi horses carry half Valdau's cavalry;
  - **grey cattle**, driven on the hoof in great drives up the Selde to Wiedenbrunn's markets by the gulyás, a sight on the roads every autumn;
  - **copper**; **Hárs Ausbruch**; **paprika**; **hides**; **pálinka**.
- **Imports**: Valdauer iron and cannon, salt from the Hallgau, cloth, books.
- **What others say of the Ormándi**: proud, melancholy, music-mad, duel-prone, extravagant, mustached, quarrelsome, magnificent horsemen. *"Two Ormándi, three opinions and four swords."* Valdauers call them **Csikós** (horse-boys) and **Paprikasch**.
- **What the Ormándi say of others**: Valdauers are **Knödelgrafen** (dumpling-counts), clerks on horseback who marry their cousins. Almagrans are black crows. The Kesrâni are the old enemy, whose coffee and baths and paprika they've kept.
- **Relations**:
  - **The Compact**: ally, rival, and a necessary nuisance.
  - **The royal tie**: **Ferdinand II** of Valdau's second wife (m. 1627) was an Ormándi princess, so **Leopold II** and **Albrecht VII** carry Ormándi blood (→ I39).
  - **Almagra**: **Queen Mariana** of Almagra is a Kerecsényi, the Palatine's niece (→ I32).
  - **Valdau's peace party** (House Holdegg) is pro-Ormánd. Its war party wants Ormándi hussars for the next Kesrâni war and Ormándi gentry to stop voting (→ I42, I40).
  - **In Wiedenbrunn**: the **Kerecsényi palace** on the Herrengasse, Ormándi magnates at court, hussar officers in the coffeehouses, and the Heiducken in every great house's hall.

## HOOKS

1. **The karikázó.** At a Hársvölgy harvest-ball, the women's ring-dance circles, and a laughing Ormándi gentlewoman reaches out of the ring and pulls the protagonist in. By custom he is chosen, and she'll tell him later for what. Her husband is dancing the verbunk at the other end of the square.
2. **The Palatine rides in.** Kerecsényi Ádám, bearer of the Chariot, arrives in Wiedenbrunn without warning and with forty hussars, having ridden from Székvár in three days. He can't stay more than three, and asks to see the Obersthofmeisterin about his niece the Almagran queen, and then the young Prinz of Ilsenau about something else.
3. **Fogadott testvér.** A young Ormándi magnate who has been the protagonist's companion at the Ballhaus asks him, very seriously, to swear sworn brotherhood in the Ormándi way. It is not the same as what Valdauers do, he insists, and he wants it understood that it isn't.
4. **The bath-cabin.** In the Kesrâni baths of Székvár, during the evening couples' hours, the protagonist's party is assigned the wrong cabin, which is already occupied by a Valdauer garrison officer's wife and an Ormándi hussar. All four of them have reasons to say nothing.
5. **The Szabadok.** A Lautere gentlewoman of the eastern rebels, in Wiedenbrunn to petition the Kronrat in disguise, is recognized at a Heuriger. She takes refuge with the nearest person who doesn't seem to be a Valdauer officer, the slight, femme son of a house that has no regiments.
6. **The Field Diet.** The protagonist attends the Field Diet on the Kölesmező, with ten thousand mounted gentry, as an observer of the Seven Chairs. A sandaled noble rides up, demands to know whether Valdau means to put a Greifenstein on the Ormándi throne, and rattles his sabre. Ten thousand others go quiet to hear the answer.
