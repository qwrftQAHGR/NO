# I21 · The Sänfte & the Plätte: Getting About

*How people and letters move around the Double Crown: closed sedan chairs and the men who carry them, hackney coaches and their gossiping drivers, link-boys, river barges and the horses that drag them back upstream, the post-roads and their tolls, the one mountain road to the sea and the five months it is shut, the sea-routes, the court's great summer migration, and how long everything takes.*

## 1. In the city

### The Sänfte (sedan chair)
Inside the walls of Wiedenbrunn, where the lanes are too narrow and the mud too deep for carriages, people of standing go by **Sänfte**: an enclosed chair with a door and curtained windows, carried on two poles by two **Sesselträger** (chairmen).
- **The Sesselträgerzunft** (chairmen's guild): licensed since **1691**. About two hundred pairs of strong men in the city's blue-and-white, each chair numbered, waiting at stands by the Graben, the Dom, the Hofoper and the Greifenburg. The fare is **4 Kupferlinge a quarter-hour**. Great houses keep their own chairs, their own chairmen and their own colors.
- **The closed chair is the city's discreet transport.** A lady in a hired Sänfte with the curtains drawn can go anywhere unseen: to the Bader, to a lover's Absteige, to a pledge-school, to the Rosengrund. She steps out in a courtyard and nobody has seen her face. The chairmen see where she goes and are paid **Schweigegeld** (silence-money) not to say. **Das Sänftengeheimnis**, the chair's secret, is a byword. A Sänfte waiting at a door after midnight is gossip whether or not anyone knows whose it is.
- **Chairmen are Naderer** (→ I20) when the price is right, and the best of them never are. A good chairman can name his price for forgetting, and the great ladies of Wiedenbrunn know their favorite pairs by name.

### The Fiaker (hackney coach)
For longer trips and outside the walls: the **Fiaker**, a two-horse hired carriage with a box-seat for the driver. There have been about three hundred licensed since the **Fiakerordnung of 1693**, each with a painted number. The fare to the Hirschau is 6 Kupferlinge.
- **The Fiaker drivers** are a type: bowler-hatted, mustached, foul-mouthed, sentimental, and fiercely proud of their horses. They sing in the evenings at their own Heuriger, and are the best gossip network in the city outside the servants' halls. They know who goes to which house at night, who leaves at dawn, and who goes to the Rosengrund in a borrowed carriage. *"Das weiß schon der Fiaker"* ("even the Fiaker knows") means it's old news (→ I12).
- They despise the chairmen, and the feeling is mutual.

### Private carriages
- **The Karosse**: the great closed state coach with glass windows, gilding and the house arms on the doors, drawn by six horses with **Läufer** running ahead and **Lakaien** standing behind (→ I04). It is used for court, the Corso and funerals.
- **The Berline**: a lighter closed coach for town.
- **The Kalesche**: an open carriage for the **Corso** in the Hirschau (→ I14) and summer drives, to see and be seen.
- **The Schlitten**: sledges in winter (→ I10).
- **The coach as bedroom**: a closed Berline with the blinds down, driven slowly round the Hirschau, is a classic venue. The coachman is paid extra and drives very carefully over the cobbles, or very carelessly.

### Walking
Nobles rarely walk in the city, except on the **Graben** promenade and the **Bastei** (the old city walls, now a public walk), where walking is the point (→ I22). Bürger walk everywhere. Ladies who walk alone in the streets are either very modern, very poor or up to something.

### At night
- The **Laternenordnung of 1688** lit the inner city's streets with oil lanterns on iron brackets, lit at dusk and out by midnight.
- After midnight, hire a **Laternenbub**, a link-boy with a torch or lantern, to walk you home for a Kupferling. Link-boys know every alley, every back door and every Absteige, and sell what they know.
- The **Rumorwache** patrols, sees what it is paid to see, and calls the hours (→ I20).

## 2. The river

The **Selde** is Valdau's high road. Everything heavy moves on it.

- **The Plätten** are long flat-bottomed wooden barges. They carry salt from the Hallgau, wine from the Sonnleiten, timber and iron, and also passengers, at a fraction of the cost of the road. They are steered downstream by long oars, with a crew of **Schiffleute** (boatmen), who are loud, hard-drinking, superstitious, and have songs about every bend in the river.
- **The Zillen** are smaller boats for local trips, ferries and fishing.
- **The Ordinari-Schiff** is the scheduled passenger boat that leaves Wiedenbrunn's quay twice a week **upstream** to the Sonnleiten and the Hallgau landing, and **downstream** to the **Ostpforte** and on into Ormánd. It has a cabin for ladies, a deck for everyone else, a cook and a fiddler. A trip on the Ordinari is a social occasion, and romances begin on it, with three days in close quarters.
- **Downstream is easy**: the current carries you. The Wiedenbrunn–Sonnleiten run downstream takes **one day**.
- **Upstream is hard.** Boats are hauled against the current by teams of horses on the **Treppelweg** (towpath). This is the **Gegentrieb** (counter-drive): twenty or forty horses roped to a string of barges, with riders and drivers, moving at walking pace, **three days** from the Sonnleiten back to Wiedenbrunn. The haulers are a rough trade with their own inns along the towpath, and a barge-convoy passing a village is an event.
- **Boat inns** along the banks, and **Schiffmeister**, the guild of river masters, who decide everything about who travels when.

## 3. Roads, posts and tolls

- **The Poststraßen** (post-roads) connect Wiedenbrunn with the provincial capitals, the frontier, the Hallgau and the pass. They are gravel in good years and mud in most.
- **The Correu** (→ I41): **Casa Mirabent** holds the postal monopoly of the Double Crown. Its **post-stations** stand every two **Meilen** (about four hours' riding) with fresh horses, an inn and a postmaster in Mirabent cinnabar.
  - **Letters** go by Correu riders: Wiedenbrunn to Llucmar in **seven days** in summer by relays at the gallop.
  - The **Mirabent express riders** (Mercury-blooded, femme, fast as rumor, → R11) do it in five, for a fee that would buy a horse.
  - **Passengers** may hire post-horses station to station, and the rich travel this way.
  - **The Cambra Negra** reads the letters (→ I12, I41).
- **The Postkutsche** (stage-coach): a novelty since **1694**. A heavy public coach on the main roads, eight passengers inside and four on the roof. Wiedenbrunn to Bad Sulzau in **three days**. Uncomfortable, crowded and gossipy. Strangers are pressed thigh to thigh for days, which the Kammerbüchl have noticed.
- **Die Maut** (tolls): at every bridge, ferry, city gate, provincial border and pass there is a **Mautner** (toll-keeper) and a barrier. Tolls are charged on goods, beasts, carriages and sometimes people. A long journey's tolls can cost more than its inns. Smugglers know every way around every Maut.
- **Passes and safe-conducts**:
  - Within Valdau, a gentleman needs no papers.
  - A commoner travelling far needs a **Passbrief** (pass-letter) from his lord, parish or guild.
  - Pledges need their **Pfandmarke** and their owner's letter, or they are runaways (→ R07).
  - Crossing into Rocalba needs a **Geleitbrief** (safe-conduct) stamped at the pass. Rocalba stamps it slowly.
- **Inns** (*Gasthöfe*): the **Goldener Greif**, the **Schwarzer Adler** and the **Weißes Rössl** are the commonest names. Inns are rated by whether they have separate rooms for ladies, beds without lodgers in them, and a landlord who doesn't sell your arrival to the Diarium.
- **Dangers**: bad roads, broken axles, floods, wolves in the Kogelgebirg, and **highwaymen**. Above all **Schwarze Vreni**, the highwaywoman of the northern roads and the pass. She holds the **Sküs**, so no lock, chain or law can hold her (→ I46). She robs coaches with a pistol and a smile, takes a kiss as often as a purse, and has never been caught, because nothing can be made to hold her.

## 4. The Brautsteig (the pass to Rocalba)

The only carriage road between Valdau and Rocalba climbs the southern ranges by **the Brautsteig**, the Bride's Climb:
- it rises from the Hallgau through forest and gorge to the high pass;
- passes below the shrine of **Brautzell** (→ I31) and the **Hospiz am Brautsteig**, a stone refuge at the summit kept by monks of the Bridegroom with dogs and brandy;
- and descends through the red **Vermell** valley, past the quicksilver country of **Cinabre** (→ I30), to **Llucmar** on the coast.

**The pass is open from about Brauttag (April) to All-Dross (early November)** and **closed by snow the other five months** (→ R01).
- In summer it is a stream of carriages, pilgrims, couriers, merchants, mule-trains and soldiers.
- In winter the only crossing is on foot along the mule-tracks with the **Säumer**, the mountain muleteers who carry goods on pack-mules over the high paths in all weathers. Quicksilver flasks go north, and salt, iron and letters go south. The Säumer are a closed brotherhood with their own songs and saints. They charge what they like in winter and often die on the passes.
- **Avalanches** close the road in spring. **The Säumerzoll** (the muleteers' toll) is paid at every bridge.
- **Winter makes Rocalba free.** For five months news from Wiedenbrunn reaches Llucmar only by Mirabent couriers on foot, or by sea through Velloran waters at Velloran prices, and Rocalba governs itself (→ R01).

## 5. The sea

- **Llucmar** is the realm's port on the Middle Sea, with its galleys and merchant ships: **sagetes**, **polacres** and **tartanes** (→ I29).
- **To Vellora**: five to eight days' sail southeast, depending on the wind, and corsairs. Passenger berths on a Velloran galley are booked through the Banco.
- **Along the coast to Almagra's ports**: four days.
- **To Kesrân's waters**: weeks, with a Velloran or Kesrâni pass and a prayer.
- **Corsairs** of the southern coasts take ships and sell crews and passengers as captives (→ R07). Rocalban galleys hunt them.
- **Seasickness**: Valdauers are famously bad sailors. Rocalbans find this endlessly funny.

## 6. The Hofreise (the court's journey)

Every year in early June the court leaves Wiedenbrunn for the summer at **Bad Sulzau** in the Hallgau (→ I24), and returns in September for the Ägidi Fair and the hunting season.
- **The procession**: some **three hundred carriages**, wagons of baggage, furniture, silver, linen and wine; the Hartschiere guard; the Hofmusikkapelle; the Hofoper's singers; cooks, horses, dogs and the royal elephant's keeper (the elephant stays home); and half of Wiedenbrunn's tradesmen following behind.
- It takes **four days** by road, with the court lodging in requisitioned castles and inns along the way. Every night's lodging is a social event, and every night's room assignment a political one.
- **The Hofreisebuch** (court journey book), drawn up by the **Obersthofmeister** for the King's household and the **Obersthofmeisterin** for the Queen's, lists **who rides in which carriage** each day, and **who lodges in which room** each night. It is the most-read document of the year. Four days in a closed carriage with a particular person, and four nights in a castle with thin walls, have made and ended marriages. The Ilsenau assign the Queen's ladies' carriages, and every lady at court knows it.

## 7. How long it takes

*From Wiedenbrunn, in summer, by ordinary means. Winter roads double it, and the Brautsteig closes.*

| To | By | Time |
|---|---|---|
| The Hirschau (park) | Fiaker | half an hour |
| The Heuriger villages of the Wiedenwald | carriage | 1–2 hours |
| Sonnleiten (wine country, Stift Rebstein) | Ordinari downstream / road | 1 day / 1½ days |
| Kornau (the stud) | road | 1 day |
| Bad Sulzau (Hallgau spa) | road (Postkutsche) | 3 days (court: 4) |
| The Eisenmark (Red Kogel) | road | 3 days |
| Schloss Ilsenau on the Spiegelsee (the Weißach) | road | 4 days |
| The Ostpforte (Ormándi border) | Ordinari downstream | 2 days |
| Székvár (capital of Ormánd) | river and road | 5 days |
| Festung Senjak (the Wehrgrenze) | road | 6 days |
| Brautzell (the pass shrine) | road | 7 days |
| **Llucmar** (Rocalba) | road over the Brautsteig | **14–18 days** |
| Llucmar | Correu letter / Mirabent express | 7 days / 5 days |
| Glarach (the Sworn Valleys) | road over the Firnwand | 8 days |
| Vellora | via Llucmar, then sea | ~3 weeks |
| Peñalcázar (capital of Almagra) | via Llucmar, then road | ~4 weeks |
| Daryabad (capital of Kesrân) | road through the frontier | ~40 days |
| Asagiri | sea and caravan | about a year |

## HOOKS

1. **The closed Sänfte.** A hired Sänfte with drawn curtains has called at the Palais Ilsenau's side gate three nights running at midnight and waited an hour. Nobody gets in or out. The chairmen won't say who sent them, but they're numbered, and the household's Portier knows the number.
2. **The Fiaker's price.** A Fiaker driver tells the protagonist, cheerfully, that he drove "a lady of your house" to the Rosengrund at two in the morning last Venustag, and back at five. He'll forget it for a Greif. He has also told it to someone else, who paid more.
3. **The Hofreisebuch.** The Obersthofmeisterin's draft of the Hofreisebuch puts the protagonist in a carriage for four days with three ladies of the Queen's household. The draft has leaked, and each of the three has been offered something to change places.
4. **Schwarze Vreni.** On the road to Bad Sulzau the household's coach is stopped by the Sküs-bearing highwaywoman, who takes a purse from each lady and, from the protagonist, *"what a gentleman owes a lady of the road"*: a kiss, at pistol-point, for a long time. Then she rides off laughing, and the ladies in the coach are not laughing.
5. **The Gegentrieb.** Traveling upstream from the Sonnleiten on a hauled barge for three slow days, the protagonist shares the passenger cabin with a Rocalban widow, a Lautere preacher and a Velloran courtesan. By the second night all three want something different from him.
6. **The pass is closing.** It is late October. A letter must reach Llucmar before the Brautsteig closes, or the matter it concerns will wait until April. The only courier who can make it in time is a Mirabent express rider, which means the Cambra Negra reads it first, unless someone from the household carries it themselves.
