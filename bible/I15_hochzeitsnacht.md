# I15 · Hochzeitsnacht: the Midsummer Wedding Night

*The great feast of the faith: the night the Bridegroom and Bride were wed, celebrated on the shortest night of the year with fires on every hill and the coast, couples jumping the flames, the Church's own blessing on the dark until dawn, and a summer court at the brine-spa making the most of it.*

**See also:** I54 The Church of the Wedding · I14 Brauttag (the Brautbaum burned tonight) · I24 Hallgau & Bad Sulzau (the summer court) · R08 Desire & Custom · I60 The Straw Court · I29 Rocalba & Llucmar · I16 The Zwölften (Erbentag, six months later)

---

## 1. The feast

**Die Hochzeitsnacht** (the Wedding Night; Rocalban **la Nit de les Noces**, or simply **la Revetlla**, the Eve) is held on the **summer solstice**, the shortest night of the year. In the faith it is **the night the Bridegroom and the Bride were wed** and the world was made of their joining (→ I54). It is the holiest night in the calendar and the merriest, and the Church sees no contradiction. *"Freude ist Gastpflicht"*, joy is a guest's duty (→ R08), and tonight everyone is a guest.

Its liturgy is the **Hochzeitsvesper** (Wedding Vespers) at dusk: the longest and most beautiful service of the year, sung by both choirs, the Bridegroom's men and the Bride's women, answering each other across the nave. It ends with the Zeuge's blessing, **"Wie oben, so unten"** ("as above, so below"), which on this one night means *go and do likewise*.

---

## 2. The fires

- **The Brautfeuer** (bride-fires): at full dark, fires are lit on every hilltop, village square and riverbank in Valdau, and on every beach and headland in Rocalba. From the Greifenburg's roof the court can see a chain of fires running along the Selde valley into the distance, and the Rocalbans say that from a ship off Llucmar the whole coast is lit like a necklace.
- **The Brautbaum burns**: in the villages, the spring's **Brautbaum** is cut down at dusk and its wood built into the fire. The crescent from its top is thrown in last (→ I14).
- **The Strohbräutigam**: in the Hallgau and the Eisenmark, a straw figure of an old man, the "straw bridegroom," old winter, is burned on the fire with jeers.
- **Hochzeitskraut** (St. John's wort, gathered this day) is woven into wreaths and thrown into the fire, or hung over the door for the year. Its yellow flowers bleed red when crushed, which every child is shown.

### The Feuersprung (fire-leap)
When the fires have burned down to a waist-high blaze, **couples jump over them, holding hands.**
- If their hands **hold** through the leap, they will stay together the year.
- If they **let go**, they won't.
- If **one falls in** and is pulled out, the other owes the rescuer a kiss, or more.
- **The unmarried jump alone.** As they land they look back into the smoke, where they will see the face of their future spouse.
- **Scorched hems and singed calves** are boasted about for weeks. **The Brandsprung**, a burn-mark from a Hochzeitsnacht leap, is shown off at the Heuriger.
- Couples who shouldn't be seen jumping together jump at another village's fire.

**Kranzlwerfen** (wreath-throwing): girls throw their wreaths of Hochzeitskraut onto a roof or into a tree. If it stays up, they'll be married within the year. If it falls, they won't. If a boy catches it, he has the right to a dance and a kiss.

## 3. What the Bride forgives

> **"Was die Braut sieht, verzeiht sie."**
> *What the Bride sees, she forgives.*

By a custom older than anyone can date and blessed by the Church with a sigh, **from midnight to dawn on the Hochzeitsnacht, love made out of doors is "under the Bride's veil."**
- In the fields, the woods, the vineyards, the hay-barns and the riverbank, couplings on this one night are **not counted** as adultery by the Straw Court (→ I60). They are not confessed as sins, and not, by custom, held against anyone in the morning.
- The custom says nothing about who with whom. The village says a great deal, the next day, and then nothing.
- Masks are not required, but darkness helps, and the fires make the dark beyond them darker.
- **Feuerkinder** (fire-children) is the name for children conceived on the Hochzeitsnacht. They are born around **Brauttag** the next spring, and are considered **lucky**, bold and touched by the Bride. *"Ein Feuerkind"* is a compliment.
  - Midwives call the spring wave of births **die Frühjahrsschwemme**, the spring flood.
  - A married woman's Feuerkind is her husband's by law and custom, whatever his opinion.
  - In great houses, Feuerkinder are assayed with interest.
- The Church's position: the Bride forgives, the Zeugen bless what they cannot prevent, and the Bridegroom's See preaches against "the abuse of the vigil" every year in the week before, which everyone attends and nobody heeds.

The **Clear Church** forbids the fires, the leap and the night. Lautere villages hold a sober evening prayer and go to bed early. Some of their young people walk to the next village.

## 4. The Ringtausch

An old custom, condemned by the Bridegroom's See, ignored by the Bride's, and denied by every respectable person, survives in the **Sonnleiten** wine-villages, in **Rocalba** and in certain circles of the court.

At midnight on the Hochzeitsnacht, married people may **exchange wedding rings** with someone not their spouse, for the night. The ring-partner is chosen by agreement, by lot, by dancing, or by the Bride's luck. Until dawn, they are each other's. At dawn the rings go back. Nobody speaks of it.

Respectable Wiedenbrunn says the Ringtausch is a peasant superstition that died out two hundred years ago. A great many respectable Wiedenbrunners have done it.

---

## 5. Rocalba: la Revetlla de les Noces

On the coast the Hochzeitsnacht is louder.
- **Fireworks** (**coets**): every town fires rockets from the beaches at midnight. Llucmar's harbor fireworks are the finest in the realm, paid for by the braç d'Oros.
- **La coca de les Noces**: a long flat sweet bread topped with candied fruit and pine nuts, eaten with **vi escumós**, Rocalban sparkling wine.
- **El bany de mitjanit** (the midnight bath): at midnight everyone goes into the sea, clothed, half-clothed or not, to wash in the year's luckiest water. Lovers go in together. The beaches of Llucmar are crowded and dark.
- **El Correfoc de les Escòries** (the Fire-Run of the Dross): bands of young men and women dressed as **escòries**, the slag-demons of the **Halde** (the slag-heap of souls, → I54), in horned masks and costumes hung with sparklers, **run through the streets with fireworks spinning on poles**, showering sparks on the crowd. The crowd dances under the sparks with cloths over their heads, or not. It is loud, dangerous, ecstatic, and the Valdauer Lloctinent hates it.
- **Bonfires on every square**, built from old furniture and the year's broken things, and jumped as in Valdau.

## 6. The court at Bad Sulzau

The court spends the summer at the brine-spa of **Bad Sulzau** in the Hallgau (→ I24), so the court's Hochzeitsnacht is the summer season's peak.
- **The Hochzeitsvesper** in the spa church, the court in white, by custom, on this one feast.
- **The lake fires**: rafts with bonfires are towed out onto the brine pond and lit at dark, so the water burns.
- **The Gartenfest der Hochzeitsnacht**: a masked garden fête in the gardens of the Kurhaus, with lanterns in the trees, orchestras in the groves, tables on the lawns, and paths leading away into the dark of the spa woods.
- **The Mondbad**: the court's moonlit brine-pool is open all night, and on this night mixed (→ I24).
- **The court's Feuersprung**: the young court jumps a bonfire on the meadow below the Kurhaus. Who jumps with whom, and whose hands hold, is watched from the terrace with lorgnettes.
- **Kurschatten** (the spa affairs of the season) begin, end and change partners tonight (→ R08).
- **At dawn**: the court goes in procession down to the brine spring to wash its face in it, by old custom. Nobody looks too closely at who comes from which direction.

**The Ilsenau summer house on the Solteich** (→ R12) is famous for its Hochzeitsnacht: the house's women swim out to the burning rafts by night, and the terrace above the pond is lit with silver lanterns.

---

## 7. After

- **The morning**: the **Morgenläuten**, the bells at dawn, then sleep. The Hochzeitsnacht's morning is the only one of the year when no Wiedenbrunn shop opens before noon.
- **The ashes** of the Brautfeuer are scattered on the fields for fertility, and kept in a pinch in Breverl (→ I13).
- **The talk**: by the law of the Bride, nothing done under her veil is held against anyone. By the law of Wiedenbrunn, everything done under her veil is known by Venustag.
- **Nine months later**: the Frühjahrsschwemme, the Feuerkinder, and the midwives' busiest weeks (→ I65).

## HOOKS

1. **The Feuersprung.** On the meadow at Bad Sulzau, a woman of the household takes the protagonist's hand and runs at the fire before he can say anything. Their hands hold. Fifty people on the terrace see it, and two of them have reasons to mind.
2. **The face in the smoke.** An Elevin of the Stiftsjahr jumps alone, looks back into the smoke, and swears she saw the protagonist's face. She is fifteen houses' gossip by noon, and very sure.
3. **The Ringtausch.** At a Sonnleiten wine-estate's midnight a married lady of the Holdegg circle slips her wedding ring off her right hand and holds it out to the protagonist, *"for the night, as the custom is."* He wears no ring. She has thought of that, and has a ring for him.
4. **Under the Bride's veil.** Two days after the Hochzeitsnacht, a woman who was with him in the spa woods "under the veil" comes to the house midwife with her stays already too tight (→ R13). By the custom nothing done that night is held against anyone. The custom never imagined this.
5. **El Correfoc.** In Llucmar, a masked *escòria* in the fire-run seizes the protagonist's hand and drags him laughing through the sparks into an alley, takes off the mask, and is someone who should be in Wiedenbrunn.
6. **The burning rafts.** The Ilsenau women swim out to the rafts on the Solteich at midnight, as they do every year. This year a man of the house is on the terrace, and one of them calls back to him from the water.
