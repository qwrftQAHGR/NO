# I19 · The Kavalierstour & the Stiftsjahr

*How the young of the great houses are "finished": the young lord's years abroad with a tutor who writes home, the album full of signatures and drawings, the debts and the children left behind; the noble daughter's year in a great household, the most prestigious being the Ilsenau's, where she learns the curtsy, the fan, discretion and a good deal else; and the university and the journeymen for everyone else.*

## 1. The Kavalierstour (the cavalier's tour)

A young nobleman of a great house, at about **eighteen**, the age of noble majority (→ R05), is sent abroad for **one to three years** to be finished. He goes with:
- a **Hofmeister**: a tutor and governor, usually an educated Bürger or a poor gentleman, responsible for his studies, his morals, his money and his letters home;
- a **Kammerdiener** (valet), and one or two footmen;
- sometimes a younger brother or cousin, sometimes a friend of his own rank;
- a letter of credit on the **Banco della Fede**, payable at its branches in every great city (→ I35).

### The standard route
1. **Down the Brautsteig to Llucmar** (→ I29): sea-air, Rocalban manners, Catalan, and the first taste of a coast where nobody cares who his grandmother was.
2. **By sea to Vellora** (→ I35), the great school. There he learns **fencing**, **dancing**, music, Velloran, banking, glass and opera; how to gamble at the ridotto and lose gracefully; and, from the **Book of Graces**, the courtesans of the lagoon. Most young Valdauers spend half their tour in Vellora, and their fathers' letters of credit spend more.
3. **Over the Serra del Vent to Almagra** (→ I32): the **riding school**, the Ceremonial at its source, Ombre, the grandees' honor, and the lessons of the **cortejo** from Almagran ladies who teach young foreigners the art of being a married woman's gallant.
4. **North through the Sworn Valleys** (→ I36): the Valleys' universities and mercenary captains, mountain air, and Clear Church sermons the Hofmeister makes him attend so that he can write home that he did.
5. **East to Ormánd** (→ I33), for horsemanship, hussars, wine, and the wild-horse fairs.
6. **Home**, by way of the Ostpforte and down the Selde.

### What he is supposed to learn
**Languages** (Catalan, Almagran, Velloran, Latin); **fencing** and **riding**; **dancing** (the Menuett, the Courante, the Sarabanda); **court manners** abroad; **drawing**, **music**, **fortification**; and **Weltkenntnis**, knowledge of the world.

### What he actually learns
**Gambling**, **wenching**, **dueling**, **debt**, and a taste for chocolate or coffee that will decide his politics (→ I05).

### The Hofmeister's letters
The Hofmeister writes home every fortnight to the young man's **father**, or in a **Spindelhaus** to his **mother** or the **Hausmutter**. He reports on studies, health, expenses, company, and every lapse in conduct. He is the family's spy, the young man's jailer, and frequently his accomplice. A Hofmeister who reports too much is hated. One who reports too little is dismissed. A good Hofmeister reports exactly enough. Young men's fathers have kept their Hofmeisters' letters in boxes, and some of those boxes would make the Kammerbüchl blush.

### The Stammbuch (the album)
Every touring cavalier carries a **Stammbuch**: a small leather-bound album in which everyone he meets of interest **writes or draws something and signs it**. In it are:
- professors with Latin mottos;
- fencing-masters with crossed swords;
- princes with their arms;
- fellow travelers with jokes;
- and **ladies**, the pages that matter. A lady writes a verse and signs with her initials or a sign. **Velloran courtesans** paint in it: a watercolor of themselves, a rose, a pair of lips, or (from the boldest) a small erotic drawing in a corner, signed with a flourish.

A man's Stammbuch is a record of his youth, his triumphs and his indiscretions. Brides have been known to read their husband's after the wedding. *"Er hat ein volles Stammbuch"* means he's a man of the world, too much of one.

### What he leaves behind
- **Debts**: Velloran tailors and gaming-houses send bills to Wiedenbrunn for years.
- **Tourkinder** ("tour-children"): children left behind in Vellora, Almagra or Llucmar by touring young lords. Some are acknowledged and supported, most aren't. A few turn up in Wiedenbrunn twenty years later with a letter and a Stammbuch page as proof.
- **The Tourkrankheit**: the Lagoon-Pox, brought home from Vellora (→ I66).
- **Lovers**: a Velloran courtesan or an Almagran lady of the cortejo, who writes letters for years.

### Bürger and journeymen: the Walz
Craftsmen's journeymen do their own tour, **auf der Walz**, "on the roll." For three years and a day after finishing their apprenticeship they must travel from town to town, working for masters, never coming within a day's walk of home. They wear the black corduroy of their guild, carry a bundle and a twisted staff, and sleep in the guild-houses. They come back with skill, stories, and sometimes a wife from far away.

## 2. The Stiftsjahr (the foundation-year)

A noble daughter of eighteen does not tour. She is finished **in a great household**, for **one year**, as an **Elevin** (pupil), before her presentation at court and her marriage or her place as a lady-in-waiting. This is the **Stiftsjahr**.

### Where
- **The most prestigious, by far: the Ilsenau household** (→ R12). Every year the **Obersthofmeisterin** takes about **twelve Elevinnen** from the great and lesser houses into Palais Ilsenau in winter and Schloss Ilsenau and Bad Sulzau in summer. A place among them is fought for. Families send gifts, letters and favors to the Hausmutter for years ahead. An **Ilsenau-Elevin** has the best start a noble girl can have at court: the Obersthofmeisterin chooses the Queen's ladies, and she chooses them from her own Elevinnen.
- **The Fräuleinstift at Brautzell** (→ I31): less prestigious, more pious, with more freedom, and notoriously more Spindelliebe.
- **Other great Spindelhäuser**, and a few great dowagers who keep Elevinnen in their Witwensitz.

### What she learns
- **The Hofknicks**, practiced until she cries (→ I01).
- **The Titelbuch**, by heart, and the Ceremonial, by heart (→ I69).
- **The Toilette**: dressing, patches, the fan (→ I07).
- **Dancing**: the Menuett, the Kontratanz, and since 1690 the Spiegeltanz (→ I70).
- **Music**: the clavichord and singing.
- **Letter-writing**, ciphers and letter-locking (→ I12).
- **Household management**: accounts, servants, linen, the Hausordnung.
- **Spinning**: in the Ilsenau household every Elevin learns to spin silver-flax in the Spinnzimmer with the house's women, and to listen.
- **Riding**, and in the Ilsenau household **swimming**.
- **Cards**: Ombre and Tarock.
- **Discretion**, above all.
- **And from the house's women, quietly**: how to read a man, how to manage a husband, Mondwasser, the courtesies of the bed, and the house's own approach to friendship between women (→ R09).

### What else happens
- **Stiftsschwestern** (foundation-sisters): the Elevinnen of a single year share rooms, secrets, triumphs and humiliations, and become a network of women for life. An **Ilsenau-Jahrgang** (a year's class of Ilsenau Elevinnen) is a political faction at court twenty years later.
- **First loves**: very often another Elevin, or one of the house's women. *"Sie hat ihr Stiftsjahr bei den Ilsenau gemacht"* ("she did her Stiftsjahr with the Ilsenau") is said with a meaningful look.
- **Zierherren**: the house's Mondbräutigame and visiting Toilettenkavaliere are the Elevinnen's first safe flirtations, and occasionally unsafe ones.
- **The Elevinnen's dormitory**, two to a room under an older woman's eye, is famously hard to keep anyone in at night.
- **Ilsenau knows every daughter at court** intimately, from her temper and her letters to her secrets and her first love. Every Elevin goes home knowing that the house knows. That is half the Ilsenau's power at court, and nobody says so.

### In 1699
The Stiftsjahr of 1698–99 was in residence when the household's son woke. **Twelve noble daughters of other houses** are living under the same roof as the realm's newest secret, and they will go home in the autumn (→ R12 §11).

## 3. The university

**Die Hohe Schule zu Wiedenbrunn**, the university, founded 1365, has **seven faculties**:
1. Theology (two chairs, one for each see, which quarrel);
2. Law;
3. Medicine;
4. Arts (philosophy, mathematics, astronomy);
5. Music;
6. **the Stundenkunde**, the faculty of the hours, astrology, joined to the Stundenamt (→ I51);
7. **the Seventh Faculty**, the Art itself, alchemy, founded by the Flask King in **1591** (→ I52).

Students (*Burschen*) are mostly Bürger sons, some poor nobles and some foreigners, from eighteen to thirty. They live in **Bursen** (student houses), drink in the taverns of the **Bäckerstraße**, fight duels with **students' rapiers** (the **Mensur**, a regulated student duel to a scar on the cheek, worn proudly for life), sing songs, chase Wäschermädel, and attend lectures when sober. Their great festivals are the **Kommers**, beer-feasts with ritual songs, and their annual brawl with the **Fiaker** guild.

Women are not admitted. Two women have attended lectures in the Seventh Faculty dressed as men, and one took the **Red Gown** before she was discovered. The Faculty kept her degree on the books and her portrait in the hall. Ilsenau's house alchemist is a woman of the Red Gown, admitted under a 1660s privilege for "the royal nursery's needs" (→ R12).

## 4. The protagonist missed all of it

He slept through eighteen. He had no Hofmeister, no Kavalierstour and no Stammbuch. He never fenced at Vellora, rode at Almagra, wenched in the lagoon or ran up a debt. He knows a great deal, from seventeen years of being read to (→ I53), and has experienced nothing.

- The court's young men will offer to take him on a tour.
- The house's women will consider whether a Kavalierstour is a fine way to keep him far from court for two years, or a terrible way to lose control of him.
- The current Stiftsjahr's Elevinnen, twelve noble daughters his own age under his own roof, will consider him their most interesting subject of study.

## HOOKS

1. **The empty Stammbuch.** On his first social evening, a lady hands the protagonist a new, empty Stammbuch bound in Ilsenau blue, "since you never had one." She writes the first page herself, and signs it with a sign rather than a name.
2. **The Elevinnen's experiment.** The current Stiftsjahr's twelve Elevinnen have devised a game: each will try, by the end of the year, to make the son of the house blush in public. They have a book, a schedule and wagers, and the most daring of them has already won twice.
3. **A Tourkind.** A young Velloran woman arrives at the Palais gate with a page from a dead Ilsenau Mondbräutigam's Stammbuch, dated 1675, a watercolor of her mother, and a letter. She isn't asking for money. She's asking to be acknowledged.
4. **The Hofmeister's offer.** A learned, charming, slightly too attentive Hofmeister applies for the post of the protagonist's tutor and governor "for a Kavalierstour at his convenience." He comes with impeccable references from three great houses, and a hidden commission from a fourth.
5. **The tour as exile.** A faction in the Spindelrat proposes sending the protagonist on a two-year Kavalierstour at once, to Vellora, Almagra and the Valleys, to keep him far from Wiedenbrunn until the house has decided what he is. Another faction points out what Vellora's courtesans would make of him, and what that might cost (→ R13).
6. **The Mensur.** A drunk student of the Hohe Schule, a Starkenberg cadet, challenges the protagonist to a student Mensur in a Bäckerstraße tavern, "to see if the Zierherr has blood or milk in him." The tavern is full of witnesses, and half of them are Ilsenau-Elevinnen in disguise.
