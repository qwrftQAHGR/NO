# I13 · Folk Belief: the Breverl, the Kartenmal & Touching Iron

*The small magic of daily life: the charms people wear, the signs they read in birthmarks and spilled metal, the things they do for luck, love and protection, the curses they fear, and what the Church thinks of it all.*

**See also:** R02 The Sky (the Praktik, Schwendtage) · R03 The Work (household tinctures) · R04 The Cards · I16 The Zwölften (Bleigießen) · I18 Eine schöne Leich · I17 Weddings · I54 The Church of the Wedding

---

## 1. Folk belief and the Arts

In a world where the Sky, the Work and the Cards are **real**, folk belief is less superstition than **amateur practice**. Most of it is guesswork about real forces: an old woman's notion of what silver does, a farmer's of what the Moon governs, a mother's of how a card might mark a child. Some of it works a little, because the Arts are real. Most of it works only on the mind, which is not nothing. The Church tolerates most of it, condemns some of it, and practices a good deal of it under other names. The Clear Church condemns all of it, and the Lautere quietly keep their iron nails over the door like everyone else.

---

## 2. Charms

### The Breverl
The realm's commonest amulet. A small folded packet of paper or cloth, sewn shut, worn on a cord round the neck under the shift, close to the skin. Inside, folded tight:
- tiny printed images of saints: **Sankt Severa** for sleep, **Sant Feliu Doble** for the doubly-made, **Saint Afra** for bath-maids, **the Two Marthas** for women who love women (→ I54);
- a pinch of herbs: rue, rosemary, Hochzeitskraut (St. John's wort, gathered at midsummer);
- **filings of a metal** for the wearer's need: silver for babies and mothers (Moon), iron for soldiers and travelers (Mars), copper for lovers (Venus), lead for the sleepless (Saturn), tin for litigants (Jupiter);
- a scrap of the year's **Praktik** with a Liebstag or a lucky day marked;
- and sometimes a lock of hair.

Breverl are made by the **nuns of Brautzell** (→ I31) and sold at the shrine and the fairs for 2–8 Kupferlinge. Opening one is bad luck, and losing one is worse. A **Liebesbreverl** (love-Breverl) contains the hair of one's beloved and is worn until they love back.

### Other charms
- **Silver** for infants: a silver spoon at the Salzung, a silver bell on the cradle, a silver coin sewn into the swaddling.
- **Iron**: a horseshoe over the stable door; an iron nail over the house door, to keep out the Schlacken of the Zwölften (→ I16); an iron ring for soldiers' wives.
- **Copper**: a copper coin in a girl's shoe to draw a lover (*"Kupfer im Schuh, ein Bursch dazu"*, copper in the shoe, a lad to go with it); a copper bracelet against aching joints.
- **Kesrâneraugen** ("Kesrâni eyes"): blue glass beads with an eye painted on, against the evil eye, sold by Kesrâni traders and worn by half of Wiedenbrunn since the Siege.
- **The Pagat**: a playing card of the Pagat, Trump I (luck), carried in a pocket by gamblers, litigants and girls going to the Kirtag (→ R04).

## 3. Signs

### The Kartenmal (card-mark)
**Every birthmark is examined.** Midwives, mothers and grandmothers look at a newborn's skin for a **Muttermal** (a "mother-mark," a birthmark) that looks like a **Blatt**, the mark of a card. The folk belief is that a pregnant woman who stares at a card-bearer, or at a picture of a Trump, or who craves something while thinking of one, **marks her child** with its image. A child with a Kartenmal is believed lucky, or destined to inherit that card.

It is **never true**. No Blatt settles on the unripe, and the Fall-Reckoners have six hundred years of records showing that no child has ever been born marked (→ R04). This does not stop:
- **the Kartenmalbeschau**, a midwife's paid inspection of a newborn for card-marks. The fee is higher if she finds one;
- **mothers** of Kartenmal children who raise them expecting greatness;
- **the scams**: tattooed "Kartenmale" on grown commoners, presented to great houses as proof of a hidden heir. Several are tried every year. One worked in 1652, for three months.

### Other readings
- **Moles**: a mole on the lip means a talker, on the cheek a flirt, on the breast a passionate woman, on the inner thigh "one who gives pleasure." The patch-language comes from this (→ I07).
- **Itching**: the right palm, money coming; the left, money going; the nose, a kiss coming or a fight; the lips, someone is talking about you.
- **A born caul**: luck, and a sailor's charm. Sold to Rocalban sailors for a fortune.
- **Born without a foreskin**: the midwives call it a **Bräutigamszeichen**, a bridegroom's mark, and say such a man *"wird nie eine Braut verweigert"*, will never be refused a bride (→ R12). It is rare, and midwives remember every one they have delivered.
- **Born in a Saturn hour**: a long life, a melancholy one, and a sleep-prone child.
- **A child born with teeth**: a Kalteneck, says the Wiedenbrunn joke.

## 4. Things done for luck

- **Eisen fassen** ("touch iron"): touch something iron, such as a key, a sword-hilt, a nail or a horseshoe, when luck is spoken of, to keep it. Valdau's equivalent of knocking on wood. Soldiers grip their sword-hilts. Ladies touch the iron clasp of their purse.
- **Den Pagat machen**: the thumb over the little finger, for an underdog's luck (→ I01).
- **The Salzwurf** (salt-throw): spilled salt is thrown over the **left shoulder** with the right hand. Salt is the body (→ R03), and spilled salt means a spilled body, an illness, unless thrown away from you.
- **First-footing**: on New Year's morning, the first visitor across the threshold decides the year's luck. A dark-haired man is good. A **futa** is double luck, so a household with a futa sends her out before dawn to come back in (→ R10). A redhead is bad, and a Zeuge is a death in the family.
- **Bleigießen** (lead-pouring) on the last night of the Zwölften, to read the year (→ I16).
- **Mondwasser on the doorstep** on a Brauttag morning, for a fertile year.

## 5. Love and marriage

- **Silber unter der Zunge** (silver under the tongue): a bride keeps a small silver coin under her tongue through the wedding vows, for fertility and so her words will be "silvery" to her husband. She takes it out at the feast and keeps it all her life.
- **The Brautschuh** (bride's shoe): the bride's shoe is passed round the feast and filled with coins (→ I17).
- **Hochzeitskraut** (St. John's wort, gathered at midsummer) laid under the bridal mattress for desire.
- **Rosemary** under a girl's pillow to dream of her husband. If she dreams of a woman instead, the village has a word for that (→ R09).
- **Liebestränke** (love-potions) from the Kräuterweiber. They mostly contain Venusbitter and wishful thinking (→ R03). Real Venusbitter in them is the only thing that works, and it doesn't do what's promised.
- **The Liebeslocke** (love-lock): exchanging locks of hair, sewn into Breverl or kept in lockets.

### The Nestelknüpferin
The realm's most feared folk curse. A **Nestel** is a lace or cord. During a wedding, at the moment of the vows, a jealous woman, a rejected suitor or a hired witch ties a **knot** in a cord, speaking the couple's names, in a **Saturn hour**, and hides the knotted cord. The groom will then be impotent with his bride, *"Saturn im Bett,"* until the knot is found and untied.
- It's believed everywhere, and denounced by the Church as maleficium.
- Women have been pilloried for it, and one was burned in Almagra.
- **Remedies**:
  - the groom urinates through his wedding ring;
  - the couple untie every knot in the house on the wedding night;
  - **Venusbitter**;
  - a Zeuge's blessing of the bed;
  - finding the knot.
- A groom who fails on his wedding night will have a Nestelknüpferin suspected and a search made of every jealous woman's sewing-box. This saves a great many grooms' pride.
- **Nestelknüpferin** is also an insult for a jealous woman.

## 6. Protection and harm

- **The Saturnblick** (Saturn-look): the evil eye. A heavy, envious stare that brings illness, impotence, souring milk, a lame horse. Some people "have it" and don't know. Remedies:
  - Kesrâni eye-beads;
  - a lead amulet ("like protects against like," the Work's principle);
  - **Abbeten**, prayer-healing: an old woman murmurs secret prayers over the afflicted while drawing a cross of salt on the brow.
- **Mondsucht** (moon-madness, sleepwalking): don't sleep with **moonlight on your face**. Village beds are turned away from the window. Ilsenau's Mondnacht is therefore thought dangerous by village women who don't understand Moon-blood (→ R12).
- **Hair and nails**: cuttings are **burned**, never thrown away, so the **Kolbenmänner**, the hedge-alchemists the village fears, can't use them in tinctures against you (→ I52).
- **Quecksilber verschütten** (spilling quicksilver): a secret will spill. Since quicksilver is in every apothecary and every mirror-maker's, there's a lot of it to spill.
- **A broken mirror**: seven years of bad luck, *"sieben Jahre Schlacke"* (seven years of dross), because a mirror is Moon and Mercury and holds a piece of the soul that looks into it. Mirrors are covered in a house where someone has died (→ I18).
- **Thunderstorms**: church bells cast in Jupiter hours are rung against them (→ R03). Hallgau farmers ring their own cowbells, and the noise is famous.
- **Schwendtage** (unlucky days): don't marry, travel, pledge yourself, or let blood (→ R02).

## 7. Sleep and sleepers

- **Touching a sleeper's hand** brings protection and rest. Pilgrims to the **Haus Sankt Severa**, the sleepers' hospice in Wiedenbrunn, pay the nuns to let them touch the hand of one of the long-sleepers (→ I53).
- **Sleepers' dreams wander.** A long-sleeper's soul is believed to walk in other people's dreams, and if you dream of a stranger asleep, someone is sleeping who shouldn't be. After the waking, more than one Weißach villager will claim to have dreamed of a pale young man for years.
- **What is said to a sleeper is heard.** Folk belief, which the Kalteneck physicians half confirm (→ I53): speak no secrets beside a sleeper. The Schlafwärterinnen of the Stille Haus have been told this all their lives, and ignored it, because he was *never going to wake*.

---

## 8. The Church on folk belief

- **Tolerated**: Breverl, which the Church's own nuns make; touching iron; the Salzwurf; first-footing; Bleigießen; the Kartenmalbeschau.
- **Frowned on**: love-potions; Liebesbreverl; Abbeten (a laywoman praying like a Zeuge).
- **Condemned**: the **Nestelknüpferin**; any claim to *see* the future (*Wahrsagerei*, → R02); selling fake Kartenmale; the use of cuttings, hair or blood by anyone but a licensed alchemist.
- **The Clear Church** condemns all of it as "Schlacke-Glaube," dross-faith, and the Lautere keep their iron nails over the door anyway.

## HOOKS

1. **The Bräutigamszeichen.** An old Weißach midwife, now in the Ammenhaus, recognizes the protagonist on the Milchsteg. She delivered him in 1680, and she says so, loudly, to her colleagues, with the midwives' proverb about the mark.
2. **The knot.** At an allied house's wedding the groom fails on the wedding night. The bride's family suspects a Nestelknüpferin, and their suspicions settle on a woman of the Ilsenau household who was seen with a cord at the vows. She was mending a fan.
3. **The Kartenmal scam.** A young woman is presented to the Ilsenau Spindelrat with a crescent-shaped birthmark and a story that she is the hidden heir of the Moon, marked in the womb. The mark is a tattoo. Her claim to have known the Moon-bearer intimately might not be a lie.
4. **The Breverl in the bed.** A Liebesbreverl is found sewn into the protagonist's mattress. It contains a lock of his own hair, taken while he slept, and a scrap of Praktik with a Liebstag marked.
5. **The Saturnblick.** A Starkenberg cousin is convinced the protagonist has the evil eye, because his own horse went lame, his mistress cooled and his Eisenmut stopped working the week the protagonist came to court. He wants an Abbeterin brought in, publicly.
6. **The dreamers.** Three villagers from the Spiegelsee shore come to the Palais gate swearing they dreamed for years of a sleeping young man in a white room, and that in the dreams he spoke to them. One of them can repeat what he said.
