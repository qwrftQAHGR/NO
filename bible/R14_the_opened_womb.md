# R14 · The Opened Womb

*Not yet relevant, and not impossible. The realm's precedents for a man who came to bear, the routes the Arts might offer, what it would cost, and how law, church and house would classify a man who carries a child.*

**See also:** R13 The Quickening · R10 Futas (the Mercurial Course) · R03 The Work · R02 The Sky (the Great Conjunction of 1702) · R04 The Cards · I53 The Bleischlaf & the Sleepers · I58 The Confraria de l'Hereu · I41 Casa Mirabent

---

## 1. Words

Nobody has needed a word often enough to agree on one:

- **Schoßwerdung**, "becoming-womb": the alchemists' word, used in the two treatises that discuss it.
- **die Öffnung**, the opening: the Mondküche's word, and the one the Ilsenau would use.
- **l'Obertura**, the opening: the Confraria de l'Hereu's word, spoken with reverence.
- **die Vollendung des Erben**, the Heir's completion: the theologians' phrase, which treats it as proof of a doctrine.
- In the street, if it ever gets there, it would be a joke: **Mutterherr** (mother-gentleman), **Bräutigamsbraut** (bridegroom's bride).

---

## 2. Precedents

There are three, none of them clean.

### Saint Severa the Awakened (legendary)
The patron saint of sleepers and of those who return. There are two versions of her legend, and the Church has never settled which is canon:
- **Montalba's version**, carved over the door of the Haus Sankt Severa in Wiedenbrunn (→ I53): Severa was a Rocalban girl who fell asleep at twelve, slept seven years, woke grown, without memories, and spent her life nursing sleepers.
- **The Confraria's version**, preserved in a Catalan manuscript in the Llucmar confraternity house: *Severus* was a youth who fell into the lead-sleep, slept seven years, woke grown and changed, took the name Severa, **bore a daughter**, and nursed sleepers thereafter.

The Bride's See calls the Confraria's version a forgery of the 1400s. The Confraria calls the See's version a cover-up of the 1100s. Both versions have a sleeper who woke without memories, and both are told in the same chapel to the same pilgrims. Anyone hearing the protagonist's history will make the connection.

### The Mirabent Bridegroom of 1588 (documented, disputed)
**En Galceran Mirabent i Vilallonga** (1557–1591) was a younger son of Casa Mirabent, assayed at **22 karat of quicksilver**: slight, beardless, beautiful, with the Mirabent trembles in his hands from boyhood.
- In 1586 he married a Moon-blooded heiress of a minor Rocalban Spindelhaus. With her consent and at her family's expense, he began the **Lunar Course** (§3) under the Mirabent laboratory at Cinabre, at the Great Conjunction of 1583's tail, and continued it thirteen moons.
- By 1588 the Llucmar notaries had recorded, with three physicians' signatures, that he had a womb. He bore a daughter in **1589** and a son in **1591**, and died of the trembles six weeks after the second birth, aged thirty-four.
- The daughter became Abbess of a Rocalban Bride-convent. The son became a Llucmar shipowner and the great-grandfather of **Na Elisenda Ferrer**, who now holds the Star (→ I45, I46). This is why the Ferrer are touchy about the story.
- **The dispute**: Valdauer physicians and the Bride's See say the "births" were the wife's, and that Galceran was a femme husband who played the part for reasons of Rocalban inheritance law. The Mirabent and the Confraria say the notaries' record is clear. The three physicians are long dead. The notarial record is in the Llotja archive, and the Mirabent keep a copy under glass (→ I41).

### The Kesrâni case (unverifiable)
A Kesrâni chronicle tells of a **zenne** of the Padishah's household, a male dancer of great beauty, who after "a course of the Sultan's physicians" bore a child in the palace in the 1630s. The child was raised in the Andarun. The chronicle is unclear on whether this was a medical wonder, a court masque, or a story told to flatter the physicians (→ I34).

### And from the other side of the world
The Asagiri envoy, asked over dinner, reported that in his country's oldest scripture **a male god bore children alone**, from his eyes and nose while purifying himself after visiting the dead. He meant it as conversation, but the Confraria's correspondent in Wiedenbrunn wrote it down (→ I37).

## 3. Routes

None is certain, and all are dangerous.

### The Lunar Course (*der Mondgang*)
The alchemical route, the one Galceran Mirabent took:
- **What**: a regimen of **silver tinctures** (Moon, to open the body to bearing) and **quicksilver** (Mercury, to make the body "willing to change"), taken over **thirteen moons**. Each dose falls in its planet's hour, with silver salves worked into the belly and hips by a Moon-blooded attendant every night.
- **When**: it must be **begun under a Great Conjunction**, when every body's nature is most open (→ R02). The next is **1702**, and after that 1722.
- **Who it can work on**: a body with **"room to change."** The treatises name three kinds: high Mercury fineness (Galceran had it), Moon fineness, and bodies the Saturn-sleep has left **"unfinished."** The last is the Kalteneck physicians' phrase for sleepers who woke after years: their bodies grew while asleep and were never fully "set" by waking life.
- **What happens over thirteen moons**: the hips widen and soften, the breasts swell a little, the voice may rise, and the belly changes inwardly. Somewhere between the eighth and twelfth moon a womb forms, with an opening behind the stones. Whatever the man was before remains as it was.
- **Risks**: the trembles, madness and death. The Mirabent treatise reckons one in three dead or ruined, with the survivors' outcomes varying.
- **Cost**: about **1,200 Greif** in materials alone, plus a Moon-blooded attendant every night for a year. The Ilsenau would be the obvious attendants, and so would the obvious opponents.
- **Who could do it**: the Mondküche (the silver), the Mirabent laboratory (the quicksilver and the only surviving method), and possibly the Seventh Faculty. All three working together would teach each of them far more about the other two than any of them would like.

### The Cards
- **III Die Kaiserin**, the Empress, rules fertility and wombs. Her touch "opens" bodies to conceiving, and might open one to bearing. She has been **lost since 1693** (→ I46).
- **XVIII Der Mond**, the Moon: the bearer walks in dreams and shapes glamours by night. A Moon-bearer working through a Lunar Course with the patient, night after night, in his dreams and in his body, is the Mondküche's private hope (→ R12).
- **XIV Die Mäßigkeit**, Temperance: the bearer can blend what is in a body by touch. It might ease the Course's dangers, the trembles and the madness, which are the parts that kill. Its bearer is the Asagiri envoy (→ I37).

### By itself
The Confraria's theory of **the Heir's Haste** (→ R13 §8) predicts that a man whose children hasten is half-Heir already, and will become whole "in the fullness of the Feast." On that view no Course is needed, and the opening will come by itself, probably under the 1702 Conjunction. Nobody outside the Confraria believes this, and the Confraria believes it very much.

## 4. What it would mean

- **In law**: Valdauer law defines a **mother** as the one who bears. A man who bore would be, in law, the **mother** of his child.
  - In a **Spindelhaus**, inheritance runs through mothers (→ R05 §6). A son of Ilsenau who *bore* a daughter would be, by the letter of the Ilsenbrief, a mother of the house, and his daughter an heir through him.
  - No court has ever been asked whether this is so. Every lawyer of the Hofkammer would enjoy the case enormously. Every woman of the Spindelrat would have a view.
- **In the Church**: the faith's third person, the Heir, is of both natures, and futas are *erbgestaltig*, heir-formed (→ R10). A man who could bear would be heir-formed too.
  - The Bride's See would have to decide whether he could receive the **Salzung of mothers**, the churching after childbed (→ I65).
  - The Confraria would declare him a living sign.
  - The Clear Church would declare him the end of the world.
- **In the blood**: a child he *bore* would carry his blood through the womb, Ilsenau silver from the mother's side, so to speak, for the first time ever. In a house that inherits through mothers, that is not a small thing.
- **In the Cards**: bearing creates a blood tie like any other (parent and child, 3). It changes nothing else.
- **With the Quickening**: unknown. Whether a pregnancy *he* carried would be quick, whether by his own seed (no one knows if that is even possible) or by another's, is a question no theory answers. The Mondküche's Moon's Overflow theory says yes. Kalteneck's Stored Years theory says yes, and fast. The Stundenamt says ask in 1702.

---

## 5. The other side of desire

Something the bible should name, since the brief does. A man who has spent his waking life being the one whose seed quickens would, on this road, become the one who is quickened. Courtiers' jokes about a femme man (the Fräuleinherr, the Zierherr) would stop being jokes. The women of the house who have watched him take would watch him be taken. Whatever he had been to them, and they to him, would turn over. In a world that reads bodies as roles, that would be a scandal, an abomination, an enormous erotic novelty, and a political event all at once.

## HOOKS

1. **The glass case.** On a visit to Llucmar, the Mirabent show the protagonist the 1588 notarial record under its glass, with exaggerated courtesy, and let it be known that they are the only house that still has the method.
2. **The Kalteneck phrase.** A Kalteneck physician's routine report on the woken sleeper uses the phrase *"room to change"*. It reaches the Mondküche's alchemist, who reads it three times and asks for an audience with the Hausmutter.
3. **Severa's chapel.** At the Haus Sankt Severa, a Confraria pilgrim stops the protagonist in front of the saint's statue and tells him the *other* version of her story, as if he ought to recognize it.
4. **1702 bookings.** The Stundenamt's waiting list for 1702 Conjunction hours includes an entry, booked through an intermediary, for "the beginning of a Course" in a Moon hour. The intermediary is the Mirabent house-alchemist, and the booking is in the name of House Ilsenau.
5. **The Ilsenbrief.** A Hofkammer lawyer with too much time on his hands writes a learned anonymous pamphlet on whether, under the Ilsenbrief of 1229, "a son of the house who bears" would be a mother of the house. It is meant as a joke. It sells out in two days, and the Spindelrat reads it.
