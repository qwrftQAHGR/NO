# I36 · The Sworn Valleys

*The Swiss-flavored confederacy beyond the northern wall: valleys that swore an oath together, burned their lords' towers and have had no nobility since; assemblies of armed men voting with raised hands in a meadow; pikemen hired to every crown; the birthplace of the Clear Church; the rule that mountain air makes a slave free; night-courting at the window; and the summer alps where the young women live alone with the herds.*

**See also:** R01 · I74 The Brides' War & the Clear Church · R07 Bondage (the Bergläufer) · I22 (the Schwurgarde at the Greifenburg) · I46 (XI, Strength; the Sküs) · R08 (the Kiltgang) · R09 (the Sennerinnen) · I21 (Schwarze Vreni)

## 1. The land

**Die Schwurtäler** (the Sworn Valleys; formally **die Schwurgenossenschaft der Täler**, the Sworn Confederacy of the Valleys) lie **north of Valdau, beyond the Firnwand**, the great glaciered wall of the north. The passes over it are snowbound for half the year (→ R01, I21). It is a country of:
- high **valleys** with green floors and steep forests;
- **glaciers** and white peaks;
- **lakes** of blue-green water;
- wooden farmhouses with stones on their roofs, villages around white churches with no images in them, and towns of guild-halls and fountains;
- and above everything the **alps**, the high summer pastures where the herds go up in June.

**Thirteen cantons** (*Orte*): **Glarach**, **Urswil**, **Schwendi**, **Ried**, **Seelis**, **Tannegg** and seven more, each fiercely independent of the others. The largest town is **Seestadt am Ried**, a lake-city of banks, printers, guilds and the Clear Church's great college. It has about 15,000 people.

---

## 2. History: the Oath and the Towers

- The valleys were once Greifenstein lands, ruled by the royal house's **bailiffs** (*Vögte*) from stone towers.
- **1307**: **the Oath at the Ried Meadow**. Men of three valleys met at night in a meadow above the lake and **swore** to defend each other against the bailiffs: *"ein einzig Volk von Brüdern"*, one people of brothers. The oath is renewed every year on its anniversary, and every Valleys child learns its words.
- **1386**: **the Night of the Burning Towers**. The sworn valleys rose, **burned every bailiff's tower** in one night, and destroyed the Greifenstein army sent against them at **the Battle of Ried**, where pikemen behind a wall of pikes broke the Valdauer knights in a narrow meadow between lake and mountain. The Greifenstein have never retaken the valleys. **The nobility was abolished**: lords were killed, exiled, or made to swear the Oath as ordinary men, and the use of a noble title in the Valleys has been a crime ever since.
- The confederacy grew from three valleys to thirteen.
- **1619**: **Ulrich Lauter**, a preacher of Glarach, posted his **Twelve Complaints of Glarach** against the sale of Nahbriefe, and **the Clear Church** was born (→ I74).
- **1619–1641**: the **Brides' War**. Most of the Valleys went Clear. The Valleys fought Valdau again, and the Peace of Rebstein confirmed their freedom and their faith.

---

## 3. Government

- **No nobility, no king, no prince.** Officials are elected, pay is small, and terms are short.
- **The Landsgemeinde** (the land-assembly): in most cantons, **every armed man** of the canton gathers once a year, in spring, **in an open meadow**, wearing his **sword or sidearm** as proof of his right to vote. They elect the canton's officers, make laws and declare war **by a show of raised hands**. A Landsgemeinde is thousands of men in black coats in a ring in a meadow, swords at their sides, the **Landammann** (canton head) on a platform, and the hands going up. Women may not vote. Clear Church women are working on it.
- **The Tag** (the Day; formally *die Tagsatzung*): the confederacy's diet, where **delegates of the thirteen cantons** meet once a year at Ried to decide war, peace, treaties and disputes. It decides by agreement or not at all, and it is famous for not at all.
- **The cities** have councils of guildmasters and an informal patriciate (**Junker**), which everyone pretends isn't a nobility.

---

## 4. The Reisläufer (mercenaries)

The Valleys' great export is **soldiers**. Valleys **pikemen** are the best infantry in the known world: disciplined, steady, terrifying in their **Gewalthaufen** (the great square of pikes), never routing, never taking prisoners unless paid to. Valleys men go abroad as **Reisläufer** (journey-runners) in regiments hired by contract (*Kapitulation*) to every crown that pays: Valdau, Almagra, Vellora, Ormánd, and Kesrân once, which caused a scandal. Valleys regiments have fought each other on opposite sides of foreign battles, and shaken hands afterward.

- **The Schwurgarde**: a company of Valleys pikemen hired as the **royal guard** of the Greifenburg in Wiedenbrunn, at the **Schwurhof** gate (→ I22). It is kept because a Clear Church soldier can't be bought with Nahbrief favors or Rosengrund women. The court believes this, inaccurately.
- **Hauptmann Jost Ab-Yberg**, captain of the Schwurgarde's parent regiment and the Valleys' most famous soldier, **bears Trump XI, Die Kraft** (Strength, → I46).
  - **Gift**: enormous strength, and the power to **calm rage**, in beasts or in men, by touch. He can stop a brawl by laying a hand on a shoulder, and a bolting horse by gripping its bridle.
  - **Burden**: if he uses his strength **in anger**, he loses it for a day. So the Valleys' greatest soldier must **never fight angry**. He is the calmest man in Wiedenbrunn, slow-spoken and kind-eyed, which frightens people far more than rage would.
- **Pay** comes home to the valleys and builds the churches, the banks and the cheese-cellars. Every Valleys family has a son abroad and a widow at home.

---

## 5. Faith: the Clear Church at home

*(The Clear Church in general is in I74.)*

The Valleys are the **heartland of the Clear Church** (*die Lautere Kirche*). Its churches:
- are **plain**: white walls, clear windows, a pulpit in the center, no images of the Bridegroom and Bride, no statues, no candles, no incense;
- hold **sermons** (two hours long) and **psalms** sung by the whole congregation in unison;
- give the **Mahl** as a plain meal at a table;
- have **preachers** (*Prediger*), not Zeugen, who marry, preach and are elected by their congregations.

What the Lautere believe:
- The Bridegroom and Bride are an **allegory** of the soul's union with God, not a sibling marriage to be imitated. **Nearness is abomination**, and the sale of Nahbriefe is **simony** (→ R06, I55).
- **Card-games**, **dancing**, the **Venusbitter**, **alchemy except honest medicine**, **astrology except the farmer's almanac** (which they keep behind the Bible anyway), the **Kleiderordnung's lace**, and **bondage** are condemned.
- Everyone should read the scriptures in their own tongue.
- The Valleys' **Consistories** (church courts of elders) police morals: fines for dancing, swearing, drunkenness and fornication; public penance in church; the stocks.

## 6. Bergluft macht frei (mountain air makes free)

By the **Freiheitsbrief of 1388**, made after the Towers, **any bound person who sets foot in the Valleys is free.** *"Bergluft macht frei"*: mountain air makes free.
- **The Bergläufer** (mountain-runners): pledges, slaves and captives who flee north over the Firnwand passes to the Valleys. There is an informal **route** of Clear Church farmhouses, inns and preachers who hide runaways, feed them and pass them north. Valdauer Seelenmakler post bounties and hire catchers. Valleys farmers set dogs on them.
- **The Freihöfe** (free-yards): refuges in the border villages where runaways wait to be taken in.
- **Extradition**: Valdau demands its runaways back, and the Valleys refuse. A **Pfandamt** clerk with a Valdauer registry number stands no chance against a Landsgemeinde. The dispute has been on the Tag's agenda for a century, beside the Kesrâni question and the cheese tariffs.
- **Eloping lovers** use the same route.

---

## 7. Customs and desire

- **Prudes in the towns**: Seestadt's Lautere Bürger are the most respectable people in the known world. Plain clothes, early bedtimes, Sunday sermons, the Consistory watching.
- **And in the mountains**:
  - **The Kiltgang** (night-courting): on Saturday nights the young men of a village go out in a band, singing, to the farmhouses where unmarried young women live, and call under the windows. A woman who wants a particular one lets him in, to talk, by custom, at her bedside, or more if she likes. This is the Valleys' **Fensterln** (→ R08), and the Consistories have fined it for eighty years without the least effect.
  - **The Probenacht** (trial night): betrothed couples sleep together, as in the Hallgau.
  - **The Sennerinnen** (the dairymaids of the alp): in June the herds go up to the **Alp**, the high summer pastures, and with them the **Sennerinnen**, young women who live alone in the alp-huts all summer, milking, making cheese, singing to each other across the valleys with the **yodel**, and receiving visitors. *"Auf der Alp gibt's kei Sünd'"* ("on the alp there's no sin"), say the Valleys, and the Consistories can't climb that high. The Sennerinnen's summers are proverbial in two directions: for the young men who climb up on Sundays, and for the Sennerinnen who share a hut (→ R09).
  - **The Alpabfahrt** (the coming-down): in September the herds come down from the alp decked with flowers, ribbons and great bells, led by the best cow wearing a crown and the Sennerinnen in their best. The village celebrates. By old joke the village counts **the cows and the waists**, and then holds the summer's weddings.
- **Kinship**: strictly taboo, with Valleys jokes about remote valleys "*where everyone is his own cousin and his own uncle*" (→ R06).
- **Men**: mercenary comradeship, of which nothing is said; Consistory fines if anything is said.
- **The yodel**, the **alphorn**, **Schwingen** (the Valleys' wrestling on sawdust), **crossbow-shooting**, and cheese.

---

## 8. Trade, names and stereotypes

- **Exports**:
  - **soldiers**, above all;
  - **cheese**: great wheels of **Bergkäse**, aged in cellars;
  - **cattle**;
  - **rock crystal**, cut from mountain clefts by the daring **Strahler** (crystal-hunters);
  - **clocks** and watch-mechanisms from Seestadt's workshops;
  - **Clear Church books**, smuggled into Valdau (→ I12);
  - and **banking**: the **Ried banks** keep secret accounts for foreign great houses and ask no questions. Every great house of Valdau has money in a Valleys vault, against the day.
- **Names**: Alemannic. Given names with fond diminutives in *-li*: *Ueli*, *Heiri*, *Ruedi*, *Vreni*, *Babeli*, *Anneli*. Surnames: *Ab-Yberg*, *Zumstein*, *Gisler*, *Imhof*, *Zgraggen*, *Hürlimann*. **No "von"**, because there's no nobility. Address: *"Grüess Ech"* and a **handshake**, as equals (→ I01).
- **What others say of the Valleys**: stubborn, sober, dour, mercenary, honest, unbribable (mostly), humorless, brave. Valdauers call them **Kuhküsser** (cow-kissers), and Valleys men break noses for it.
- **What the Valleys say of others**: Valdauers are a sink of lace and incest that keeps slaves and calls it service. Almagrans are proud beggars. Velloran are cheats. And the Valleys will sell all of them soldiers and cheese.

---

## 9. Relations with the Double Crown

- **Old enemies** (1386, the Brides' War) and **now partners**. Valdau hires Valleys regiments, buys Valleys cheese and deposits its gold in Valleys vaults. The Valleys buy Valdauer salt and iron.
- **The Schwurgarde** guards the Greifenburg.
- **The Bergläufer** dispute (§6).
- **The Clear Church** in Valdau, the **Lautere** minority, looks to Glarach and Seestadt. Valdau suspects the Valleys of funding the Lautere preachers in the Hallgau, and of funding the Ormándi Szabadok (→ I24, I33).
- **Schwarze Vreni**, the highwaywoman who bears the **Sküs** and robs Valdauer coaches, is a Valleys woman. The Valleys don't extradite her either (→ I21, I46).

## HOOKS

1. **The Landsgemeinde.** On a mission to the Valleys, the protagonist attends a Landsgemeinde in a spring meadow: three thousand armed men in black, hands going up and down. Under Valleys law any man present may speak. A bound runaway from the Ilsenau household is in the crowd, and stands up to ask the meeting for protection from the Ilsenau delegation.
2. **The calm captain.** Hauptmann Jost Ab-Yberg, bearer of Strength, has noticed the protagonist at the Greifenburg gate every day. He finally speaks, slowly: he would like to know why, when the young Prinz passes, his own famous calm is harder to keep.
3. **Kiltgang.** In a Valleys village a band of young men singing under a farmhouse window on a Saturday night mistake the protagonist, a guest in the house, for the farmer's daughter at the window. When they see their mistake, they make him an offer.
4. **The Alp.** Two Sennerinnen who share a hut on a high alp shelter the protagonist in a summer storm. They have been alone with the cows for ten weeks, and have long since stopped caring what the Consistory thinks.
5. **The Ried vault.** House Ilsenau keeps gold in a Ried bank against the day. The bank writes, with Valleys courtesy, that someone has presented an Ilsenau seal and a request to withdraw it all, and asks whether the house would like to confirm.
6. **Schwarze Vreni's rest.** The Sküs-bearing highwaywoman, who can't sleep two nights under one roof, turns up at the Ilsenau party's Valleys inn on her one night there. She has heard something about the son of the Moon-house, and wants to know if it's true, for reasons of her own.
