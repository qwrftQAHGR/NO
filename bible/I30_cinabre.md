# I30 · Cinabre

*The quicksilver mines: a red mountain range, a red river, furnaces that sweat liquid silver from red stone, the iron flasks that carry it to every mirror-maker, alchemist and army in the known world, the men who dig it and tremble for the rest of their short lives, the revolt they made, the living mirror in the deepest gallery, and the three powers who each say the lease is theirs.*

**See also:** I29 Rocalba & Llucmar · I41 Casa Mirabent · R03 The Work (Merkurwasser, the trembles) · R07 Bondage (Strafknechte) · I66 The Lagoon-Pox & the Quicksilver Cure · R10 (the Mercurial Course) · I26 (the amalgam offer)

---

## 1. The land

Forty miles inland from Llucmar, the **Serra del Cinabre** rises: low, bare, rust-red mountains of crumbling stone, scrub and dust, where nothing grows well and the summer heat is a furnace. The red comes from **cinnabar**, the ore of **quicksilver**, a vermilion stone that stains everything it touches. The river **Vermell** ("red") drains these mountains and **runs pink after every rain** all the way to the sea at Llucmar (→ R01).

In the heart of the Serra stands the mine-town of **Cinabre**, which Rocalbans call **la Vila Vermella**, the red town. Everything is red there: its walls, its dust, its dogs and the creases of its people's hands. Above it on a crag stands **el Castell de Cinabre**, the castle of **Casa Mirabent**, Mercury house and lords of the mines (→ I41).

---

## 2. The quicksilver

- **The ore**: cinnabar is cut from galleries in the mountain called **la Mina Major** (the great mine) and a dozen lesser mines. The veins are rich, among the richest quicksilver mines anywhere. They have been mined since 1512, when a Mirabent prospector found the great vein, "**la Vena de la Núvia**" (the Bride's vein).
- **The furnaces**: the ore is roasted in long **aludel-furnaces**, brick ovens linked to chains of clay pipes. The heat drives the quicksilver out of the stone as vapor, and it condenses in the cooling pipes and runs out as **liquid silver**. Hundreds of furnaces smoke day and night on the hillsides below the town. At night the Serra glows red, and the fumes are poison.
- **The flascons**: quicksilver is shipped in **iron flasks**, wrought-iron bottles with screw stoppers. Each holds a weight of quicksilver that takes two strong men to carry, and is stamped with the **Mirabent drop** and the year. The flascons go by mule down the valley to Llucmar, and by ship to the world.
- **What it's for**:
  - **mirrors**: quicksilver amalgam on glass, mostly for **Vellora**, which takes half the output (→ I35);
  - **silver**: dissolving silver from poor ore by **amalgamation**, for **Almagra**'s overseas silver mines and potentially the **Weißach** (→ I26);
  - **alchemy**: Merkurwasser, Quecksilbersalbe and the **Mercurial Course** (→ R03, R10);
  - **medicine**: the **cure for the Lagoon-Pox**, a fortune in itself (→ I66);
  - **gilding** of altarpieces, frames and buttons, by fire-gilding with gold amalgam;
  - **felt-hats**, cured with quicksilver, which is why hatters go mad;
  - **cosmetics**: **Merkurpaste** (→ I07);
  - **war**: fulminates for the Hofartillerie's newest priming-powder, and the Mirabent couriers' Merkurwasser (→ I41).
- **It is the realm's most strategic export**, after cannon. Whoever controls Cinabre controls the world's mirrors, much of its silver, its pox-cure and a share of its alchemy.

## 3. The workers

- **Free miners** (*miners lliures*): Rocalban, Ormándi, Grenzer, and runaways from everywhere. They are paid **three times** a Valdauer miner's wage, because nobody would come for less. They work **three days a week** by the **Regla dels Tres Dies** (Rule of Three Days) of 1620, to limit their exposure, and spend the rest drunk in Cinabre's taverns. They die young, rich and shaking.
- **Forçats** (convicts): **Strafknechte** (→ R07), sentenced by Valdauer and Rocalban courts to labor in the mines for terms or for life. They include thieves, smugglers, deserters, forgers, men convicted under the Widernatur statute, and rebels. They live in **la Galera de Terra** ("the land-galley"), a walled barracks below the furnaces, chained at night, and work the worst galleys and the furnace-pipes **six days a week**. The Regla dels Tres Dies was extended to them on paper after 1671. On paper.
- **Captives**: Kesrâni and corsair prisoners bought at the Llotja dels Captius (→ I29) and put to work beside the forçats. They have their own **Lamp-house** in the Galera, and their own prayers.
- **Women**: miners' wives and daughters sort ore in the open sheds, cook, wash and work the taverns. **Les Cinabreres**, the Cinabre women, are famously tough, with red-stained hands and sharp tongues.

### The tremolosos (the tremblers)
Quicksilver poisons, slowly and then quickly (→ R03):
- the hands **shake** (*tremolor*), then the head and the whole body;
- teeth loosen and fall; gums blacken;
- moods swing wildly from shyness to rage;
- memory fails; the mind wanders; madness comes at the end.

A miner who has worked ten years is a **tremolós**, a trembler. A forçat who has worked five often is. In Valdau they are called **Zittermänner**. The town is full of them, shaking in doorways, cared for by their wives, or not. **Mercury-blooded** people (the Mirabent above all) resist the poison far better, which is one reason Mirabent can stand to live above the furnaces (→ I41).

- **L'Hospital de les Mans** (the Hospital of the Hands, named for the trembling hands): founded by the **Confraria de l'Hereu** in 1588 and funded since by Mirabent (→ I58). It cares for the tremolosos with milk (believed to draw out the poison), sweat-baths, prayers and laudanum, and buries them.
- **Santa Clara of the Flask**: the miners' patron saint. A legendary woman of Cinabre, she carried flascons on her back for forty years, never trembled, and at her death her body was found to weigh as much as quicksilver. Her statue in the miners' church holds a flascó, and the miners touch it going down.

## 4. Customs

- **The red hands**: Cinabre's people have red-stained skin in the creases of their hands that never washes out. *"Mans vermelles"* (red hands) is what Rocalbans call a Cinabre native, and a Cinabre native is proud of them.
- **"Tremola!"** ("tremble!"): the miners' farewell, a black joke.
- **La Festa de la Gota** (the Feast of the Drop): each spring, when the **first quicksilver of the year** runs from the furnaces, the whole town gathers at the great furnace. A drop is poured into the palm of the year's **Reina de la Gota**, the queen of the drop, a miner's daughter chosen by lot. She holds it up to the sun, shining, and the town cheers and gets drunk. A drop in the palm is supposed to bring a year of luck. A drop swallowed is supposed to bring something else, and young men dare each other.
- **Quicksilver games**: children (and drunk miners) roll beads of quicksilver across tavern tables, race them, and split and join them, which is both a fortune-game and slow poison. *"Jugar a la gota"* (to play the drop) means to gamble with your health.
- **Gilded coins**: miners rub gold coins with quicksilver so they look silver, and pay with them in Llucmar to people who don't know. The trick is very old.

## 5. El Mirall Viu (the Living Mirror)

In the deepest gallery of the Mina Major, the **Gallery of the Bride**, the miners in **1559** broke into a natural cave where **quicksilver had pooled** over ages: a **lake of liquid mercury** perhaps ten paces across, perfectly still, reflecting the lamplight. It is **el Mirall Viu**, the Living Mirror.
- The miners say it shows your face **more truly than any glass**: older, younger, the face you will have or had. Mirabent alchemists say this is a trick of the fumes on the mind. The miners say Mirabent alchemists have never looked.
- **Casa Mirabent uses it for ceremonies.** By house custom, Mirabent heirs are presented to it at eighteen. Mirabent **marriages** are sealed there, the couple looking into the Mirror together. The **Twin Bride of 1661**, the Mirabent heiress who married her twin brother, did it here, before a Zeuge the family paid, in front of the Living Mirror (→ I41).
- A stone dropped into it doesn't sink, it floats. A person can lie on it, as on a bed, and some have. The fumes would kill anyone who stayed long.
- It is the Mirabent's most guarded place, kept by a hereditary warden family, and visitors are rarely admitted.

---

## 6. The Trembling Revolt of 1671

In the drought summer of **1671**, after a furnace explosion killed thirty men and the Galera's ration was cut, the **forçats rose**, then the free miners joined them.
- They seized the furnaces and the Mina Major.
- They took a Mirabent steward hostage.
- They **threatened to flood the mine** by breaking the drainage, which would have closed Cinabre for a decade and stopped the world's mirrors.

For six weeks Cinabre was ruled by a council of miners and convicts under a tremolós named **Pau Roig**, who could no longer hold a cup but could still give a speech.

- **Mirabent negotiated**, since nobody else could: the Regla dels Tres Dies for forçats, better rations, the Hospital's funds doubled, and no reprisals.
- The miners opened the furnaces.
- In the spring the Lloctinent's troops came and **hanged Pau Roig and eleven others** on the Castell's walls. Mirabent said it had kept its word and that the Lloctinent had not given his.
- The miners still sing **the Ballad of Pau Roig**, and the Mirabent still don't hear it.

## 7. The lease (three claimants)

Who owns Cinabre? Three parties say they do:
1. **The Crown**, by its **regalia**, the royal right to all mines in Valdau. It claims this extends to Rocalba under the Capitulations of Montalba of 1291, which are ambiguous on the point (→ R01).
2. **The Corts**, by the **Constitucions**, which say Rocalban soil belongs to the Principat. The Quatre Pals claim the right to lease the mines and collect the duty.
3. **Casa Mirabent**, which **found the vein**, has **worked the mines since 1512**, built the furnaces, owns the castle and the town, and holds **the Arrendament del Cinabre** (the Cinabre lease), granted jointly by Crown and Corts and renewed every twenty years. That joint grant is the only compromise anyone has ever achieved.

**The lease comes up for renewal in 1702**, the year of the **Great Conjunction** (→ R02). The Crown wants to take the mines into direct royal management, the Hofkammer having worked out what Mirabent earns. The Corts want a larger share. Mirabent wants the lease for ever. **Almagra** wants Cinabre for its silver mines and has been courting Mirabent and the Rocalbistes (→ I32). **Vellora**'s Banco finances the furnaces and would like to keep doing so (→ I35). Everyone expects 1702 to be a fight.

---

## 8. The black market: la gota negra

**Smuggled quicksilver** (*la gota negra*, the black drop) leaves Cinabre in water-skins with false bottoms, in hollow walking-sticks, in miners' bellies (swallowed in balls of wax, with obvious risk) and in the luggage of Velloran mirror-makers' agents. It is sold without the Corts' duty to Winkelalchimisten in Wiedenbrunn (→ I52), mirror-makers in Vellora, and anyone making the Mercurial Course on the cheap. The Corts' customs officers at the Vermell bridges are bribed or shaken by a tremolós's handshake.

---

## 9. Local custom, tension and a place worth visiting

- **Custom**: red hands; "Tremola!"; the Festa de la Gota; Santa Clara of the Flask; playing the drop.
- **Tension**: the 1702 lease; the forçats' Galera; the Hospital's funds; Almagra's interest; the smugglers.
- **Worth a visit**:
  - **the furnaces of the Serra at night**, glowing red;
  - **the Festa de la Gota**, to see the queen hold up the drop;
  - **the Living Mirror**, if a Mirabent will take you, which says something about you.

## HOOKS

1. **The Living Mirror.** A Mirabent invites the protagonist to Cinabre and, unusually, down to the Mirall Viu. The invitation is phrased as courtesy. In the house's custom it is how a Mirabent presents someone they intend to marry, or to bind.
2. **La Reina de la Gota.** At the Festa de la Gota, the queen of the drop, laughing, pours the year's first quicksilver from her palm into the protagonist's. She dares him to do with it what the young men do.
3. **The tremolós's letter.** A tremolós at the Hospital de les Mans, who served as a footman in the Ilsenau Torre de les Dides before he came to the mines, dictates a letter to the Confraria's nurses for the protagonist. His memory is failing. He wants to tell what he remembers of the Torre in 1680 while he still can, but he can no longer tell what is memory and what is quicksilver.
4. **The 1702 lease.** The Quatre Pals, the Crown's Hofkammer and Casa Mirabent each approach the Ilsenau, the Moon Chair, for its support in the 1702 renewal. Each offers something only one of them can deliver.
5. **The Galera.** Among the forçats of the Galera de Terra is a man convicted under the Widernatur statute in Wiedenbrunn, the former music-master of a great house. He sends word, through the Confraria, that he will trade a secret of that house for a transfer to the Hospital.
6. **La gota negra.** A Velloran mirror-maker's agent, caught smuggling quicksilver at the Vermell bridge, claims protection as an agent of House Ilsenau, and produces a letter on blue paper with a silver crescent seal. The letter is either forged or very embarrassing.
