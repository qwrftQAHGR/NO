# I06 · The Kleiderordnung & Tracht

*What people wear in the Double Crown and who is allowed to wear it: the sumptuary law of 1686 and the inspectors who enforce it, the court fashions of 1699 (men's and women's, outer and under), the regional dress of the provinces and the codes sewn into it, colors and what they mean, and the jewelry that tells you who someone belongs to.*

## 1. The Kleiderordnung of 1686

**Die Kleider- und Standesordnung von 1686** was issued by King Leopold II after a decade of complaints that Bürger wives were dressing like countesses and countesses like Velloran courtesans. It is the fourth such ordinance since 1552, and the most detailed. It sorts the realm into **five Stände** (estates of dress), each with the fabrics, furs, laces, metals and colors it may wear in public.

| Stand | Who | May wear |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | the royal family; the Seven Chairs; foreign princes | anything: cloth of gold and silver, **ermine** and **sable**, unlimited lace; **red heels** (*Greifenabsatz*) if they have Kammerzutritt; the **planetary embroidery** (metal-thread patterns of a house's planet) reserved to the Seven Chairs |
| **II** | other titled nobility; ambassadors; bishops; Hofräte of noble birth | silk, velvet, brocade; gold or silver lace up to **two fingers** wide; marten and fox, not ermine or sable; red heels with Kammerzutritt only |
| **III** | untitled nobility; Briefadel; doctors; Hofräte; great merchants; ciutadans honrats in Valdau | silk and velvet in **dark colors only**; silver lace one finger wide, no gold; fur trim of fox or lamb; no red heels |
| **IV** | Bürger masters, shopkeepers, master craftsmen and their wives | wool and linen; silk **ribbons** only, no silk garments; lace collar no wider than a hand; no metal lace; rabbit fur |
| **V** | servants, journeymen, peasants, pledges, laborers | wool and linen; no silk, no lace, no fur but sheepskin; shoes of plain leather |

**Exemptions:**
- **Livery** is the house's dress, not the servant's, so a liveried footman of a Stand-I house may wear silver lace his Bürger cousin would be fined for (→ I04). Nothing in Valdau is resented more by the Bürger.
- **Fasching** and the Zwölften suspend the Ordnung entirely. This is **Narrenfreiheit**, fools' freedom, when the masks come out (→ I70, I16).
- **Brides** may dress one Stand higher on their wedding day.
- **The stage**: actors may wear anything in performance and nothing outside it.

**Special provisions:**
- **Rosenbrief courtesans** (the licensed women of the Rosengrund, → I23) must wear a **Rosenband**, a rose-red silk ribbon, on the left sleeve in public. In 1690 the fashionable ladies of the court began wearing rose ribbons on their sleeves in solidarity, or in fun, or for reasons of their own. The **Rosenband fashion** lasted three seasons, and nobody could tell who was licensed and who was being witty. The Kleidervisitatoren gave up.
- **Pledges** wear the **Pfandband** at the throat in the house's colors, and only pledges may (→ R07).
- **Kesrâni captives** may not wear turbans in public. Freed Kesrâni may, and do, defiantly.
- **The Lautere** (Clear Church) choose plain grey and black, by conviction, and are often fined for wearing less than their Stand permits. That is technically also an offense, and makes them furious.
- **Men in women's garments outside Fasching** are fined. Women in men's garments are fined only if they don't look good in them; the Hofoper's Hosenrollen actresses have never been fined (→ R11, I68).
- **Mourning** suspends the Ordnung's colors (→ I18).

### The Kleidervisitatoren
The **dress-inspectors**, eight officers of the Rumorwache with a book of the Ordnung. They walk the Graben, the Spiegelmarkt and the church doors on Sundays and fine those who dress above their Stand, from **one to ten Greif**. They are bribed more than any officers in the city. A Bürger wife can wear silk on the Graben for a Bräutling a month paid to the right Visitator. They were the Chastity Commission's most enthusiastic allies, and became its most effective enemies when the fines stopped going into their own pockets (→ I77, I20).

## 2. Court fashion, 1699

### Men
- **The Leibrock**: the knee-length coat, cut close to the body and flaring at the skirts, with huge turned-back cuffs and buttons down the front, most of them never fastened. In velvet, brocade or fine wool, in the colors the Stand permits.
- **The Weste**, waistcoat: nearly as long as the coat, embroidered, and the place where a gentleman shows his taste.
- **Kniehose**: knee-breeches, buckled below the knee.
- **Silk stockings**, white or colored, rolled over the knee, and **the calves** in them. A gentleman's calves are his beauty, and some pad them.
- **Shoes**: square-toed, buckled; **red heels** for those with Kammerzutritt.
- **The Halsbinde** (neck-cloth): a long scarf of fine linen or lace tied loosely at the throat, the ends tucked into a buttonhole. It came from the **Grenzer**, whose frontier soldiers wore knotted neck-cloths. Wiedenbrunn's young officers copied it in the 1660s, and it is still called the **Grenzerbinde** by everyone who isn't a tailor.
- **The Allongeperücke**: the great full-bottomed wig, curls falling past the shoulders, for the formal and the old. Since the 1680s young men and **Zierherren** wear **Eigenhaar**, their own hair, long and loose or tied, unpowdered or lightly powdered (→ R11). Powder is the new fashion of the 1690s, and it is spreading.
- **The Degen**, the small-sword, worn by every man with Degenrecht (→ R05).
- **The hat**: the three-cornered hat, carried under the arm indoors.
- **At home**: the **Schlafrock** (the dressing-gown of Kesrâni silk, worn all morning over shirt and breeches) and a soft **Hauskappe** (house-cap) over the shaved or cropped head of a wig-wearer. *"Ein Wolf im Schlafrock"* comes from this garment (→ R11).

### Women
- **The Mantua**: a gown of one piece, open at the front over a contrasting petticoat, the skirts drawn back and pinned into a train. Of silk, brocade or velvet by Stand.
- **The Stecker**, stomacher: a stiff embroidered triangle pinned over the front of the stays, often jeweled. It is pinned, not sewn, with a dozen pins, and the jokes about how long a Stecker takes to unpin are older than the Stecker.
- **The Schnürleib**, stays: boned bodice laced at the back, which shapes the body into the fashionable cone with the bosom raised high above it. Lacing is done by a maid, and so is unlacing. The **Doppelschnürung** fashion of futa ladies (a busk and a different cut of petticoat) is discussed in R10.
- **The Hemd**: the shift of fine linen, worn next to the skin under everything. Its lace edge shows at the neck and sleeves. Ladies sleep in it.
- **Petticoats**, several, the outer one embroidered and meant to be seen.
- **No drawers.** Valdauer women of every class wear nothing under their petticoats. **Unterhosen** (drawers) are a man's garment, worn by women only to ride astride, by Hosenrollen actresses and by Sennerinnen on the alp. A woman in drawers is thought eccentric, mannish or Clear Church. This has consequences for sledging, swings, ladders, the **Dreher** dance and the Damenkarussell, every one of them known, joked about and occasionally the point (→ I10, I11).
- **The Turmhaube** ("tower-cap"): the tall headdress of starched, wired lace standing up over the forehead in tiers, from Vellora (*la torretta*). Very fashionable in the 1690s, and still rising. Some are two hands high. Doorways are a hazard and church pews a scandal, since the lady behind can't see. Its fall from fashion is overdue, and ladies are waiting for someone brave to go without.
- **The Mouches** (patches), **fans**, **gloves**, **muffs**: see I07.
- **Shoes**: high-heeled, pointed, silk or brocade, with buckles. Red heels with Kammerzutritt.
- **The Contouche**, a loose sack-gown worn at home in the morning, the women's equivalent of the Schlafrock. It is comfortable, and suspect: a lady receiving in her Contouche has made her visitor intimate.

### High days: the Almagranischer Mantel
On the great days of the court (coronations, royal weddings, the **Brauttag** procession, state funerals) the court wears **Almagran court dress**, the Ceremonial's black:
- **for men**: black silk with a short black cape, a stiff white ruff, black hose and a black hat with one plume;
- **for women**: black silk to the floor with a white lace mantilla.

It was imported with the Ceremonial in the 1630s (→ I69). It is hot in summer and cold in winter, and makes everyone look like a Velloran undertaker. The court loathes it and wears it with tremendous pride. The **Schokoladenpartei** wear it more often than they need to.

## 3. Regional dress (Tracht)

Outside the court, each region dresses its own way, and the country's young people are as vain about their Tracht as courtiers about their lace.

### The Hallgau and the mountains
- **Women**: the **Leibl** (a tight laced bodice over a white blouse with puffed sleeves), the full **Kittel** (skirt), and the **Fürtuch** (apron). The **Fürtuchmasche**, the apron bow, is a code everybody in the mountains reads at a glance:
  - **tied on the left**: unmarried and willing to be courted;
  - **tied on the right**: married, betrothed or otherwise taken;
  - **tied at the back**: widowed;
  - **tied in the front center**: betrothed within the month, a "bride-bow";
  - **no apron**: a girl too young to be courted, or a woman who doesn't care.

  Girls who tie the bow on the left and then glare at the boys who notice are a type in the Gstanzl. Wives who tie theirs on the left at the Kirtag are another type, and the Straw Court's favorite (→ I60). Court ladies at Bad Sulzau wear Hallgau Tracht for the summer as a costume, tie their bows on the left, and mean it about as much as Fasching masks (→ I24).
- **Men**: **Ledene** (knee-length leather breeches, embroidered braces), white shirt, a short green **Janker** (jacket) of loden, white knee-socks, and a hat with a **Gamsbart**, the tuft of chamois hair. The bigger the tuft, the better the hunter. The best tufts are bought.

### The Eisenmark (the miners)
- The black **Bergkittel** (miner's smock), with a row of brass buttons and a short leather apron worn *behind*, over the backside: the **Arschleder**, the miner's seat-leather for sliding down the shafts. It is the miners' proudest badge. At **Gießnacht** the miners parade in their Arschleder, and the girls of Kogelsdorf have a dance in which they pull them off the boys (→ I25).
- The miners' **Kogelmütze**, a leather cap.

### The Weißach (the silver valley)
- The white **Knappenrock**, the silver-miner's festival coat, with silver buttons sewn on by each man's wife, one per year of marriage. A widower keeps his. A young man's first silver button is sewn on by his betrothed at the Knappenhochzeit (→ I26).

### Sonnleiten (the wine country)
- Vintners' **blue aprons**; women's white headscarves knotted under the chin; and, at the Lesenacht, **masks** (→ I27).

### Rocalba
- **Women**: the **faldilla** (full skirt), the **gipó** (bodice), and above all the **mantellina**, a white lace head-shawl worn to church and festivals. A girl's first mantellina is given by her godmother, and the lace pattern tells which village made it. **Espardenyes**, rope-soled shoes with ribbons laced up the ankle.
- **Men**: the red **barretina** (a long red cloth cap, folded over), the **faixa** (a long sash wound round the waist, red or purple), and a dark jacket. In Llucmar the merchants dress in Valdauer court fashion and wear the barretina only at festivals, to show they are still Rocalban.

### The Grenzmark
- **Men**: red caps; the **Kabanica** (a long heavy cloak of undyed wool, the Grenzer's bed and coat); a sash with two pistols and a knife; the neck-cloth (the original Grenzerbinde); a **single gold earring**, the mark of a man who has taken a prisoner.
- **Women**: embroidered white linen, coin-necklaces holding the dowry, and red-and-black aprons.

### Ormánd (worn by Ormándi in Wiedenbrunn, and by Heiducken)
- The **dolmány** (a short fitted jacket heavily frogged with braid), the **mente** (a fur-trimmed pelisse slung over one shoulder), tight trousers, tall boots (**csizma**), a fur **kalpag** cap with a feather. Ormándi gentry wear more braid than any Zierherr, and do not see the joke.

### Kesrân (the embassy, and captives on feast-days)
- Kaftans, robes, sashes, turbans; for women, veils and trousers. Kesrâni dress is a fashionable costume at Fasching.

### Asagiri (the embassy)
- Layered silk robes tied with sashes; the envoy's retinue's swords; women's elaborately dressed hair and blackened teeth (→ I07, I37).

## 4. Colors and what they mean

| Color | Planet | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Black** | Saturn | formality, court (the Almagran black), deep mourning, the clergy of the Bridegroom |
| **White** | Moon | maidenhood, brides, the Moon-houses, the Bride's nuns |
| **Blue** | Moon | fidelity, Ilsenau (the midnight blue is theirs, and copying it is cheeky) |
| **Red** | Mars and Sun | Greifenstein scarlet, the military, passion, and the **red heel** of privilege |
| **Green** | Venus | love, hope, Holdegg's verdigris; a green ribbon is a lover's favor |
| **Grey** | Saturn and Jupiter | half-mourning, the Lautere, clerks, Ehrenreith |
| **Yellow** | Sun and the pox | gold (good), jealousy (bad), and since the 1690s a joke about the Lagoon-Pox. Nobody wears yellow ribbons now |
| **Purple** | the Bride | clergy of the Bride's See; Rocalban sashes |
| **Rose** | Venus | the Rosenband of the licensed courtesan; since 1690, also the fashion |

## 5. Jewelry and marks of belonging

- **House metal**: a great house's women wear their metal. Ilsenau wears silver, Holdegg copper and rose-gold, Mirabent quicksilver set in crystal pendants (dangerous and beautiful), Greifenstein gold, Kalteneck black lead-glass and jet, Starkenberg **iron rings** set with garnets, Ehrenreith pewter-grey pearls.
- **The Ilsenau crescent**: every Ilsenau woman wears a small silver crescent somewhere, every day. The Mondbräutigame wear a single silver crescent earring (→ R12).
- **Rings**: **true wedding rings are worn on the right hand** in Valdau. A ring on the **left** ring finger means a **Left-Hand bond** (→ I56). Rocalbans, who wear wedding rings on the left, cause no end of confusion at court, and some Rocalban ladies have used the confusion shamelessly.
- **The Pfandmarke**: the pledge's tin tag, hung from the Pfandband (→ R07).
- **The Zopfband**: a braided ribbon at the wrist in a color not one's own, which announces a heart-sister (→ R09).
- **Earrings**: on women, always. On men, only the Mondbräutigame's crescent, the Grenzer's gold ring and the sailor's hoop.
- **Mourning jewelry**: jet, iron, and lockets with the hair of the dead (→ I18).
- **Breverl**: the folded-paper amulet worn under the shift (→ I13).
- **The Ilsenau gift-crescent**: the tiny silver crescent the house gives its departing guests (→ I02). Half the court owns one, and wearing it means *I have been received there*.

## HOOKS

1. **The Visitator's book.** A Kleidervisitator stops the protagonist on the Graben. He wears Eigenhaar with a silver Mondlocke ribbon, and a coat cut in a way the Visitator chooses to call a woman's garment. The fine is five Greif, and the bribe is one. Several ladies are watching from a confectioner's window to see which he pays.
2. **The left-hand ring.** A Rocalban lady at the Cercle wears her wedding ring, by her own custom, on the left ring finger. Half the court assumes she is someone's left-hand partner, and she is enjoying it far too much to correct them.
3. **The apron bow.** At Bad Sulzau a court lady in borrowed Hallgau Tracht tied her Fürtuchmasche on the left as a summer joke. A local Hallgau lad has taken it seriously and climbed to her window at night with his own ladder.
4. **No drawers.** At the first sledge-party of the winter, a sledge overturns on the Spiegelsee bank and two ladies of the household and a guest are pitched into the snow in full view. One of the guests is a Lautere lady in drawers, which the court finds far more scandalous than anything else it saw.
5. **The Turmhaube's fall.** A woman of the household wants to be the first to appear at a Redoute without the Turmhaube, the end of the fashion, and needs a partner bold enough to appear beside her.
6. **The Almagran black.** For the Brauttag procession the whole household must wear the Almagranischer Mantel. The protagonist's is tailored overnight from an old Mondbräutigam's black, and fits in a way that makes the Obersthofmeisterin look twice.
