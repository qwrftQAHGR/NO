# I26 · The Weißach & the Spiegelsee

*The silver valley that made the Ilsenau, now nearly worked out and mortgaged to a Velloran bank; its miners' mass weddings and silver buttons; the mirror-lake with the white castle on one shore and the quiet house on the other; and the full-moon swims, the drowned daughter, and the bankers counting the shafts.*

**See also:** R12 House Ilsenau · I53 The Bleischlaf & the Sleepers (the Stille Haus) · I35 Vellora (the Banco della Fede) · I50 The Pit-Blood Trade (Silberbräute, Silberknechte) · I73 The Flask King (the mortgages) · I10 (the Wasserjagd) · I06 (the Knappenrock)

---

## 1. The land

**The Weißach** (the "white water") is a long mountain valley **four days west of Wiedenbrunn**, named for its river, which runs milky-pale with stone-flour from the glaciers at its head. Above the valley rises the **Weißkofel**, a grey-white mountain honeycombed with **silver mines**. Below it lies the town of **Weißach**, once the richest in Valdau. Farther down the valley, where it widens, lies a long, still, extraordinarily clear lake: **the Spiegelsee**, the mirror-lake.

---

## 2. The silver

- **The mines**: since the 1100s the Weißkofel has been mined for **silver** (argentiferous copper ore, the "**Fahlerz**") through hundreds of shafts and galleries. They are named for saints and patrons: the **Sankt-Daniel-Stollen** (Saint Daniel the Miner, the valley's patron), the **Agnes-Stollen**, and the oldest, the **Ilse-Stollen**, where a crude carving of a woman with a touchstone and a key is cut into the rock at the entrance (→ R12).
- **The great age**: from about **1450 to 1580** the Weißach was the richest silver country in the known world. Twenty thousand miners, a mint, a town of painted houses and silver altars, the **Knappenkirche** with its silver roof-bosses. Its silver paid for the union with Rocalba, the wars with Kesrân and the Flask King's laboratory, and made the house of **Ilsenau**.
- **The decline**: since the 1580s the rich veins have thinned. Shafts go deeper, water floods them, and pumping costs more than the ore is worth. There are now about **three thousand** miners, and the young leave for the Eisenmark, Cinabre or the army.
- **The mortgages**: the **Flask King**, Maximilian III, borrowed against the Weißach's silver to pay for his transmutation laboratory (→ I73). When he failed in 1604, the **Banco della Fede** of Vellora took the mortgages on the royal shares of the mines, and on much of the Ilsenau shares too, through loans the house took to keep up appearances (→ I35). For ninety years the Banco has collected its interest in silver. **Since 1695 it has been foreclosing on the outer shafts**, one by one, closing them and selling the timber, the pumps and the miners' houses.
- **The Fondaco**: the Banco's agent lives in Weißach in the **Fondaco Lunardin**, a Velloran trading-house with a loggia, a counting room, Velloran clerks in black, a Velloran cook and a private chapel with a Velloran saint. The agent is **Ser Alvise Lunardin**, a cousin of the Ca' Lunardin who hold the **Wheel** (→ I46). He is gracious, cultured, patient and very good at arithmetic. Weißach calls the Fondaco **das Rechenhaus** (the counting-house) and spits as it passes.
- **The Mirabent offer**: Casa Mirabent has proposed to the Crown and to Ilsenau to **revive the Weißach** with a new process: dissolving poor silver ore with Cinabre's **quicksilver** (*amalgamation*), which can take silver from ore too poor to smelt. It would work. It would also make the Weißach's silver depend on Mirabent quicksilver forever, and poison its river. Ilsenau has neither accepted nor refused, and Mirabent's letter has been on the Hausmutter's desk for two years (→ I41, R12).

## 3. The people and their customs

- **The Knappen**: the silver-miners of the Weißkofel, with a **Knappschaft** (brotherhood) as proud as the Eisenmark's and poorer. **"Glück auf!"** here too. Their festival dress is the **white Knappenrock** with silver buttons (→ I06).
- **The silver buttons**: a Weißach miner's betrothed sews his **first silver button** on his Knappenrock at their wedding, and one more every anniversary. An old couple's coat is a shining column of silver. A widower keeps his buttons and never adds another. A coat with buttons cut off means a wife who left.
- **The Knappenhochzeit** (miners' wedding): on **Saint Daniel's Day** in July, **all the year's miners' weddings are celebrated together**, thirty or forty couples at once. It starts in the **Knappenkirche** at dawn, continues with a procession of white coats and white wreaths through the town, and ends with a feast on the mine-meadow below the Ilse-Stollen. The Ilsenau Hausmutter traditionally attends and gives each bride a silver spoon, and the dancing goes on till the next day.
- **The Probiertag** (assay-day): each spring the valley's assayers (the descendants of Ilse's trade) compete in reading ore and coin by the **touchstone streak**, blindfold by touch and by sight. The winner is crowned **Probierkönig** or **Probierkönigin**. Since 1229 the Ilsenau have presented the prize, a silver touchstone-needle.
- **The pit-blood**: six centuries in the silver have given the Weißach's old mining families **Moon-blood**, silver fineness of 8 to 14 karat (→ R03, I50). It shows in the valley's people: pale, silver-eyed, prone to vivid dreams and twins. The silver houses court them:
  - **Silberbräute** (silver-brides): Weißach daughters taken by silver houses as concubines or pledges to anneal brittle lines.
  - **Silberknechte** (silver-servants): Weißach sons taken into the **Ilsenau household**, pledged or freed, as quiet studs to breed silver back into the house's daughters. This is never mentioned at court (→ R12).
  - The valley is proud of its blood, bitter about its uses, and has never forgotten that Ilse was one of them.
- **Dreams**: Weißach folk believe the Moon-blood lets them dream true. Village dream-tellers read the meaning of dreams, and after the waking several villagers came forward who swore they had dreamed for years of a sleeping young man in a white room (→ I13).

## 4. The Spiegelsee

The **Spiegelsee** lies at the valley's lower end. It is about five miles long and less than one across, deep, cold and so clear and still that on windless days it **doubles the world perfectly**, mountains and sky and castle and all. Painters can't decide which way up to hang their pictures.

- **Schloss Ilsenau**: the white castle on its wooded promontory on the **north shore**, the house's seat (→ R12). It holds the Mondsaal with its mirrors, the Spinnzimmer, the Schwesternbad over a cold spring, the Mondküche, the boathouse with its white boats, and the Ahnengang of painted daughters.
- **Spiegelau**: the lake-village below the castle. It has fishermen, boatmen, a few inns for pilgrims and painters, and the house's servants' families, who have served the Ilsenau for generations and wear blue ribbons at their wrists (→ I04).
- **The Stille Haus**: on the **far shore**, opposite the castle, reached by boat or by a long path through the woods round the lake's end. It is a plain, beautiful stone house built in the 1460s as a small **Damenstift** for unmarried Ilsenau daughters. Since 1682 it has been the house of the sleeper (→ R12, I53): six Schlafwärterinnen, a house physician, a chapel, a garden of lavender and poppies, and a quiet visitors find hard to describe. **The boatmen say the water is warmer on that side.** Since the spring of 1699 the Stille Haus has been a question nobody in Spiegelau will answer.
- **The Mondnacht**: at the **full moon** in summer, the **Ilsenau women swim the lake by night**, naked, from the castle boathouse toward the Stille Haus shore and back. It is an old house custom; Moon-blood loves the full moon in water (→ R12). The men of the house are not invited and not forbidden. Spiegelau's villagers have their own full-moon swimming at the lake's foot, the **Mondschwimmen**, where young people swim at midnight in pairs. Courtships start there, and the Zeuge pretends not to know.
- **The Drowned Daughter**: in **1601** a daughter of the **Wilburg generation**, a **Mondsüchtige** (sleepwalker), walked into the Spiegelsee in her sleep and drowned at nineteen (→ R12, R10). Fishermen say that on still full-moon nights she can be seen **swimming toward the Stille Haus** and never reaching it. She is the lake's ghost and the house's warning. Someone has begun lighting a lantern on her grave (→ I18).
- **The Wasserjagd**: the Ilsenau's September **lake-hunt** (→ I10). A stag is driven into the lake and shot from white boats while music plays on the shore. The stag always swims toward the Stille Haus side, and no stag of the Wasserjagd has ever been allowed to reach it.
- **In winter**: the Spiegelsee freezes **black and clear as glass**, so you can see the fish under your feet. The court of the Weißach skates on it, sledges race down the castle bank, and the women of the house skate across to the Stille Haus and back by moonlight (→ I10).

## 5. Tensions

- **The Banco against the valley**: foreclosures, closed shafts, miners' families evicted. Ser Alvise Lunardin is courteous and won't be hurried, and the Weißach is learning to hate a smile.
- **Ilsenau and the Banco**: the house's silver is shrinking. Its debts to the Banco are a lever Vellora has never pulled hard, yet.
- **The Mirabent amalgam offer** (§2).
- **The miners and the house**: the Weißach loves the Ilsenau as its own (Ilse was a miner's daughter) and resents the **Silberknechte** custom and the silver houses' taste for its daughters (→ I50).
- **The Lautere**: Clear Church preachers have found the closing shafts fertile ground, and preach that the Banco and the silver houses are two hands of the same thief.

---

## 6. Local custom, tension and a place worth visiting

- **Custom**: silver buttons; the Knappenhochzeit; the Probiertag; the Mondnacht and the Mondschwimmen; dream-telling.
- **Tension**: the Banco's foreclosures; the Mirabent amalgam; Silberbräute and Silberknechte; the Lautere.
- **Worth a visit**:
  - **the Ilse-Stollen**, by lantern, to see the founder carved in the rock;
  - **the Spiegelsee at dawn**, when the castle hangs upside down in the water;
  - **the Knappenhochzeit** in July;
  - **the Fondaco's loggia**, where Ser Alvise serves Velloran wine and asks after your debts with genuine interest.

## HOOKS

1. **The Mondnacht.** The first summer full moon after the waking: the Ilsenau women go down to the boathouse at midnight and wade in, and the protagonist is standing on the jetty. One of them calls back across the water. Halfway across, someone else is swimming who isn't one of the house's women.
2. **The foreclosure.** Ser Alvise Lunardin's clerks post a foreclosure notice on the **Ilse-Stollen**, the founder's own shaft. The miners are gathering, and the Banco's agent courteously asks for a meeting with "the young Prinz, since the house's women are so occupied," to discuss terms.
3. **The silver button.** At the Knappenhochzeit, a miner's bride sews a silver button onto the protagonist's coat instead of her groom's, laughing, in front of the whole meadow. By Weißach custom that is either a joke or a betrothal, and she isn't saying which.
4. **The Silberknecht.** A tall, silver-eyed Weißach miner's son of 14 karat, freed from the Ilsenau household years ago, comes back to Spiegelau and asks the protagonist, man to man, whether the house will want him again "now that there's a son." He doesn't explain what he means, and doesn't need to.
5. **The drowned daughter.** On a still full-moon night the protagonist sees from the castle terrace a pale figure swimming toward the Stille Haus. The boatmen won't row out, and the Mondsüchtige grave on the shore has a fresh lantern on it.
6. **The amalgam.** Mirabent's alchemist arrives at Schloss Ilsenau with a flask of Cinabre quicksilver and a demonstration: silver drawn out of worthless ore in a basin, before the Hausmutter's eyes. He has been instructed to make the demonstration *to the son*.
