# R02 · The Sky (astrology)

*The first of the three Arts. The Sky decides **when**: when a tincture is strong, when a cannon should be poured, when a child should be conceived, when a contract should be signed and when it would be wiser to stay in bed.*

**See also:** R03 The Work · R04 The Cards · I51 The Stundenamt & the Lost Days · I08 The Chamber Lexicon & the Thirty-Two Hours · I13 Folk Belief · X02 Calendar

---

## 1. What everyone knows

There are **Seven Wanderers** (*die Sieben Wandelsterne*): Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn. They move through **twelve houses** of the sky:

| German | Catalan | English |
|---|---|---|
| Widder | Àries | Ram |
| Stier | Taure | Bull |
| Zwillinge | Bessons | Twins |
| Krebs | Cranc | Crab |
| Löwe | Lleó | Lion |
| Jungfrau | Verge | Virgin |
| Waage | Balança | Scales |
| Skorpion | Escorpí | Scorpion |
| Schütze | Sagitari | Archer |
| Steinbock | Capricorn | Goat |
| Wassermann | Aquari | Water-bearer |
| Fische | Peixos | Fishes |

Each Wanderer rules a metal, a temper and a kind of hour.

The Sky does **not** decide events. Nobody sane in Valdau believes the stars announce that the King will die on Merkurtag. What the Sky does, and this is measured rather than believed, is set the **Witterung**, the weather, of the other two Arts and of bodies. A tincture made in its planet's hour is stronger. Iron poured in Mars's hour takes a temper that iron poured at Saturn's doesn't. A Mars-blooded officer is angrier at the hour of Mars, a Moon-blooded woman is more fertile at the hour of the Moon. The differences are real and modest, on the order of a good harvest against a poor one. They are not miracles. Like weather, they can be read in advance, and anyone with an ephemeris can read your weather as well as you can.

The court's **Stundenamt** (Office of Hours, → I51) has kept yield-ledgers since 1568. Every tincture batch, cannon pour and noble conception the office could get its hands on has been recorded with its hour. Those ledgers turned astrology from folklore into administration. The Kesrâni have tables older and better than Valdau's (→ I34), which Valdauers resent and copy.

## 2. The Hours

### How they are counted
The day from sunrise to sunset is divided into twelve **unequal hours**, and the night from sunset to sunrise into another twelve. A summer day-hour is long and lazy, about eighty of our minutes. A winter day-hour is short, about forty. Church bells and the town clocks of Wiedenbrunn ring *equal* hours for business, but anyone doing anything that matters counts **Planetenstunden**, planetary hours. Every great house keeps an **Stundenuhr**, an hour-clock with an adjustable dial, and a servant whose only job is to set it at dawn and dusk.

Each planetary hour is ruled by a Wanderer, in the old order: **Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, Sun, Venus, Mercury, Moon**, then round again. The first hour after sunrise belongs to the day's own planet. That gives the week its names:

| Day | Planet | Court name | Common Valdauer name | Catalan |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sunday | Sun | Sonntag | Sonntag | diumenge |
| Monday | Moon | Montag | Montag | dilluns |
| Tuesday | Mars | **Marstag** | **Ertag** | dimarts |
| Wednesday | Mercury | **Merkurtag** | Mittwoch | dimecres |
| Thursday | Jupiter | **Jupitertag** | **Pfinztag** | dijous |
| Friday | Venus | **Venustag** | Freitag | divendres |
| Saturday | Saturn | **Saturntag** | Samstag | dissabte |

The court and the educated use the planetary day-names. Peasants use the old ones, and Ertag and Pfinztag mark a speaker as a country cousin. Rocalban Catalan names the days for their planets as a matter of course, which Rocalbans consider proof that they were astrologers before Valdau was baptized.

### What an hour does
- **Stundengerecht** ("hour-true"): a tincture or preparation made in its own planet's hour is about half again as strong as the same work done at a neutral hour. Done in an *opposed* hour (Venus work in Saturn's, Mars work in Venus's) it comes out weak or sour. Every apothecary's label carries the hour of making. A forged hour on a label is the commonest fraud in the trade (→ I52).
- **Metal**: iron poured in Mars's hour, silver worked in the Moon's, gold minted in the Sun's all take a **Tugend** (virtue) that shows in use. Blades hold edges, cannon don't burst, coins ring true (→ R03).
- **Bodies**: everyone has an **Eigenstunde**, the hour of their nativity's ruling planet, and feels a little more themselves in it. For ordinary people the lift is about what a good breakfast gives. For high-karat nobles it is strong. A Starkenberg at the hour of Mars is easy to provoke and hard to stop, and an Ilsenau woman at the Moon's hour can feel her sister cry from two rooms away (→ R03 on blood).
- **Conception**: this is where the hours matter most to the great houses. A child conceived in its mother's house-planet hour tends to assay about **one karat finer** than its siblings. A child conceived in an opposed hour is more often sickly. This is why noble bedchambers have clocks, and why the **Stundenglöckner** exist (see §8).

## 3. Nativities

Every birth in the realm is supposed to be entered with its hour in the parish **Wiegenbuch** (cradle-book), kept by the sworn midwives (→ I65). From that hour a **Stundenrechner** (hour-reckoner) can cast a **Stundenbrief**, a nativity-letter. A printed one with the blanks filled in by hand costs a village family 4 Kupferlinge at the fair. A full **Nativität** for a noble child, bound in vellum with the planets painted, costs 3 Greif and is kept with the family's assay certificates.

A nativity determines three things:

1. **Komplexion**, the temperamental leaning. A Mars-heavy nativity is quick to anger and to heal; Venus is warm and susceptible; Saturn is patient and gloomy; Mercury is restless and clever. People use their Komplexion as an excuse: *"I'm a Mars child, I can't help it."* Others accept the excuse about as often as we accept "I'm tired."
2. **Aufnahme**, reception: which tinctures sit well in you. A Saturn-strong nativity takes lead tinctures like milk and gold tinctures badly. A Moon-strong one does well on silver. Physicians check the Stundenbrief before dosing. Poisoners do too.
3. **Stundengeschwister**, hour-siblings: two people born in the same planetary hour of the same day, anywhere in the realm. Hour-siblings share a faint pull. They "agree": each takes the other's tinctures well and feels easy in the other's company, and folk romance makes much of finding yours. Twins are the strongest case. Siblings born in different years but in the same hour of the same feast are considered lucky to marry, if marrying them were allowed at all (→ R06).

### The King's hour-children
By a custom dating to 1499, any child born anywhere in Valdau in the same planetary hour as a royal heir may be registered as one of the **Stundenkinder des Königs**, the King's hour-children. They receive a small pension (12 Bräutlinge a year), a bronze medal, and the right to petition the Crown in person once in their lives. Royal heirs are often given their Stundenkinder as companions, pages and maids of honor. There is a lively trade in falsifying birth hours to qualify, through midwives who will "remember" a birth an hour earlier for a fee. Such a midwife is a **Stundenschieberin**, an hour-shifter.

## 4. The peasant's Sky: the Praktik

Every household that can afford 3 Kupferlinge owns this year's **Bauernpraktik**, the farmer's almanac printed in Wiedenbrunn each autumn. Rocalba's equivalent is **el Pronòstic de Montalba**, printed at the abbey press. Village Zeugen read it aloud after the weekly Mahl for those who can't read. It contains:

- **Planting and harvest days** by the Moon and the signs.
- **Lostage**, the days that forecast weather: rain on Saint Medard's means forty days' rain.
- **Schwendtage**, the unlucky days. Do not marry, travel, pledge yourself or let blood on a Schwendtag. There are thirty-two in an ordinary year. Seelenmakler won't register a pledge on one, because a pledge made on a Schwendtag is believed to run away.
- **The Aderlassmann**, the bloodletting man: a woodcut of a naked man with the twelve signs placed on his body (Ram at the head, Fishes at the feet) and a table of which days are good for opening a vein in which limb. The Bader follow it (→ I64).
- **Haircutting, nail-paring, weaning and gelding days.**
- **The Liebstage**, love-days: the dates when the Moon and Venus favor courting, conception, and making up a quarrel. The Liebstage page, **das Liebesblatt**, is the most-thumbed page in every almanac in the realm. Many a village marriage was planned around it, and many a village child was conceived nine months before a date the parents can name.
- **A prophecy for the year** so vague it is always right ("the great shall suffer, and the small shall be merry"), and a woodcut of a comet whether or not there is one.

A peasant's experience of the Sky is the Praktik, the Stundenbrief from the fair kept in the family Bible-chest, and the bloodletting calendar. Above all it is the conviction that the Moon governs everything wet: crops, wombs, wounds and wine.

## 5. The great events of the Sky

### Eclipses (*Finsternisse*)
- **A solar eclipse** spoils every tincture in preparation anywhere it is visible. Retorts cloud, salts fail to crystallize, and a day's distillation turns to sludge. Apothecaries shut their shops. By a Patent of 1574, the day of a solar eclipse is a **Ruhetag** (rest-day): no debt may be called in, no pledge registered, no contract signed, no duel fought. Debtors pray for eclipses. The Stundenamt publishes eclipse dates years ahead, so nothing about this is ever a surprise.
- **A lunar eclipse** troubles the Moon-blooded. High-karat silver families feel faint, feverish and disoriented. Ilsenau women take to their beds, and the house's enemies know the dates (→ R12). Sleepwalking spikes. Village custom shuts the windows and covers the milk.

### The Great Conjunction (*die Große Konjunktion*)
Every twenty years Jupiter and Saturn meet in the sky.
- In a **Konjunktionsjahr** every temper runs strong. High-karat nobles feel their Handwerk sharpen. Brittle bloodlines have more fits. Trump-gifts run fierce. Children conceived under the Conjunction, the **Konjunktionskinder**, are believed to assay finer and live longer. The Stundenamt's ledgers half support this, which is enough.
- **1683** was a Conjunction year. The Kesrâni Chief Reckoner of Hours timed the Second Siege of Wiedenbrunn to it, and the Valdauer relief was timed to break it (→ I76).
- **The next is 1702.** Great houses are already competing for the best conception hours of that year. The Stundenamt has a waiting list. Midwives are booked. The Alchemists' faculty is buying silver for the Mondwasser demand. Some Kalteneck elders are said to be waiting to *die* in 1702, so that their cards fall at the strongest moment. Nobody is sure whether that is a joke.
- Theory also holds that a Great Conjunction is the only moment a body can be "opened" to change its nature, which matters for futas made by alchemy and for the possibility of a man's womb (→ R10, R14).

### Retrogrades (*Rückläufigkeit*)
When a planet seems to walk backwards in the sky, its works go sour. In **Mercury's retrograde** letters are delayed, contracts are disputed and Mirabent tempers falter. The Mirabent Correu raises its express rates "to cover the risk," which fools no one. Treaties are never signed in Mercury retrograde. *"Merkur ist rückläufig"* is a universal excuse for incompetence.

### Comets (*Schweifsterne*)
Comets are portents of something, which the broadsheets will explain. The Comet of 1680, the year the protagonist was born, was blamed in turn for the plague in Ormánd, the Almagran queen's miscarriage, and a two-headed calf at Kornau.

## 6. The Sky at war

- **The Marskalender**: armies time their violence. Opening barrages are fired in the hour of Mars, sieges are opened under Mars in a strong sign, and assaults are timed so the attackers' Mars hour falls while the defenders' Saturn hour runs. The Hofkriegsrat employs six Stundenrechner. Every regiment has a "hour-sergeant" (*Stundenfeldwebel*) with a pocket dial.
- **The Stundenkrieg**, the hours-war: each side knows the other reads the same sky. Commanders feint in good hours to draw the enemy's Mars-blooded officers into fighting at their weakest hours. The Kesrâni Reckoners are better at this, and everyone knows it.
- **Cannon** are poured only in Mars hours (→ R03, I25). The Hofartillerie keeps its powder dry in Saturn and fires in Mars.
- **The Mars ration** of courage tincture is issued so that it peaks in the attack hour (→ R03).
- **Treaties** are signed in Jupiter hours, when oaths bind best, and never in Mercury retrograde. The Peace of the Willow Bridge was signed in a Jupiter hour on a Jupitertag in 1684. Its renewal is already being scheduled.

## 7. The Sky at court and in law

- **Contracts** are dated with day and hour. A marriage contract, a pledge or a bill of exchange written in an opposed hour can be challenged in court as **unstündlich** ("unhourly"). Lawyers love this.
- **Weddings and Beilager** (the witnessed bedding of high marriages) are scheduled by the Stundenamt for Jupiter or Venus hours (→ I17, I69).
- **Coronations** are held at noon on a Sunday, under the Sun.
- **Kin marriages**: the 1633 Annealing Edict requires that a nearest-kin couple married by dispensation conceive in a Jupiter hour with the Moon well placed. Their Stundenbrief and the Stundenamt's certificate of the conception hour must be filed with the Hofprobieramt (→ I55, I75). Nobody believes all such certificates are honest.
- **The Hofkalender**: the Stundenamt's annual calendar for the court, which lists for each great house its favorable hours of the year for conception, travel, lawsuits and duels (→ I51). Each house gets a sealed private copy. A stolen Hofkalender tells an enemy exactly when your temper will flare and when your Moon-women will be faint.

## 8. The Sky in bed

The Sky has a great deal to say about sex, and Valdauers listen.

- **Venus hours** are for pleasure. Desire comes easier, skin is more sensitive, and a lover who couldn't be warmed at noon will melt at the hour of Venus after supper. Courtesans in the Rosengrund charge half again for an appointment in a Venus hour (→ I23).
- **Moon hours** are for conception. Mondwasser contraceptives are least reliable then (→ R03), and wives who want children and wives who don't both keep track.
- **Mars hours** are rough, quick and quarrelsome. Duelists and soldiers like them. Brides are warned.
- **Mercury hours** are for affairs: playful, fast, in unlikely places, half-dressed. *"Mercury hides,"* say the women of Wiedenbrunn. A rendezvous set in a Mercury hour is assumed to be adultery.
- **Jupiter hours** are for duty: the marital hour, the conception of heirs, the bed of state.
- **Sun hours** are daylight and display: making love in front of mirrors or windows, or before witnesses.
- **Saturn hours**, Saturn nights especially, are notoriously bad. Men fail and women feel nothing. *"Saturn im Bett"* means impotence in any context. The Saturn hour has its own devotees, though, for slowness, denial, binding with ribbons and waiting (→ I08).

**The Venus Vigil** (*die Venuswacht*) is a noble custom. When a couple has been assigned a conception hour by the Stundenamt, they keep vigil together from sunset until that hour begins: in bed, undressed, touching, forbidden only the act itself. A well-kept Venus Vigil is said to make finer children. A badly kept one is a famous source of comedy, of tears, and of children conceived three hours early in the wrong planet.

**The Stundenglöckner** (hour-ringer) is a servant who stands outside a noble bedchamber with an hour-dial and a small silver bell. He rings once when the assigned hour begins and twice when it ends. Couples race the second bell. *"Vor dem zweiten Glöckerl"* ("before the second bell") is Viennese for premature anything. A Glöckner who can be bribed to ring early or late is worth his weight in Greif. Glöckner hear everything and are among the best-paid informants in the city (→ I20).

## 9. Who practices it

- **The Stundenamt** at court: the Hofastronom, his reckoners, the Hofkalender clerks and the observatory on the Greifenburg roof (→ I51).
- **Stundenrechner**: town hour-reckoners organized in a guild with a patron saint (**Sankt Albuin der Rechner**). They cast nativities, calculate conception hours for the Bürger, set dials and sell ephemerides.
- **Kalendermacher**: the almanac printers of Wiedenbrunn and the Montalba press, and their rivals.
- **Midwives** record the birth hour. Without them nothing works, and they know it (→ I65).
- **Village Zeugen** read the Praktik aloud and bless the planting by it.
- **Kesrâni Reckoners**: the embassy keeps one, and everyone wants to bribe him.
- **The Asagiri embassy** keeps a diviner of its own, who works from a different system of directions and lucky days. The envoy will not travel north on certain days, which has caused one cancelled audience and a minor diplomatic crisis (→ I37).

## 10. Cost and limits

**What it costs.** Mostly *time*. You cannot make Venus rise; you can only wait. A winter night-hour is short and a summer one long, and a whole household can be kept up until three in the morning for a conception hour. Then *money*: a full nativity costs 3 Greif, a conception-hour reckoning 10 Bräutlinge, the Hofkalender's private pages are beyond price, and the Stundenglöckner's wages are paid on top. And *exposure*: your good hours can be calculated by anyone who knows your birth hour, which is why great houses guard their children's Stundenbriefe like jewels, and why stolen nativities are sold in the Rosengrund.

**What it cannot do.**
- It cannot foretell events, names, deaths or the outcome of a battle. The Sky is weather, not prophecy. The Church condemns anyone who claims otherwise as a **Wahrsager**, and the Stundenrechner's guild expels them.
- It cannot make the barren fertile or the impotent potent. It can only tip a balance that already exists.
- It cannot override a Card. A Trump-gift is a Trump-gift in any hour, though it runs stronger in its planet's hour.
- It cannot change blood. A conception hour can add a karat; it cannot make a commoner noble.

## 11. The black market

- **Forged Stundenbriefe** are used to qualify as one of the King's hour-children, to make a match look better on paper, or to shift a child's "official" hour so its inheritance or its karat expectations change.
- **Bribed midwives**, the Stundenschieberin (see §3).
- **Winkelpraktiken**: cheap pirated almanacs with dangerous mistakes. Villages have planted, wed and bled on the wrong days because of them.
- **Love-hour sellers**: men in the Hirschau who for a Kupferling will tell you the Venus hours of the woman you want, if you know her birth date. They are usually wrong, but sometimes right.
- **Stolen Hofkalender pages**: the real thing, sold for real money, usually by a clerk with gambling debts.

---

## 12. What foreigners think

- **Kesrân** treats the sky as a state ministry. The Chief Reckoner of Hours sits in the Divan, and the Padishah does nothing without him. Kesrâni find Valdauer astrology provincial and their almanacs crude, but they still buy them for the woodcuts.
- **The Sworn Valleys**: the Clear Church calls planetary hours "heathen clocks" and forbids them in church. Every Valleys farmer owns a Praktik anyway, kept behind the Bible.
- **Almagra**: the Santo Crisol licenses astrologers and burns the unlicensed ones. Almagran grandees are more superstitious about hours than anyone and time everything, even when they get up.
- **Vellora**: bankers lend in Jupiter hours and collect in Saturn hours. Courtesans know every client's nativity.
- **Ormánd**: horse-breeders swear by the Moon for foaling, and otherwise Ormándi think Valdauers fuss.
- **Asagiri**: they have their own system of lucky directions and days, which clashes with Valdau's at every audience.

## 13. Sayings, slang and jokes

- **"Saturn im Bett"**: impotence; any failure.
- **"In Venus geboren"**: a flirt, a soft touch.
- **"Er hat Mars im Aszendenten"**: a hothead.
- **"Merkur ist rückläufig"**: excuse for any mess.
- **"Vor dem zweiten Glöckerl"**: too soon.
- **"Ein Finsterniskind"**: a child born at an eclipse, believed unlucky; also an insult for anyone gloomy, unwanted or a bastard.
- **"Stundenhure"**: someone who will only make love by the almanac.
- **"Konjunktionskind"**: anyone suspiciously lucky.
- **"Jetzt schlägt's dreizehn"**: "now it's striking thirteen," i.e. that's the limit. There are only twelve hours, and the thirteenth belongs to the Death-card (→ I46).

## HOOKS

1. **The stolen page.** A page from House Ilsenau's private Hofkalender (the Moon-women's faint days, the house's conception hours for 1702) is offered for sale in a Rosengrund coffee-room. The seller wants it bought by someone inside the house. Otherwise it goes to the Starkenbergs.
2. **The second bell.** A Stundenglöckner rings the second bell twenty minutes early outside a newly married noble couple's door, and the scandal is whether it was a mistake or a bribe. He comes to someone in the household for protection, offering what he heard through the door on the nights he rang correctly.
3. **Hour-siblings.** A young woman arrives at the Ammengasse with a cheap fair Stundenbrief showing she was born in the same planetary hour as the protagonist in 1680. She has felt "a pull" toward the Weißach all her life and wants to know why. She may also be lying.
4. **The eclipse debt.** A creditor of the household plans to call in a large debt on the morning of a solar eclipse, which is illegal. He has bribed a Stundenamt clerk to publish a corrected eclipse date one day later, so that on the real eclipse day he can call it legally. Someone has to prove which day the sky actually darkens.
5. **The Venus Vigil.** A Stundenamt reckoning assigns a cousin's marriage-bed a conception hour at four in the morning, preceded by a full Venus Vigil. The bride's family needs a witness from a Seven Chairs house to certify it under the 1633 Edict. That means sitting up all night outside the door, or inside it.
6. **Konjunktion 1702.** Three great houses have booked the same Moon hour in 1702 for their heirs' conceptions. The Stundenamt can assign it to only one, and the Hofastronom has let it be known that his decision can be influenced, and not with money.
