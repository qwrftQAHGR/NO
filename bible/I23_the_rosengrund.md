# I23 · The Rosengrund

*Wiedenbrunn's licensed pleasure-quarter: its ranks of women and men for hire, its houses, cabarets and schools, the city's licences and inspections, the watch's very regular bribe calendar, and the people who live there, visit it, preach against it and profit from it.*

**See also:** R08 Desire & Custom · R07 Bondage · I61 The Seelenmakler & the Pfandamt · I64 The Bader · I66 The Lagoon-Pox · I08 The Chamber Lexicon · I06 (the Rosenband) · I20 The Rumorwache · R10 (the Doppelrose) · R11 (the Herrenhöfe)

---

## 1. Where and what

The **Rosengrund** ("rose-ground") lies **outside the Bastei**, in the low damp ground between the **Glacis** and the **Wieden** arm of the Selde, southeast of the inner city. It is a tangle of lanes, courtyards, bath-houses, taverns and tall narrow houses with lamps over their doors. The name comes from the rose-gardens of a convent that stood there before 1529. The Kesrâni burned it in the First Siege, and what grew back was something else.

By the **Rosenpatent of 1602**, the city licenses commercial love **only in the Rosengrund**:
- a licensed woman (or man) holds a **Rosenbrief** (rose-letter), renewed yearly at the Rathaus for **2 Greif**;
- licensed people may ply their trade in the Rosengrund freely, under inspection;
- in public anywhere in the city they must wear the **Rosenband**, a rose-red ribbon on the left sleeve (→ I06);
- soliciting inside the walls is forbidden. Discreet arrangements are not soliciting.

The Patent was passed after the **pox closures** of the 1590s (→ I66), as a way of corralling, inspecting and taxing a trade the city had failed to stop. It has been a great success, by all three measures.

---

## 2. Who works there: the ranks

Wiedenbrunn grades its sellers of pleasure as precisely as it grades its nobility:

1. **Damen von Welt** ("ladies of the world"): **courtesans** with salons, carriages, their own houses and their own servants. They hold Rosenbriefe and **live inside the walls**, by a special permit everybody pretends is about "music lessons." They choose their lovers, are kept by great men, receive at their Toilette, hold Basseta banks and talk politics. Some are Velloran, trained in the lagoon. The greatest of them is known to the whole city by one name. They never set foot in the Rosengrund except to buy a girl from Madame Rosl's school.
2. **Rosen** ("roses"): licensed women in the **Rosenhäuser**, the licensed houses of the Rosengrund. Each house is run by a **Rosenwirtin** (rose-landlady, the madam), with a salon downstairs, rooms upstairs, a piano or a Hackbrett, wine, and a strict house-order. The Rosen of the best houses are educated, dressed and coached. Many of them are **Röslein**, graduates of a pledge-school, and some are still pledged.
3. **Kammerlmädel** ("little-room girls"): independent licensed women who rent a **Kammerl**, a small room, by the night or the hour, in the **Kammerlgasse**, and bring clients there from the taverns. They are free, poorer, braver and shorter-lived.
4. **Gassenmädel** ("lane-girls"): unlicensed, and in the lanes and taverns and the dark of the Glacis. The Rumorwache arrests them on raid-days and takes their money on the others.
5. **Lustherren** ("pleasure-gentlemen"): men for hire. Some are rough boatmen and soldiers, and the dearest are powdered **Zierherren** (→ R11). They work in the **Herrenhöfe**, the men's houses (see §3), and serve men and, more discreetly, women.
6. **The Doppelrose's performers**: **futas** (→ R10), Zierherren, acrobats, dancers and singers, the stars of the cabaret.
7. **Pledges**: body-pledges (*Leibpfand*) owned by the houses, working off their terms (→ R07). The Rosenpatent says only free people may hold a Rosenbrief. The houses get round this by holding the Rosenbrief in the house's name and the pledge in their cellar's ledger.

## 3. The houses and places

- **Die Doppelrose** ("the double rose"): the most famous cabaret in Wiedenbrunn, founded in **1675** by **Madame Rosl**, a futa and former body-pledge who bought her own freedom at twenty-six (→ R10). It is a long low hall with a little stage, candle-footlights, a gallery of boxes with curtains, wine, and the best futa performers in the realm:
  - singing, dancing, comic songs;
  - the famous midnight show **"Die zwei Schlüssel"** ("The Two Keys"), which has been banned twice and sold out every night between bans;
  - masked noble audiences in the boxes.
- **Madame Rosl's Pfandschule**: the realm's most famous **pledge-school** (→ R07), in the same building, upstairs. Body-pledges sent by their owners or bought by the house are trained for six months to two years in music, reading aloud, dancing, the toilette, massage, conversation, discretion, and the arts of the **Thirty-Two Hours** (→ I08). Graduates receive a **Schulbrief** and are called **Röslein**. A Röslein's price is half again any other pledge's.
- **Das Pfandhaus zum Goldenen Ring** ("the Golden Ring pawnhouse"): the Rosengrund's pledge auction house and brokerage, run by the **Widow Gruber**, the most successful **Seelenmakler** (soul-broker) in Wiedenbrunn (→ I61). Here the quarterly showing, the **Quartalsschau**, is held, and bodies, contracts and debts change hands.
- **The Rosenhäuser**: about twenty licensed houses. The best known:
  - **Zum Silbernen Mond** ("At the Silver Moon"), whose Rosenwirtin dresses her girls in blue and silver. Ilsenau has complained of the impudence for forty years.
  - **Zur Blinden Kuh** ("At the Blind Cow"), named for the game (→ I09), where the game is played every night in the salon.
  - **Das Gelbe Haus** ("the Yellow House"), the cheapest licensed house, where students and boatmen go.
  - **Die Venusstube** ("the Venus parlor"), which prices its rooms by the **planetary hour** and has a Stundenglöckner ringing them (→ R02).
- **The Herrenhöfe**, the men's houses:
  - **Zum Hirschen** ("At the Stag"), for gentlemen;
  - **Der Fiakerhof**, for everyone else.
  - Both raided under the Widernatur statute when the Rumorwache needs an arrest (→ R09).
- **The Kammerlgasse**: a long crooked lane of tall houses with a room on every floor rented by the hour, each with a lamp in the window when free.
- **The Badstube zur heiligen Afra** ("Saint Afra's bath"): the Rosengrund's great bath-house, where **Bademägde** (bath-maids) wash, rub and serve (→ I64). It is named for **Saint Afra the Bath-Maid**, the patroness of bath-maids and of the Rosengrund (→ I54).
- **Taverns**: **Zum Roten Hahn** (the Red Rooster), with a cockfighting pit in the yard; **Zum Schwarzen Kater** (the Black Tomcat), the Kammerlmädel's tavern; **Die Kesrânerin** (the Kesrâni Woman), with Kesrâni dancers.
- **The Rosenbrunnen**, the rose-fountain in the little square, where independents meet clients by daylight "by chance."
- **Back-room presses** that print **Kammerbüchl** (→ I08, I12).
- **The Pockenhaus**, the pox hospital, on the edge of the quarter, which everyone knows is waiting (→ I66).

## 4. Money

| What | Price |
|---|---|
| a Kammerl hour | 6 Kupferlinge to 1 Bräutling, depending on the girl and the hour |
| a Rose for an hour in a good house | 1 Bräutling |
| a Rose for the night | 3–5 Bräutlinge |
| a **Venus hour** | half again (→ R02) |
| an hour of the Thirty-Two by name | priced by the house's list, "**die Stundenkarte**" (the hour-card); the **Saturnstunde** and **Konjunktion** are dearest (→ I08) |
| a Doppelrose box for the midnight show | 1 Greif |
| a Dame von Welt for a night | 5–20 Greif, and she decides |
| a Röslein bought outright (a pledge's remaining term) | 60–200 Greif (→ I61) |

**The Rosengeld** is always paid **first**, on the table, before the door is shut. Anything after is a gift (→ I08).

## 5. The city's rules and the watch's calendar

- **The Pockenbeschau** (pox-inspection): every licensed Rose and Lustherr is examined monthly by the **Stadtphysikus** (the city physician) or his deputy, and given a stamp in their Rosenbrief, **"rein"** (clean). The unclean are sent to the Pockenhaus. Stamps can be bought. Everybody knows which deputy sells them (→ I66).
- **The Rosenband** in public (→ I06), and the fashion of 1690–1693 that made it meaningless.
- **No Rosen at Mahl** in the inner-city churches. The Rosengrund has its own, **Saint Afra's**, whose Zeuge is the most tolerant in Wiedenbrunn.
- **The Rosenkalender** (rose-calendar), the Rumorwache's bribe calendar:
  - Every Rosenwirtin pays the Rumorwache a monthly fee, on the **first Montag** of each month, by a schedule that has not changed since 1660.
  - Extra is paid in **Fasching** (busy season) and in the **Läuterwochen** (when the Rumorwache must be seen to be pious).
  - **Twice a year**, in the week before Brauttag and the week before Erbentag, the Rumorwache holds a **public raid** for show. The date is announced **in advance** to the paying houses, so that the unlicensed can be hidden, the pledges kept upstairs and the Gassenmädel warned. The Rumorwache arrests a few unlucky people for the Diarium, and the Rosengrund throws a party the night after.
  - A house that doesn't pay its Rosenkalender gets raided on a day that isn't on the calendar.
- **The Lautere preachers** stand at the Rosengrund's edge on Venustag nights with lanterns and sermons, calling out the names of clients they recognize. Some clients now come masked. Some Lautere preachers have been seen leaving the Kammerlgasse.

## 6. People and customs

- **Afratag** (the feast of Saint Afra the Bath-Maid, early August): the Rosengrund's own festival. The Rosen, the Bademägde and the Kammerlmädel process from Saint Afra's church through the quarter with roses and candles, in their best, behind the saint's statue (a bath-maid with a bucket and a crown). Then the houses throw open their doors and the night is free to anyone who brings a rose. The clergy of the inner city look away. The Zeuge of Saint Afra's walks at the head of the procession.
- **Dorltag**: the bath-maids' second holiday, for **Dorl**, the bath-slave who became the Empress (→ I72). The bath-houses close and the bath-maids get drunk at the Ilsenau's expense, by a 1440 bequest the house has never managed to escape.
- **Rosen and their own**: many Rosen have heart-sisters among their colleagues (→ R09). Many keep children in the country with a foster-mother. Many save to buy a Kammerl, a house, or a pledge-girl of their own. Many end in the Pockenhaus. A few become Damen von Welt, and one or two have married counts.
- **Clients**: every class, from Hofräte to boatmen. Students come on the first of the month, soldiers on pay-day, and nobles masked. Great ladies come too, in veils, to the Herrenhöfe, the Doppelrose's boxes and certain discreet rooms, in closed Sänften (→ I21).
- **Gossip**: the Rosengrund knows a great deal about Wiedenbrunn's men and sells it. The Fall-Reckoners pay well for who visits which Rose (→ I48), and so does the Cambra Negra (→ I41).

---

## 7. Tensions

- **Licensed against unlicensed**: the Rosenhäuser want the Gassenmädel cleared out, and the Gassenmädel want to eat.
- **Free courtesans against the pledge-schools**: pledged Röslein undercut free Rosen, and free Rosen spit when a Röslein passes.
- **The Lautere** against everyone.
- **The Codex Ehrenreith** (→ I43), which would register every pledge in the Rosengrund by name and end the houses' trick with Rosenbriefe. The Widow Gruber is said to be paying Ehrenreith clerks to lose the relevant pages.
- **The Chastity Commission's memory** (→ I77): in 1693 the Commission closed the Rosengrund for eight months. The Rosen moved into palaces as "nieces," "lectoresses" and "companions," the city discovered how many great houses had spare rooms, and when the Rosengrund reopened, half of them didn't come back.

---

## 8. Worth a visit

- **The Doppelrose's midnight show**, from a curtained box, masked.
- **Afratag** night.
- **Madame Rosl herself**, now in her late fifties, magnificent, futa, in emerald silk at the head of her stair. She has known every great man in Wiedenbrunn and forgets nothing.

## HOOKS

1. **The Silver Moon.** The Rosenhaus *Zum Silbernen Mond*, whose girls dress in Ilsenau blue and silver, has a new attraction since the waking: a slight, long-haired Zierherr in a silver ribbon, billed as *"der Mondbub."* The Ilsenau want it stopped, and want to know who he is copying, and how the house knew whom to copy.
2. **Madame Rosl's invitation.** Madame Rosl sends the protagonist a box for the Doppelrose's midnight show and a note: she knew his mother when she was young, and has something of hers that she'll give only to him.
3. **The raid that isn't on the calendar.** The Rumorwache raids the Rosengrund on a night not in the Rosenkalender, while the protagonist is in a Doppelrose box with a lady of the court. The back stairs are full, and the lady's husband is among the Rumorwache's honored guests that night.
4. **The Röslein.** A Röslein of Madame Rosl's school, about to graduate, has been bought by a buyer who won't be named. She has a week. She writes, in very good letter-locked Briefsteller style, to the only person she knows of who has more power than her buyer and no reason to want her: the son of a Spindelhaus.
5. **Afratag.** On the night of Saint Afra, the Rosengrund's doors are open to anyone who brings a rose. The protagonist is brought by a lady of the household who has been coming, masked, every year for a decade, and who knows exactly which door she is taking him to.
6. **The Kammerlgasse lamp.** A Kammerlmädel of the Kammerlgasse claims, loudly, in the Schwarzer Kater, that a pale young man in a silver ribbon came to her room last Pfinztag, paid in Ilsenau silver, and was a Wolf. It wasn't the protagonist. Somebody is wearing his face, or his reputation.
