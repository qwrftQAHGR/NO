# I27 · Sonnleiten & Stift Rebstein

*The wine country: stone terraces stepping down to the Selde, a great abbey on a rock, a castle of music and copper on the hill opposite, a lawsuit between them older than anyone living, new wine poured under a pine-bush, cellars dug into the hills, vineyard watchmen in their huts, and a masked harvest night when the rings come off.*

**See also:** I42 House Holdegg · I22 Wiedenbrunn (the Heurigen) · I11 The Dreher, the Gstanzl · I15 (the Ringtausch) · I54 The Church · X03 Kitchen & Cellar · I33 Ormánd (the wine rivals)

---

## 1. The land

**Sonnleiten** ("sun-slopes"; a *Leite* is a steep hillside) is the stretch of the **Selde valley upstream of Wiedenbrunn**, a day by the Ordinari boat downstream and a day and a half by road (→ I21). For thirty miles the river runs through a narrow gorge between hills whose **south-facing slopes** are terraced from the water to the woods: dry-stone walls, vines in rows, apricot trees, little chapels at the corners of the vineyards, and white villages along the river. In spring the apricots blossom, in autumn the vines turn gold and red, and the whole valley smells of fermenting must.

The villages are **Sonnleiten-Markt** (the market town), **Steinriegel**, **Hollerbrunn**, **Leitenbach** and **Rebhof**, each with its church, its inns, its cooperage and its own opinion of its neighbors' wine.

The wine is **white**: the dry, flinty, peppery **Grüner Leitner**, the aristocratic **Rieslinger** from the steepest terraces, and from the best sites the honeyed late-harvest **Sonnleitner Auslese**. A little red is grown at the valley's warm eastern end, and the Sonnleitners say it is for Rocalbans.

---

## 2. Stift Rebstein

On a sheer rock above the river at the valley's center stands **Stift Rebstein**, a great abbey of the **Bräutigamsbrüder**, the Bridegroom's monks (→ I54). It was rebuilt in the 1660s in yellow and white Baroque, with twin towers, a dome, and a long façade that seems to float above the Selde.
- **The abbey** owns perhaps a third of Sonnleiten's vineyards outright and holds **tithe rights** (*der Zehent*, a tenth of every harvest) on much of the rest.
- **The Prälat** (abbot) is a great lord of the realm. He sits in the Landtag's first estate, rides in a coach-and-six, and keeps a table famous for its wine and its conversation.
- **The library**: forty thousand volumes in a painted hall, among them the abbey's **chronicle**, which records every vintage since 1089, and a locked cabinet the monks call **die Giftkammer** (the poison-room), where the banned books are kept, for study.
- **The Prälatenkeller** (the abbot's cellar), cut into the rock under the abbey, holds a century of vintages. The **Rebsteiner Prälatenwein** from the abbey's best terraces is served at royal weddings.
- **The monks** are vintners, scholars, confessors and enthusiastic drinkers. Rebstein's brothers are proverbially jolly, and *"ein Rebsteiner Gesicht"* (a Rebstein face) means a red, cheerful one.

---

## 3. Schloss Holdegg

On the hill **opposite** the abbey, across the river, stands **Schloss Holdegg**, the seat of **House Holdegg**, the Venus house of copper (→ I42). It is a graceful castle of rose-colored stone with gardens terraced down to the water and a music-room famous across the realm. The house owns the other great share of Sonnleiten's vines and the **Holdegger Rieden** (the Holdegg vineyard-sites) on the slopes beneath the castle. It holds its summer **Musikfest**, when the royal music chapel plays in the gardens at night and the river carries the music to the abbey. And it keeps the cellars where **Venusbitter** is made and aged under the Holdegg monopoly (→ R03).

Abbey and castle face each other across the Selde like two lawyers across a table, which is what they have been for ninety years.

---

## 4. The Zehentstreit (the tithe lawsuit)

In **1609** the Prälat of Rebstein claimed the tithe on the **Holdegger Rieden**, the castle's vineyards under the walls, under an old charter of 1220. House Holdegg answered that a charter of 1291 exempted them. The case went to the Landtag, then the Hofgericht, then the Church courts of both sees, which disagreed. Then it went back to the Hofgericht and on to the Kronrat. It is still pending.

- **Ninety years.** Four Prälaten and six heads of House Holdegg have died during it. The documents fill a room in the Hofkammer archive.
- **Three generations of lawyers** (the Wiedenbrunn advocates of the families **Pfeiffer** and **Zangerl**) have made their entire livings from it. The present Advocate Zangerl was born the year his grandfather filed the third appeal.
- Each harvest, the **abbey's tithe-collectors** come to the Holdegger Rieden with their wagons, and the **Holdegg vintners** meet them at the gate with a notary, refuse, and record the refusal. Then everyone drinks together at the Heuriger, because they are cousins.
- **The saying**: *"Das wird erledigt, wenn die Selde bergauf fließt"* (that'll be settled when the Selde flows uphill), of any interminable matter.
- **In 1699**: the Kronrat has scheduled a hearing. Nobody believes it.

## 5. Customs of the wine country

### The Buschenschank and the Heuriger
By the **Buschenschank-Privileg of 1587**, a vintner may sell **his own new wine** in **his own house and courtyard** without a tavern licence, for a set number of weeks a year, when he hangs a **Buschen** (a bunch of pine branches) over his gate.
- The pine-bush means **"ausg'steckt"** (hung out): open, with wine.
- Inside are long wooden tables under walnut trees, wine by the **Seidel** and the **Viertel**, bread, cold pork, cheese and pickles, and fiddle, Hackbrett and Gstanzl (→ I11). Everyone sits together at long tables, every rank. The **Dreher** is danced on the trampled ground.
- The Wiedenbrunn Heurigen of the Wiedenwald do the same (→ I22), but Sonnleitners say theirs is the real thing and the city's is for tourists.

### Sturm
In September, before the harvest is pressed, the **half-fermented must** is sold: cloudy, sweet, fizzy, mildly alcoholic, and treacherous. This is **Sturm** ("storm"). It tastes like grape juice and hits like wine. **Sturmzeit** (storm-time) is Sonnleiten's happiest month: everyone drinks Sturm at the Heuriger and is surprised by it. *"Der Sturm ist schuld"* (the Sturm is to blame) excuses any September indiscretion.

### The Weinhüter and their huts
In late summer, as the grapes ripen, each village hires **Weinhüter** (vineyard watchmen): young men who guard the vines against thieves and birds for six weeks. They live alone in little stone **Hüterhütten** (watch-huts) among the vines, with a gun, a wooden rattle and a dog.
- By custom they wear a tall hat decorated with feathers, ribbons and fox-tails.
- They are **forbidden to come down to the village** until the harvest, so the village comes up to them. The young women bring the Weinhüter their food, and stay. *"Zum Hüter gehen"* (going to the watchman) is a Sonnleiten euphemism.
- The Weinhüter's hats come down at the harvest decorated with ribbons from every woman who visited, and the watchman with the most ribbons is mocked and envied in equal measure.

### The Kellergassen
In the loess hills behind the villages, long sunken lanes are lined on both sides with **cellar-doors**. These are **Kellergassen** (cellar-lanes): wine cellars dug deep into the soft yellow earth, each with a little press-house in front. In autumn vintners sit in their press-houses with doors open, offering passers-by a taste from the barrel. On summer nights the cool deep cellars are the valley's trysting places, and *"im Keller"* (in the cellar) is understood.

### The harvest and the Lesenacht
- **The Lese** (harvest) in October: pickers from the whole region, villagers, students, soldiers on leave, Rocalban seasonal workers and Wiedenbrunn's poor, crowd into the vineyards. They pick, carry the **Butten** (back-baskets) down the terraces, and sing. The work is hard, and the evenings are not.
- **The Lesenacht** (harvest-night): on the last night of the harvest each village holds its harvest feast, and the custom is **masks**. Everyone, vintner and picker, married and unmarried, comes **masked** to the dancing in the press-yards and the Kellergassen. By custom married people take their wedding rings off for the night, *"ringlos,"* which means nothing, officially. The **Lesekönigin**, the harvest-queen, is chosen by lot from the women pickers. She wears a crown of vine-leaves and grapes and chooses a king.
  - The Lesenacht is less formal than the Ringtausch of midsummer (→ I15) and more anonymous. Nobody exchanges anything except, perhaps, a cellar key.
  - The Straw Court of each village holds no court for what happened on Lesenacht (→ I60).
  - The **Feuerkinder** of midsummer have their autumn cousins, the **Lesekinder**, born in July.
- **The Weinweihe** (wine-blessing) on **Saint Leonhard's Day** in November: the abbey's monks bless the new wine in procession through the villages, sprinkle the barrels, and taste. The Sonnleiten year ends with the Prälat drunk on a white mule.

## 6. Tensions

- **The Zehentstreit** (§4).
- **Abbey against the Heuriger**: the Prälat wants the 1587 Buschenschank privilege **revoked** or taxed, since every pint sold at a vintner's gate is a pint not sold at the abbey's taverns. The vintners say they'd rather burn the abbey, and have tried twice.
- **Ormándi wine**: the Ormándi sweet wines, the honeyed **Hárs Ausbruch** above all, flood Wiedenbrunn since the Compact. Sonnleiten's vintners want tariffs, House Holdegg (pro-Ormánd, → I42) opposes them, and the vintners have stopped inviting the Holdegg steward to the Heuriger.
- **Venusbitter**: Holdegg's monopoly on the liqueur. Sonnleiten's cellars make cheap imitations, which Holdegg's agents smash on discovery.
- **The Lautere**: Clear Church preachers in the valley condemn the Lesenacht and the Weinhüter's huts. The pickers throw grapes at them.

---

## 7. Local custom, tension and a place worth visiting

- **Custom**: the pine-bush; Sturm; the Weinhüter's ribboned hats; the Kellergassen; the masked Lesenacht and its ringless dancers; the Prälat on his white mule.
- **Tension**: the Zehentstreit; abbey against Heuriger; Ormándi wine; Venusbitter.
- **Worth a visit**:
  - **a Steinriegel Kellergasse on Lesenacht**, masked;
  - **the Prälatenkeller** under Stift Rebstein, if the Prälat likes you;
  - **the Holdegg Musikfest** on a summer night, with the music crossing the water;
  - **a Hüterhütte** in late September, if you bring food.

## HOOKS

1. **Ringlos.** On Lesenacht in a Steinriegel Kellergasse, a masked woman takes the protagonist's hand in the dance, and her hand is bare. She slipped her ring off at the door, as custom allows. Her voice is one he knows from the Ilsenau Jour, and she is sure he doesn't recognize her.
2. **The Zehentstreit hearing.** The Kronrat finally schedules the tithe hearing, and both parties court the Seven Chairs' votes. The Prälat of Rebstein invites the protagonist to dine in the Prälatenkeller, and the Holdegg send an invitation to their Musikfest for the same night.
3. **The Weinhüter.** A Weinhüter of Hollerbrunn, forbidden to come down until the harvest, has been visited in his hut by a lady of the court in disguise every night for a week. He comes down early, against custom, to find out who she is, and arrives at the Ilsenau party's inn with a ribbon in Ilsenau blue.
4. **The Lesekönigin.** The harvest-queen of Rebhof, chosen by lot, is a Rocalban seasonal picker. By custom she must choose a king, and she chooses the protagonist in front of the whole press-yard. As king, by custom, he must kiss her, share her cup, and lead the last dance into the cellar.
5. **Sturmzeit.** At a Sturm evening at a Sonnleiten Heuriger, the protagonist drinks three Seidel of the sweet, harmless-tasting must, and is told by his companions, too late, what Sturm does. *"Der Sturm ist schuld"* covers what happens next, by custom, until morning.
6. **The Venusbitter cellar.** A Sonnleiten vintner has found an old cellar under his press-house full of Holdegg Venusbitter from 1688, from the barrels the Philter Trial said were destroyed (→ I42). He wants to sell it to someone who knows what it is.
