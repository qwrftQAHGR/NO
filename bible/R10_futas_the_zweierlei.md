# R10 · Futas: the Zweierlei

*Women born with both natures: who they are, what they are called, what the law makes of them, why the matriarchal houses quietly depend on them, how the court desires them, and what the Work can and can't do to make more.*

**See also:** R05 Nobility (§6 Spindelhäuser) · R06 Kinship & Nearness · R09 Sapphic & Queer Life · R12 House Ilsenau · I58 The Confraria de l'Hereu · I41 Casa Mirabent · I23 The Rosengrund (the Doppelrose) · I54 The Church of the Wedding · I37 Asagiri

## 1. Who they are

A futa is born with a working womb and vulva and also a working cock, usually rising where a clitoris would be, with the stones small or drawn up inside. Bodies vary: some are mostly woman with a modest second nature, some are evenly both, and a few are built heroically in both directions, which gets songs written about them. They menstruate, can conceive and bear, and can sire, though their seed is generally weaker than a man's. A futa siring on a woman succeeds perhaps half as often as a man would at the same hours. Their desire runs in every direction a woman's or a man's might.

**How common**: about **one woman in sixty** across the realm. That is not rare; every village has a couple and every court a dozen. Much commoner in **Mercury- and Moon-fine lines**: in Casa Mirabent nearly one daughter in eight (→ I41), and in the silver Spindelhäuser perhaps one in fifteen. The alchemists say Mercury makes bodies "willing to be two things," and they are probably right.

**Not a norm and not a scandal.** People notice a futa the way they notice a redhead with green eyes: with interest, with assumptions, with jokes, and without surprise.

## 3. In law

- **A futa is a woman** for every legal purpose in Valdau and Rocalba: inheritance, marriage, majority, office, dress, bath-house and sumptuary law. The law doesn't know what else she could be, and has never been asked to decide.
- **She can marry a man** in the ordinary way. **She can be joined to a woman** by Verschwisterung (→ R09) or in the Left Hand register (→ I56). Since the 1620s the Bride's See has quietly allowed a futa and a woman to marry *as such*, with the Zeuge simply leaving the words "bride" and "groom" out of the vows. It is called a **Doppelhochzeit** and is not common.
- **Paternity**: the **1558 Patent on Distaff Paternity**, issued three years after the Distaff Sanction, recognizes **a futa's fatherhood** of children she sires *within a Spindelhaus*, for purposes of inheritance and karat. Outside the Spindelhäuser the law has no category for it. A child a futa sires on a married woman is legally the husband's, and practically the subject of a duel.
- **The Church**: futas may become nuns of the Bride, and abbesses. They may not become Zeugen of the Bridegroom. The Confraria de l'Hereu makes them priestesses (→ I58).

## 4. The hidden engine of the Spindelhäuser

This is where futas matter politically, and why the matriarchal houses guard theirs closely.

A Spindelhaus inherits through its daughters (→ R05 §6). To have daughters it needs fathers, and fathers come from outside: the in-married husbands, chosen for blood, money or alliance. Every husband brings in another house's blood, another house's interests and another set of in-laws.

**A futa daughter can sire heirs inside the house's female line.** With a futa, a Spindelhaus can keep its blood, its karat and its secrets among its own women for a generation:

- **Karat climbs.** A futa siring on her sister or cousin produces Nahkinder of high fineness (→ R06). It is **Ernst**, not Spiel: it can make an heir, so the house takes the **Nahvermerk**.
- **Falls stay home.** A futa who is both blood-kin and bed-partner to a Trump-bearing woman of the house is a nearly certain heir (→ R04).
- **Brittleness follows.** Every Spindelhaus remembers the **Wilburg generation** of Ilsenau. In the 1580s the futa Fürstin **Wilburg von Ilsenau** sired seven daughters on her sister and cousins. The house's silver climbed to 21 karat, three of the seven daughters were sleepwalkers who never fully woke from their dreams, and the house swore the **Wilburg Vow**: no futa-Ernst inside the house for three generations (→ R12). The three generations ended in the 1680s. The question of whether to resume is very much alive in 1699.

So futa daughters in a Spindelhaus are **prized, watched, courted by their own kin, resented by their sisters' husbands, and argued over in the Spindelrat.** A futa born to a matriarchal house is born into politics.

## 5. At court and in society

- **At court** futas are fashionable and desired, and much discussed. A futa lady can take a married noblewoman as a lover, which is ordinary Spiel among women and no adultery in anyone's ledger, **unless she gets her with child.** Hers is the one female affair that can end in a bastard, and everyone knows it. Ladies who choose futa lovers either use Dunkelwasser with great care (→ R03), keep to Spiel, or take the risk for the thrill. A futa's attentions to a married woman are watched by the woman's husband with an anxiety other women's attentions don't earn.
- **Among the Bürger**: scandal and fascination. A Bürger family with a futa daughter marries her young, to a widower who won't ask, or sends her to the Damenstift.
- **In the villages**: luck and suspicion together. In the Hallgau a futa bridesmaid, the **Glücksbraut**, is believed to bring fertility to a marriage, and families pay for one. The Clear Church preaches against futas as a confusion of creation. Lautere villages are unkind to them.
- **In the Rosengrund**: the **Doppelrose** cabaret, whose futa performers are the most famous and expensive entertainers in Wiedenbrunn (→ I23). Futa courtesans command the highest prices in the Book of Graces in Vellora. A futa body-pledge with a Schulbrief sells at four times the price of a woman (→ R07, I61).
- **In the Church**: futas are **erbgestaltig**, heir-formed, after the Heir. They are carved into altarpieces as the Heir's attendants and asked to touch a sick child's cradle for luck. The Confraria de l'Hereu, a Rocalban confraternity, makes them priestesses and its **Mercurial Supper** its high rite (→ I58).
- **In Kesrân**: the **mihrimâh guards** of the Andarun. Futas are the women's palace's guardians and enforcers, because they aren't men and so may enter, and aren't eunuchs and so may desire. Kesrâni captives of this kind are valued in Valdau too (→ I34).

## 6. Dress and the body

- **No law** sets futa dress apart. Most dress as women of their rank, with stays laced in the **Doppelschnürung** fashion (a busk and a cut of petticoat that flatters what's beneath), and think nothing of it.
- **Some dress as men**, in Hosenrollen on stage and off (→ I68). A few have lived as men entirely. The law, which calls them women, is not consulted.
- **The bath-house**: the **Badstuben Ordinance of 1602** assigns futas to the women's baths, but allows them the men's baths "on request." This produced the realm's most quoted line of legal drafting, *"auf ihr Begehren"* ("upon her desire"), and a century of jokes (→ I64).
- **Sport and duels**: futa noblewomen fence in the Damenduell like any other women (→ I71). The bare-breasted rules mean their second nature is not on display above the waist, and is discussed below it anyway.

## 7. The Mercurial Course (alchemy's version)

The Work can sometimes give a woman a second nature. It cannot do anything simple.

- **What**: a course of quicksilver tinctures and salves taken over **thirteen moons**, begun ideally in a Great Conjunction year (the next is 1702, → R02), each dose in a Mercury hour, under the supervision of a Mercury-blooded alchemist. The best come from Mirabent's Cinabre laboratory and the Seventh Faculty (→ I41, I52).
- **Results vary**: sometimes a full second nature, sometimes a small one, sometimes nothing. Sometimes the woman is left sterile in both natures.
- **Risk**: the **trembles** (→ R03), tooth loss and madness. About **one woman in six** dies of it or never fully recovers.
- **Cost**: about **800 Greif**, more than a good estate.
- **Who does it**: rich widows bored of men; ladies of Spindelhäuser who want to sire inside the house without waiting for a futa to be born (scandalous, because it is a deliberate path to Ernst); courtesans planning an investment; and the occasional lover who wants to give her lady a child. The Clear Church calls it the vilest thing a woman can buy. The Bürger find it more shocking than incest.
- **It works only on women.** On men it does nothing useful (→ R14 for what *might* work on a man).
- **Cheap imitations**: the Winkelalchimisten of the Kesselgasse sell a "Mercurial draught" that promises a second nature for a night. It gives a woman a fever and the shakes. There is also a famous leather version from Rocalba, the **consolador de la hereva**, which works every time (→ I08).

## 8. History and famous futas

- **Sant Feliu Doble** ("Saint Felix the Double"). A Rocalban hermit of the seventh century who, when the brothers prepared the body for burial, was found to be of both natures. Patron of futas, of mixed marriages and, curiously, of locksmiths. His feast is in Llucmar, with a procession in which a futa carries the reliquary.
- **Wilburg von Ilsenau** (1541–1609), the Fürstin of the **Wilburg generation** (§4). Her portrait hangs in the Ilsenau Mondsaal, a stout woman in silver with a spindle in one hand and a riding-crop in the other.
- **La Contessina Mirabent**. A Mirabent daughter of the 1640s rumored to have taken the Mercurial Course to sire on her twin sister. She was the grandmother of the Twin Bride of 1661, which says something about Mirabent (→ I41).
- **Madame Rosl** (b. 1641). Founder of the **Doppelrose** cabaret and of the realm's most famous pledge-school, herself a futa and a former body-pledge who bought her own freedom at twenty-six (→ I23).
- **The Mihrimâh Commander** of the Kesrâni siege-camp, 1683. A futa officer of the Andarun's guard who led the women's baggage-train out of the rout. She was captured by Ormándi hussars and later married one of them (→ I76).

## 9. Sayings and slang

- **"Doppelt hält besser"**: "double holds better." A proverb about belt-and-braces caution, and about futas.
- **"Sie hat zwei Schlüssel"**: she has two keys; she can open any door.
- **"Glücksbraut"**: a futa bridesmaid; luck at a wedding.
- **"Eine Zweierlei im Haus ist ein Erbe im Haus"**: "a two-kinds in the house is an heir in the house." Spindelhaus proverb.
- **"Mit Latz"** ("with a flap"): dirty joke, from the cut of the old codpiece.

## HOOKS

1. **The Wilburg Vow has run out.** The Spindelrat of Ilsenau must decide this year whether the house will again allow futa-Ernst inside its female line. A futa daughter of the house has her own opinion, and a sister she has been in love with since the Stiftsjahr.
2. **The Glücksbraut.** A Hallgau village asks for a futa bridesmaid from the summer court for the wedding of the Hallamt overseer's daughter, as is custom. Someone volunteers a futa of the household without asking her, and she has opinions about being luck.
3. **Two keys.** A married noblewoman of an allied house is pregnant. Her husband has been at the Wehrgrenze for five months, and her futa lover is a guest under the Ilsenau roof. The husband is coming home.
4. **The Mercurial Course.** A rich widow, a friend of the house, has begun the Course in anticipation of the 1702 Conjunction. Six moons in, she has the trembles, an unsteady second nature, and an intense interest in the household's only son.
5. **The Confraria's invitation.** The hereves of the Confraria de l'Hereu send an invitation to their Mercurial Supper, addressed jointly to the house's futa daughter and to the protagonist, in the same hand, with the same seal.
6. **The Doppelrose.** A Doppelrose performer, Madame Rosl's newest star, has a debt she can't pay and a pledge-contract offered for her. She would rather sell it to a house she chooses than to the house that's buying it, and she chooses Ilsenau.
