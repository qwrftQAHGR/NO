# I22 · Wiedenbrunn, the Residenz

*The capital of the Double Crown: a walled city on the Selde with the royal palace at its heart, a ring of earthen walls turned into promenades, a park of deer and clowns, wine-villages on the hills, coffee on every corner, a Rocalban quarter and a Kesrâni one, and a hundred thousand people who complain about all of it with love.*

## 1. At a glance

- **Population**: about **110,000**, half inside the walls and half in the **Vorstädte** (suburbs).
- **Setting**: the south bank of the **Selde**, where the wooded hills of the **Wiedenwald** come down to the river plain. The river divides into arms and islands here. The Selde's main arm runs past the city's north side, and a smaller arm, **die Wieden** (from which, says one etymology, the city has its name), curls round the east.
- **Character**: Baroque on a medieval skeleton. Narrow, crooked lanes, opened into squares, with palaces and churches crammed in among the houses of craftsmen. Domes and towers, bells every quarter-hour, coffee, horses, incense, river-mud, cesspits, lilac in spring. Grand and shabby, devout and lewd, gossipy, sentimental, ironic.
- **Dialect**: **Brunnerisch**, with its two moods, **Schmäh** (charming mockery) and **Grant** (cultivated grumpiness) (→ R01).
- **The city's self-image**: *"Wiedenbrunn bleibt Wiedenbrunn"* (Wiedenbrunn stays Wiedenbrunn). Everything changes, and nothing does.

---

## 2. The shape of the city

### The inner city (*die Innere Stadt*)
Inside the **Bastei**, the old walls, which are massive earthen ramparts faced with brick. They were rebuilt after the **Second Siege** of 1683 (→ I76) and are now planted with lindens and walked on.
- **The Greifenburg**, the royal palace. A sprawling town of courtyards, wings, chapels and stables, added to by every king since 1270. Its parts:
  - the **Schwurhof**, the old core, its gate guarded by the Sworn Valleys' Schwurgarde;
  - the **Kanzleitrakt** (offices);
  - the **Leopoldstrakt** (the royal apartments, built by Leopold II);
  - the **Damenstock** (the Queen's ladies' wing, ruled by the Obersthofmeisterin, → R12);
  - the **Winterreitschule** (the riding school, → I10);
  - the **Hofburgkapelle** (court chapel);
  - the **Planetensaal**, where the Seven Chairs sit (→ I38);
  - the **Hoflaboratorium** of the court alchemist (→ R03);
  - the observatory of the **Stundenamt** on the roof (→ I51);
  - and the **Ratsstube** of the Kronrat.
  
  Its gardens run down to the Bastei.
- **The Dom zum Bräutigam**: the cathedral of the Bridegroom's See (→ I54). A Gothic giant with one tall spire, nicknamed **der Alte** (the old one) and visible from everywhere, and a roof of glazed tiles in a zigzag of red and green. Its great bell is **die Pummerin**, cast in a Venus hour, said to keep couples faithful (→ R03). Behind its altar is the **Herzgruft**, where royal hearts lie in silver urns (→ I18).
- **The Graben** ("the ditch"): the city's grandest street, a broad promenade built over the old Roman moat. It is lined with palaces, confectioners, silk-merchants, wig-makers and the best coffeehouses. At its center stands the **Brautbrunnen** of 1662 (the Bride's Fountain, a riot of marble clouds, nymphs and a silver-crowned Bride pouring water from a ewer), and everyone walks there at noon and after Mahl on Sundays.
- **The Spiegelmarkt** ("mirror-market"), the street of luxury: the court confectioner **Demelius**, jewellers, lace-sellers, and the Velloran glass-shop.
- **The Herrengasse** ("lords' lane"): the street of the great palaces. Holdegg, Ehrenreith and the Landhaus where the Landtag sits.
- **The Ammengasse** ("nurse-lane"): **Palais Ilsenau** and the **Ammenhaus**, joined by the covered bridge **the Milchsteg**. At the head of the lane stands **the Milchsäule**, the Milk Column of 1352: white marble, topped by a gilded ewer, raised for the end of the Grey Death and the Ilsenau wet-nurses who saved a royal child (→ R12, I57). Lampoons are pasted on it at night (→ I12). Wet-nurses touch it for luck on their way to their first royal child.
- **The Hoher Markt**: the old market, with the **Schandplatz** and the **Pranger** (→ I20), fish-wives and the Bänkelsänger's bench (→ I11).
- **The Kesselgasse** (kettle-lane): the crooked street of the **Winkelalchimisten**, the hedge-alchemists. Smoke, smells and things for sale nobody asks about (→ I52).
- **The Bäckerstraße**: the university quarter, with students' taverns and lodgings, and the **Bücherbogen**, the booksellers' arcade (→ I19, I12).
- **The Ballhausgasse**: the **Ballhaus** for court tennis (→ I10).
- **Palais Kalteneck**, with the **Bleidächer** prison under its lead roofs and the **Kaltgruft**, the royal crypt, beneath its chapel (→ I44).
- **The Pfandgasse**: the street of the pawnbrokers, the second-hand dealers, and the **Pfandamt**, the Pledge Office (→ I61).
- **The Mirabent post-house** in the **Postgasse**, where the Correu's riders come and go and the **Cambra Negra** works at night (→ I41).

### The Vorstädte (suburbs)
Outside the Bastei, beyond the **Glacis**, a wide band of open ground kept clear for cannon, where people now walk and duel, rings of suburbs spread toward the hills.
- **The Rosengrund**: the licensed pleasure-quarter, in the low ground by the Wieden arm (→ I23).
- **The Carrer Rocalbès** ("Rocalban street"): the Rocalban quarter on the east side near the river quays. Rocalban merchants, shipping-agents, dides waiting for places in the Ammenhaus, Catalan taverns and bakeries (the right bread, at last), a Rocalban church of **Sant Feliu Doble** (→ R10), and the house of the **Confraria de l'Hereu** (→ I58).
- **The Kesrâner Viertel** ("Kesrâni quarter"): around the quays where the coffee and silk barges land. Kesrâni traders, freed captives, coffee-roasters, the hammam (→ I64), the Kesrâni embassy (→ I34), and the **Lamp-house** where the captives pray.
- **The feud**: the Carrer Rocalbès and the Kesrâner Viertel are next door, and they hate each other. Rocalban galleys took many of the Kesrâni quarter's people captive, and the Kesrâni coffee trade undercuts Rocalban chocolate (→ I05). There are brawls every month in the lanes between them. On Erbentag the Rocalbans burn a straw Kesrâni on their Tió-bonfire, and every Hochzeitsnacht the Kesrâni throw firecrackers into the Rocalban procession. The Rumorwache stays out of it.
- **The Färberau**, **the Wieden** and **the Hohlweg**: craftsmen's suburbs. Weavers, tanners and dyers by the water; the **Bettgeher** lodging-houses where beds are rented by the shift (→ R06).
- **The Laimgrube** ("loam-pit"): brickworks, poor housing, and Wiedenbrunn's roughest taverns.

### The Hirschau (the park)
Down the Selde from the city, a vast river-meadow forest of oaks, chestnuts and willows, once the royal deer-park (*Hirsch-Au*, "stag-meadow"), opened to the public in **1677** by King Leopold II.
- **The Hauptallee**: a straight chestnut avenue running five miles through the park, where the **Corso** is driven on Brauttag and every fine Sunday (→ I14).
- **The Lusthaus**, a domed hunting pavilion at the end of the Hauptallee. Since 1690 it has been a restaurant, with private rooms upstairs.
- **The Wastlwiese** ("Wastl-meadow"), named after the city's stock comic figure, **der Krautwastl** (the cabbage-Wastl), a peasant clown in a green hat, a red waistcoat and a cabbage-leaf collar, impudent, greedy, lecherous and wise. It is the popular part of the park:
  - wooden **Stegreif** (improvised) stages where the Krautwastl and his troupe mock the court, the Church, the Kesrâni and the Ilsenau, changing the jokes daily;
  - puppet booths and swings;
  - a carousel of wooden horses turned by a mule;
  - sausage-stalls, beer-tents, card-readers, a wax-works of famous murderers, and a booth where a strongwoman lifts gentlemen;
  - and the **Ägidi Fair** every September (→ I63).
- **The royal menagerie**: a lion, bears, a camel, an Asagiri crane, and **Selim the elephant**, the Padishah's gift of 1685 (→ I10).
- **The thickets** at dusk: the most democratic bedroom in the city.

### The Wiedenwald and the Heurigen
The wooded hills southwest of the city, with **vine-villages** on their lower slopes: **Lindenbichl**, **Rebdorf**, **Seidlach**, **Kahlhof**. By the **Buschenschank-Privileg of 1587**, growers may sell their own new wine in their own courtyards without a tavern licence, when they hang a **pine-bush** over the gate.
- The **Heuriger** ("this year's," the new wine and the place where it's poured): long wooden tables under walnut trees, wine by the **Seidel**, cold meat and bread, fiddle and Hackbrett music, the **Dreher** danced on the trampled earth (→ I11), Gstanzl, and **every class at the same table**. Coachmen sit beside counts, Wäschermädel beside Stiftsdamen in disguise.
- It is where Wiedenbrunn goes to be happy and sad at once, and where mistakes are made.

### The river
- **The Plättenkai** quays: the landing for the **Plätten** and the **Ordinari** boats (→ I21), with fish-market, salt-store and the boatmen's taverns.
- **The Badeplätze**: river bathing places with a fence between men and women that doesn't reach far enough into the water (→ I10).
- **The islands of the Selde**: duels at dawn, lovers at night, the city's ice-houses in winter.

## 3. The city's people

- **The court**: the royal family, the Seven Chairs and the great houses with their thousands of servants. A quarter of the inner city's people live in or off the Greifenburg.
- **The Bürger**: guildmasters, merchants, confectioners, coffeehouse-keepers, physicians, lawyers and Hofräte. Respectable, prudish in parlors, avid in coffeehouses.
- **The little people**: Fiaker drivers and Sesselträger (→ I21); **Wäschermädel**, the laundresses of the river-meadows, loud, free and in songs (→ R08); Wasserer (water-carriers); market women; Bänkelsänger; servants; Zettelweiber; link-boys; and **Bettgeher**.
- **The bound**: pledges in every great house, Kesrâni captives in the coffee-shops, Rocalban Erbleute in the Carrer Rocalbès (→ R07).
- **Foreigners**: Almagran grandees, Ormándi gentry, Velloran bankers and courtesans, Sworn Valleys pikemen, Kesrâni traders and envoys, and **the Asagiri embassy** at the **House of Nine Screens** (→ I37).
- **The clergy**: the Archbishop's household, the Bride's nuns at the Milchsäule chapel, the Open Hand's ransomers, the Lautere preachers in their plain meeting-house in the Hohlweg (→ I74).
- **Students** of the Hohe Schule (→ I19).

## 4. A year in the city

- **Fasching** (January to Aschentag): balls, Redouten, sledging, Schlackenball (→ I70, I16).
- **Läuterwochen**: closed theatres, sermons, Biberschwanz (→ I05).
- **Brauttag**: the procession, the Damenkarussell, the Corso, the Mondempfang (→ I14).
- **Lichtmess** (2 February): hiring day for servants (→ I04).
- **Spring**: the Fensterl season, the Brautwochen weddings, the Kornau stud show.
- **June**: the **Hofreise** to Bad Sulzau. The city empties of the court and fills with Bürger enjoying the quiet (→ I21).
- **The Hochzeitsnacht**: fires on the Wiedenwald hills and the Selde islands (→ I15).
- **September**: the court returns for the **Ägidi Fair** in the Hirschau (→ I63), the Sturm in the Heurigen, the hunts.
- **November**: **Haldentag**, the churchyards lit with red lanterns (→ I18).
- **Erbentag** and the **Zwölften**: candles in every window, the Haldenläufer in the Vorstädte (→ I16).

## 5. Local custom, tension and a place worth visiting

- **Custom**: the **Wiedenbrunn funeral** (*eine schöne Leich*, → I18), the **Heuriger** (§2), the **coffeehouse** (→ I67), the **hand-kiss** (→ I01), the **Jour** (→ I02), and the habit of saying the opposite of what you mean in a tone that makes it clear.
- **Tensions**:
  - **The Rocalban and Kesrâni quarters** (§2).
  - **The guilds against the pledge-trade**: the weavers' guild riots against cheap pledged labor once a decade. The last was in 1691, when they burned a Seelenmakler's office (→ I61).
  - **The Bürger against the court**: the Kleiderordnung, the Rosenband fashion, the Chastity Commission, all fought out in the coffeehouses (→ I06, I77).
  - **The Lautere** preachers in the Hohlweg against the Rosengrund.
  - **Bread prices** in hard winters.
- **Worth a visit**:
  - **the Heuriger at Lindenbichl** on a Sturm evening in September;
  - **the Wastlwiese** on a Sunday afternoon, to hear the Krautwastl mock someone you know;
  - **the Milchsäule** at dawn, to read the night's lampoons before the Rumorwache scrapes them off;
  - **the Bastei** at sunset, walking on the old walls with the whole city's roofs below;
  - **Café Sküs** on the Graben, for the betting-book (→ I67).

## HOOKS

1. **The Krautwastl's new joke.** The Wastlwiese's Krautwastl has a new routine: "the sleeping prince who woke up in a house full of women and asked where the men had gone." The audience roars. The Ilsenau want it stopped. The protagonist, in disguise in the crowd, is recognized by the actor mid-joke.
2. **The quarter-brawl.** A brawl between the Carrer Rocalbès and the Kesrâner Viertel breaks out while the protagonist is visiting the Confraria de l'Hereu's house. He is pulled to safety into a Kesrâni coffee-roaster's cellar by a young woman who would be in serious trouble if her quarter knew whom she rescued.
3. **The Bastei at sunset.** A lady who will not be received at the Palais Ilsenau asks the protagonist, by a card left with a torn corner, to walk with her on the Bastei at sunset. Everyone walks on the Bastei at sunset, which is either the point or the problem.
4. **The Heuriger table.** At a Lindenbichl Heuriger the protagonist, in disguise, finds himself at a long table between a Fiaker driver, a Stiftsdame in disguise, a Lautere preacher's runaway daughter and a Velloran courtesan, all of whom have recognized him.
5. **The Milchsäule.** One dawn the night's lampoon pasted on the Milk Column is not a mockery. It is a love poem to the protagonist, in very good verse, signed with a crescent. The Rumorwache scrapes it off, and three copies are already in the coffeehouses.
6. **Selim.** The royal elephant has taken a liking to the protagonist on a visit to the menagerie and won't let go of his sleeve with its trunk. The Kesrâni ambassador, who visits Selim every Venustag, watches with interest, and says through his interpreter that in Kesrân this would be an omen.
