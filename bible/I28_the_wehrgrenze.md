# I28 · The Wehrgrenze (the Military Frontier)

*The armed border with Kesrân: soldier-farmers who hold their land in return for lifelong service, great family households of forty cousins, watch-houses on stilts every mile along the river, a star-fortress, raids that the truce was supposed to end, the market in captives and ransoms, women who swear to live as men, and the bridge where the peace was signed and will have to be signed again.*

## 1. The land

**Die Wehrgrenze** (the defense-border; in the Grenzer's own speech **Krajina**, "the borderland") is a belt of land some twenty to forty miles deep along the river **Velika**, **six days southeast of Wiedenbrunn**. It stretches from the southern mountains to the Ormándi plains. Across the Velika lies **Kesrân** (→ I34).

It was created in **1532**, after the **First Siege of Wiedenbrunn** (1529), when the Crown settled the empty, raided borderland with **refugee families** fleeing Kesrâni rule from the south: **Grenzer** of mixed Slavic stock, with their own speech, faith-customs and family law. They were given land free of rent, tax and Robot in return for **lifelong military service**, every man from sixteen to sixty, and for **guarding the river**. It is not governed by any Landtag. It belongs body and soul to the **Hofkriegsrat** (war council), and through it, since 1650, mostly to **House Starkenberg** (→ I40).

The country: river-forests of willow and oak; marshes full of frogs, herons and fever; wheat and maize; plum orchards; watch-towers; and villages of wooden houses with deep eaves around a church and a well. In the south it is mountains of karst and pine.

## 2. The Grenzer and the Hauskommunion

- **The Grenzer** (borderers) are farmers and soldiers at once. They plow with a musket slung on their back, and when the signal-fires go up they leave the plow in the furrow.
- **The Hauskommunion** (house-community; Grenzer **zadruga**): the frontier's family unit. **Twenty to fifty people**, several generations of brothers, cousins, their wives and children, live together in a cluster of houses around a great central hall. They hold all land, cattle and property in common, eat at one table and work under one head:
  - **The Hausvater** (Grenzer *domaćin*), the house-father, usually the eldest capable man (elected by the house's men if the eldest is a fool), who rules the work, the money and the marriages.
  - **The Hausmutter** (*domaćica*), his counterpart, who rules the women, the kitchen, the weaving, the dowries, and in practice a great deal else.
- **Cousins as brothers**: in a Hauskommunion first cousins grow up as brothers and sisters, under one roof. This has produced the frontier's peculiar kinship rule. **Cousin love is ordinary and common**, and many marriages are between cousins of neighboring houses. But **sibling love is a horror**: with forty cousins in one house, the line has to be drawn somewhere hard. A sibling pair caught together are driven out of the Hauskommunion forever. The Grenzer are the strictest people in the realm about it, and find Wiedenbrunn's nobility disgusting (→ R06).
- **Sworn kin**:
  - **Pobratimstvo** (sworn brotherhood): two men who drink each other's blood from a cut palm in wine, before witnesses and an icon, are brothers for life, closer than blood. They avenge each other, inherit from each other and are buried together. Some sworn brothers are also lovers. The Grenzer say this is different, and it is.
  - **Posestrimstvo** (sworn sisterhood): the same between two women, or between a woman and a man who swears to protect her as a brother, which forbids them to each other forever, by the strongest taboo on the frontier.
- **Schwurjungfern** (sworn maidens): in a Hauskommunion with **no sons** to inherit, take the place at the table and carry the gun, a daughter may **swear before the elders to live as a man**. She cuts her hair, wears men's clothes, carries arms, takes a man's name, sits with the men, inherits as a son, and is **addressed and treated as a man for life**. She vows never to marry. The Grenzer respect Schwurjungfern absolutely. Several have been officers. Wiedenbrunn finds them fascinating, and the Doppelrose has had one perform, who left after one night in disgust (→ R09).

## 3. The defenses

- **Festung Senjak**: the frontier's chief fortress-city, on a bend of the Velika. It is a **star-fort** of low thick earthen bastions faced with brick, moats and ravelins, rebuilt after the Second Siege on the newest pattern, with a garrison of four thousand. Inside are a town of barracks, magazines, churches, taverns and a market. The **Generalkommando** (frontier command) sits there. Senjak's fortress commander is always a Starkenberg man.
- **The Tschardaken** (watch-houses): wooden **watch-houses on stilts**, one every mile or two along the river. Each holds a squad of six to twelve Grenzer, a signal-beacon, a drum and a dog. A Kesrâni raid is signaled from Tschardak to Tschardak by **fire at night and smoke by day**, and the whole frontier can be under arms within hours.
- **The Grenzer regiments**: infantry (*Grenzinfanterie*), excellent light troops who fight in skirmish order from cover, with long rifles; and **Grenzhusaren** (frontier hussars) for raids. Grenzer sharpshooters win the Schützenfest prizes every year (→ I10).
- **Saturnblei**: Grenzer captains cast **Schlafkugeln**, sleep-shot of Saturn-lead, in their own molds, though **Article Nine** of the Willow Bridge truce forbids it (→ R03). The Kesrâni call them "the dirty bullets." The Hofkriegsrat officially knows nothing.

## 4. The Willow Bridge

**Die Weidenbrücke** (the Willow Bridge) is a long wooden bridge on stone piers over the Velika at the village of **Vrbica** ("willow-place"). It is the frontier's one great crossing, with a **gate in the middle** and a guardhouse at each end, Valdauer and Kesrâni.

- In **1684**, after the Second Siege, the **Peace of the Willow Bridge** was signed **in a tent pitched over the bridge's middle gate**, half on each side, in a **Jupiter hour on a Jupitertag** (→ R02, I76). It was a **twenty-year truce**: no raids, no new captives, no Saturnblei (Article Nine), an exchange of captives, open trade at the bridge, and the Padishah's gift of an elephant.
- **It expires in 1704.** Renewal must be negotiated at the bridge. **The war party** (Starkenberg, the Hammerherren, the Grenzer who miss raiding) wants it to lapse and the lower Velika retaken. **The peace party** (Holdegg, the coffee trade, the Kornau landowners who don't want their peasants conscripted) wants it renewed (→ R01).
- **Exchange days**: on certain feast days each year, captives ransomed or exchanged under the truce are walked across the bridge from each side at the same moment. The two lines pass each other at the middle gate. Families wait at each end. It is the frontier's most moving sight.
- **The bridge market**: on market days traders meet at the middle gate to sell coffee, silk, salt, iron, horses and people.

## 5. Raids, captives and ransom

The truce **never quite stopped the raids**.
- **The Wind-riders** (*Windreiter*): Kesrâni light cavalry raiders of the border-beys, who cross the Velika by night in small bands, burn a farm, take cattle and **captives** (women and young men above all, for the markets of Daryabad), and are back over the river by dawn. The Kesrâni court disowns them. Their beys share the profits.
- **Grenzer counter-raids** (*Streifzüge*): Grenzer bands cross the other way and do the same. The captives they take are sold in **Rocalba**, through Llucmar's **Llotja dels Captius** (→ I29), or kept on Grenzer farms. The Barons **Vranić**, a frontier robber-noble house now respectable, made their fortune this way (→ I45).
- **The Loskauf** (ransom): a captive's family may buy them back. Ransom is arranged through brokers who cross freely under truce:
  - Valdau's **Order of the Open Hand**, ransoming monks who offer themselves as hostages (→ I62);
  - Kesrâni ransom-dealers called **fidyeci**;
  - Velloran bankers, who lend the ransom at interest;
  - and the Grenzer themselves.
  
  A **Loskaufbrief** (ransom-letter) is a contract specifying the captive, the price and the exchange day. Prices run from 20 Greif for a peasant to thousands for an officer or a pretty woman of good family.
- **The single gold earring**: a Grenzer who has taken a captive in a raid wears a gold earring in his left ear. Some Grenzer have two.
- **What captives become**: Valdauer captives in Kesrân become household slaves, soldiers, concubines and occasionally viziers (→ I34). Kesrâni captives in Valdau become galley-oarsmen, coffee-servants, concubines and Grenzer farmhands (→ R07). Some of each marry their captors. A few come home and want to go back.

## 6. Customs of the frontier

- **The greeting**: *"Pomoz' Bog!"* ("God help you!"), answered *"Bog ti pomogo!"*, the old formula the Grenzer keep despite the Wedding Church (→ I01).
- **The Grenzerbinde**: Grenzer soldiers knot a cloth at the throat. Wiedenbrunn's officers copied it, and it became the **cravat** of court fashion (→ I06).
- **The Kabanica**: the great undyed wool cloak that is a Grenzer's coat, blanket and tent.
- **The Kolo**: the long chain-dance of linked hands, men and women, circling faster and faster to bagpipe and drum until somebody falls (→ I11).
- **The Gusle**: a one-stringed bowed fiddle. Old singers, the **guslari**, chant long **epic songs** of frontier heroes, raids, captivities, sworn brothers and doomed loves, for hours, by the fire. A new song about a living Grenzer is the highest honor, and the old men compose them about every raid.
- **The Hausheiligentag**: each Hauskommunion's own patron saint's day, its great feast of the year, with a candle, a cake, the whole house and every neighbor.
- **Plum brandy**: **Zwetschkenbrand**, distilled in every yard, drunk at every meeting, offered to every guest, refused by nobody (→ X03).
- **Women's arms**: Grenzer women can shoot and defend the Tschardaken when the men are away. A woman who held a tower is sung by the guslari.

## 7. Tensions

- **Peace or war in 1704** (§4).
- **The Grenzer against the Hofkriegsrat**: the Grenzer's privileges (no tax, no Robot, their own law, their own officers) are always under threat from Wiedenbrunn's clerks, who want them taxed, and from Starkenberg, who want them as regular regiments. The Grenzer mutinied in 1658 and 1689, and the Crown gave way both times.
- **Article Nine**: Saturnblei. Both sides break it, and the war party is collecting evidence the Kesrâni broke it first (→ R03).
- **The raids**, and the captive trade that profits so many on both banks.
- **Deserters**: soldiers of the Inhaber regiments who flee into the Grenzer's forests, where nobody will give them up.
- **The Open Hand's double trade** (→ I62).

---

## 8. Local custom, tension and a place worth visiting

- **Custom**: the Hauskommunion and the Hausvater's table; sworn brothers and sworn sisters; the Schwurjungfer; the gold earring; the Kolo; the gusle; plum brandy for every guest.
- **Tension**: 1704; the privileges; the raids; Article Nine.
- **Worth a visit**:
  - **an exchange day on the Willow Bridge**;
  - **a Tschardak at night** when a signal fire goes up down-river;
  - **a Hauskommunion's Hausheiligentag**, forty cousins at one table and a guslar singing.

## HOOKS

1. **Exchange day.** At an exchange on the Willow Bridge, a Valdauer woman captive of six years walks back across from the Kesrâni side, and then stops at the middle gate and won't come further. Her husband is waiting at the Valdauer end, and her Kesrâni master at the other. She asks for "someone of the Seven Chairs" to hear her before she chooses.
2. **The Schwurjungfer.** A Grenzer Schwurjungfer, an ensign of the Senjak garrison sent to Wiedenbrunn with dispatches, is billeted, through the Hofkriegsrat's carelessness, in the Palais Ilsenau's men's wing as "Fähnrich Marko." The Ilsenau women find this delightful, and Marko does not.
3. **The pobratim.** A young Grenzer officer the protagonist meets at the Ballhaus offers, half-drunk and wholly serious, to swear blood-brotherhood with him in the old way: a cut palm, wine and an icon. In Grenzer law it is the closest bond there is, and it would make the protagonist responsible for his blood-feuds.
4. **Article Nine.** A Grenzer captain brings Wiedenbrunn a box of Saturnblei bullets he swears he took from Kesrâni Wind-riders, proof the Kesrâni broke the truce first. If it's true, the war party has its war. If he cast them himself, someone in the household's circle paid him to.
5. **The Wind-riders.** On a visit to the frontier the protagonist's party is caught in a Tschardak by a night raid. The signal-fires go up, the Grenzer women load the guns, and in the morning there's a Kesrâni rider in the ditch, alive, who turns out to be a woman.
6. **The ransom.** A Loskaufbrief arrives at the Palais Ilsenau for a Valdauer captive in Daryabad: a Weißach silver-miner's daughter, taken by Wind-riders, who has given the name of the Ilsenau house as her ransomer, "because the Moon looks after its own." The price is high, and the broker is a Velloran.
