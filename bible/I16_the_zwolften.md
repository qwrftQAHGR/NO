# I16 · The Zwölften: Erbentag, the Haldenläufer & the Lead-Pouring

*Midwinter: the feast of the Heir's birth, and the twelve nights after it when the year is outside itself. Masters serve servants, the dead walk, masked runners chase the living through the snow, nobody spins (so the Moon-house's council cannot sit), and on the last night the realm pours lead into water to read the year.*

## 1. Erbentag: the Heir's birth

**Erbentag** (Heir's Day; Rocalban **el dia de l'Hereu**) falls on the **winter solstice**, the longest night. In the faith it is the day **the Heir was born**: the androgynous third person, child of the Bridegroom and Bride, whose two natures are the world's soul (→ I54).

The Heir was conceived on the Hochzeitsnacht at midsummer and born at midwinter: **six months, half the time.** The Church calls this **die Eile des Erben**, the Heir's haste. Zeugen preach it every Erbentag as the urgency of grace, the Heir hurrying to join the Feast. Since the Quickening, that sermon will be heard differently by certain people in certain houses (→ R13).

### The day's customs
- **The Erbenkerze** (Heir's candle): a single white candle in every window from dusk to dawn, so the Heir can find the house. In Wiedenbrunn the whole city's windows are lit, and the Greifenburg's thousand windows are a sight.
- **The Erbenmahl** (Heir's feast): the great family dinner of the year, with roast goose, red cabbage, chestnuts and **Erbenbrot**. That is a braided loaf of two doughs, one sweet with honey and raisins and one savory with caraway and salt, twisted together, the two natures of the Heir. Each person tears off a piece and must take some of both. A child who picks only the sweet will be teased all year.
- **Gifts "from the Heir"**: exchanged on Erbentag morning. Family to family, lovers to lovers, masters to servants. Servants' gifts are traditionally a length of cloth, a coin, and a pair of shoes.
- **Die Verkehrte Tafel** (the upside-down table): at the Erbenmahl in great houses, by old custom, **the masters serve the servants**. The Fürstin carries the soup, the young lord pours the wine, the daughters of the house wait at the Gesindetisch. It is half charity and half carnival, and the servants enjoy it enormously and carefully. A pledge served by her master is a famous moment in a hundred songs. What a servant says to a master while being served at the Verkehrte Tafel can't be punished. It is remembered.
- **Erbentag Mahl**: the midnight liturgy, with the choir of the Bride's nuns singing the **Erbenlied**, the oldest hymn of the faith.

## 2. The Zwölften (the twelve nights)

From Erbentag to **Neujahr** (New Year) run **die Zwölften**, the Twelve: twelve nights that belong to neither the old year nor the new. The church year has ended, the next hasn't begun, and **the year is outside itself.**

### What happens in the Twelve Nights
- **The dead walk.** The **Schlacken**, souls of the dross not yet refined on the **Halde** (→ I54, I18), are believed to wander in the Zwölften, looking for the warmth of their old homes. Villagers leave a bowl of milk and a piece of Erbenbrot on the table at night.
- **Houses are smoked** (*Räuchern*): on the first, sixth and twelfth nights the head of the household walks every room, stable and attic with a pan of embers, incense, juniper and a pinch of sulphur, smoking out the Schlacken. Great houses hire a Zeuge to do it with a silver censer.
- **Doors are chalked**: on the first night the Zeuge chalks the **Stundenkreide** over every door, the seven planetary signs and the new year's number, to bar the door to the dead and bless the house's hours (→ R02).
- **Work rests.** No washing is hung out, since the dead might wear it. No wheels turn, meaning no carts and no mills. And **no spinning**: the dead walk on spun thread, and a household that spins in the Zwölften will have a death within the year. Spinning-wheels are tied up with ribbon and spindles put away.
- **Dreams in the Zwölften are prophetic.** Each night's dream forecasts the matching month of the coming year.
- **Animals speak** at midnight on the sixth night, and anyone who hears them will die within the year, so nobody goes to the stable at midnight on the sixth.

### The Spindelrat cannot sit
At House Ilsenau, where the women's council governs **while spinning** and votes by **passing the spindle** (→ R12), the custom of the Zwölften has a famous consequence. **For twelve nights the Spindelrat cannot meet, and the house has no council.** The **Hausmutter rules alone.**
- Decisions made in the Zwölften are hers and hers only, and by house custom **cannot be undone** by the council when it sits again at Neujahr.
- Hausmütter have used the Zwölften for the house's boldest acts for two centuries: marriages arranged, heirs designated, debts settled, secrets kept or told.
- The house's women watch the Hausmutter very carefully every Erbentag, and some of them watch each other.

## 3. The Haldenläufer (the Slag-Runners)

On the **fifth night** and the **twelfth night** of the Zwölften, the **Haldenläufer** run.

They are bands of masked young people, mostly the village **Zeche** (→ I60) and in towns the guild apprentices, who dress as the dead of the **Halde** and run through the villages and streets in the snow. They come in two troupes:

**Die Schlacken** (the Dross):
- **hideous**: carved wooden masks with horns, tusks, long tongues and goat-hair beards;
- coats of **sheepskin and furs** turned inside out;
- huge **cowbells** at the waist that clang as they run;
- **switches** of birch or willow.

They are the unrefined dead: ugly, noisy, wild. They chase people through the streets, especially **young women**, and catch them and **switch** them on the legs and backside. This is the **Pfeffern** ("peppering"), which is said to bring health and fertility for the year. Then they demand a forfeit, a kiss, a schnapps or a coin, before letting go. Being caught by the Schlacken is half terror and half the point. Girls who are never caught are teased for it, and girls caught too often are teased for that.

**Die Feinen** (the Fine Ones):
- **beautiful**: delicate painted masks; tall headdresses hung with **mirrors**, silver foil, ribbons and bells; white embroidered costumes;
- often **young women**, **futas** and **Zierherren** (→ R10, R11).

They are the refined dead, the souls already purified. They walk, slow and silent, flashing their mirrors into windows to bless the houses, and leave a ribbon on the doors of houses where a wedding or a birth is wished for. They receive gifts of food and coin. They do not chase. They sometimes choose.

The two troupes meet in the village square at midnight. There is a mock battle, the Feinen "refine" the Schlacken by touching them with their mirrors, everyone unmasks, and the night ends in the inn.

### In Wiedenbrunn
The guild apprentices run in the **Vorstädte**. Inside the walls, since 1688, the Rumorwache "forbids" the Haldenläufer and stands aside while they run (→ I20). The court has its own version, the **Schlackenball** on the twelfth night: a masked ball in which the court's young men come as Schlacken and the court's ladies as Feine. It is the wildest night of the court year and the opening of the Fasching season (→ I70).

## 4. The last night: the Bleigießen

On the twelfth night, **Neujahrsnacht** (New Year's Night), everyone reads the coming year by **pouring lead**.
- A small lump of **lead** is melted in an iron spoon over a candle-flame and **poured into a bowl of cold water**. It hisses and sets into a strange shape.
- The shape is held up against the candle, its shadow read on the wall, and interpreted:
  - a **ship**: a journey;
  - a **ring**: a wedding;
  - a **heart**: love;
  - a **cradle**: a child;
  - a **crown**: advancement;
  - a **key**: a secret learned, or a door opened;
  - a **tree**: health;
  - a **coin**: money;
  - a **snake**: an enemy;
  - a **broken shape**: a loss;
  - a shape like **a Blatt** (a card-mark): **a fall in your house this year**, which is the one shape nobody wants.
- **Lead is Saturn's metal**, which rules time and the turning of the year. The Bleigießen is the one folk custom that the Work, the Church and the Stundenamt all approve.

### Kalteneck's Bleinacht
At the court, the most famous Bleigießen is **House Kalteneck's Bleinacht** (Night of Lead), held each Neujahrsnacht in the great hall of Palais Kalteneck (→ I44).
- The Saturn-house, keeper of lead and of the royal crypt, pours **its own lead**, from the Bleiküche, into **black water** in silver basins.
- The readings are made by the **bearer of the Thirteenth Trump**, the Death-card that knows the day of a person's death (→ I46).
- The court comes, half for the spectacle and half in dread. The bearer never says *when* anyone will die, only what the lead shows. Courtiers have been known to leave the Bleinacht and make their wills.
- Invitations are coveted and feared in equal measure.

### Silbergießen
**The Ilsenau** pour **silver** instead of lead, a Moon-house's privilege and a showy expense, in the Mondsaal with all the mirrors lit. Silver shapes, the house says, show **the heart's** year, not fortune's. It is the house's private custom, with the Spindelrat's women and the house's guests, and it ends the Zwölften. Next morning the spindles come out again.

## 5. Neujahr (New Year)

- **The Neujahrsbesuch**: on New Year's morning the **first person over the threshold** decides the house's luck (→ I13). A dark-haired man is good luck, a **futa** double luck, a Zeuge bad. Households arrange their first-footer carefully.
- **Neujahrsgeld**: New Year's tips to every servant of every house one visits (→ I04).
- **Neujahrswünsche**: New Year's greetings, written on the backs of playing cards and left at every door one knows (→ I02). Hearts and Copes for friends, Kreuz and Bastos for family, an Ace for the only one.
- **The Spindelrat sits again**, and learns what the Hausmutter did in the Zwölften.

## 6. Rocalba: el Tió and les Escòries

- **El Tió de l'Hereu** (the Heir's log): a log with a painted face, a red barretina and a blanket over its back is set by the hearth on the first night of the Twelve. The household **feeds** it each night with bread, nuts and dried fruit. On the last night everyone **beats it with sticks** while singing the Tió song, and then lifts the blanket to find **gifts** "the Tió has given": sweets, nuts, small presents. Children love it. Adults love it more.
  - **El Tió de les Dames** is the ladies' version at Rocalban parties, with gifts of naughty objects, love-tokens and Velloran glass Tröster (→ I08). It is a scandal every year and an institution.
- **El Llit de les Noces** (the Wedding Bed): Rocalban households build a **miniature scene** on a table. It shows the Bridegroom and Bride in their wedding bed, the Heir's cradle beside them, the guests of the Feast arriving from every direction (shepherds, kings, washerwomen, soldiers), and the hills of Montalba behind. It is built anew each year with clay figures bought at the **Fira de Figures** in Llucmar. By old custom, **somewhere** in every scene, hidden, is **el Pixaner**, a little clay figure of a peasant relieving himself behind a bush. Children search for him, adults swear he's essential to the Bride's luck, and the Bridegroom's See has tried to ban him since 1580.
- **Les Escòries**: the Rocalban slag-runners, masked and running with fireworks rather than bells (→ I15).

## HOOKS

1. **The Verkehrte Tafel.** At the Ilsenau Erbenmahl, by custom the son of the house must serve the servants' table. The Schlafwärterinnen of the Stille Haus, who served him for seventeen years, sit at it, and one of them has prepared what she will say to him while he pours her wine. By custom it can't be punished.
2. **The Hausmutter's Zwölften.** On the second night of the Zwölften, with the spindles tied up and the Spindelrat unable to sit, the Hausmutter summons the protagonist alone to her Kabinett. Whatever she decides tonight can't be undone.
3. **Caught by the Schlacken.** On the fifth night the Schlacken run through the village below Schloss Ilsenau. A masked runner with a horned mask and a birch switch catches the protagonist in the snow, mistaking him in the dark for a girl. Then the runner realizes, and doesn't let go.
4. **The Feinen's ribbon.** On the twelfth night a band of Feinen passes the Palais, mirrors flashing, and a slender masked figure leaves a white ribbon on the door: *"a wedding or a birth is wished for."* The household spends the night arguing about which, and who wished it.
5. **The Bleinacht.** At Kalteneck's lead-pouring, the protagonist's lead sets in a shape the Thirteenth-bearer looks at for a long time. She says only, *"I've seen this shape once before,"* and moves on to the next guest. Later that night a Kalteneck footman delivers a sealed note asking when the protagonist might call.
6. **El Tió de les Dames.** At a Rocalban party in Wiedenbrunn the ladies' Tió is beaten, the blanket lifted, and among the gifts is one clearly meant for the protagonist, with a card on it in a Rocalban hand he doesn't know. It is not a toy for anyone else in the room.
