# I07 · The Toilette: Beauty, Patch and Fan

*The late-morning ritual of being made beautiful in front of an audience; the cosmetics of the Work and what they cost the skin; the nine places of a patch and what each one says; the fan, the glove and the hair; and the realm's ideals of beauty, read against the protagonist's face.*

**See also:** I06 The Kleiderordnung & Tracht · I03 The Palais & its Hours · I02 The Card Visit · R03 The Work · R08 Desire & Custom · R11 Femme Men · I37 Asagiri

---

## 1. The Toilette as a reception

Between about **ten and noon** a lady of standing, or a gentleman of fashion, is made fit to be seen. Being dressed is a public event. They sit at the **Toilettentisch**, a dressing-table hung with lace, set with a mirror of Velloran glass and laid out with silver boxes, pots, brushes, patch-cases, pins and powder. Around them, while the Kammerfrau and Zofen work, a small court comes and goes:

- **intimate friends**, who sit on the low stools and gossip;
- **the Toilettenkavalier** (see below);
- **the Friseur** (hairdresser), with irons and pomade and the latest from the Graben;
- **tradesmen**: milliners, lace-merchants and jewelers with boxes;
- **musicians** practicing a song, a **poet** with verses, and a **house Zeuge** with a letter;
- **petitioners** who have bribed the Kammerfrau for a quarter hour;
- and in the great houses, **the lady's children**, brought in by their governess to kiss her hand and be sent away.

The lady sits in her **Negligé**, a loose silk gown over her shift with her hair down, and is undressed and dressed by degrees in front of everyone. The order is fixed: shift, stays, stockings and garters, petticoats, stomacher pinned, gown, hair, face, patches, jewels. Which stage a visitor is admitted at is a precise measure of intimacy. **Being present when the stays are laced** is a friend's privilege. **Being present for the stockings** is a lover's, or a scandal's.

A gentleman's Toilette (his *Lever*) is the same with a razor, a wig-block and a valet.

**The Toilettenkavalier**: a gentleman, often a Zierherr (→ R11), who attends a lady's toilette **daily**. He hands her the patches, holds the mirror, reads her letters aloud, approves the gown and carries her gossip to the next house. Every great lady has one. His position is public, harmless, and assumed by all to be more than it looks. Husbands tolerate him because he is so obviously ornamental. Husbands are sometimes wrong (→ R08).

## 2. Paint and powder

All paint is the Work's (→ R03), and all of it costs the skin something.

### White
- **Bleiweiß** (lead-white): ceruse made from Saturn's lead and vinegar, mixed with egg-white or oil, laid on face, throat and bosom for the fashionable dead-white skin. It covers everything: pox scars, freckles, age, the flush of the Mondhaut. It also slowly poisons, giving grey skin underneath (**Bleiblässe**), headaches, lost teeth and lost hair. Women who wear it for years can't stop, because what's underneath is worse. House **Kalteneck's** Bleiküche makes the finest and safest, which is to say the slowest (→ I44).
- **Perlweiß** (pearl-white): powdered pearl and Moon-silver salts. Safer, luminous, and ruinously expensive. The Ilsenau women wear it, and every lady who can afford it copies them.
- **Puder** (powder): wheat starch or orris root, white or faintly tinted, puffed onto the face over the paint, and since the 1690s onto the hair. Powder-closets with a hole for the face are the latest luxury.
- **Blattschminke**: thick lead-white paste for covering a **Blatt** (a card-mark) on the skin, used by card-bearers who prefer their gift unknown (→ R04).

### Red
- **Venusrot** (Venus-red): rouge of cochineal and copper salts. Laid on the cheeks, and in the Almagran fashion very heavily: two perfect round red circles, the **Almagraner Rot**, which Valdauers think looks like a fever and Almagrans think looks like health. The Valdauer fashion is a softer flush high on the cheekbone.
- **Lippenpomade**: tinted lip-salve of beeswax and alkanet root.
- **Kupfertropfen**: copper drops taken by mouth to bring a natural blush, for ladies who want the Mondhaut without being born to it.

### Dark
- **Kesrâner Schwarz**: kohl from the embassy, lining the eyes in the Kesrâni way. It was scandalous in 1685 and fashionable by 1695.
- **Brows**: plucked very thin and redrawn with burnt cork or a lead pencil. Bushy brows are peasant brows.
- **Eyelashes**: there is no cosmetic to lengthen them yet, and long lashes are envied, admired and written about in bad sonnets.

### The skin itself
- **Merkurpaste**: quicksilver salve used as a **depilatory** and as a **skin-peel** (it strips the top layer of skin and freckles with it). It is dangerous, burning, and fashionable. The smooth court fashion (**glatt**) calls for ladies to remove all body hair below the brows. Rocalban ladies don't, and find the Valdauer fashion childish. Zierherren do (→ R11).
- **Schönheitswasser** (beauty-water):
  - **Das Wasser der Königin von Ormánd** ("the Queen of Ormánd's water"): rosemary distilled in spirit, splashed on the face and body. By legend it was made for an Ormándi queen of seventy who used it and was then proposed to by a king of twenty-five. Ladies over forty buy it by the bottle.
  - **Aigua de tarongina**: Rocalban orange-flower water, for the face and the bath.
  - **Rosenwasser** from Kesrân.
- **Teeth**: cleaned with powdered pumice, coral and cuttlefish bone on a linen rag; breath sweetened with cloves and cardamom. Missing teeth are hidden behind a fan, since missing teeth mean either age or mercury, and mercury means the pox (→ I66). The **Asagiri** ladies of the embassy **blacken their teeth** deliberately, which Valdauers find first horrifying and then, by some, unbearably erotic (→ I37).

### Perfume
- **Musk**, **civet** and **ambergris**: heavy animal scents, for evenings and for hiding the smell of a crowded ballroom.
- **Orange-flower** and **rosemary** for day.
- **Ambrahandschuhe**: Almagran gloves perfumed with ambergris, so the scent lingers on everything touched (see §5).
- **Pomanders** of silver filigree, worn on a chain and filled with spices.
- **Ilsenau's scent**: the house's women wear a faint perfume of white violet and silver salts, made in the Mondküche. It is the only perfume in Wiedenbrunn nobody can buy. Its absence from the shops is the point.

## 3. Patches

**Mouches** (Valdauers say **Mucken** or **Schönheitsfliegen**, "beauty-flies"): tiny shapes of black silk taffeta or velvet, gummed to the face and bosom. They began as a way to cover pox-marks and set off a white skin, and became a language.

They are kept in a **Muckendose**, a little patch-box with a mirror in the lid, carried everywhere. Shapes:
- **circles** (the classic);
- **crescents** (Ilsenau's fashion, now general);
- **stars**, **hearts**, **diamonds**;
- tiny **coaches-and-horses**, **cupids**, **birds** and **playing-card pips**;
- and on fashionable days a **Pagat**, the card for luck.

### The nine places, and what they say
The placements have names, fixed by the 1680s, and anyone at court can read them across a room:

| Place | Name | Says |
|---|---|---|
| **corner of the eye** | **die Schmachtende** (the Yearning One) | passion: "I am in love" |
| **middle of the cheek** | **die Galante** (the Gallant) | flirtation: "I am open to it" |
| **the smile-line, in the dimple** | **die Lustige** (the Merry One) | playfulness: "make me laugh" |
| **beside the lips** | **die Küssende** (the Kissing One) | an invitation, plainly |
| **on the nose** | **die Freche** (the Cheeky One) | impudence: "I don't care what you think" |
| **on the forehead** | **die Majestätische** (the Majestic) | dignity: "approach with respect" |
| **on the chin** | **die Verschwiegene** (the Discreet One) | "I can keep a secret" |
| **on the bosom** | **die Freigebige** (the Generous One) | an explicit invitation |
| **over a pimple** | **die Hehlerin** (the Fence, a receiver of stolen goods) | nothing; she's hiding something. It's a joke-name, used of anyone's patch that seems to be covering a blemish. |

Combinations make sentences. *Schmachtende* plus *Verschwiegene* means *I love you, and nobody will know*. Two patches of the same shape on one cheek, the **Zwei Mucken**, is a joke at a woman's expense, suggesting two lovers. **A crescent high on the cheekbone** is Ilsenau's fashion and is read as *Moon*: tender, fertile, and sapphic in certain company (→ R09). A **Pagat** patch means a gambler, or a woman who wants luck tonight.

Men wear patches too, the Zierherren especially. A man's patches are read the same way, with the added complication that the *Freigebige* on a man's chest is visible only when his shirt is open.

## 4. The fan

The **Fächer** is in every lady's hand from the Toilette to the Coucher, and in many gentlemen's.

- **Fahnenfächer** (flag-fans): the old rigid kind, a stiff screen on a handle. Grandmothers' fans.
- **Faltfächer** (folding fans): pleated paper, silk or lace on sticks of ivory, tortoiseshell or sandalwood, that open into a half-circle. They came from **Asagiri** by way of Kesrâni traders and Velloran merchants, and since the embassy arrived in the 1690s the real thing has been the rage. **Asagiri lacquered fans** painted with cranes, waves and ladies in layered silk are the most coveted object in Wiedenbrunn after a good Velloran mirror (→ I37).
- **Die Kehrseite** (the reverse side): a fan whose ribs open both ways, showing an innocent picture opened to the right and **a different picture** opened to the left, usually a lover or an act from the Thirty-Two Hours (→ I08). A lady can open her Kehrseite fan "by mistake" in front of exactly one person.

### Fächersprache (the language of the fan)
Every lady learns it in her Stiftsjahr (→ I19), and every gentleman learns it by getting it wrong:

| Gesture | Means |
|---|---|
| fan held shut to the lips | "be silent" or "kiss me," depending on the eyes |
| fan opened fully and held still | "wait for me" |
| fan snapped shut | "enough; we're done" |
| fan closed, pointed at the heart | "you have my heart" |
| fan tapped against the left palm | "follow me" |
| fan tapped against the chin | "you are being watched" |
| fan drawn across the cheek | "I love you" |
| fan drawn through the hand | "I hate you" |
| fan fluttered quickly | "I'm hot," which is to say "I'm interested" |
| fan dropped | "let's be friends," or an accident, which nobody believes |
| fan held over the left ear | "you have betrayed me" |
| fan held over the right ear | "you're mine" |

## 5. Gloves and other instruments

- **Gloves**: long kid gloves for evening, short for day. **Ambrahandschuhe** from Almagra, perfumed with ambergris, so whatever she touches smells of her for a day. *Taking off one glove* to offer the bare hand for the hand-kiss (→ I01) is intimate. *Giving* a glove is a token of love. *Dropping* one at a man's feet is an invitation or, in the Damenduell, a challenge (→ I71).
- **Muffs**: fur in winter, carried by ladies and Zierherren. A muff will hide a note, a lapdog, a pistol or a hand that is holding another's.
- **The Muckendose** (patch-box) and the **Tabatière** (snuffbox, → I05).
- **Handkerchiefs** of lace, embroidered with initials. Dropping one is old-fashioned and still works.

## 6. Hair

- **Women**: high in front over a wired frame, with the Turmhaube above (→ I06), and curls massed at the sides and falling behind. Much of it false: **Haarteile** (hairpieces) bought from the Rocalban hair-merchants, who buy it from poor women in the hill villages. The **Haarkauf**, the cutting and selling of a girl's hair, is the last resort before pledging (→ R07). Pomade of bear-grease or beef-marrow scented with jasmine. Powder, lightly, since the 1690s.
- **Men**: the **Allonge** wig, or **Eigenhaar** (→ I06, R11). The **Mondlocke** (a single lock loose at the temple with a silver ribbon) is Ilsenau's fashion for men, now general among Zierherren.
- **At night**: the **Nachthaube** (night-cap), lace for ladies and linen for gentlemen. A woman seen with her hair down outside her family and her women is either very intimate with the one who sees it, or ill.

## 7. Ideals of beauty

| Where | Women | Men |
|---|---|---|
| **Valdau** | **voll und weiß**, full and white: plump arms, a round bosom, very pale skin, a small mouth, white hands, dimples (*Grübchen*), a high flush on the cheek | good **Waden** (calves), small white hands, a good leg for the minuet, a fine profile; and since the 1680s, for the young, the slender Zierherr look |
| **Rocalba** | **la bellesa morena**: dark hair, dark eyes, olive skin, slenderness, a quick tongue | dark, lean, sharp, Mercury-quick |
| **Almagra** | dead-white skin with heavy red cheeks, rigid dignity, tiny feet | gravity, black clothes, a pointed beard, pallor |
| **Ormánd** | strong, ruddy, braided, laughing | mustaches, braid, horsemanship, size |
| **Kesrân** | the **moon-face** (round, pale, full), arched joined brows, kohl, henna | slenderness, grace, poetry, the beloved's down on the cheek |
| **Asagiri** | blackened teeth, shaved and repainted brows, white powder, hair to the floor, layered robes | elegance of hand, poetry, restraint |

### The protagonist, under Wiedenbrunn's eye
Wiedenbrunn will read him in one look, before anyone has learned anything else:
- **His lashes** are long enough that ladies will ask what he uses, and won't believe the answer.
- **His skin** flushes at a glance (**Mondhaut**), a natural *Venusrot* that every lady at the Toilette has to paint on, and that he can't paint off. He will be told this as a compliment, a joke and an accusation.
- **His hair**: long, dark, loose waves, the **Eigenhaar** fashion at its best. Someone will try to give him a Mondlocke ribbon by the end of the first week.
- **His mouth and eyes**: sharp and expressive, the house's face. Several ladies will want a crescent patch on him just to see it.
- **His frame**: slight, narrow-shouldered, long-fingered, femme: the **Zierherr** at its most fashionable.
- **And below the waist**: the court's oldest joke, the **Wolf im Schlafrock** (→ R11). Whoever is present at his Toilette when the Schlafrock comes off will have a story to dine on for a year, and the Toilette is, by custom, a reception.

## HOOKS

1. **His first Toilette.** By custom a member of a great house newly out in society receives at the Toilette. The household argues for a week over who may attend the protagonist's: which women, which friends, whether the Stiftsjahr girls, and at which stage. Then the day comes, and someone uninvited is already sitting on the stool.
2. **The Schmachtende.** At the Cercle, a lady across the room places a patch at the corner of her eye while looking straight at the protagonist. Her husband sees it, and so does the protagonist's cousin, who had placed a *Küssende* half an hour earlier.
3. **The Kehrseite.** A fan is returned to the household after a ball, having been "left behind" by a guest. Opened to the right it shows cranes. Opened to the left it shows two people, one of whom is recognizably a son of the house.
4. **Bleiblässe.** A great lady who has worn Bleiweiß for twenty years asks the Ilsenau Mondküche for Perlweiß she can't afford. She offers, in payment, something she knows about one of the household's women.
5. **The Toilettenkavalier.** A pleasant, witty Zierherr who has been Toilettenkavalier to an Ilsenau woman for eight years finds himself, since the waking, no longer needed at her toilette. He is hurt, jealous and full of eight years of things he heard in that room.
6. **The Pagat patch.** Before a card evening at which a great deal will be lost, a woman of the household asks the protagonist to put a Pagat patch on her for luck, "because a man's hand brings it." She tells him where.
