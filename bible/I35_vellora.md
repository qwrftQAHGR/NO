# I35 · Vellora

*The Venetian-flavored lagoon republic across the Middle Sea: a city on a hundred islands whose elected prince marries the sea every spring, a bank that owns half the debts of the known world and a family that holds the Wheel, glass that may not leave its island, a printed catalogue of courtesans with their prices, masks for half the year, night-wardens who arrest men for loving men while courtesans dress as boys, and brothers who share one marriage.*

**See also:** R01 (the Banco's debts) · I26 The Weißach (the mortgages) · I73 The Flask King · I08 (the Thirty-Two Hours, Velloran glass) · I09 (Basseta, the Lotto) · I19 (the Kavalierstour) · I23 (the Damen von Welt) · I66 The Lagoon-Pox · I46 (X, the Wheel; XV, the Devil)

---

## 1. The city

**La Serenissima Repubblica di Vellora** (the Most Serene Republic of Vellora) lies **across the Middle Sea**, five to eight days' sail southeast of Llucmar (→ I21). It is a city built on **a hundred islands** in a shallow lagoon, joined by four hundred bridges and threaded by canals, with the sea's long sandbar between it and the open water. About **150,000** people.
- **The Grand Canal**, lined with the patricians' palaces, the **Ca'** (from *casa*): *Ca' Lunardin*, *Ca' Morosin*, *Ca' Zenobio*, *Ca' Varisco*.
- **The Piazza Maggiore**, the great square before the cathedral of the **Sposa** (the Bride) and the **Palazzo del Sposo**, the palace of the Bridegroom of the Sea (§3).
- **The Ponte dei Cambi**, the bridge of the money-changers, where the **Banco della Fede** has its hall (§4).
- **The Isola dei Forni**, the island of furnaces, where the glass is made (§5).
- **The Isola del Merletto**, the island of lace.
- **The Arsenale**, the republic's shipyard, which builds a galley a day in wartime.
- **The Riva delle Catene**, the Pier of Chains, where slaves are sold (§8).
- **The Ridotto**, the state gaming-house (§6).
- **The terraferma**: Vellora's mainland holdings behind the lagoon, villas, farms and subject towns.

The light, the water, the decay, the masks, the music, the smell of the canals in August. Every Valdauer young lord on his Kavalierstour spends half his tour here and comes home changed and in debt (→ I19).

---

## 2. Names and ranks

- **The patriciate**: the noble merchant families inscribed in the **Libro d'Argento** (the Silver Book) since 1297. Only they sit in the councils. They are styled **N.H.** (*Nobil Homo*) and **N.D.** (*Nobil Donna*), and in speech **ser** and **siora**.
- **The cittadini** (citizens): the secretaries, notaries, physicians and great merchants who run the state's offices without ruling it.
- **The popolani**: gondoliers, glassblowers, arsenal-workers, fishermen and servants.
- **Names**: Venetian forms. *Zuane* (Giovanni), *Alvise*, *Bortolo*, *Marco*, *Piero*; *Marieta*, *Zanetta*, *Cate*, *Bianca*. Family names end often in *-in* or *-o*: *Lunardin*, *Lissandrin*, *Morosin*, *Gradenigo*.

---

## 3. Government: lo Sposo

- **Lo Sposo** (the Bridegroom) is the head of state, **elected for life** by the patricians through an elaborate ten-stage process of lots and votes designed to prevent any family from controlling it. He wears a horned cap of gold cloth and lives in the **Palazzo del Sposo**. In theory he is the republic's living symbol. In practice he is a prisoner of the councils, with less power than the youngest clerk of the Council of Nine.
- **The Sposalizio del Mare** (the Marriage of the Sea): every spring, on the octave of **Brauttag**, the Sposo is rowed out to the lagoon's mouth in the **Nave Nuziale** (the wedding-ship), a gilded state-barge with two hundred oarsmen. He throws a **gold ring** into the sea with the words *"Ti sposiamo, o mare, in segno di vero e perpetuo dominio"* ("We wed you, O sea, in sign of true and perpetual dominion"). In Velloran theology the Sposo is the Bridegroom's image and the Sea is his Bride, and Vellora is their marriage. **Montalba** thinks this is presumptuous. The **Bridegroom's See** thinks it is very presumptuous. Vellora doesn't care. Divers retrieve the ring afterward and sell it to a different buyer every year.
- **The Consiglio Grande** (Great Council): every adult male patrician, about two thousand, meeting on Sundays.
- **Il Senato**: foreign policy, trade and war.
- **Il Consiglio dei Nove** (the Council of Nine): the real power. Nine patricians elected for one year who handle state security, treason, secrets and spies. They meet in secret, judge in secret, and drown people in the lagoon at night. Their **bocche** (mouths), stone letterboxes in the walls of the city, receive anonymous denunciations.

## 4. The Banco della Fede (the Bank of Faith)

The greatest bank in the known world. It was founded in **1402** as a deposit-bank for merchants on the Ponte dei Cambi, and is now:
- **the treasury of the republic**;
- **lender to every crown**: Valdau (about eleven million Greif, rolled over since the Flask King, → R01, I73), Almagra, Ormánd, the Kesrâni beys, the Rocalban Corts, the Sworn Valleys' cantons, and half the great houses of all of them;
- **holder of mortgages**: the **Weißach silver mines** above all, on which it has been foreclosing since 1695 (→ I26);
- **issuer of letters of credit**, honored at its branches in every great city. A young lord on his Kavalierstour carries one (→ I19);
- **financier** of the Cinabre furnaces (→ I30), the Llucmar galleys, the Kesrâni coffee trade and the slave-trade.
- **The Lotto di Vellora**: the numbers lottery, drawn monthly, with agents in Wiedenbrunn and Llucmar. The poor bet Kupferlinge on numbers from their dream-books (→ I09).

### The Ca' Lunardin and the Wheel
The **Lunardin** family, the Banco's principal house for two hundred years, bear **Trump X, Das Rad**, the Wheel (Velloran *la Ruota*, → I46).
- **Gift**: **once a moon** the bearer can **reverse a fortune**: turn a winning hand into a losing one, a rising price into a falling one, a ship's luck, a bank's run, a battle's tide. Wherever the bearer is, luck swings wildly.
- **Burden**: **the bearer's own fortune swings** endlessly between riches and ruin, never settled.
- The Lunardin manage it as bankers manage anything. **Diversification**: the bearer owns nothing personally, and the family holds the money. The Wheel's swings fall on the bearer's own small purse while the family uses the gift for the Banco. They have kept the card in the family since 1491 by the usual means: the heir in the bearer's bed, cousins married to cousins, contracts drawn up by the family notary specifying who sleeps where.
- The Banco's agent in the **Weißach**, **Ser Alvise Lunardin**, is a cousin (→ I26).

## 5. Glass

- **The Isola dei Forni**: since 1291 all Velloran glass has been made on this island, to keep the fires away from the city and the secrets inside the republic. The **glassblowers** (*fornaci*) are a privileged guild. Their daughters may marry patricians without losing their children's rank. They **may not leave Vellora on pain of death**, and a runaway glassblower is hunted by the Nine's agents. Some have been found in the canals of foreign cities.
- **Products**:
  - **mirrors** silvered with Rocalban quicksilver (→ I30), the clearest in the world;
  - **cristallo**, clear glass;
  - colored glass, beads, chandeliers;
  - **alchemical glassware**: retorts, alembics and pelicans (→ R03);
  - and the **vetro di grazia**, the famous blown-glass **Tröster** (→ I08), which every great lady in Wiedenbrunn owns and none admits to.
- **Mirror-rooms**: Vellora invented the **Spiegelkabinett** (→ I03).

## 6. Courtesans, carnival and the night

### The cortigiane oneste
Vellora's **honest courtesans** are educated, musical, poetic and ruinously expensive women. They have their own palaces, gondolas, salons and libraries, and are the most famous women of the city, often more famous than its patrician wives.
- **Il Libro delle Grazie** (the Book of Graces): a **printed catalogue** of the city's courtesans, first published in **1565** and reissued every few years. It gives each one's **name**, **address**, **price** and **accomplishments**: *"sings to the lute; speaks four tongues; receives on Venus-days; ten scudi; the Thirty-Two by arrangement."* Every Kavalier carries one. Wiedenbrunn reads it as a novel (→ I08).
- **Cassandra Moro**, author of the **Thirty-Two Hours** (1641), was a cortigiana onesta, and her house on the Rio della Sensa is a pilgrimage for a certain kind of traveler.
- **The Devil**: the most famous living courtesan, **Marieta Lissandrin**, bears **Trump XV, Der Teufel** (→ I46).
  - **Gift**: anyone who desires her and is **touched** by her **cannot stop desiring her for a moon**.
  - **Burden**: she **feels the pull of every one of them** as a dull weight. The more she binds, the heavier she grows, and she must choose carefully whom she touches.
  - She is in her thirties, poet, Basseta-banker and correspondent of three kings, and she has never yet been seen to fall in love. She comes to Wiedenbrunn some Fasching seasons and holds a Basseta bank in a hired palace, and the city's men pretend not to be afraid of her.
- **Courtesans in breeches**: Velloran courtesans often dress **as young men**, *in braghe*, in breeches and doublets with their hair tied back, to attract clients who prefer young men, since the Night Wardens make that dangerous (see below). The fashion spread to Wiedenbrunn's Hosenrollen actresses (→ R11, I68).

### Carnival
Carnival in Vellora runs from **Erbentag to Aschentag**, and again for the Sposalizio and in early autumn: half the year in masks.
- **Masks** let anyone go anywhere as anyone: patricians among popolani, wives without husbands, nuns out of convents, men as women and women as men.
  - **la Larva**: a white full-face mask with a tricorn and a black cape. The standard, worn by both sexes.
  - **la Muta**: a black velvet oval mask held on by **biting a button** behind the lips, so the wearer **can't speak**. Women's, mysterious, and only removed to speak, which is to say to kiss.
  - **la Sposa**: a white bride's mask with a veil, worn by women and by men who want to be taken for women.
- **The Ridotto**: the state-licensed gaming-house, open in carnival, where masked patricians hold the Basseta banks and anyone masked may play. Fortunes, and bodies, are lost there (→ I09).
- **Compagnie del Ventaglio** (fan-companies): clubs of young patrician women, each with its own colors and rules, which give private balls, plays and masquerades. Their members' intimacy with each other is a famous subject of satire (→ R09).

### The Guardiani della Notte (the Night Wardens)
Six patrician magistrates and their constables who patrol by night and prosecute **sodomy** between men, as well as brawling, theft and blasphemy. Convicted men are fined, exiled, sent to the galleys, or in the worst cases beheaded and burned between the columns of the Piazza Maggiore. Women's love is not their concern, and courtesans dressed as boys are a grey area they prefer not to examine.

## 7. The fraterna

Velloran patrician families keep their capital whole by the **fraterna**, the brotherhood-partnership:
- the **brothers** of a house hold its fortune **in common**, under one roof, in one company;
- **only one brother marries**, to keep the heirs and the estate undivided;
- the others remain **unmarried**, living in the family palace, working in the family business, and keeping mistresses, courtesans and each other's company.

What the unmarried brothers' relation is to their brother's wife, and to their sisters who also never marry, is a matter for the family notary, who drafts very precise contracts about who lives on which floor. **Fraterna scandals** (a child who looks like the wrong brother; a sister who stays home too long) are hushed with money. The Velloran word for an unmarried brother living in the family palace is **barba** (uncle), and *"zio barba"* is a byword (→ R06).

---

## 8. Slavery

Vellora is **the great broker** between Kesrân and the west (→ R07).
- **The Riva delle Catene**: the slave-market on the waterfront, where captives from every shore are sold before a state notary, every sale recorded and taxed.
- **Household slaves**: maids, gondoliers, cooks. Courtesans own their maids, and the maids sometimes become courtesans.
- **Galley-slaves**: convicts on the republic's galleys.
- Velloran brokers buy at the Llotja dels Captius in Llucmar (→ I29) and sell in Daryabad, and the reverse.

---

## 9. Faith, kinship, trade and stereotypes

- **Faith**: the Wedding Church under the **Patriarch of the Lagoon**, who acknowledges both sees and obeys neither. The Sposalizio is its great feast. Velloran churches are full of paintings of the Bride that look a great deal like famous courtesans, because they were.
- **Kinship**: legalistic. Cousin marriage is common, to keep capital together. Nearest-kin scandals are hushed. Dispensations are bought from whichever see is cheaper that year (→ R06).
- **Exports**:
  - **glass** and **mirrors**; **lace**;
  - **banking**, **credit**, the **Lotto**;
  - **printed books**: Vellora prints what Wiedenbrunn bans, including the Thirty-Two Hours (→ I12);
  - **sugar** and spices re-exported from the east; silk;
  - **courtesans' fame**.
- **What others say of the Velloran**: greedy, masked, two-faced, charming, decadent. *"A Velloran would sell his mother and lease you his sister."* *"Vellora owns everything and believes nothing."*
- **What the Velloran say**: Valdauers pay late but always pay. Almagrans pay never but always beautifully. Rocalbans haggle over a Kupferling and then buy the ship. Kesrâni are the best customers in the world.

---

## 10. Relations with the Double Crown

- **Creditor**: Valdau's debt to the Banco is the Banco's lever and Valdau's chain. The Banco has never pulled it hard. The Weißach foreclosures are a gentle tug (→ I26).
- **Neutral**: Vellora trades with Kesrân and Valdau alike, and sells cannon to both through intermediaries.
- **Teacher**: every young Valdauer lord learns fencing, gambling and love in Vellora (→ I19).
- **Rival** of Llucmar for the Middle Sea's trade, and the Rocalban galleys and Velloran galleys have fought twice this century.
- **Supplier** of the realm's mirrors, glass, credit, courtesans and the pox (→ I66).
- **In Wiedenbrunn**: the Banco's office on the Graben; Velloran courtesans among the Damen von Welt (→ I23); the Lotto agents; the glass-shop on the Spiegelmarkt; the gondola and gondolier on the Greifenburg's garden canal (→ I10).

## HOOKS

1. **The Devil comes to town.** Marieta Lissandrin, bearer of the Devil, arrives in Wiedenbrunn for Fasching and opens her Basseta bank in a hired palace. On her first night she asks to be introduced to "the Moon's son." Everyone in the room knows what her touch does, and the protagonist doesn't.
2. **The Muta.** At a Redoute a woman in the Velloran Muta, the mute black mask held by a button in her teeth, takes the protagonist's hand and leads him away. She can't speak without dropping the mask. When she does, it will be to kiss him, or to say who she is.
3. **The Book of Graces.** The newest edition of the Book of Graces, just arrived from Vellora, lists a courtesan under the name *"la Luna di Ilsenau, of the white castle, who sings in Catalan"*. She is not Velloran.
4. **The Wheel turns.** At the Café Sküs a Lunardin cousin is visiting, and the protagonist's card-luck reverses so violently, a whole evening's winnings gone in a single hand, that the table knows exactly what just happened. The question is why the Wheel's bearer, or someone close to it, wanted to turn the Ilsenau son's luck.
5. **The runaway glassblower.** A Velloran glassblower who fled the Isola dei Forni is hiding in Wiedenbrunn, making vetro di grazia for the Rosengrund under a false name. The Nine's agents are close. He offers the protagonist one of his pieces, a very particular piece, for sanctuary.
6. **Zio barba.** A Velloran patrician on embassy in Wiedenbrunn, the unmarried brother of a fraterna, asks the protagonist frankly over wine how the Spindelhäuser manage to keep their blood whole without men, because his family's notary has run out of ideas.
