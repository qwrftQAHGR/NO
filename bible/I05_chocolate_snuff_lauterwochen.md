# I05 · Chocolate, Snuff & the Läuterwochen

*The court's small vices and the Church's fasts: chocolate in bed and the theological quarrel over whether it breaks a fast, the two drinking parties at court, snuff and the snuffbox with two lids, pipes, and the forty days each spring when the theatres close and the marriage bed is supposed to as well.*

**See also:** X03 Kitchen & Cellar · I67 Tarock & the Coffeehouses · I54 The Church of the Wedding · I14 Brauttag · I03 The Palais & its Hours · I32 Almagra · I34 Kesrân

## 1. Chocolate

**How it came**: with the Almagran Ceremonial in the 1630s (→ I69). The Almagran court drank chocolate morning and evening, so the Valdauer court began to, and by 1660 no great house in Wiedenbrunn could hold up its head without a **Schokoladiere**, a chocolate-pot. The cacao comes by sea from Almagra's far western islands, a trade Almagra guards jealously. It arrives in Llucmar and Vellora as dark pressed tablets stamped with the Almagran royal arms, and is very expensive. A cup costs **8 Kupferlinge** at a Wiedenbrunn **Schokoladestube**, nearly three times the price of coffee.

**How it's made**: a tablet is grated into hot water or milk in the tall silver or copper **Schokoladiere**, which has a hole in its lid. Through the hole goes the **Quirl** (Almagran *molinillo*), a turned wooden whisk spun between the palms until the chocolate foams. It is spiced with **vanilla**, **cinnamon**, sometimes a **chili** (the Almagran way, for the bold), and sweetened heavily. It is served thick, in tall cups, with **Biskotten** (ladyfingers) to dip, and a glass of water, which one drinks afterwards in the Almagran manner.

**When**: in bed, in the morning. The **Bettfrühstück** (→ I03). A great lady's day begins with chocolate brought by her Kammerfrau or Bettmagd, and many love affairs in the Kammerbüchl begin with *"she brought the chocolate."* The **Schokolademädchen**, the chocolate-girl, is a Leibmagd whose only morning duty is to bring and serve the chocolate. Wiedenbrunn understands the position as a sinecure for a favorite.

**Venusschokolade**: chocolate with a measure of **Venusbitter** (→ R03). The fashionable aphrodisiac of the 1690s, served at certain suppers and in certain Kabinetts. The Chastity Commission tried to ban it in 1693, which made it twice as popular.

### The Chocolate Question
Does chocolate break a fast?

The Church's fasts forbid meat on every **Saturntag** (Saturday) and all through the **Läuterwochen** (see §5), and forbid solid food before the weekly **Mahl** (the Feast, the Church's communion). Chocolate is food enough to live on, and the court wanted to drink it on fast days. From **1641 to 1662** the realm's theologians argued over whether chocolate was food or drink, in treatises, sermons, faculty disputations and one fistfight on the steps of the Dom.

In **1662** the Abbess of Montalba ruled: **"Liquidum non frangit."** A liquid does not break the fast. Chocolate, if drunk and not eaten, is permitted on fast days. The **Archbishop of the Bridegroom's See** refused to accept her ruling, on the grounds that chocolate thick enough to stand a spoon in is not a liquid. He was right, and was ignored, and the feud between the two sees took on a new front (→ I54). Ever since, the pious of the Bridegroom's party drink their fast-day chocolate thin, and everyone else drinks it thick and quotes the Abbess.

## 2. Coffee and chocolate: the two parties

Coffee came to Wiedenbrunn in the sacks the Kesrâni left behind when the **Second Siege** broke in 1683 (→ I76). Within a decade there were forty coffeehouses (→ I67). Coffee is cheap (3 Kupferlinge a cup), bourgeois, Kesrâni, and drunk in public. Chocolate is dear, aristocratic, Almagran, and drunk in bed.

The court has divided along the line:
- **The Schokoladenpartei**, the chocolate party. They favor the **Almagran claim**: Archduke Ferdinand Karl to the throne of the Glass King, a closer union with Almagra, the Ceremonial kept strictly, and the old ways (→ R01, I32). They drink chocolate at their gatherings, and a cup of chocolate offered in a pointed way is a recruitment.
- **The Kaffeepartei**, the coffee party (the name is the chocolate party's sneer): those who favor peace with Kesrân and renewal of the **Willow Bridge** truce (→ I76); Holdegg's peace party; the reforming clerks of Ehrenreith; the Bürger, the merchants, and the coffeehouse wits. They don't drink only coffee. They just don't take the chocolate.
- The **Starkenberg war party** drinks neither publicly, being soldiers, and wine privately. They want war with Kesrân, which makes them coffee's enemies, and they despise the Almagrans, which makes them chocolate's.

At any great house's Jause the hostess's choice of drink is read. *"Chocolate or coffee, Frau Gräfin?"* is not only a question about taste. Ilsenau serves milk-coffee to the family and chocolate to guests, and has been carefully neutral about it for thirty years (→ R12).

## 3. Snuff

**Schnupftabak**: tobacco ground fine, scented, and taken in a pinch to the nose. It came to Valdau from Almagra too, along with chocolate. It is the habit of every gentleman, many ladies, all clergy, and every clerk of the Hofkammer.
- **The kinds**: **Almagraner Puder** (fine, yellowish, scented with orange-flower, very expensive); **Kesrâner Schnupf** (coarse, dark, strong, scented with rose); **Hausschnupf** (local, cheap, made in the Kornau, and smelling of the Kornau).
- **Etiquette**:
  - Offering a pinch from one's own box is a gesture of friendship. Refusing is a slight.
  - The box is opened with the left hand and the pinch taken with the right thumb and forefinger.
  - Sneezing is taken as a compliment to the snuff, and answered with **"Helf' die Braut!"** ("The Bride help you!"), or in Bürger houses **"Gesundheit!"**
  - Ladies take snuff discreetly, from tiny boxes, in the Kabinett.
  - **Taking snuff from another's box without asking** is an intimacy of the first rank between equals. Between lovers it is foreplay.

### The Tabatière
The snuffbox is a love-gift, a diplomatic gift and a family heirloom. It is enameled, gold, silver, mother-of-pearl, or lacquered (Asagiri lacquer is the rage, → I37), with a **miniature portrait inside the lid**: a lover's, a sovereign's or a saint's.
- **Die Doppeldeckel-Tabatière**, the box with two lids. Inside the ordinary lid, under the miniature of a wife or a saint, a hidden catch opens **a second lid**, and under that is a second picture, which is almost never a wife or a saint. Usually it is a nude: a lover painted from life, a courtesan, a scene from the **Thirty-Two Hours** (→ I08). The best miniaturists in Wiedenbrunn make their living painting second lids. Discovering one's spouse's second lid is a classic comedy scene, and also a classic duel.
- **The Velloran Tabatière** is musical, with a tiny mechanism that plays a tune when the lid opens. Some play love-songs, and one notorious model plays the Gstanzl about the Glass King (→ I11).

## 4. Pipes, wine and other comforts

*(Fuller lists in X03.)*
- **Pipes**: clay pipes for Grenzer, soldiers, boatmen and peasants. Long Kesrâni pipes with amber mouthpieces for the fashionable **Türkenzimmer** (→ I03). Women do not smoke pipes in public, except Grenzer women, who do whatever they like.
- **The Kesrâni water-pipe** (*Nargile*): at the embassy and in two coffeehouses. It is considered an exotic pleasure and is suspected of being a vice.
- **Wine**: Sonnleiten whites, Rocalban reds, Ormándi sweet wines, the new **Sturm** in autumn (→ I27).
- **Beer**: Hallgau breweries; the Bürger's drink.
- **Spirits**: Grenzer **Zwetschkenbrand** (plum brandy); Rocalban **ratafia** (a cherry and herb liqueur); Kornau **Kümmel**.
- **Venusbitter**, everywhere (→ R03).

## 5. The Läuterwochen

The **Läuterwochen** (the weeks of refining) are the Church's great fast: forty days before **Brauttag**, the spring feast of the Bride (→ I14). The idea is alchemical. The soul is refined in fire before the Bride comes down, and the dross is burned off.

### What changes
- **No meat** for forty days, except on the two Sundays when the fast "breathes." Milk, eggs and butter are allowed in Valdau, by a 1580 dispensation that the Bürger call the Butter-Letter. In Rocalba they are not, and Rocalbans eat a great deal of salt cod.
- **No weddings.** The Church will not marry anyone in the Läuterwochen, so the weeks before are packed with weddings.
- **No balls.** Fasching ends at midnight on **Aschentag**, Ash-Day, the first day of the Läuterwochen, with the **Kehraus** (sweep-out), the last dance of the season. Then the ballrooms close.
- **No theatre**: the Hofoper and the popular stages close (→ I68). Musicians go hungry, and actresses go to the country houses.
- **The Läuterpredigten**: sermons twice a week, which the court attends in black. Some are fierce and some are famous. The fashionable preacher of the season is discussed like an opera singer.
- **Zeugnis** (confession) before Brauttag is expected of everyone.
- **And the marriage bed is supposed to rest.** By old custom, married couples abstain on fast days, and in the Läuterwochen many abstain altogether. These are the **Läuternächte** (refining nights). The custom is honored by the pious, ignored by most, and turned into a game by many. A couple keeping the Läuternächte strictly is a figure of fun and also of a certain admiration.

### What doesn't change
- **Affairs.** The Läuterwochen are notoriously the best season for them. Husbands are abstaining, theatres are closed, evenings are long, and everyone is bored. The Kammerbüchl have a genre for it: *Läuterwochengeschichten*.
- **Chocolate** (see §1).
- **Fish.** And by an old ruling of the Bride's See, **beaver** counts as fish, because it lives in water. **Biberschwanz**, beaver tail, is the court's Läuterwochen delicacy, and the Kornau beaver has been hunted nearly to extinction for it. Wits have extended the principle to other things that live in water. A fashionable lady in Lent will refer to her lover as *"mein Biber."*

### Fast-day food
**Fastenbrezel**, Lenten pretzels, sold on every street corner. Salt cod from Rocalba (**Stockfisch**). Carp from the Kornau ponds. **Fastensuppe**, a thin vegetable soup. Snails. Frogs' legs. Almond milk. And **Biberschwanz**, for those who can afford it.

### The other fasts
- **Saturntag** (Saturday): no meat, all year. Saturn rules lead, death and patience, so it is a day for restraint.
- **The four Probetage** (assay days): quarterly fast days when the Church "assays" souls. Confession is expected. Assayers of the Hofprobieramt don't work on these days, because blood assayed on a Probetag is said to read false (→ I49).
- **The vigils** before Brauttag, Hochzeitsnacht and Erbentag.

## HOOKS

1. **The chocolate-girl.** A pledged maid assigned as the protagonist's Schokolademädchen appears every morning at his bed with the Schokoladiere and the Quirl. Several women of the house assigned her, each separately, each for different reasons, and none has told the others.
2. **The second lid.** A gift snuffbox arrives for the protagonist from an anonymous admirer. Its first lid shows a saint. Its second lid shows a portrait, painted from life, of someone he knows.
3. **The chocolate test.** At a Jause in an allied house, the hostess offers chocolate to some guests and coffee to others, and the protagonist is offered a choice. Everyone at the table knows what his answer will say about Ilsenau's position on the Almagran claim.
4. **Biberschwanz.** During the Läuterwochen a lady of the court tells the protagonist, with a look, that she is "keeping the fast, but my confessor says beaver counts as fish." It is not a remark about beaver.
5. **The Läuternächte.** A newly married couple of the household's circle have sworn to keep the Läuternächte strictly, for luck, on the advice of the Stundenamt. On the thirtieth night the bride comes to an Ilsenau woman in some distress. The groom is keeping the fast, she isn't sure she can, and there are ten nights left.
6. **The Archbishop's chocolate.** The Archbishop of the Bridegroom's See has had his household's chocolate thinned on fast days for forty years. He now accuses the Abbess's ruling of being used to cover Venusschokolade at court, and asks a member of the Seven Chairs to bring him the evidence.
