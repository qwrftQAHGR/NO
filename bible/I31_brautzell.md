# I31 · Brautzell

*The mountain shrine of the Bride of the Snows: pilgrims on the Bride's Climb, the hall where barren wives sleep a night and wake with child, inns that rent more than beds, the nuns who fold the amulets, the urns of noble entrails, and the house of unmarried noblewomen whose famous freedom is everybody's open secret.*

**See also:** I54 The Church of the Wedding · I21 (the Brautsteig pass) · R09 Sapphic & Queer Life · I19 (the Stiftsjahr) · I13 Folk Belief (the Breverl) · I18 (the entrails' burial) · R08 (fertility)

## 1. The place

**Brautzell** ("the Bride's cell") lies **seven days south of Wiedenbrunn**, high on the **Brautsteig**, the road over the southern ranges to Rocalba, in a green bowl of meadows below the pass and ringed by snow-peaks (→ I21). It is a village of inns around a great white **pilgrimage church** with twin onion-domes, and behind it a long quiet building with a garden wall: the **Fräuleinstift**, the noblewomen's house.

The church holds the **Bride of the Snows** (*die Schneebraut*): a carved wooden statue of the Bride, a little under life-size, dark with age and candle-smoke, crowned and robed in white silk embroidered with silver, which is changed with the liturgical seasons. By legend she was found in **1157** by a shepherd, standing in a snowdrift in July on the spot where the altar now stands, unmelted snow around her and roses at her feet. Pilgrims have come ever since. She is the **Bride's shrine of Valdau**, as Montalba is of Rocalba, and the two sees argue about her (→ I54).

---

## 2. Pilgrims

Brautzell draws perhaps **fifty thousand pilgrims a year**, mostly in the summer months when the pass is open:
- **Barren wives**, the great majority, who come to pray for a child (see §3).
- **Brides-to-be**, to ask the Bride's blessing on a marriage.
- **The sick**, and those who pray for them.
- **Penitents**, some barefoot from Wiedenbrunn, some on their knees up the last mile of the **Kniesteig** (knee-path), a stone stair from the village to the church door.
- **Whole parishes** in procession behind their banners, singing.
- **Noble households** in carriages with servants.
- **Travelers** on the Brautsteig, who stop because everyone stops.

They bring **votive offerings**, which cover the walls of the church's ambulatory: wax and silver models of arms, legs, eyes, hearts, babies and wombs, paintings of miraculous recoveries, crutches, and wedding wreaths. A section near the altar holds the **silver babies**, small silver figures of infants left by mothers whose prayers were answered, hundreds deep.

---

## 3. The Inkubationshalle (the sleeping-hall)

Behind the high altar, down a short stair, lies the **Inkubationshalle**, the incubation hall. It is long, low and dim, with a vaulted ceiling painted with the Wedding's stars, lined with forty narrow wooden **beds**, each curtained. A lamp burns before the Bride's statue at the far end, visible from every bed.

By a rite older than the church, a **barren wife** may, after confession and fasting, **sleep one night in the Inkubationshalle**:
- she lies in a curtained bed in her shift;
- the **Brautschwestern** (the Bride's nuns) give her a cup of warm wine with herbs and a few drops of **Vollwasser** (→ R03), and draw the curtain;
- the lamps are put out except the Bride's;
- she sleeps, and **dreams of the Bride**.

Very often, in the months that follow, **she conceives.** The shrine's book of graces records thousands of children *"given at Brautzell."*

**How** this works is a question Valdau has answered in several ways:
- **The Church** says it is the **Bride's grace**: the dream, the prayer, the Vollwasser and the faith.
- **The physicians** say it is the **Vollwasser**, the rest, the mountain air and the hope.
- **Everybody else** knows the Inkubationshalle is **dark**, that the curtains **open**, that **the village is full of young men**, that some husbands are **not the problem**, and that **what happens in the hall is the Bride's business.** *"Ein Brautzeller Kind"* (a Brautzell child) is a child conceived on pilgrimage, and in Wiedenbrunn it is said with a particular smile. Rich families have been known to send a trusted footman, a handsome cousin or a hired **Kogel stud** up the Brautsteig a day behind the lady (→ I50). The nuns have seen everything, and say nothing. Some of the women who come know exactly what they're coming for, and some don't.

A wife who sleeps a night in the hall is under the **Bride's veil**, like the Hochzeitsnacht's lovers (→ I15). Her husband may not question her about it, and a child born of it is his. That is the shrine's custom and Valdau's law.

## 4. The pilgrims' inns

The village of Brautzell is almost entirely **inns**: the **Goldene Braut**, the **Schneerose**, the **Hirschen**, the **Weißes Lamm** and thirty more. They are crowded in summer, with pilgrims six to a bed in the cheap ones and private rooms in the dear ones. Brautzell's innkeepers are famous for overcharging, for stretching the pass's dangers to keep guests longer, and for **renting more than beds**. A pilgrim's room can come with a maid who stays, a "guide" for the walk to the spring, or a key to a door that connects to another pilgrim's. *"In Brautzell gibt's ka Sünd'"* (in Brautzell there's no sin), the innkeepers say, since everyone has come to be forgiven anyway. Brautzell's priests have tried for a century to get the innkeepers' licenses revoked.

**The Brautquelle** (the Bride's spring): a spring above the village where pilgrims drink and wash. Brides-to-be wash their faces in it at dawn, and couples drink from it together. The path to it runs through a wood, and the walk is long.

---

## 5. The Brautschwestern and their trade

The church and the hall are served by the **Brautschwestern**, nuns of the Bride's order, under a **Priorin** who answers to the **Abbess of Montalba**, which infuriates the Archbishop of the Bridegroom's See (→ I54).

The nuns:
- keep the Inkubationshalle;
- dress the statue;
- hear women's confessions (the Bride's See allows nuns to hear women; the Bridegroom's See doesn't recognize it);
- and run the shrine's **workshops**:
  - **Breverl**, the folded amulets worn by half of Valdau. The nuns fold them by the hundred thousand, and they sell at the shrine for 2–8 Kupferlinge (→ I13);
  - **wax votives**;
  - **Brautzeller Lebkuchen**, honey-cake hearts with the Bride's image in sugar;
  - **Brautkerzen**, candles for the Bride, which every pilgrim buys;
  - and **Vollwasser**, the fertility tincture, from the shrine's own herb-garden and a Moon-blooded sister's still.

The shrine is very rich.

**The entrails**: by custom since the Flask King, great houses send the **entrails** of their dead to Brautzell in copper urns, "*the inward parts to the Bride*" (→ I18). They lie in the **Eingeweidekapelle**, the entrails-chapel, on shelves of copper urns named and dated. It is quiet, and smells faintly of embalming spices. Ilsenau sends its dead's entrails to the Stille Haus instead, by a 1499 privilege, and Brautzell has never forgiven the loss of income.

## 6. The Fräuleinstift

Behind the church's garden wall stands the **Fräuleinstift zu Brautzell**, a **Damenstift**, a residence for **unmarried noblewomen of good family**, founded **1482** by the widowed Queen Kunigunde for "the daughters of the realm who are not called to marriage."
- About **thirty Stiftsdamen** live there, each with her own apartment, her own maid, her own income (the **Präbende**, a prebend paid from the Stift's estates), and her own front door.
- They keep a **light rule**: chapel twice a day in a modest black habit with a white collar and the Stift's silver cross, and otherwise as they please.
- They are **not nuns**. They take no vows and may leave to marry. Few do.
- They elect their own head, **die Äbtissin des Stifts** (the Stift's abbess), always a great noblewoman.
- Admission requires the **sixteen ancestors** (→ R05) and a dowry to the Stift.
- **Stiftsjahr**: the Stift also takes **Elevinnen**, noble daughters of eighteen spending their finishing-year there. It is less prestigious than the Ilsenau household and more free (→ I19).

**What the Fräuleinstift is famous for**: its women's **freedom**, and what they do with it. A Stiftsdame has her own income, her own house and no husband, in a mountain village full of pilgrims and inns, a long way from her family. The Fräuleinstift's women are known across Valdau for their **heart-sister bonds**, their salons, their music, their libraries, their love affairs with each other and with the inns' guests, and their total discretion. In Valdauer slang **"eine Stiftsdame"** is a woman who loves women, and every Stiftsdame knows it and doesn't mind (→ R09).
- **The chapel of the Two Marthas** in the Stift's garden is hung with hundreds of braided ribbons left by heart-sisters (→ R09).
- The **Stift's library** has a locked cabinet of books the Bücherkommission has never seen.
- The **Äbtissin's Saturday salon** is the best conversation south of Wiedenbrunn.

The shrine's priests and the Stift's ladies have been at war for two centuries over money, precedence, the garden wall, the use of the church, and the ladies' habit of entertaining pilgrims.

---

## 7. Tensions

- **The two sees**: the Bridegroom's See's Archbishop claims Brautzell as a Valdauer shrine, and the Abbess of Montalba claims it as the Bride's. The Priorin obeys Montalba and the parish priest obeys Wiedenbrunn, and they don't speak.
- **The shrine against the Stift**, over money, the wall and the ladies.
- **The priests against the innkeepers.**
- **The Clear Church**: Lautere preachers come up the pass to preach against "the Bride's brothel." The pilgrims throw Lebkuchen at them.
- **The pass**: open seven months, shut five. Brautzell lives off summer and starves in winter, when the inns close, the Stift's ladies are snowbound with each other, and the nuns fold Breverl by candlelight.

---

## 8. Local custom, tension and a place worth visiting

- **Custom**: the Kniesteig on the knees; the night in the Inkubationshalle; silver babies; the Brautquelle at dawn; Brautzeller Lebkuchen; Breverl; the Stift's heart-sister ribbons.
- **Tension**: the two sees; shrine against Stift; priests against innkeepers; the Lautere.
- **Worth a visit**:
  - **the church at Vespers** on the Bride's feast, when the statue is carried round the meadow by torchlight and the pilgrims sing;
  - **the Äbtissin's salon** on a Saturday, if you can get an invitation;
  - **the Brautquelle path** at dawn.

## HOOKS

1. **A night in the hall.** A noblewoman of an allied house, married ten barren years, goes to sleep in the Inkubationshalle. She has asked, delicately, through an Ilsenau woman she trusts, that the protagonist happen to be staying at the Goldene Braut that night. She has heard nothing about him except his face.
2. **A Brautzeller Kind.** Two weeks after a lady's night in the Inkubationshalle, she is visibly with child, three months' worth (→ R13). The nuns, who have seen everything, have never seen this. The Priorin writes to the Abbess of Montalba, and the Abbess's reply comes by fast courier.
3. **The Äbtissin's salon.** The Fräuleinstift's Äbtissin, a magnificent woman of sixty who did her Stiftsjahr with the Ilsenau, invites the protagonist to her Saturday salon. All thirty Stiftsdamen are present, and he is the first man to attend in eleven years. She says it is an experiment.
4. **The entrails.** A copper urn arrives at the Eingeweidekapelle from a great house with no name on it, only a crescent. The Priorin asks the Ilsenau whom it belongs to, and the Ilsenau say it isn't theirs.
5. **The Kniesteig.** A penitent climbing the knee-path from Wiedenbrunn, bleeding, turns out to be a Starkenberg cousin of the protagonist's acquaintance, doing penance for something he will only confess to a man. The only man of rank in Brautzell is the protagonist.
6. **Snowbound.** The pass closes early with a September blizzard, and the Ilsenau party is snowbound in Brautzell with the Fräuleinstift's thirty ladies, the pilgrims of the Goldene Braut and a Lautere preacher, for three weeks, with nothing to do.
