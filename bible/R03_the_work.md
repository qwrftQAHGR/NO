# R03 · The Work (alchemy & metallurgy)

*The second Art. The Work decides **how**: how a body is healed, quickened, calmed or poisoned; how iron becomes a cannon that does not burst; how a bloodline's fineness is measured; and why some families break like overhardened steel.*

**See also:** R02 The Sky · R04 The Cards · R06 Kinship & Nearness · I49 The Hofprobieramt · I50 The Pit-Blood Trade · I52 The Seventh Faculty & the Winkelalchimisten · I25 The Eisenmark · I30 Cinabre · I66 The Lagoon-Pox

---

## 1. What it is

Valdauers call it **die Kunst**, the Art, or among practitioners **das Werk**, the Work. Rocalbans call it **l'Obra**.

The theory every apprentice learns in the first week:

- Everything that exists is a mixture of **three principles**: **Salz** (salt: body, fixity, the flesh that holds), **Schwefel** (sulphur: desire, heat, will, the soul that burns) and **Quecksilber** (quicksilver: spirit, change, quickness, the thing that moves between). A person is a mixture like any other. The temperaments are proportions of the three.
- The **Seven Wanderers** each rule a **metal**, and each metal holds its planet's **Tugend**, its virtue:

| Planet | Metal | Virtue in the Work |
|---|---|---|
| Sun | gold | life, sovereignty, healing, presence |
| Moon | silver | fertility, sleep and dream, kinship, milk |
| Mercury | quicksilver | change, speed, languages, the in-between |
| Venus | copper | desire, beauty, music, persuasion |
| Mars | iron | war, wounds, courage, heat |
| Jupiter | tin | law, preservation, memory, authority |
| Saturn | lead | time, sleep, death, endurance, poison |

- The method is **"Löse und binde"**, dissolve and bind: separate a substance into its principles, purify them, and recombine them in the right proportion at the right hour. Most of the Work is this, done patiently, in glass, over fire, for weeks.

The Work is a **craft anyone can learn** with enough years, money and fingers. It is the realm's main non-blood supernatural. Every town has an apothecary and every boudoir a tincture-chest, and every cannon is the Work's handiwork. What the Work cannot do is make the *blood*. It can only feed it, test it and ease its failures.

## 2. Tinctures

A **Tinktur** is a preparation that carries a metal's virtue into a body: drops in wine, a salve on the skin, a powder under the tongue. Tinctures are made **stundengerecht**, in their planet's hour (→ R02). Their strength in a given body depends on the taker's **Aufnahme**, the reception set by the nativity, and on the taker's **blood** (see §5). A Moon-blooded woman gets twice the good of a silver tincture that a commoner does. A Mars officer barely notices one.

### The seven house tinctures

**Trinkgold** (Sun, gold). *Drinkable gold.* Gold leaf dissolved through a chain of salts and spirits into a clear, faintly gleaming cordial.
- *Does:* slows aging measurably, speeds healing of wounds and fevers, steadies the heart, gives the skin a fine glow. A course of Trinkgold over years gives a sixty-year-old a fifty-year-old's vigor.
- *Costs:* about 5 Greif a dose, so a craftsman's yearly wage buys a week. Kings, cardinals and very rich widows take it.
- *Side effects:* **Goldglanz**, a gleam in the eyes and a growing hardness of heart. Long users become proud, cold and lonely. Heavy overuse brings **Vergoldung** ("gilding"): the skin yellows, the joints stiffen and the user stops being able to feel pity. Courtiers say a gilded old man "has more gold in him than blood."

**Mondwasser** (Moon, silver). *Moon-water.* The most important tincture in the realm, because it governs wombs. It is made in two preparations:
- **Dunkelwasser** ("dark water"), begun at the dark of the moon and taken nightly for a month, prevents conception. In noble and bourgeois households it is about as reliable as a careful husband: good for most women with most men, and weaker against high-karat partners and in Moon hours. The court's ladies take it with their morning chocolate (→ I05).
- **Vollwasser** ("full water"), begun at the full moon, promotes conception, calms the womb and strengthens milk.
- Both also cool fevers and bring vivid dreams.
- *Costs:* 2 Bräutlinge a month from a licensed apothecary, affordable for the Bürger. Poor women buy **Mondsud** (moon-decoction), a cheap herb-and-silver-filings brew from the Kräuterweiber that works when it feels like it.
- *Side effects:* the dreams. Long users dream of water and of their kin, and some sleepwalk.
- Ilsenau makes its own in the **Mondküche** (→ R12), and its Dunkelwasser is the best in the realm. That is half of why every noblewoman of Wiedenbrunn is fond of the Ilsenau women.

**Merkurwasser** and **Quecksilbersalbe** (Mercury, quicksilver). *Mercury-water and quicksilver salve.*
- *Does:* sharpens and quickens the mind; helps memory and languages (Mirabent couriers learn a tongue in a season on it); quickens a sluggish body; and, as salve, is the only real cure for the **Lagoon-Pox** (→ I66). Mercury also makes mirrors and depilatories, and it is the active principle of the dangerous **Mercurial Course**, which can give a woman a second nature (→ R10).
- *Side effects:* **Zittern**, the trembles. Hands shake, teeth loosen, gums blacken and moods fly. Long exposure brings madness, the kind the miners of Cinabre call *tremolor* (→ I30). *"A night with Venus, a lifetime with Mercury,"* as the saying goes.
- The Mercury-blooded house of **Mirabent** tolerates it far better than anyone else.

**Venusbitter** (Venus, copper). *Venus-bitters.* A red-brown liqueur of copper salts, rose, cardamom and twelve secret herbs. Its recipe is a Holdegg monopoly, imitated in every tavern.
- *Does:* heightens desire that is already present. Skin becomes sensitive, blood comes to the surface, inhibition falls away. It is the realm's aphrodisiac. Every wedding table has a bottle; every courtesan keeps one. The saying is *"Venusbitter zündet das Feuer, aber es legt kein Holz"*: Venusbitter lights the fire, but it doesn't lay the wood. It cannot make anyone want someone they don't want at all. It can make a little wanting into a great deal.
- *Also:* **Grünspansalbe** (verdigris salve) for cuts and sores.
- *Side effects:* a raging thirst, the **Kupfermund** ("copper mouth") hangover, and jealousy, which some say is the point.

**Eisenmut** (Mars, iron). *Iron-courage.* A dark, sharp-tasting tincture of iron salts and blood-herbs.
- *Does:* dulls fear and pain, makes wounds clot fast, and fills the taker with a forward-leaning heat. The army issues it before battle as **the Mars ration**, timed to peak in the attack hour (→ R02). Duelists are forbidden it by the code of honor (→ I20, I71), and many take it anyway.
- *Side effects:* rage, followed by a crash of trembling exhaustion. It is addictive: veterans become **Eisensüchtige**, iron-sick men with red-rimmed **Rostaugen** (rust-eyes) who drink it daily to feel anything. After years, impotence, the dreaded **rostiger Kolben** ("rusty retort"). Garrison towns have whole streets of Eisensüchtige (→ I25).

**Zinnsalz** (Jupiter, tin). *Tin-salt.* The unglamorous one, and one of the most important.
- *Does:* preserves things. Food packed with Zinnsalz keeps for a year. Army biscuit and navy beef depend on it, and so does Valdau's ability to fight a long campaign. Corpses keep for the journey home. Paper treated with it doesn't rot, so the Hofkammer's archives are salted. Taken in small doses it sharpens memory for facts and figures, and clerks take it before audits.
- *Also:* **Jupiterlack**, tin-salt sealing wax that warms faintly under a thumb when the seal is genuine and stays cold when it is forged (→ I43).
- *Side effects:* stolidity; a dulled, heavy, satisfied mood. Clerks who take too much become *zinnern*, "pewtery."

**Saturnsaft** and **Bleitinktur** (Saturn, lead). *Saturn-juice and lead tincture.*
- *Does:* brings sleep, deep and dreamless; dulls pain as poppy does; slows the body, which preserves the dying and steadies surgeons' patients; and in other proportions it poisons. Lead is also **Bleiweiß**, the fashionable white face-paint (→ I07).
- House Kalteneck's **Bleiküche** (lead-kitchen) makes the finest, and its antidotes (→ I44).
- *Side effects:* melancholy, forgetfulness and a slow grey heaviness. In the brittle, or in an infant given too much, there is the risk of the **Bleischlaf**, the lead-sleep that can last years (→ I53). Rumor has always said that some sleepers were *put* to sleep. Physicians answer that Saturnsaft cannot do that alone, and nobody believes the physicians.

### Common and household preparations
Every household with any money has a **Hausapotheke**, a medicine chest. Typical contents:

- A **silver spoon**, given at the newborn's Salzung (→ I54). Milk given from it doesn't sour, and the child sleeps.
- A **copper bracelet** for aching joints and for luck in love.
- An **iron ring** worn by soldiers' wives while their men are at war.
- **Schwefelbad** salts: sulphur baths that warm cold husbands and cold wives.
- A paper of **Kupfertropfen**, cheap copper drops to bring a blush.
- A little lead pot of **Schlafsalbe** (sleep-salve) for teething children and for the master's sleepless nights.
- **Zinnsalz** for the pantry.
- And a **Breverl** amulet hung on the chest's lid (→ I13).

## 3. The workshop

- **The athanor** is the slow furnace that keeps an even heat for weeks. Every serious workshop has one. They are fed by apprentices in shifts, day and night, and an athanor that goes cold ruins a month's work. *"Er hat den Athanor ausgehen lassen"* ("he let the athanor go out") means a man who ruined something through negligence.
- **The glass**: retorts (**Kolben**), alembics, the round-bellied **Pelikan** (in which a liquid feeds back on itself), and crucibles (**Tiegel**). The best glass comes from Vellora's furnace-island (→ I35). A cracked Kolben in a Mercury batch can poison a whole street.
- **The hours**: work must be begun, and often finished, in the planet's hour, so alchemists keep strange schedules. The Mondküche's women work at night.
- **The weeks**: a Mondwasser batch takes a lunar month; Trinkgold takes a full year of a dozen stages; Eisenmut takes nine days.
- **The fumes**: **Kolbenfieber**, the workshop fever (headaches, shakes, strange tastes in the mouth), is a recognized occupational illness. Apprentices are paid in milk.
- **Eclipses** spoil everything in progress (→ R02). Workshops close, and on eclipse days alchemists get drunk.

## 4. Metallurgy: where the Work goes to war

Valdau is a mountain country and a metal country. The Work's most strategic products come out of a furnace, not a flask.

- **Marsstahl** (Mars-steel): iron from the **Red Kogel** (→ I25), smelted and worked in the hour of Mars by smiths with iron in their blood (Starkenberg's Kogelsöhne, pit-blood families). Blades of it hold an edge through a battle. **Kogelrohre**, cannon barrels cast from it in Mars hours, take a charge a third heavier than ordinary iron without bursting, which means longer range, heavier shot and fewer dead gunners. Output is limited by the hours, which is why the arsenal guards the Stückgießerei's casting calendar like a state secret.
- **Saturnblei** (Saturn-lead): musket balls cast in Saturn hours from lead treated with Saturnsaft. A wound from one festers and brings on a heavy sleep, so a man hit in the arm may sleep for two days. It is considered dishonorable. **Article Nine of the Peace of the Willow Bridge forbids it** on both sides. The Grenzer use it anyway and call it **Schlafkugel**, sleep-shot.
- **Glockenspeise** (bell-metal): copper and tin, cast in Venus or Jupiter hours. Bells cast right carry farther and *move* the people who hear them: Venus bells to tenderness, Jupiter bells to order. Holdegg's foundry casts the realm's church bells (→ I42), and alarm bells are cast at Jupiter. The great bell of the Dom zum Bräutigam, **die Pummerin**, is said to make couples who hear it at a wedding faithful for a year.
- **Spiegel** (mirrors): quicksilver amalgam on Velloran glass, made in Moon or Mercury hours. A good mirror shows "true," without the faint flattering haze of a cheap one. The mirrored rooms of the great houses, the **Spiegelkabinette**, are a Velloran fashion, and an alchemical one (→ I03).
- **Coin**: gold struck at the Wiedenbrunn mint in Sun hours **rings true**. Assayers and moneylenders drop coins on stone to listen, the **Klangprobe**, and false coin sounds flat. Counterfeiters who know this strike in the Sun's hour too, so the mint changes its alloys every year.
- **Silver plate**: a silver cup blackens at the touch of a Saturn poison. Every great house drinks from silver, and every poisoner knows it, so poisoners use Venus poisons, which silver doesn't catch.
- **Pewter** (Zinngeschirr) is the commoner's plate. It is Jupiter-metal, solid and slow, and the joke is that it is why the Bürger are so respectable.

## 5. The blood: karat and temper

This is where the Work touches nobility, and where nobility starts to get ideas.

### Fineness
Bloodlines carry a planetary metal the way an ore does. The Work measures it as it measures gold: in **karat** (*Karat*, Catalan *quirats*), from 0 to 24. The **Hofprobieramt**, the Royal Assay Office of Blood (→ I49), takes a drop of blood on a black touchstone and reads the streak's color and sheen against a set of graded needles, the **Strichprobe**. It then enters the result in the **Feinbuch**, the book of fineness. A noble's karat is as public as his title and more consequential.

| Karat | Name | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 0–4 | *ohne Gehalt* ("without content") | Most people. Nothing visible. |
| 5–10 | **Anklang** (resonance) | Tinctures of your metal work noticeably better on you. Common in minor nobles and in mining families. |
| 11–16 | **Temper** | Bodily traits of the metal and minor Handwerk (see below). Most of the titled nobility. |
| 17–21 | **Feinblut** (fine blood) | Strong Handwerk; the great houses. |
| 22–24 | **Hochfein** (high fine) | Very strong, and increasingly likely to be **brittle** (§6). |

### How karat comes to be
1. **Inheritance.** A child of two parents fine in the same metal assays at about their average, plus one: *"Gleiches löst sich in Gleichem,"* like dissolves in like. A child of close kin assays at the average plus two or three. That is the whole temptation in one line (→ R06).
2. **Mixed parents.** A child of two different metals usually takes the stronger parent's metal at a lower fineness. Sometimes it carries two weak tempers. This is **Mischblut**, mixed blood, which assayers dislike because the streak smears.
3. **Occupation over generations.** Families who live, work, breathe and eat beside a metal for generations take some of it into their blood: roughly one karat every two generations, up to about fourteen. Iron-miners of the Red Kogel, silver-miners of the Weißach and salt-boilers of the Hallgau carry real fineness. This is **Grubenblut**, pit-blood, and it is a source of pride, resentment, money and sex (→ I50).
4. **The conception hour**, which can add about a karat (→ R02).
5. **Tinctures in pregnancy**, which can steady a child's fineness but not raise it much.

The Work *cannot* put karat into an adult. No tincture makes a commoner fine. Blood changes only by generations.

### Handwerk: what fine blood can do
At about 11 karat and up a person develops **Handwerk**, "hand-work," the metal's virtue working through their body and their touch. It is modest at the low end and formidable at the high end, and it is strongest in the planet's hour.

| Metal | The trait | Handwerk at high fineness | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sun / gold** | **Glanz** (presence): people turn toward you, defer, feel warmed; you heal quickly. | A royal Greifenstein can make a room fall silent by entering it, and sworn men feel their oaths tighten under his displeasure. | Pride; the Glanzwahn (see §6). |
| **Moon / silver** | **Gespür** (kin-sense): feeling the distress, pain and arousal of blood-kin nearby. | Sharing dreams with sleeping kin; drawing a fever out by touch; brightening or tarnishing silver at a touch; strong fertility; at the highest, bringing in milk without pregnancy. | Lunar eclipses; Mondsucht (sleepwalking, confusion); daughters, daughters, daughters. |
| **Mercury / quicksilver** | **Behändigkeit** (quickness): reflexes, wit, a tongue for languages. | Amalgamating metals by hand; loosening knots and seals by touch; learning a language in weeks; bodies that change easily, so high Mercury lines throw futas and femme sons (→ R10, R11). | The trembles; restlessness; the Zitterblut. |
| **Venus / copper** | **Gunst** (favor): sensing what others desire; a voice that moves. | Reading a room's desire like a page; voices that make strangers weep at an aria or agree to a treaty; making bells sing. | Jealousy; the Liebeswahn. |
| **Mars / iron** | **Härte** (hardness): pain dulled, wounds clotting, heat in a fight. | Sensing flaws in iron and steel by touch; making a forge-fire take; fighting on with wounds that would drop another man. | Temper; the Rostwut. |
| **Jupiter / tin** | **Gewicht** (weight): authority; a memory for documents. | Feeling whether a seal, coin or signature is true; recalling a ledger page seen once; a voice that ends arguments. | Rigidity; the Starre. |
| **Saturn / lead** | **Geduld** (patience): endurance, slow aging, resistance to poison. | Putting a person to sleep with a hand on the brow; sensing how near someone is to death (not the exact day, which belongs to the Thirteenth Trump); living to a hundred. | Melancholy; the Bleischlaf. |

### Why it matters in war and state
The officer caste is Mars-blooded, so armies are commanded by men who feel less pain and more rage than their troops. Couriers, translators and spies come from Mercury families, which is why the Mirabent hold the post (→ I41). Judges, auditors and the Hofkammer are Jupiter's. The Crown is Sun. The royal nursery, and with it the court's intimate intelligence, belongs to the Moon (→ R12). Diplomats and the music chapel are Venus. Jailers, physicians of the dying and the keepers of the royal crypt are Saturn.

### What commoners feel
Mostly nothing, and they know it. Ordinary people assay at 0–4. What they feel is the **difference**: the noble officer who doesn't flinch, the Moon-lady who knows you're lying about your sick child, the Holdegg envoy whose voice makes your knees go soft. And they feel the **market**: at the slave-fairs, pledges are assayed and priced partly by karat. A miner's daughter at 13 karat of iron is worth ten times a farm girl, because a Mars house will buy her womb to anneal its line (→ I50, I61).

## 6. Brittleness

Fineness bought too fast and too close goes **spröde**: brittle, like steel quenched too hard and never tempered. Above about 20 karat, and especially when that fineness comes from close kin breeding over several generations, the blood begins to fail.

- **In the body:** fragile bones, bleeding that won't stop (**Bluter**), the long heavy jaw the court calls the **Greifenkinn** after the royal family, bad teeth, short lives, many stillbirths, and in the end sterility. The proudest lines end quietly in an empty nursery.
- **In the temper** (each metal fails its own way):
  - **Sun: Glanzwahn**, grandiose delusion. The sufferer believes he is made of glass, or of gold, or that he is the sun. Fernán VI of Almagra, *el Rey de Vidrio*, will not let anyone touch him without gloves (→ I32).
  - **Moon: Mondsucht**, sleepwalking, confusion of dream with waking, nights of wandering.
  - **Mercury: Zitterblut**, the blood-trembles, a restlessness that never settles. The Mirabent call it *tremolor*.
  - **Venus: Liebeswahn**, love-madness: obsession, an inability to stop wanting one person. It is the reason House Holdegg will not marry close (→ I42).
  - **Mars: Rostwut**, rust-rage, berserk fits. Starkenberg dinners are seated with this in mind (→ I40).
  - **Jupiter: Starre**, rigidity, catatonia: hours spent unmoving, staring at a ledger.
  - **Saturn: Bleischlaf**, the lead-sleep, which can last years (→ I53).
- **On the touchstone:** brittle blood leaves a streak that **splittert**, breaking into flecks instead of drawing a clean line. Assayers note it in the Feinbuch with a little cross. Every great house dreads the little cross.

## 7. Annealing

**Anlassen** (Catalan *recuit*) is the Work's answer to brittleness. In the smithy, annealing is the gentle reheating that takes the brittleness out of hardened steel without softening it entirely. In the blood, it means reducing brittleness while keeping as much fineness as possible. The methods, roughly from cheapest to dearest:

1. **Gleichmetall-Ehe** (same-metal marriage): breed with an unrelated partner fine in the *same* metal. The child keeps most of the fineness and loses most of the brittleness. This is the respectable route, and the reason great houses of the same metal marry each other across borders. It costs dowries, alliances, and the trouble of having in-laws.
2. **Pit-blood**: breed with a commoner of the same metal from a mining family, at 10–14 karat. The child loses a few karat and nearly all its brittleness. It is far cheaper than a noble marriage, and it is done through concubines, pledges and Left-Hand bonds rather than marriage (→ I50, I56). This is why Mars houses keep **Kogelbräute** and why silver houses have quietly bought Weißach miners' sons for three centuries.
3. **The Anlasskur**, the annealing course: for a close-kin pregnancy, the mother takes a regimen of silver and tin tinctures, each in its hour, through all nine months. Conception must have been in a Jupiter hour with the Moon well placed. It costs 300 to 600 Greif and works about two times in three. The 1633 Annealing Edict requires it for any nearest-kin marriage dispensed by the Church (→ I55, I75).
4. **The Drittel rule**: after a close-kin generation, two generations outward. It is not law, only the custom the Edict encourages and sensible houses follow (→ R06).
5. **Temperance.** The Trump **XIV Die Mäßigkeit** can anneal blood by touch: a bearer's hand on a pregnant belly through a night, or on a brittle child, takes out the splitter. It is the rarest and most coveted method in the realm. The current bearer is the envoy of Asagiri, a man who did not know what he had until the Fall-Reckoners started following him (→ I37, I46).

None of these *cures* a brittle person once born. They can only prevent the next generation from being worse.

## 8. Practitioners and institutions

- **The Hofalchimist** (court alchemist) and his **Hoflaboratorium** in the Greifenburg's east wing, which serves the royal family.
- **The Seventh Faculty** of the University of Wiedenbrunn, founded by the Flask King in 1591, which trains and licenses alchemists. It issues the **Kolbenbrief** (licence) and the **Red Gown** of a doctor of the Art (→ I52). The Hofalchimist and the Faculty's Dean have hated each other for three generations of holders.
- **The Apothecaries' Gremium**, which sells licensed tinctures. Every bottle must carry the hour of making and the maker's mark.
- **Kräuterweiber**, the village herb-women, who make folk tinctures and Mondsud and attend births where there is no sworn midwife.
- **Winkelalchimisten** ("corner-alchemists"): the unlicensed hedge-alchemists of the **Kesselgasse** in Wiedenbrunn, who will make anything for money (→ I52).
- **House laboratories**: Ilsenau's **Mondküche** (→ R12); Kalteneck's **Bleiküche** (→ I44); Mirabent's quicksilver works at Cinabre (→ I41); Starkenberg's **Stückgießerei** (→ I40); Holdegg's bell-foundry and its Venusbitter cellars (→ I42).

## 9. What the Work cannot do

- **Transmute.** Lead does not become gold. The Flask King spent the Crown's credit proving it, and in 1604 his own alchemists declared the **Great Failure** (→ I73). Every apprentice learns the line: **"Gold macht Gold, Blei bleibt Blei"** (gold makes gold, lead stays lead). Anyone selling transmutation is a swindler, and the Faculty will say so in writing for a fee.
- **Raise the dead.** Saturnsaft can hold a dying man at the edge for days. It cannot bring him back.
- **Make desire from nothing.** Venusbitter fans a fire. It does not start one.
- **Change blood.** See §5. Only generations change blood.
- **Cure the brittle.** It can only manage them.
- **Change sex outright.** The Mercurial Course can give a woman a second nature, sometimes, at great risk (→ R10). Nothing gives a man a womb, except perhaps in combination with things the Work does not control (→ R14).
- **Touch the Cards.** No tincture makes a Blatt, moves one, or stops a fall (→ R04).

## 10. The black market

- **Pantsch**: adulterated tinctures. Mondwasser cut with river water and silver filings, Venusbitter that is mostly brandy and cochineal. *"Pantschen"* (to water the wine) is also slang for cheating on a spouse.
- **Katzengold-Tropfen** ("fool's-gold drops"): counterfeit Trinkgold sold to the vain. It is brass-colored brandy and does nothing, except that some of it contains enough lead to do something.
- **Smuggled quicksilver**, carried out of Cinabre in false-bottomed water-skins by miners, sold to Winkelalchimisten and Velloran mirror-makers without the Corts' duty (→ I30).
- **Erbschaftspulver** ("inheritance powder"): Saturn poison. It is slow, white and tasteless, and it passes a silver cup if cut with a Venus salt. It is the commonest weapon in a long-lived family. Kalteneck sells the antidote, which some find suspicious.
- **Forged assay certificates**, for marriages and for pledges' prices (→ I49).
- **Stolen Mondküche Dunkelwasser**, the best contraceptive in the realm, resold at four times the price in the Rosengrund (→ I23).

## 11. Foreign Works

- **Kesrân** distills better than anyone: rosewater, attar, and the **Golden Elixir** of the Padishah's physicians. Its glassblowers are as good as Vellora's. Kesrâni alchemy is part of medicine and astronomy rather than a separate guild (→ I34).
- **Vellora** makes the best glass and mirrors in the world and buys Rocalban quicksilver by the shipload. Velloran courtesans know every Venus preparation (→ I35).
- **Almagra**: the **Santo Crisol** (the Holy Crucible) licenses alchemists, burns frauds, and assays the blood of grandees with an obsession beyond Valdau's. Almagran assayers are the finest and the most corrupt (→ I32).
- **Ormánd** has copper mines and Venus-craft. Its **queen's water**, rosemary distilled in spirit, keeps old women desirable, according to Ormándi women (→ I33, I07).
- **The Sworn Valleys**: the Clear Church allows "honest medicine" and forbids "the Art's vanities." Valleys apothecaries are excellent and dour (→ I36).
- **Asagiri** has its own system, five phases instead of seven metals, and the envoy's physicians and the Seventh Faculty have been arguing about it in Latin for a year (→ I37).

## 12. Sayings and slang

- **"Gold macht Gold, Blei bleibt Blei"**: you can't make something out of nothing; also, people don't change.
- **"Er hat den Athanor ausgehen lassen"**: he ruined it through neglect.
- **"Spröde"**: brittle-blooded; also, in women, coy or prudish. *"Sie ziert sich, sie ist spröde"* is said of a woman who won't, or who is pretending not to want to.
- **"Feinling"**: a fine-blooded snob.
- **"Katzengold"**: anything fake, especially fake nobility.
- **"Quecksilbrig"**: restless; can't sit still.
- **"Zinnern"**: dull, solid, pewtery; a clerk.
- **"Blei im Hintern"**: lead in the backside, i.e. lazy.
- **"Ein rostiger Kolben"**: an impotent man, especially an old soldier.
- **"Kolben"** (retort) and **"Tiegel"** (crucible) are vulgar slang for the sexes, **"Tinktur"** for what passes between them, and **"Athanor"** is the poets' word for a womb (→ I08).

## HOOKS

1. **The Mondküche batch.** A batch of Ilsenau Dunkelwasser was made in the wrong hour; a clerk misread the Stundenuhr. It has already gone out to eleven noblewomen. In about three weeks some of them may learn that it didn't work, and the house has to decide whether to warn them, which means admitting the mistake and what the bottles were for.
2. **The splitter.** A noble family's newest child has just been assayed, and the Hofprobieramt's streak shows the little cross. The family wants the Feinbuch entry "re-tested." The assayer who can be bribed to re-test has a price that isn't money.
3. **Article Nine.** A Grenzer captain returns from the Velika with a box of Schlafkugeln taken from a *Kesrâni* patrol. If the Kesrâni are using Saturn-shot, they are breaking the truce first, which is very useful to the war party. Unless the Grenzer made them himself.
4. **Venusbitter, unlabelled.** At a private supper someone pours a Venusbitter so strong it can only have come from Holdegg's own cellar. Nobody admits to bringing it, and by the end of the evening several people have done things they wanted to do anyway.
5. **Temperance for hire.** A brittle great house quietly approaches the Asagiri envoy to ask for a night's annealing touch on a pregnant daughter. The envoy, who does not understand Valdauer customs about bodies and bedrooms, asks a member of the Ilsenau household to explain what exactly is being asked of him.
6. **A protagonist's drop of blood.** The Hofprobieramt sends its routine notice: every noble of the Seven Chairs over eighteen must be assayed for the Feinbuch, and an Ilsenau son of nineteen has never been. Whatever his streak shows (a karat higher than anyone expects, a splitter, or something the needles have never been graded for) will be in a public book by Merkurtag.
