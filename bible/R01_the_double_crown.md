# R01 · The Double Crown of Valdau and Rocalba

*The realm the story lives in: its shape, its government, its money and army, its peoples, and the pressures that are live in the spring of 1699.*

**See also:** I22 Wiedenbrunn · I29 Rocalba & Llucmar · I38 The Seven Chairs · I39 House Greifenstein · I21 The Sänfte & the Plätte · X01 Chronicle · X02 Calendar, Feasts, Coin & Prices

## 1. Names

The formal style is **die Kronen von Valdau und Rocalba**, in Catalan **les Corones de Valdau i Rocalba**. Only heralds and notaries say all of it. In Wiedenbrunn the realm is **die Doppelkrone** (the Double Crown) or simply **Valdau**, which annoys Rocalbans. In Llucmar people say **la Corona** when they are being polite and **els de dalt**, "the ones up there," when they are not.

The two halves:

- **Valdau** is the upland crown: mountains, the river Selde, salt, iron, silver, wine and the capital **Wiedenbrunn**. It speaks German with a Viennese grain, and has about five and a half million souls.
- **Rocalba** is the coastal principality on the **Middle Sea** (*die Mittelsee*, *la Mar Mitjana*): ports, coral, tuna, cloth, the quicksilver of Cinabre and the mother-shrine of the faith at **Montalba**. It speaks Catalan and has about one and a quarter million souls. Its capital is the port of **Llucmar**.

*Rocalba* means "white rock." That is the limestone massif of Montalba, which a sailor can see forty miles out, where the first Witnesses of the Wedding came down to preach in the year from which everyone counts (→ I54). Rocalbans never let anyone forget that the faith came down from *their* mountain. Valdauers never let Rocalbans forget who pays for the army.

Dates are given **n.H.**, *nach der Hochzeit* ("after the Wedding"); in Rocalba **d.N.**, *després de les Noces*. The present is the spring of **1699**.

## 2. The lay of the land

Picture a long upland country lying west to east, with a warm coast hung below its southwestern corner.

**The Firnwand** is the northern wall: glaciers, high pastures and passes that freeze shut. Beyond it lie **the Sworn Valleys** (→ I36), which make cheese, mercenaries and trouble.

**The Selde** is Valdau's spine. It rises in the western mountains, runs through the vine terraces of **Sonnleiten**, broadens past **Wiedenbrunn** and leaves the realm in the east through **the Ostpforte**, a gap between wooded hills where the river slips into the plains of **Ormánd** (→ I33). Everything heavy in Valdau travels on the Selde: salt down from the west, grain up from the east, timber both ways, and wine to every tavern in between.

**The southern ranges** (the **Kogelgebirg** of iron, the salt mountains of the **Hallgau**) separate Valdau from the sea. One road crosses them that can take a carriage, and only in summer: **the Brautsteig**, the Bride's Climb. It passes below the shrine of **Brautzell** (→ I31) and comes down the red valley of the **Vermell**, a river that runs pink after rain because it drains the cinnabar country of **Cinabre** (→ I30), to the sea at **Llucmar**. The Brautsteig closes with snow from about All-Dross (November) until Brauttag (April). For five months a year Rocalba can only be reached by sea, around through Velloran waters at Velloran prices, or by Mirabent couriers on foot over the mule-tracks. Rocalbans say *"l'hivern ens fa lliures"*, winter makes us free. The Lloctinent's power swells every November and shrinks every April (→ I21).

**The southeast** is the **Wehrgrenze**, the military frontier (→ I28), a belt of fortified villages along the river **Velika**. Across it lies **Kesrân** (→ I34).

**West of Rocalba**, over the dry **Serra del Vent**, the red plateau of **Almagra** begins (→ I32). **Across the Middle Sea**, five to eight days' sail southeast from Llucmar, the lagoon-city of **Vellora** sits on its islands (→ I35). **Asagiri** is a year away by sea and caravan, and Wiedenbrunn knows it only through its embassy (→ I37).

### The Kornau plain (no separate file)

East of Wiedenbrunn the hills open into **the Kornau**, flat wheat country running to the Ostpforte. It is a land of great estates, stork-nests on every church, long white roads and lime trees around wells. The old **Kronadel** families who own it live in Wiedenbrunn and visit for the harvest and the hunts.

- **Known for:** bread grain for the capital; and the **Hofgestüt Kornau**, the royal stud where the white horses of Almagran blood are bred for the riding school (→ I10). People who have seen the stallions at the **Kornauer Hengstschau** in May never quite stop talking about it.
- **Custom:** the harvest is reaped by bands of hired **Schnitterinnen**, women reapers who walk in from the hill villages. They choose a **Kranzlkönig** from among the estate's young men, crown him with the last sheaf, and claim the right to "bind" him: he is tied with straw and has to buy his way out with wine or kisses. Estate managers have learned that the harvest goes faster if they are not around that week.
- **Tension:** **Robot**. A Kornau peasant owes his lord three days' labor a week in harvest and two otherwise. House Ehrenreith's reformers want the **Robotpatent** capped at two days year-round. The landowners call this "Ehrenreith arithmetic" and block it in the Kornau Landtag every year. Unrest flickers whenever the price of bread rises.
- **Worth a visit:** the stud. Also the inn **Zum Storch** at Kornau-Markt, where the horse dealers drink and where an Ormándi horse-coper will sell you anything, including information.

## 3. The Crown and how it is held

**The King** is **Albrecht VII** of the House of **Greifenstein** (→ I39), reigning since 1687. His personality and household are left to the story. Constitutionally he is:

- **König von Valdau**, an absolute monarch in theory and a negotiated one in practice. He needs the Landtage for money and the Seven Chairs for anything touching the succession.
- **el Comte-Príncep de Rocalba**, a sworn and conditional ruler. Each new king must ride over the Brautsteig within two years of accession and swear on the Bride's altar at Montalba to keep the **Constitucions** and the **Usos de Montalba**. This is **el Jurament**. Until he swears, the Corts may lawfully refuse every tax. Albrecht swore in 1688, in a rainstorm that Rocalbans still describe as the Bride weeping and Valdauers as ordinary weather.

**Succession.** The crown passes by male primogeniture. Failing all male heirs of the body it passes to daughters, because since **the Distaff Sanction of 1555** the crown has been a **Kunkellehen**, a fief that can fall to the distaff (→ R05). Disputed cases go to **the Seven Chairs** (→ I38), the seven planetary houses who confirm successions and regencies.

**In Rocalba** the King rules through a **Lloctinent** (lieutenant-general) resident in the **Palau del Lloctinent** in Llucmar. The current Lloctinent is a count of House Ehrenreith. He is competent, punctual and loathed. Rocalbans call him **el Rellotge**, the Clock.

## 4. Government

### In Valdau
- **The Kronrat** (crown council) meets in the **Ratsstube** of the Greifenburg on Merkurtag and Saturntag mornings. Members by office:
  - the **Obersthofmeister**, Lord Steward of the King's household. Currently a prince of Holdegg.
  - the **Obersthofmeisterin**, Mistress of the Queen's household. Hereditary to **Ilsenau** since 1499 (→ R12).
  - the **Hofkanzler**, chancellor, the realm's letter-writer and seal-keeper.
  - the **Hofkammerpräsident**, at the treasury. Habitually an **Ehrenreith** (→ I43).
  - the **Hofkriegsratspräsident**, head of the war council. Habitually a **Starkenberg** (→ I40).
  - the **Archbishop of the Bridegroom's See**.
  - the **Oberster Probiermeister**, head of the royal assay office of blood (→ I49).
  - and whoever the King happens to like that year.
- **The Hofstellen** are the court offices that actually do things: the Hofkanzlei, the Hofkammer, the Hofkriegsrat, the **Hofprobieramt** (→ I49), the **Stundenamt** (→ I51), the **Hallamt** (salt, → I24), the **Pfandamt** (pledges, → I61), and the **Titelkanzlei**, which publishes the Titelbuch of forms of address (→ I01). Their clerks, ennobled by the hundred, are the **Tintenadel**, the ink-nobility (→ R05).
- **The Landtage.** Each Valdauer land (Wiedenbrunn-Land, Hallgau, Eisenmark, Weißach, Sonnleiten, Kornau) has its diet of four **Stände**: prelates, lords, knights and towns. The Landtage vote the **Kontribution**, the land tax, and grumble for weeks first. The Wehrgrenze has no diet. It belongs to the Hofkriegsrat body and soul.

### In Rocalba
- **Les Corts** sit in the **Palau de les Corts** in Llucmar in four chambers, the **braços** ("arms"). Since 1340 each braç has been named after a suit of the Rocalban card-deck (→ I29):
  - **braç d'Oros**: merchants, shipowners and the *ciutadans honrats* (honored citizens).
  - **braç de Copes**: the clergy, led by Montalba.
  - **braç d'Espases**: nobles and *cavallers*.
  - **braç de Bastos**: towns, guilds, and the free farmers, the *pagesos*.
- **Els Quatre Pals** ("the Four Suits") is the Corts' standing deputation, one deputy per braç. They collect the **dret del General** (Rocalba's own tax), keep the Constitucions, and write to Wiedenbrunn in a Catalan so correct it reads as an insult.
- **El Consell dels Quaranta** governs the city of Llucmar.
- **The Llotja** is the merchants' exchange and court, which judges sea-law, bills of exchange and the sale of captives (→ I29).

### The Seven Chairs
The planetary college of the seven great houses (→ I38): Greifenstein (Sun), Ilsenau (Moon), Mirabent (Mercury), Holdegg (Venus), Starkenberg (Mars), Ehrenreith (Jupiter) and Kalteneck (Saturn). It confirms successions, appoints regents, and since the Flask King's reorganization of 1591 it has fixed the **planetary precedence** at court. The Moon sits second only to the Crown, which is one of the quiet reasons everyone curtsies to Ilsenau women.

### Law
- **Valdau** has customary **Landrecht** and royal **Patente**, and a jungle of exemptions. Nobles go before the **Hofgericht** or the **Landmarschall's court**. Commoners go before city and manorial courts. Pledges and pledge disputes go to the **Pfandgericht** (→ I61). Marriage and kin matters go to the church courts (→ I55).
- **Rocalba** has the **Usos de Montalba** (old custom), the **Constitucions** (statutes made by the Corts with the prince), and sea-law at the Llotja. The **Sentència de Montalba** of 1486 abolished labor dues on Rocalban peasants. That is why a Rocalban *pagès* is freer than a Valdauer peasant, and also why Rocalba buys more slaves.

## 5. Money, trade and debt

*Coin values and a full price list are in X02.*

- **Valdauer coin:** the gold **Greif** (a griffin; in Wiedenbrunn slang a *Vogel*, a bird), the silver **Bräutling** (with the Wedding couple on it, so called *a couple*) and the copper **Kupferling**. 1 Greif = 10 Bräutlinge = 240 Kupferlinge.
- **Rocalban coin:** the gold **núvia** (the Bride alone, a jab at the Valdauer couple), the silver **sou** and the copper **menut**.
- **What Valdau sells:** salt (to Ormánd and the Valleys), iron and cannon (to anyone not at war with it), wine, grain, and silver (less every year).
- **What Rocalba sells:** quicksilver (the realm's most strategic export, → I30), coral, salt tuna, woollen cloth (*drap de Llucmar*), ratafia, and captives resold from the frontier.
- **What the realm buys:** coffee and silk from Kesrân, glass and mirrors from Vellora, chocolate by way of Almagra, cheese and soldiers from the Valleys, and horses and copper from Ormánd.
- **Taxes:** the **Kontribution** (land tax, voted by the Landtage), the **Ungeld** (excise on wine and meat), the salt revenue (Hallamt), customs and Maut tolls (→ I21), the Pfandamt's registry fees, and the Rocalban *dret del General*, which the Crown cannot touch without the Corts.
- **What the realm cannot tax:** the **Nahbrief money**, the fees the Church charges for kin marriages (→ I55). Under the Capitulations of Montalba, the Bride's See is exempt from royal taxation. The Abbess lends that money to the Quatre Pals at three in the hundred. The result is that **every Valdauer noble who pays to marry a cousin is helping to finance Rocalba's ability to say no to Valdau.** Ehrenreith's clerks have calculated this to the Kupferling. Rocalbans find it hilarious.
- **The debt:** the Hofkammer owes the **Banco della Fede** of Vellora roughly eleven million Greif, secured partly on the Weißach silver mines. The debt has been rolled over since the Flask King (→ I73).

## 6. The army and the sea

- **The standing army** has about thirty-eight thousand men in regiments owned by their **Inhaber**, the proprietor colonels, who raise them, clothe them in their own colors, and expect the Crown to pay them back eventually. House Starkenberg owns six regiments.
- **The Grenzer** are the frontier militia of about twenty-five thousand soldier-farmers (→ I28). They are cheap, tough, and impossible to march more than a week from home without desertion.
- **The Hofartillerie** has its cannon cast from Red Kogel iron in the hour of Mars, the **Kogelrohre**, which take a heavier charge without bursting (→ R03, I25). This is Valdau's real military advantage, and the reason the Eisenmark is guarded more closely than the Treasury.
- **The galleys of Llucmar** are fourteen war-galleys rowed by penal and captive oarsmen (→ R07). They are maintained by the Corts and lent to the Crown when the Crown asks nicely.
- **The Leibgarde** of the royal palace is made up of the **Hartschiere** (noble archers in red and gold) and a company of pikemen hired from the Sworn Valleys, the **Schwurgarde**. The Valleys' men are kept on because they come from a Clear Church country where no one sells pledges, and because the court believes, inaccurately, that they cannot be seduced.
- **How the Arts go to war:** the **Mars ration** of courage tincture before battle; cannon cast in the right hour; opening barrages timed by the Stundenamt; Mirabent riders carrying orders at a speed no one else can match; and the Cards. Starkenberg holds the **Tower** (XVI), which cracks walls. The Ormándi ally's Palatine holds the **Chariot** (VII). The enemy Padishah holds the **Emperor** (IV) (→ R02, R03, R04).

## 7. Peoples, tongues and faiths

- **Brunnerisch** is the Wiedenbrunn dialect: soft, ironic, full of diminutives and Almagran and Kesrâni loanwords (*Kaffee*, *Sofa*, *Schokolad*). Its two moods are **Schmäh** (teasing charm) and **Grant** (cultivated grumpiness). A Wiedenbrunner can insult you so politely that you thank him.
- **Alpine dialects** of the Hallgau and Eisenmark are thick enough that court ladies at Bad Sulzau keep a maid to translate the bath-attendants.
- **Grenzerisch** is the frontier's mixed speech, Slavic-flavored, with its own oaths.
- **Catalan** is spoken in Rocalba at every level. Rocalban nobles speak German at court and Catalan to their servants, their lovers and each other.
- **Court Almagran** is the language of ceremony. Since the Ceremonial was imported from the Almagran cousins (→ I69), certain formulas at court are spoken only in Castilian-flavored Almagran, which most courtiers pronounce badly.
- **Latin** is used for church and law. **Ormándi** is spoken along the eastern border. **Kesrâni** is spoken by captives, coffee-sellers and the embassy.
- **Faith.**
  - About nine in ten belong to **the Church of the Wedding** (→ I54).
  - About one in sixteen in Valdau are **Lautere**, members of the **Clear Church** (→ I74). They are strong among the Hallgau miners and the respectable Bürger of the capital.
  - A small community of Kesrâni captives and traders keep **the Lamp**.
  - The Asagiri embassy keeps its own household shrine and draws a crowd whenever it burns incense.

## 8. Society at a glance

*The detail lives in R05 (Nobility), R07 (Bondage), I04 (Below Stairs) and I22 (Wiedenbrunn).*

- **The great houses**: the Seven Chairs and a dozen others, rich in karat, offices and debt.
- **The lesser nobility**: counts, barons, knights, the poor **Krautjunker** of the provinces and the new ink-nobles of the offices.
- **The Bürger**: guildmasters, merchants, physicians, coffee-house keepers. Respectable, prudish in public, and avid readers of every scandal sheet.
- **The little people of the capital**: Fiaker drivers, washerwomen, sedan-chair carriers, Bänkelsänger, servants, and *Bettgeher* who rent a bed by the shift.
- **Peasants**: in Valdau they owe Robot labor to their lords. In Rocalba they have been free *pagesos* since 1486, and are poorer and prouder for it.
- **The bound**: pledged debtors, war-captives, born slaves and convicts (→ R07). They are everywhere, from a duchess's dressing room to the bottom of the quicksilver mines.
- **Guilds** (*Zünfte*, Catalan *gremis*): every trade has one, with its own saint, tavern and ball. Some guilds exist only in this world: the **Fall-Reckoners** (→ I48), the **Seelenmakler** (→ I61), the **Kaffeesieder** (→ I67), the **Sesselträger** (→ I21) and the sworn **Hebammen** (→ I65).

## 9. Climate and the year's rhythm

- **Valdau** is continental. Winters are snowy and long; Fasching balls are danced with frost on the windows, and in the hardest years the Selde freezes at Wiedenbrunn. Spring is short and violent. Summers are hot and stormy. Autumn is golden and wine-soaked.
- **Rocalba** is Mediterranean: mild wet winters and dry blazing summers. The **tramuntana** wind comes down off the mountains for three days at a time and makes everyone quarrelsome. Rocalbans blame it for murders, adulteries and votes in the Corts.
- **The court's year:**
  - **Fasching** in Wiedenbrunn, January to the Läuterwochen (→ I70).
  - **The Läuterwochen**, forty days of fasting and closed theatres (→ I05).
  - **Brauttag** and spring (→ I14).
  - **The Hofreise** to Bad Sulzau for the summer, including the **Hochzeitsnacht** at midsummer (→ I21, I24, I15).
  - **The hunts** and the Ägidi Fair in early autumn (→ I10, I63).
  - Back to the capital for **Haldentag**, **Erbentag** and the **Zwölften** (→ I18, I16).

## 10. What is live in the spring of 1699

None of these is a plot. They are pressures already loaded into the world on the morning the protagonist wakes.

1. **The Glass King's empty cradle.** **Fernán VI** of Almagra (→ I32) is thirty-seven, sickly, assayed at 23 karat of Sun and brittle as his nickname, *el Rey de Vidrio*. He has no heir and holds the **World** (XXI). When he dies, two things fall at once. His crown will go to whoever can take it: the Greifenstein candidate, **Archduke Ferdinand Karl**, the King's younger brother, against at least two native Almagran claimants. His card will fall to whoever is nearest his heart, and the Fall-Reckoners' betting books on that are thicker than the Diarium (→ I48). The court is split into a **Schokoladenpartei** (pro-Almagran, chocolate-drinking, eager for the claim) and everyone else (→ I05).
2. **The Willow Bridge.** The twenty-year **Peace of the Willow Bridge** with Kesrân, signed in 1684 after the Second Siege (→ I76), expires in **1704**. Starkenberg's war party wants to let it lapse and take back the lower Velika while the Padishah is busy in the east. Holdegg's peace party wants it renewed with better coffee terms. The Kesrâni have sent a new ambassador, who has so far said nothing whatsoever, beautifully.
3. **The Great Conjunction of 1702.** Jupiter and Saturn meet every twenty years. The last time, 1683, the Kesrâni Reckoner of Hours timed a siege to it. The Stundenamt predicts that in 1702 every temper in the realm will run hot, Trump-gifts will run strong, and children conceived under it will be strong in karat (→ R02, I51). Noble houses are already booking conception hours three years ahead. Midwives are already raising prices.
4. **The Banco's silver.** The Weißach mines are nearly worked out. The Banco della Fede holds their mortgages and has begun foreclosing on the outer shafts (→ I26, I35). Ilsenau's silver income, and therefore part of its independence, is shrinking.
5. **The pit-blood petition.** In 1679 the miners' brotherhoods of the Red Kogel and the Weißach petitioned the Crown to recognize **Grubenadel**, a lesser nobility for commoner families whose generations in the mines have given them noble-grade karat (→ I50). The petition was tabled. In 1698 it was renewed with four thousand marks and crosses. The Hofprobieramt has to give an opinion, which it does not want to do.

**Also simmering:**
- **The Galley Subsidy.** In 1698 the Corts refused the Crown a special tax for new galleys until the King confirms three Rocalban privileges he would rather not.
- **The lost Empress.** Trump III, the Empress, has been missing since 1693, when the Holdegg bearer died and the Blatt appeared on no one anyone knows (→ I46).
- **The Codex Ehrenreith**, a draft law code that would register every pledged person in the realm (→ I43). Owners hate it. The Seelenmakler hate it more.
- **The Dreher question.** Will the scandalous turning dance be permitted at this Fasching's court balls (→ I11)? The Obersthofmeisterin has not ruled.
- **The Salt Chair.** House Sulzbach is petitioning for an eighth chair among the Seven, for salt (→ I38).
- **The Asagiri envoy.** He holds Temperance, the one Trump that can anneal brittle blood, and he has no idea what that makes him worth (→ I37).
- **The Clear preachers.** Lautere preachers are working the Hallgau salt villages, and the Hallamt is nervous.

## 11. What people say

- **Valdauers about themselves:** *"Bei uns ist nichts erlaubt und alles verziehen."* ("Here nothing is permitted and everything is forgiven.") Also: *"Die Lage ist hoffnungslos, aber nicht ernst."* ("The situation is hopeless, but not serious.")
- **Rocalbans about Valdauers:** *Knödels*, dumplings. Slow, sentimental, ruled by priests and titles, and they marry their cousins and then complain about the chin.
- **Valdauers about Rocalbans:** *Pfennigfuchser*, penny-pinchers, who count the change after a funeral. Also *Rauxa-Leut'*, people of sudden madness: sensible for a year, and then they burn a customs house.
- **Almagrans about Valdau:** a court of second cousins who learned their manners from Almagra and their morals from Vellora.
- **Ormándi about Valdau:** clerks on horseback.
- **Kesrâni about Valdau:** a people who keep slaves but will not let them become viziers. Stingy even in tyranny.
- **Velloran about Valdau:** an excellent debtor. Pays late, pays always.
- **The Sworn Valleys about Valdau:** a sink of lace and incest, and a fine place to sell cheese.

## HOOKS

1. **The Jurament in winter.** A Rocalban notary arrives over the Brautsteig in late October with a document claiming the King's oath of 1688 was sworn on the wrong altar, a side-chapel rebuilt in 1652. If the Corts accept it, Rocalba can refuse all taxes until he swears again, and the pass will close in a fortnight. Someone has to decide whether to race the snow.
2. **The Kornau binding.** At harvest, a party of court visitors to a Kornau estate is caught by the Schnitterinnen, and a young noble is crowned Kranzlkönig and tied in straw. By custom he must buy his way out, and the reapers' leader has named her price.
3. **The Clock and the Bride's money.** The Lloctinent *el Rellotge* has evidence that the Abbess of Montalba lent Nahbrief money to the Quatre Pals to buy arms. Publishing it would expose which Valdauer houses paid for kin marriages that year. Several of those houses are at court, and some of them are near the protagonist's.
4. **The Chocolate Party's dinner.** An invitation arrives to a Schokoladenpartei supper whose guests are all quietly counting days until the Glass King dies. Accepting puts a name on a list. Refusing puts it on another.
5. **Four thousand marks.** A miner's daughter from the Weißach comes to the capital carrying the original 1698 pit-blood petition to deliver to "a member of the Seven Chairs in person." She has been told the Ilsenau will listen, because Ilsenau is silver. She has also been followed.
6. **Winter makes us free.** In December a Rocalban cousin, stranded in Wiedenbrunn by the closed pass, proposes to spend the months until Brauttag in the Ilsenau household. With the Brautsteig closed, anything she sees cannot be reported home until April. Unless she writes by sea, through Vellora, where letters are read.
